# Topic: a namespace is not a rung, and a stamp nothing follows is not a stamp

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `mock/tools/obligation-coverage`

`mock obligation-coverage` sorts every obligation into a tier by walking the typed `obligation` edges.
The tier comes from one constant, `EDGES`, which maps a namespace to a tier: a `ruling` meets an
obligation, a `proposal` proposes against it, a `retirement` closes one route to it.

The string `rung` does not appear in the file. So the instrument that measures whether the canon can
carry a design is blind to whether the thing reaching an obligation is canon, direction, or an unblessed
proposal, and it is wrong in both directions on the committed registry at once.

## Over-reporting: the one met row is the one row he refused

Exactly one `ruling` in the repository carries an `obligation` edge:

    ruling::ingest_is_the_consumers_and_the_c_abi_is_where_it_ends_up
      rung = "stated"
      obligation = ["an_exact_width_container_a_consumer_can_alias_and_pin"]

That single row is the whole of `met 1`. It sits at `stated`, and its own `note` records that op was
asked to bless it and declined, in terms. There is a second row recording the refusal as a statement of
its own, `ruling::he_declined_to_bless_the_ingest_row_as_written`, and a question opened to unblock the
promotion, `question::what_the_ingest_row_claims_and_what_turns_on_it`.

So the tool's best tier is produced by the one row op looked at and would not bless, and the doc comment
justifying that tier reads "A ruling names it and op has been in the loop, which is the only tier that
is an answer." He was in the loop. The loop's output was a refusal.

**Zero obligations are reached by a direct edge from a ratified ruling**, which is what the report should
have been saying and could not.

## Under-reporting: the stamp is in the registry and nothing follows it

`proposal.obligation` is documented in `mockspace.toml` as the obligations a proposal would meet **if it
were stamped**, and `ruling.ratifies` is the stamp: "Proposals this stamps." The two compose, and the
walk reads the naming namespace only, so a stamped proposal is filed as proposed forever.

There is one live instance:

    ruling::behaviour_is_stated_per_declared_signature_and_the_premise_dissolves
      rung = "ratified", ratified_by = "experts"
      ratifies = [..., "every_operation_arvo_declares_is_a_function_of_the_declared_width", ...]

    proposal::every_operation_arvo_declares_is_a_function_of_the_declared_width
      obligation = ["every_standard_convention_expressible_as_an_alias_over_the_primitives"]

Reported `proposed`. Reached by a ratified ruling through the stamp.

**This is not a new class and the registry says so about itself.**
`question::adaptation_in_identity_or_realisation` records of its own answer that nothing was decided to
close it, that the edge was already there and nothing followed it, "because it runs `ruling.ratifies`
into `proposal.answers` and every reader of the settled set read `ruling.answers` alone". The same
unfollowed hop, one field over, found by a different seat for a different reason.

## Why the suite did not catch either

Twenty tests, with real negative controls in them, and none plants a rung. The fixture builds a row
carrying an `obligation` field and nothing else, so every ruling any arm ever sees is rungless, and
`a_ruling_meets_an_obligation_and_a_proposal_only_proposes_it` asserts `Met` for that rungless row.

Setup that helps, exactly: every input is one the implementation handles, so the path that breaks is
never entered, and the one property the file asserts about itself in prose has no arm anywhere.

## The tier vocabulary is the decision, not just the arithmetic

Splitting `Met` by rung is the obvious repair and it leaves the harder question open, which is what the
tier words should be. Three shapes were considered.

**Keep `Met` at the top and add one tier under it for every non-ratified ruling.** Fewest moving parts,
and it is what `235` recommends. It folds `in_force` and `stated` into one word, which is the same lossy
collapse the defect came from one level down: enforced by this repo's lints without convergence is not
the same state as an ack, and the schema separates them.

**Report no tier at all, and name what reaches each obligation with its rung.** Honest, and it makes the
reader do the ordering. They will do it from the namespace, which is where this started.

**Make the tier word the rung.** `ratified`, `in_force`, `stated`, `proposed`, `unsettled`,
`route-closed`, `nothing`, strongest first. Six of the seven are the schema's own words for a rung or a
namespace, so a tier in the report can be grepped for in `mockspace.toml` and found, and nothing is
folded. This is the one that lands.

`met` goes as a tier word. It is a claim about the obligation where every other word is a fact about the
row that reached it, and it is the word that carried the false claim, so retiring it makes a reader
re-read what the tier means rather than assume it. The report says in one line that `ratified` is the
only tier that is met, which is the sentence the word was doing badly.

## What the ladder rests on, and the one comparison nothing makes

`ratified` over everything is the provenance ladder rather than a reading. `in_force` over `stated`
follows the schema's own ordering of the four rung values and the difference it states between
enforcement and an ack. `stated` over `proposed` is the ladder again: his direction over an agent's
claim, which no `standing` value changes.

`proposed` over `unsettled` is a reading and nothing in the canon makes it. The ladder is how far an
obligation has got toward met, a proposal is argued content that would meet it once stamped, and a
ruling at `open` is him declining to settle, which contributes none. Nothing rests on it: no ruling at
`open` carries an obligation edge. It is one line to move.

`unsettled` also holds a ruling whose rung cannot be read, which is the pessimistic direction on
purpose. The rung prints verbatim beside the row either way, so the two are distinguishable on the line
while sharing a tier.

## What does not change

The tool stays a tool. Nothing here adds a state to refuse: an unanswered obligation is still the state
of unfinished work and gating on a count would still invent a deadline nobody set. The four questions
land where they landed.

No new lint is owed either. `an-unratified-ruling-stamps-a-proposal` already refuses a stamp from
anything below `ratified`, at HARD_ERROR, so the hop this now follows is guarded at the gate. The walk
checks the rung anyway rather than assuming the gate has run, since a measurement that trusted it would
report a proposal as canon on exactly the row the gate exists to catch.

## Out of scope, and reported rather than fixed

`ratified_by` is described in the schema as required on a `ratified` row. Eighteen of the thirty-one
ratified rulings carry none. The requirement is conditional and lives in a field description, which the
loader cannot enforce, so nothing checks it. That is a lint somebody owes and it is not this round.
