# Src changelist: the identity quantifies over the magnitude range

Doc changelist: `202609011445_changelist.doc.lock.md`.

## The arithmetic this implements

From `phase + i * quantum(m) = 0` with `phase = (PN/PD) * r^BASE` and
`q(m) = r^(BASE + SLOPE*m)`, dividing by `r^BASE` and clearing the denominator:

```
i * PD * r^(SLOPE*m) = -PN
```

`BASE` cancels and does not appear, which the sketch checks by moving it and
watching no answer move. The remaining work is one exact integer division per
magnitude, and the existential is a search over the admitted magnitudes.

## CHANGE: free fn `has_additive_identity` FROM evaluating at magnitude zero TO searching the admitted magnitudes
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/format.rs`
Was `PHASE_NUM % PHASE_DEN == 0 && slot_in_range(-(PHASE_NUM / PHASE_DEN))`,
which reads no coordinate of the quantum. Becomes a search for an admitted
magnitude whose cancelling slot is in the slot range. A format admitting no
magnitude now answers false, because the loop does not run.
Verification: `grep -n "MAGNITUDES" mock/crates/arvo-format/src/format.rs`
returns a hit inside `has_additive_identity`, where there was none.

## CHANGE: const `MAGNITUDE_SEARCH_BOUND` ADDED
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/format.rs`
Both loops stop at 127 and that is exact rather than approximate. `i128` holds
magnitudes below `2^127`, so at a radix of at least two the running product
overflows within 127 steps and every later magnitude answers `None` anyway. At a
radix below two the product never changes, so every magnitude answers the same
and stopping early answers it. Either way the bounded search returns what the
unbounded one would.
Verification: two tests below pin both halves of that derivation.

## The tests this adds, and what each is for

The existing phase arms all run through `Biased`, which fixes the denominator at
two, the quantum at `Constant` and the slots at `Signed`. So they measure one
point of three axes. A test-local format generic over both phase coordinates,
the quantum and the slot range is what lets the sweep move them.

**The cancelling slot, on its own.** It is the right slot where one exists, and
`None` where the phase is fractional at that magnitude. Both directions, because
a function stuck at `None` passes the second alone.

**The denominator sweep.** Every denominator from one to sixteen, plus a spread
past it, against numerators that are and are not multiples. Previously two was
the only denominator any arm used.

**Both quantum families and both slot families.** The identity law asserted over
the cross product rather than at `Constant` and `Signed` only.

**The whole phase whose cancelling slot is out of range.** Phase four against a
slot range that stops at -2, which answers false, against the same phase and a
wider range, which answers true. This is the case the previous round's four arms
could not reach, because all four asserted true.

**The two magnitude-range witnesses.** The growing quantum whose cancelling slot
comes into range at a higher magnitude, and the shrinking quantum whose
fractional phase becomes whole at a higher magnitude. Each with the control that
cuts the magnitude range to one and asserts the answer goes back to false, so the
search is what found it.

**The empty magnitude range.** `MAGNITUDES = 0`, asserting no identity and that
the quantum is inadmissible. The construction stays in the file permanently.

**The zero phase denominator.** Asserting no identity and that the format is
inadmissible. The construction stays permanently. Nothing asserted this before;
the branch was added by the previous round and no arm entered it.

**The search bound.** That a magnitude past the bound answers `None` at a radix
of at least two with a nonzero slope, and that a zero slope answers the same at
every magnitude. Those two are the derivation above, pinned.

**The obligations, each with its control.** Each verdict function rejects the
wrong construction and admits every shipped instance, and a further test asserts
the two verdicts differ, so a verdict stuck at one value fails rather than
passing one of the pair.
