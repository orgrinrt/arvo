# Doc changelist: the identity quantifies over the magnitude range

Source topic: `202609011445_topic.the-identity-quantifies-over-the-magnitude-range.md`.

## `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`

### The phase paragraph

It says the deciding property is whether the phase is a whole multiple of the
quantum. That is right for the constant family and under-determined for the
indexed one, where there is no single quantum: the step changes with magnitude,
so "the quantum" names a different value at each of them.

It is stated against the membership predicate three paragraphs above it instead.
Zero is on the grid exactly when some admitted magnitude and slot cancel the
phase, which is the same existential the predicate already carries and not a new
rule. The whole-multiple sentence survives as what that reduces to in the
constant family, which is where it was measured and where it is true.

The paragraph gains the two consequences the shipped code did not have. A whole
phase can be one whose cancelling slot is outside the slot range, and then the
identity is off the grid despite the phase being whole. A fractional phase can
become whole at a higher magnitude when the step shrinks, and then the identity
is on the grid despite the phase being fractional. Both are witnessed in
`mock/research/sketches/phase-over-the-magnitude-range/`.

The clause saying only a fractional part takes the identity off goes rather than
being narrowed. It is false in the indexed family in both directions, and the
half-step geometry it was reaching for is stated in the sentence after it.

### The law paragraph

It says a phase that is not a whole multiple removes the identity and one that is
leaves it at a shifted slot, and that the test asserts both directions. It states
the predicate form instead, and says the sweep runs both quantum families, both
slot families and more than one denominator, because the previous cut of it ran
at one point of each and could not see any of this.

### A new section on what the other three contracts owe

`Slots` states its obligations as a checked const and the other three state
theirs in prose. The section says what each now asserts and, following the
existing one for `Slots`, exactly when it fires: at codegen, so `cargo build`
refuses and `cargo check` does not, and the verdict-returning form beside it is
what a test uses at check time.

It says what each obligation names and nothing further, because the design
already had to correct a sentence that claimed something was total when nothing
was checking, and a half-stated obligation invites the same reading.

### The contracts table and the function list

The table's `Ambient`, `Quantum` and `Format` rows gain the obligation each
supplies, as the `Slots` row does not yet say either. The list of free functions
gains `cancelling_slot`, `is_admissible_ambient`, `is_admissible_quantum` and
`is_admissible_format`.

## What the source changelist carries and this one does not

Three rustdoc sites carry the same claim: the `PHASE_NUM` comment and the
`has_additive_identity` comment in `src/format.rs`, and the `Biased` point's own
doc in `src/lib.rs`. They are `.rs` and belong to the source phase. They are
named here so the class is fixed rather than the two instances that happen to sit
in a template.

## What is not touched

`proposal::membership_of_the_representable_set_is_one_affine_predicate` closes on
a sentence saying a nonzero phase decides whether the set carries an additive
identity at all. A ratified ruling stamped that row and a design round does not
correct canon. This round derives from the affine-predicate sentence the row
opens with, which is the existential over the magnitude and the slot, and the
closing sentence is a gloss on it rather than a rule it contradicts.

Nothing here answers `question::is_the_cross_kind_join_closed_or_priced` or
`question::arbitrary_width_demands_in_the_canon`, and nothing here depends on
either being closed.
