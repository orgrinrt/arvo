# The slot review

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Scope:** `arvo-format`'s completion in range, the applied map's oracle and its wide sweep, the
four-limb integer's own laws and file, addition's carried refusal, and the prose the review named.

The first review of `work/arvo-format-slot`, recorded in
`mock/research/202611140405_review-arvo-format-slot-1/REVIEW.md`, passed the canon gate and found the
arithmetic correct. It found two blockers, three things to fix and three nits.

Completion answers a position in range by reducing it modulo the span, the same as a wrap. The answer is
right, since a slot in `[lo, hi]` is its own residue on the range, but it divides the four-limb floor
every time, where before this branch the in-range answer was the floor plus the step. The design says the
two regions are separable and the completion region the identity on a slot already in range, and the
function's own comment says the same, so the code drifted from both. In range the floor lies in
`[lo - 1, hi]`: the index holds it except at `lo - 1` when `lo` is the index's bottom, and that floor is in
range only stepping up, onto `i128::MIN`. So the in-range answer is the floor narrowed plus the step, and
that one case answers the bottom. How much the division cost is a bench's question, and the bench ticket
filed with the round stands; the fix restores what the design says and does not wait on a number.

The applied map's law against its independent oracle is fed only floors `Exact::between` can name, at most
`2^63` past an end of the index, and the oracle's own integer, two limbs, cannot hold a floor much past
`2^191`. So `lands_within`, the wrap's reduction and saturation are checked against nothing independent at
the floors a formed step reaches up to `2^256 - 1`. The oracle's integer becomes the shared wide oracle,
the signed integer below `2^256` on a pair of the host's unsigned 128-bit integers that the edge sweeps
already read, so it shares none of the map's four-limb arithmetic; its wrap reduces the position before
subtracting the range's lowest slot, so nothing it forms leaves `2^256`. The wide sweep then feeds floors
at `±2^128`, `±2^192` and `±2^255` with remainders into every range under every mode, policy and dither,
and `±(2^256 - 1)` on the grid, since a formed step off the grid is at most `2^255`.

The four-limb integer's direct laws are missing for the four reads the map takes from it: `of_sum`,
`residue`, `end` and `is_even`. Two tests also form their expected values with the code under test. Each
gets values written as literal limbs. The file has passed 500 lines, and its seam is the long division,
which moves to a module of its own.

Addition keeps its refusal of a sum the slot coordinate cannot carry, left for its own round, and its
ticket, `arvo_addition_s_sum_past_the_slot_index_could_ride_the_four`, was filed with round `239`, but the
refusal carries no marker naming it. It gets one, and the design's paragraph names the ticket.

The prose: the oracle's module doc and one test still describe the pinned slot and its distance, one
saturation test derives its expectation through the removed reduction, the product's and conversion's
placing comments say the obligation rules out a floor past the index, conversion calls a numerator not
formed one past the index, and the wrapping sweep's doc names the wrapping admission. The crate summary's
sentence on the product names four 64-bit limbs beside the paragraph saying the crate has no machine
container, and is reworded to the exact integer's reach.
