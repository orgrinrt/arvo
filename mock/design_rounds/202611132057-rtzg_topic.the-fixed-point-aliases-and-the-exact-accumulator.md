# the-fixed-point-aliases-and-the-exact-accumulator

**Date:** 2026-11-13

## What the round is for

Two things a consumer that simulates needs and the value does not have. Kaski's lane is fixed-point
arithmetic under a rounding it declares, half to even on the product, and today a fixed-point value
is written out as `Num<Signature<Fixed<32, -16>, Adapt<HalfEven, Saturate>>>` because the aliases stop
at the integers. And a sum of many values has only the signature's own `add`, which completes at
every step, so a long sum under saturation depends on the order it was taken in and a sum under
either policy cannot be asked whether it left the range anywhere on the way.

This round ships the fixed-point aliases and an accumulator that sums exactly and comes back to a
value once, through the signature's adaptation.

## The aliases

`Fix<BITS, FRAC, A, C>` is a value of `Fixed<BITS, FRAC>` and `UFix<BITS, FRAC, A, C>` of
`UFixed<BITS, FRAC>`, each reading its adaptation through `NamesAdaptation` at its own format, as the
integer aliases do, with `C` defaulting to `Abort` and `A` without a default. Kaski's lane is
`Fix<32, -16, Adapt<HalfEven, Saturate>>` or whatever width it picks. No alias fixes the rounding:
the obligation is that the consumer declares it, and half to even is what this consumer declares
today.

Nothing else is needed for the lane. The product rounds by the declared mode since the first round of
this track, the quotient and the conversion since the third, and the arithmetic is integer arithmetic
on the declared width with no float and no host-dependent step in it.

What the lane's consumer has to know about order is said where the alias is described: under wrapping
the sum is associative and any reduction order gives the same member, and under saturation over a
signed range it is not, which `law::additive_associativity_under_saturation` counts. A consumer that
splits a sum declares its order, or takes the accumulator below, which has none.

## The accumulator

An accumulator holds the exact sum of at most `CAP` members of a signature `S`, in a value of a
second signature `T` that is wide enough for it. `Accumulator<S, T, CAP, C>`.

`T` is named by the consumer and checked by the build. Its format sits on `S`'s grid, the same radix
and quantum exponent, and its range holds `CAP` times each end of `S`'s range. Where it does not, the
type is refused as a failed constant when anything is built of it. So a width written by hand is
either sufficient for the declared capacity or does not compile, and the probe's arm, an 8-bit
accumulator over 4-bit terms, is admitted at a capacity of sixteen and refused at thirty-two.

`T` is not derived from `S` and `CAP`, although it is a function of them. The width would be
arithmetic on a const parameter in a type position, which the language does not have on stable. The
check is the same fact stated the way that compiles, and the consumer's line names a width the build
has confirmed.

The capacity is how many terms have gone in, and it is kept by the shapes of the functions that put
terms in, with nothing counted at run time:

`of::<N>(terms)` takes an array of `N` terms and is refused at build where `N` is above `CAP`.

`take(terms)` takes a slice of any length, sums its first `CAP` terms or all of it if it is shorter,
and hands back the rest. A slice longer than the capacity is therefore summed a block at a time, each
block exact, and the consumer says what joins the blocks: the signature's `add` on the finished
values, or a wider accumulator. Nothing is validated. The split is the operation.

`plus(a, b)` joins two accumulators of capacities `A` and `B` into one of `CAP`, refused at build
where `A + B` is above `CAP`. `minus(a, b)` is the exact difference, with its own bound checked at
build, since a difference of two unsigned sums goes below zero and `T` has to reach there.

`total()` gives the exact sum as a value of `T`. `finish()` gives a value of `S`: the sum put onto
`S` by `S`'s own adaptation, once, which is `convert` from `T` onto `S` and, the grids being one,
only the completion. `fits()` is whether that completion acts. Under `Abort` a debug build aborts in
`finish` where the sum has left `S`'s range, as every other site does.

A term enters by its slot index, which is its index on `T`'s grid as well, and the sum is `T`'s own
carrier arithmetic, which cannot leave `T`'s range by the bound. So `T`'s rounding and overflow never
act and any admitted adaptation does for it.

What this does not reach: a term signature whose capacity needs more than 64 bits, since `T` is a
value and a value is at most 64 bits wide. A 64-bit term can be accumulated at a capacity of one and
no further. A wider accumulator needs a wider carrier, which is not in the tree.

## What is proved

The aliases: each is the written type, a product under `Fix` with `HalfEven` rounds its ties to even
where `Floor` cuts them, and a preset names an adaptation for a fixed-point format through the alias.

The accumulator against the exact sum computed in the test's own wide integers: for every array of
terms at small widths and lengths exhaustively, and seeded slices above that, `total` is the exact
sum and `finish` is the adaptation of it, under both policies and both slot families. `take` on
slices shorter than, equal to and longer than the capacity, with the rest handed back. `plus` and
`minus` against the exact sum and difference. The order of terms does not change `finish`, on the
signed saturating cell where the signature's own `add` does depend on it, with a named case.

The probe's numbers through the real type: sixteen terms of fifteen in an 8-bit accumulator give 240,
and thirty-two terms taken at a capacity of sixteen give 240 and sixteen terms back.

`mutant-accumulator` makes `take` ignore the capacity and sum the whole slice, which is the
hand-written fold. It turns the capacity tests red, with 224 where 240 and a rest are asked.

Compile-fail pairs: an accumulator too narrow for its capacity beside one wide enough, the probe's
8 bits at thirty-two beside sixteen; `of` with more terms than the capacity beside one within it;
`plus` past the capacity beside one within it; an accumulator on another grid beside one on the
term's.

The debug check in `finish`, under `Abort` and `Quiet`, with the release twin. The consumer reaches
every new item.

## What the round leaves

A capacity type that proves a slice's length, which would let `take` hand back nothing by its type,
is PLAN step 9. An accumulator wider than a value, and one for products, are not here.
