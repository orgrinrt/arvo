roadmap puts inside it, A-new-1b, and with it A-new-1a, the fractional product, which carries the same
marker under the same ticket, `arvo_numeral_s_fixed_point_product_goes_through_the_128_bit`
(`num.rs` in `mul`, `ratio.rs` in `div` and `convert`).

## What is slow, and where it is

Every exact step past the sum forms its numerator and denominator four 64-bit limbs wide, divides once
with Knuth's long division, and reduces the residue to lowest terms by a greatest common divisor in the
same four limbs. For a value that is three products in eight limbs checked back to four, a long
division and a gcd loop over four limbs, all for a ratio whose terms fit the host's 128-bit integer at
every width a value has at a zero phase.

## Where the fast path lives

The numeral's design already says whose the cure is: "The cure is the format's to ship". The exact
position of a product, a quotient and a conversion is the format's work (`product_position`,
`quotient_position`, `converted_position`), and a numeral that formed the floor and the residue itself
would be a second version of the format's step. So the fast path goes into the format, behind the three
position functions and with no public item added, and every caller of the format gets it, the numeral
among them.

Three shapes were weighed.

1. The numeral forms the ratio's floor and residue in `u128` and hands `Exact::at` to `adapt`. The
   rounding keeps one home, but the placing is done twice, once here and once in the format. Left.
2. `Wide::floor_divided` and the products take a host branch when the magnitudes happen to fit. That
   is a branch on a value at run time, and it pays the four-limb products before it can know. Left.
3. The three position functions take a host arm chosen by a constant of the instantiation, where every
   member's ratio fits. Taken.

## The host arm

At a zero phase the three steps are ratios of slot products and radix powers. With `r` the radix and
`e` the quantum's exponent, `u = r^max(e, 0)` and `v = r^max(-e, 0)`:

1. the product of the members at `a` and `b` sits at `a b u / v`;
2. the quotient of `a` by the divisor at `b` sits at `a v / (b u)`;
3. the member of `F` at `a` sits on `T`'s grid at `a u_F v_T / (v_F u_T)`.

The arm takes the magnitudes and the sign apart, forms the numerator and the denominator in `u128`,
divides once, floors toward negative infinity on the sign, and reduces the residue by a gcd in `u128`,
storing `Remainder::ZERO` where nothing is left so the position is the one the four-limb route builds
(the remainder's equality is structural). It is chosen where the phase is zero, the quantum constant,
and the bound over the range holds: the largest numerator and the smallest denominator are in `u128`
and their quotient is below `2^127`, so the floor and the slot under it are in the index. That is a
constant per format, or per pair for the conversion, so each instantiation lowers to one arm and
nothing is decided at run time. Where the bound does not hold, the four-limb step runs as today.

The 64-bit unsigned integer's product at a zero exponent does not take the arm (`2^64` squared over one
passes `2^127`), and does not need to, since the numeral computes it in the carrier. Every fractional
product at width 64, every quotient at a radix-two exponent above `-63` and every conversion whose
scaled range stays under `2^127` takes it.

## The proof

Equality with the oracle is the proof, as A-new-1a asks: the existing suites, exhaustive at widths 1 to
8 and sampled at named wider widths (the numeral's `the_product_at_every_shape_*`,
`the_quotient_at_every_shape_*`, `the_conversion_at_every_*`, and the format's own), against
`tests/common/oracle.rs`, which is written apart from the format. A mutant, `arvo_mutant_host`, takes
the floor of a negative ratio as the negated quotient without the step down, and its run is committed
red. A bench against the hand quotient and the hand product at a declared profile records the factor.

## The member divisor

`Divisor::member` shipped in `b687fc01`, before the numeral's design said "a value cannot yet be divided
by a value". With it a value is divided by a run-time value: the caller's format `G` on the value's grid
leaves the zero out, `Member::<G>::adapted` lands a slot read at run time in `G` by its totality, and
`Divisor::member` takes it. The sentence goes, and a test shows `Num::div` by such a divisor equal to the
oracle.

## The remainder is not in this round

The canon has no row on a remainder as an operation. At a fractional exponent `a - b div(a, b)` is in
general off the grid, so what a remainder is there is a definition, not a transcription. The question is
filed as `what_a_remainder_over_a_fractional_arvo_format_is_beside_the`, with three options, and the
remainder stays out until it is answered.

## The divisor review

`mock/research/202611192040_kaski-division-sites/NOTES.md` reads 55 division sites of kaski's design
against the typed forms. No site divides by a divisor of either sign, so no signed door is proposed
(`division.rs`, the marker on `may_arvo_divide_by_a_signed_divisor_read_at_run_time_of_eith`). The
eleven run-time sites are counts and lengths whose zero has its own outcome in the text; the caller
branches on that outcome, and on the other arm the member divisor above settles the division. The six
read from a pack row are the loader's: a figure refused out of its band lands in `G` the same way. So
no door is proposed.

## The arm moves to the value, after the doc changelist first locked

The first lock put the host arm in the format's three position functions. Reading the format before the
source changelist: its step is total over every slot, members or not, and `Divisor::of` takes any non-zero
slot at run time, so a slot outside the range can pass any bound written over the range. An arm in the
format would then need a test of the operands at run time to stay exact, which is two lowered paths and a
run-time check, both refused here. A value holds only members, and the divisors a value takes,
`Divisor::constant` and `Divisor::member`, are both in the range (`Divisor::of` is not admitted over a
format whose range holds the zero, which every value format does). So the bound is a constant at the
value and nowhere else, and the arm is the numeral's: it forms the floor and the residue and hands
`Exact::at` to the format's `adapt`, which keeps the rounding in one home. This is the first of the three
shapes above, and the objection to it, the placing done twice, is answered by the placing at a zero phase
being the value's own statement already ("sits at `a b r^e`" in the numeral's design) and by equality with
the oracle on both arms. The old design sentence said the same thing in other words: "an adaptation
computed at the container's width that this crate calls". The format's design is not changed, and the
doc changelist is reopened with `cargo mock unlock` and corrected.

The mutant is the numeral's, `arvo_mutant_room`, beside its other flags, in place of `arvo_mutant_host`.

## The residue is not reduced, and the division is the narrowest that holds

The first bench run (`numeral-ratio`, with the residue reduced by a gcd and every division in `u128`)
put the arm four to ten times ahead of the four limbs and still seven to twenty times behind the hand
version. Two of its costs buy nothing for a value. A value only rounds and completes the position, by
`adapt` and `panic_on_overflow`, and neither reads the residue's terms apart from their ratio, so the gcd
loop goes and the residue is handed over as it is. And where the bound keeps both terms in 64 bits, a
constant of the instantiation like the bound itself, the division is the machine's 64-bit one rather
than the 128-bit routine. The first run stays committed beside the second.
