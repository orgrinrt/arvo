# Topic: what the surfaces claim about capacities, format points and `Bool`, and what the suite feeds the map

**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `PRINCIPLES.md.tmpl`, `WORKFLOW.md.tmpl`, `README.md`, `crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`,

**Source:** the second review of `feat/platform-width-types-on-a-wider-slot-range` at `14242859`,

## The canon this answers to

`ruling::a_platform_width_type_is_a_target_bound_member_of_the_format_family`,
`obligation::a_platform_sized_unsigned_integer_at_an_api_position`,
`obligation::a_primitive_for_every_position_a_bare_number_would_take` and
`ruling::the_numeric_door_carries_the_coordinate_set_and_the_two_type_bound_is_not_canon` govern.

What they say about a capacity, and nothing more. `question::what_a_platform_width_type_is`, answered:
"`Cap` is not a platform-width type. It is the const-generic capacity position the obligation excepts,
and nothing here bears on it." The obligation: "A const generic parameter is excepted, and even there
the bare form is used only where the alternative is genuinely painful." The platform-width ruling's
note puts `Cap` out of its scope and reads the door ruling as answering a capacity as a coordinate the
door carries; the door ruling's own `says`, `because` and `note` name no capacity at all. No row says
what type spells a capacity, and no row says whether a capacity is a case where the alternative to the
bare form is genuinely painful. Two independent reads of the registry reach the same silence.

What they say about a value: `question::what_a_datum_stands_for` is open, with no `answered`, and op
refused to bound its option set.

## 1. The capacity spelling

Round `202609190948` took `Cap` out of the agent surfaces and put `const N: usize` in its place:
`agent/rules/no-heap.md.tmpl:25`, `agent/skills/type-surface-review/SKILL.md.tmpl:33` and `:56`,
`PRINCIPLES.md.tmpl:187`. That answers the spelling question the other way, and the canon answers it
neither way. `PRINCIPLES.md.tmpl:186-192` also says a capacity is the excepted position and then that
whether it is such a position is not settled, which is one paragraph disagreeing with itself.
`no-heap.md.tmpl:25` also places the `const N: usize` spelling in another repository's crate, which
this repository has no business asserting either way.

The surfaces say instead: a capacity is a type-level parameter, which is the const generic position
the obligation excepts; what type spells it, the host's `usize` under the exception or a type the
stack owns, is open; the bare form is used only where the alternative is genuinely painful, and
whether a capacity is such a case is not decided. No surface names `Cap` or `const N: usize` as the
spelling. `arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`'s capacity paragraph says the door ruling answers a capacity as
a coordinate, which is the platform-width ruling's note reading it and not the door ruling's text; it
says that.

## 2. The format points described as values

The same round gave `USize` and `ISize` the right treatment and left their siblings with the old one.
In the tree, `arvo-format`'s `points` are `Integer<BITS>`, `UFixed<BITS, FRAC>`, `Biased`, `Floating`
and the two aliases, every one a unit struct naming a format and holding no value. `IFixed`,
`FastFloat` and `StrictFloat` are not in the tree at all, and `UFixed` takes two parameters, not
`<I, F, S>`. The surfaces still present them as value newtypes with a fixed representation and
validation at construction, as the replacement for a bare `u8..u128` or `f32`/`f64` field, as types
carrying `impl const` arithmetic, and as a `pub type Priority = UFixed<2, 0, Hot>` field alias that
"packs to exactly `N` bits". A field typed as any of them carries nothing.

They get what `USize` got: the points are zero-sized format markers, no numeric value type exists,
what a value is is `question::what_a_datum_stands_for` and open, and nobody invents one to fill the
gap. The value types that do exist are named for what they are: `Bool` in `arvo-format` and
`Bits<N>` in `arvo-bits`, neither a numeral.

## 3. Access paths that do not exist

`PRINCIPLES.md.tmpl:112-113` sends a reader to `*x` through a `Deref` on `Bool` and to
`Transparent::raw(x)`. `agent/rules/cookbook.md.tmpl:105-109` says `Bool` is in `arvo-bits`, that it
implements `Try`, that `*val.is_zero()` goes through `Deref`, and cites `arvo/docs/ARVO_NEWTYPE-DEREF.md`.
`agent/rules/type-surface.md.tmpl:115` lists `Bool: Try`. `SKILL.md.tmpl:38` names `arvo_bits::Bool`.
None of that exists: `Bool` is `arvo_format::width::Bool`, its unwrap door is `Bool::get()`, it has
`of`, `and`, `or`, `not`, `TRUE` and `FALSE` and no trait impl beyond the derives; `Bits<N>`'s unwrap
door is `Bits::raw()`; neither carries a `core::ops` impl. The surfaces name those.

## 4. The carry in `Exact::between`

`DESIGN.md.tmpl:511-514` and `src/apply.rs:191-194` say the carry lands as an ordinary slot "everywhere
a slot any range admits could start from", and that past the index it saturates "like every other
position this crate computes". Both are false. `AtTheTop` admits `i128::MAX`, and a positive carry
from it leaves the index; and `DESIGN.md.tmpl:803` says the applied map computes no position that
saturates.

The saturation is real and its place is the constructor. `Exact::between(Slot::at(i128::MAX),
Fraction::of(9, 4))` names `MAX + 9/4` and stores `MAX + 1/4`, and `between(Slot::at(i128::MIN),
Fraction::of(-7, 4))` names `MIN - 7/4` and stores `MIN + 1/4`: the slot pins at the index's end and
the normalised remainder stays. So the map adapts the stored position and not the named one. Under
`Ceil` and `Wrap` over `AtTheTop`, the first answers the lowest slot where the named position would
land two above it. That is one position the index cannot name being replaced by one it can, before
anything reaches the map, which is what `DESIGN.md.tmpl:798-801` already says of a position computed
by a chain of carries. The design names `between` as one of those sites, says what the stored
position is, and says that an in-range answer for a named position past the index is the answer for
the saturated one. No code semantics change. A test pins both ends.

`apply/tests/the_edges.rs:168` says the carry "saturates the same way" as a step that no longer
saturates, and `src/apply.rs:18-22` says the wide intermediate "lives inside two private functions",
where `Rounded` and `round_slot` are `pub(crate)` and addition reads them. Both are rewritten.

## 5. What the translation law is fed

`the_top_of_the_index.rs:165-219` feeds offsets 0 to 255 from a reference at zero, so nothing below
the lowest slot and nothing past one above the highest, and it gives the sign-reading modes as the
reason, which is a fact about a reference at zero. The law moves to a file of its own with references
off zero whose lowest slot is not a multiple of the span, so a wrap anchored at zero rather than at
the lowest slot disagrees too: `[1000, 1255]` against the range at the top over offsets -300 to 255,
`[-2000, -1745]` against the range at the bottom over 0 to 555, and the same two pairs at a span of
`2^64`, the widest an admitted range has, near both ends. Every shift is even, so half-even's parity
survives it, and each pair keeps one sign on both sides. The cross-end positions are fed by hand:
`i128::MIN` into the range at the top, and `i128::MAX` plus a step into the range at the bottom, both
at 256 and at `2^64`.

The law is written once, as a function returning the first disagreement for a given completion, so
the negative controls live in the suite: a wrap that subtracts before reducing, a wrap anchored at
zero, and an in-range test that drops the step onto the lowest slot are each fed to it and each
must be reported.

## 6. The per-target arms

`DESIGN.md.tmpl:929-936` and `tests/the_platform_width.rs:177-179` cite a library check at
`msp430-none-elf`. A type alias's right-hand side is not checked for well-formedness, so that check
says nothing about `USize`. It goes.

The 32-bit and 16-bit arms remain. Two ways to keep them honest were weighed. A `mock/tools/` entry
running `cargo check --tests --target i686-unknown-linux-gnu`: the shipped contract admits a tool for
exactly two reasons, `takes-a-question` and `no-failing-case`, and a build that can fail is neither;
a check with a failing case is a lint, and a cost concern is not a third reason. And the arms' bodies
are runtime `assert_eq!`, which `cargo check` type-checks without evaluating, so the build the tool
would run verifies nothing the arms assert. There is no 32-bit runner on the machines this suite runs
on.

So the arms are deleted, in both crates, with the host's 64-bit arm, and the design says why. What
they asserted is that the alias equals the literal point at the target's width. The alias is spelled
over `usize::BITS`, which the language defines as the pointer width, so on every target that is true
by construction whenever the spelling is. The only way it fails is an alias hard-coded to a literal
width, and on the host that is indistinguishable from the correct spelling by any test, so no arm run
here could catch it. That one property is a fact about the source text, checkable on every commit
without a second build, so it becomes a lint: the two aliases in `arvo-format`'s `points` read
`usize::BITS`. The host tests that read `usize::BITS`, `usize::MAX` and `isize::MIN` stay, and the
family laws at 16, 32 and 64 stay, on the host.
