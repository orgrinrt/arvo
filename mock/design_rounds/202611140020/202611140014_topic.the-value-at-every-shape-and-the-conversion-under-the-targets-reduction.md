# Topic: the value at every shape, and the conversion under the target's reduction

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Job:** `arvo-values-fin`, branch `work/arvo-values`.
**Source:** the third reading of the branch, `mock/research/202611132145_review-arvo-values/PASS_3.md`,
and the two readings of the open question under
`mock/research/202611140010_q36-two-readings/`.

## Current state

`Num::convert` places a member on the target's grid, rounds it by the target's mode and completes it by
the target's policy. That is the second option of `question::whose_reduction_governs_a_lossy_crossing`,
which is open, and the design builds it without naming the question. The registry does not answer it and
neither do the maintainer's captured words.

The quotient, the conversion and the product at an exponent other than zero are proved at a handful of
widths above 8 bits and at radix two only, and the tests' arithmetic skips a case whose intermediate
leaves a 128-bit integer. The host door is proved in full at widths 1 to 8 and at chosen widths above.
The design's section on what is proved says so in those words.

## What the two readings found

Two seats read the question one after the other, the second without the first's text. Both picked the
second option, the target's reduction. What both examined is a change of width, of range and of exponent
at radix two and zero phase, under the wrapping and the saturating policy and the five modes a value
admits, at widths of 8 bits and under. Neither ran anything at another radix or a nonzero phase.

## Changes proposed

The conversion stays as built, on a provisional stamp over the region both readings share. The stamp is
recorded on `proposal::a_conversion_is_one_placement_onto_the_target_grid` with a question ticket to the
maintainer, and the design names the question and the stamp where it states the conversion.

Both readings say their arguments read neither the radix nor the phase, so the conversion stays at every
radix a value is held over, and the stamp records that the probes behind it stopped at radix two and at
widths of 8 bits. Two phases cannot meet in a value, since a value is held at zero phase only. The
conversion between two radixes gets a sweep of its own at every width.

The three delegated operations, the host door and the debug check on the door are proved at every width
from 1 to 64, both ranges, both policies and the five modes, at radix two, ten and three, with a family
of exponents per width that ends at the last one the format admits. The tests' arithmetic gains a wide
integer for the cases a 128-bit one cannot hold, so nothing is skipped. The conversion is a law over
pairs of shapes: it is proved between every pair of widths and ranges within a radix family, and between
every pair of exponents of the family at each width. The design's section on what is proved is rewritten
to say what the sweeps hold.
