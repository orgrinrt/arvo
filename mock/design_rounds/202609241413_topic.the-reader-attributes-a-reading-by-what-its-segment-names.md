# Topic: the reader attributes a reading by what its segment names, and still refuses the canon

**Date:** 2026-09-24
**Governing canon:** `ruling::half_up_denotes_ties_toward_positive_infinity`, ratified, and
`ruling::the_rounding_mode_vocabulary` for the six names.
**Where this came from:** the third review of pull request #150, blocker 1 and findings 2 to 8. The
brief for this round makes it the last one on `half-up-is-not-a-magnitude-rule`: either the class the
review names is shown closed, or the lint is demoted from hard error to a warning, everything still
wrong is catalogued as known-red, and the module doc says the gate is incomplete in both directions.

## What the review found, re-measured

Every sentence the review lists reproduces against the reader at `db634837`, driven from a copy of
`reading.rs`. The six false positives of blocker 1 fire, the four false negatives of finding 2 are
silent, the two of finding 3 are silent, and the one of finding 8 is silent. Two more false positives

## Every place the reader decides whether a reading attaches to the mode

The brief asks for the enumeration rather than the review's list of four. Each entry says what it
consults.

1. `clause_hit`, choosing which name a clause is about: its own spellings, else the carried name
   behind a pronoun, else the carried name or the doc block's item where the clause names nothing,
   else nothing. Consults names. Sound.
2. `antecedent`, choosing what the next clause's pronoun points at: the last spelling with no other
   rule named after it. Consults names, but not whether the spelling is the thing the clause denies.
   "Ties away from zero is a different operation and is not `half_up`: it is what IEEE 754's
   `roundTiesToAway` computes" is the ruling's own `says`, and the pronoun there is the operation, not
   the mode. Defect.
3. `names_nothing`, deciding a clause has a subject of its own: a rounding rule named, or a code span
   opening the clause. The second half is punctuation standing in for a subject the reader has no
   word for. Kept, because it is the only subject marker the reader has, and its cost is recorded
   below as the thing that decides the ending.
4. `opens_with_a_pronoun`: the first word against a list. Sound.
5. `negator_binds`, with `bound_in_segment` and `reaches`: where a negator sits relative to the name,
   the reading and the conjunctions between. The segment cut is punctuation, which is what an
   escape's scope is. What was missing was not in here but beside it: nothing asked which rule the
   reading's own segment is about. That is blocker 1.
6. `listed_apart`: a list is a punctuation structure, and the arm consults what each item holds.
   Sound.
7. `denotation_before`: order alone. A clause stating the denotation and then conjoining the other
   reading is excused. Finding 8. Defect.
8. `mentioned`: a code span plus a word beside it saying the span is a piece of a corpus. The word
   list includes `reading`, which is the word that gives a reading. Finding 3. Defect.
9. `elsewhere` and `directed`: word lists over the clause and the segment. Sound.
10. `row`: the first cell names the row, and a reading in a later cell pairs with it unless that cell
    denies it. The same missing attribution as 5.
11. `named_in` with `capitalised`: what counts as another rule. Finding 2 blames `capitalised`, and
    that diagnosis is wrong. `Round`, `Fix`, `Ceiling`, `Convergent` and `Stochastic` are in
    `OTHER_NAMES` as written, capitalised, so `capitalised` adds nothing for them. They are there
    because `crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl` writes them bare at the head of consecutive
    sentences meaning the MATLAB methods ("Round does not. MathWorks documents Round as ties toward
    the greater absolute value, which is ties away from zero"). A reader that stopped taking the bare
    capital as a name refuses that page. The ambiguity between an imperative and a method name
    opening a sentence is real in both directions and the reader has nothing to separate them with.
    Kept, erring silent, catalogued.
12. `readings_in` against `OTHER_NAMES`: five tokens are both a reading and a rule's name,
    `roundTiesToAway`, `ties-away`, `ties_away` and the two `tiestoaway` spellings. Naming the alias
    beside the mode without a negator fires, and corpus pair 22 pins that as intended. It refuses
    "`half_up` is nearest, whereas `roundTiesToAway` goes away from zero", which is true.
13. `clauses`: the cut itself, punctuation, which is what a clause is. Sound.

## What is fixed, and how

Blocker 1 and entry 10: a reading belongs to another rule when its own segment names that rule ahead
of it, or names nothing and binds back with a pronoun or a relative, and the last rule standing
before it is not the mode. A segment naming the mode ahead of the reading settles it the other way,
which keeps "`half_up`, like `floor`, sends a tie away from zero" firing. The same attribution runs in
table cells.

Entry 2: a spelling a negator is bound to, in its own segment ahead of it, is not an antecedent, since
a pronoun after "X is not `half_up`" means X.

Entry 7: the denotation excuses a later reading only where no conjunction stands between the two,
the same rule a negator's reach already obeys.

Entry 8: `reading` and `readings` come out of the mention words. Neither is used in the registry or
on the published surface to quote a term; checked with a grep over `mock/registry`.

Each goes into the corpus as a pair, one side firing and a near twin silent, and each new arm is
mutated so a test only it holds is named.

## What is not fixed, and why that decides the ending

Run over every string field of every ratified row in `ruling.toml`, the reader at head refuses two
fields of the governing ruling, `says` and `promotion`. The lint does not read a ratified ruling, so
the gate has never met them, and that is the only reason nobody has. The fix for entry 2 takes out
`says`. `promotion` stays refused: "270 read blind, barred from 228, 229, 267, 268 and 269, and first
answered ties away from zero on the external names" is a clause opening on a seat number, a subject
the reader has no word for, and the carry from the clause before it, which named `half_up(x)` inside
a relative, walks straight in. Recognising that subject means adding a numeral to the code span as a
second punctuation proxy for "this clause is about something else", which is exactly the shape the
review asked this round to stop adding.

Entries 11 and 12 are ambiguities the reader cannot resolve with anything it has, one in each
direction. Entry 3 has a cost in each direction too.

So the class is not closed. It is closed for every false positive the review and this round could
construct from formatting, and it is not closed for sentences the canon itself contains, which is
the one outcome the brief rules out for a hard-error gate. The ending taken is the second: the lint
drops to a warning at every gate, with the reason where its severity is declared and in the message
it prints, every sentence still wrong is catalogued as a known-red pair marked as catalogue, the
module doc says the gate is incomplete in both directions and names them, and an arm over the live
ratified rows is committed ignored as catalogue so its red is the record.

This is my call, taken under the brief's hard stop, and no human has ratified it. The alternative,
keeping hard error with the residue catalogued, was weighed and refused: it would ship a gate that
refuses a sentence of the ruling it defends.
