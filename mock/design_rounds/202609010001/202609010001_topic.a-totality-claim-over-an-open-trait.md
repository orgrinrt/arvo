# Topic: a totality claim over an open trait, and what admission is supposed to be

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** the `Slots` contract, the three sentences claiming totality over it, and what the design
owes an implementor it cannot see
**Status:** the finding is right, the proposed remedy is half right, and the canon decides the half it
gets wrong

## The finding is right and I built the construction rather than taking it

The reviewer's probe is uncommitted so its claim was uncitable. I built it, with its values verbatim,
and it is confirmed. Committed at `mock/research/sketches/open-trait-admission/`.

**Without a check, an outside implementor supplying `MIN = 4611686018427387904`,
`MAX = -4611686018427387905`, `WIDTH = 63` compiles**, and `slot_count` returns
`-9223372036854775808`. That is the inverted range and negative count the previous two rounds closed,
walked back in through the front door.

**So all three of my sentences are false.** `slots.rs:28` says "Implemented at widths 1 through 62 and
nowhere else", the `on_unimplemented` note says "declared widths run from 1 to 62 bits", and
`DESIGN.md.tmpl:345` says "implemented at the admitted widths and nowhere else". None of them is true
of an open trait, and nothing enforced any of them.

This is `a-claim-of-totality-names-what-enforces-it` exactly, including its own worked case: a guard
improving every round while the paragraph above it stays false, and the paragraph being what the next
round trusts. I wrote three of them in one file.

## Where I part from the proposed remedy, and the canon is why

The remedy offered was to narrow the claims to `Signed` and `Unsigned` and state the obligation on the
contract. **Half of that is right and the other half leaves the design worse than the finding says.**

Narrowing the claims is right and is not optional: they are false and they get fixed.

**Stating the obligation as prose is the thing that just failed.** A sentence saying an implementor
owes coherence, with nothing checking it, is the same construction as the three sentences being
replaced. It would read as a repair and change nothing an implementor does.

### Sealing is against canon, and the reviewer is right about that

`ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon` is ratified and ratifies
`proposal::the_concept_is_closed_and_the_inventory_is_open`: the set of admitted instances is open.
`Slots` is a coordinate of a format, and a numeral wanting a slot range that is neither of my two
shapes has to be able to supply one. **A seal would close the inventory in a real way**, so it is out.

### But the same sentence requires a check, which is the half the remedy misses

The proposition does not say anyone may implement anything. It says a new instance **"earns admission
by supplying the concept's obligations"**, and its `because` says closing the concept while opening the
inventory **"is what makes admission a check rather than a negotiation."**

**An implementor supplying an inverted range has not supplied the obligations.** Under the canon that
is a failed admission, and admission is supposed to be checked. Leaving it unchecked is as far from
the canon as sealing is, in the other direction.

So the canon forbids the seal **and** requires the check, and the design has to do both at once.

## What does the checking, and it is expressible

A defaulted associated const on the trait, forced by every function that reads the range:

```
const ADMITTED: () = { assert!(MIN <= MAX, ...); assert!(...); };
```

Measured, not assumed. With it, the rogue implementor above is refused:

```
error[E0080]: evaluation panicked: slot range is inverted: MIN exceeds MAX
  evaluation of `<Rogue as Slots>::ADMITTED` failed here
```

**At compile time, on an open trait, for an implementor the crate cannot see.** The trait stays open,
anyone may implement it, and supplying obligations that do not hold together stops the build rather
than producing a negative count.

That is `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path` satisfied rather than bent: invalids
caught at compile time, nothing added to any lowered path.

And it is not a panic in the I18 sense. `ruling::the_overflow_panic_is_permitted_and_bounded` is for
what **cannot** be caught at compile time through the typestate, and its correction
`ruling::the_panic_bound_names_a_concern_not_a_marker` says the intent is the inferrable thing. This
is caught at compile time, so I18 does not reach it and nothing here relies on a debug build.

## The second inconsistency, which the coordinator found by reading

`DESIGN.md.tmpl:207` lists `Slots` in the contracts table with `Signed` and `Unsigned` as **examples**,
beside "a consumer's own member sits beside them with no edit to anything below". Line 345 says the
impls exist at the admitted widths **and nowhere else**.

Those two cannot both be true and the table is the one that matches the canon. Line 345 is the one
that goes.

## What the construction becomes

Not a probe. A law that returns a verdict and a test asserting the law rejects the rogue, per
`catalogue-edge-cases-as-tests`: the wrong construction lives permanently in the test module, and
`is_admissible` reports on it without forcing the check that would refuse it. Plus a `trybuild` case
showing that using it does not compile.

Both halves are needed. The law test says the design can tell; the compile-fail case says the design
does refuse.
