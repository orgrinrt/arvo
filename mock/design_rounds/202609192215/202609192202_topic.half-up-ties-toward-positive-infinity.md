# Topic: `half_up` ties toward positive infinity, and the instruments that would have caught it

**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Governing canon:** `ruling::half_up_denotes_ties_toward_positive_infinity` (ratified by two expert reads
under `ruling::the_panel_finishes_the_canon_without_him`, which op ratified), and
`ruling::the_ambiguous_rounding_word_is_retired_for_six_explicit_names` (ratified by op).

## What the canon says

The ruling's `says`: `half_up` is nearest with a tie going toward positive infinity at every sign, which is
`floor(x + q/2)`. Ties away from zero is a different operation on a signed domain and is not `half_up`; it
is reached as an alias, `toward_zero(x + sign(x) q/2)`. The two coincide on unsigned domains and wherever
no tie is representable. Its `note` names what it corrects below the canon: `Mode::HalfUp` in
`arvo-format` rounds ties away from zero, in `rounding.rs`, in `apply.rs`'s `round_slot`, in
`symmetry.rs`'s classification and in the tests pinning each.

`arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl` declares the vocabulary ruling and does not declare this one. Where the design
says what `half_up` means it says the meaning is open (the applied map's oracle paragraph, the MATLAB
section) or it states the old reading as fact (the rounding region, where half-up "reads the sign of the
slot, at a tie only", and the reflection section, where the rounding region "commutes with reflection for
toward zero, half-up and half-even"). All of those are drift against a ratified row and change first.

## What the shipped tree does, measured

The whole suite is green at `05489db5`, 213 unit tests in `arvo-format`, 17 compile-fail, 13 parity plus
one ignored, and every tool and lint suite. Green here is the defect being pinned rather than the design
being met:

- `apply/tests/mod.rs`, `half_up_goes_away_from_zero_on_a_tie_and_half_even_goes_to_the_even_slot`, asserts
  a tie at `-2.5` gives `-3`, the value the ruling says is wrong.
- `apply/tests/the_oracle.rs` reads a `HalfUp` tie "as the `Mode` rustdoc says", which is the
  implementation's own reading. On the one cell that decides this ruling the oracle restates the map, so
  the two sweeps built on it (`the_oracle_sweep`, `the_wide_oracle_sweep`) compare the map against a copy
  of itself there and cannot fail.
- `symmetry/tests/the_classification.rs` derives the classification from the map, which is sound as an
  instrument, and then pins the translating and reflecting counts at three and three, which is the old
  rule's partition.
- `tests/matlab_fi_parity.rs` asserts the shipped mode sends `-2.5` to `-3` and calls it MATLAB's Round,
  and carries a catalogue-red arm for MATLAB's Nearest that the ruling closes.
- `addition/tests/the_verdict.rs` pins per-mode refusal counts in which the `half up` row is the old
  rule's.

Nothing in the tree could notice a tie rule being chosen in source: every oracle took its tie rule from the
code it checked, and no sweep planted a wrong tie rule and required the sweep to see it.

## What this round does

1. The design declares the ruling and states `half_up` as `floor(x + q/2)` wherever it names the rule, with
   the note the ruling carries: it is not Java's or Python's `HALF_UP`.
2. The classification follows from the rule rather than from a table: `half_up` reads nothing besides the
   residue, so it commutes with translation everywhere and with reflection nowhere a tie is reached. The
   partition over the six still holds, now four and two.
3. MATLAB's Nearest lands on `HalfUp`. MATLAB's Round is ties away, the ruling's alias, and has no mode.
4. The oracle's rounding half is rewritten from the canon's formulas, computed by exact rational
   arithmetic that does not share a branch with `round_slot`. Planted wrong tie rules (the old ties-away
   rule, ties down, ties toward zero, ties to odd) become maps every oracle sweep has to report.
5. A sweep over every signed tie at every admitted width small enough to walk exhaustively, and at both
   ends of every wider range, against that oracle.
6. A test pinning the equivariance partition of the six under translation by one quantum, by odd and even
   shifts, across the sign, and under negation, with the failing modes asserted to fail at a named witness.
7. A lint refusing prose that gives `half_up` the magnitude reading.

## The alias, and whether a consumer can write it

The ruling says ties away from zero is reached as `toward_zero(x + sign(x) q/2)`. What the shipped surface
offers towards that: `Exact::between(slot, ratio)`, `Exact::slot`, `is_on_grid`, `is_tie`, and nothing that
reads a position's remainder or sign back. So a consumer holding an `Exact` from anywhere else cannot form
the shifted position at all, and one holding the slot and ratio it built the position from can form it
only where the doubled denominator fits the ratio's integer. The design names no exact operation on a
position. This is a design gap, it is named in the design by this round, and no API is invented for it:
the alias's home is a question for the dispatcher.

## What is not taken

The reflection predicate takes a mode and no reach, so it answers no for `half_up` on a reach with no tie,
where the map reflects. That region is conservative rather than unsound and is named rather than expressed
here, for the same reason as the parity region already named beside it: expressing it wants reflection to
read a reach, and the stochastic mode's reflection region at a fixed dither depends on the dither, which
is a larger change than this ruling asks for.
