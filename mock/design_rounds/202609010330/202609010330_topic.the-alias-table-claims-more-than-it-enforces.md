# Topic: a rewrite that preserves a referent is not the same as a mode not being in doubt

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `mock/tools/rounding-vocabulary`

`mock rounding-vocabulary` sorts every rounding mode named in a predicate into four groups. The first is
the spellings, and its blurb reads:

> Mechanical. The mode is not in doubt, only how it is written, so each of these can be rewritten to the
> name on the right without reading anything.

Two sentences joined, and only the second one holds.

## The two claims are not the same claim

**That a rewrite preserves the referent** is true of every row in the table. `ceiling` and `ceil` name
one function, `round to nearest even` and `half_even` name one, `nearest-half-up` and `half_up` name
one. Rewriting either side to the other changes no region, which is what makes the repair mechanical
and what licensed rewriting fifteen of them in place.

**That the mode is not in doubt** is a claim about the name on the right, and for one of the six rows
it is false. `nearest-half-up` points at `half_up`, and `half_up` denotes two different functions that
disagree exactly where the retired word's two readings disagree, which is on a signed domain.

One row rather than two, and the earlier draft of this file said two. Lamport puts two of the six
ratified names outside "one operation each", `half_up` and `stochastic`, but `stochastic` has no
spelling in the alias table, so the blurb licenses nothing about it. Six alias rows, six targets,
one of them in doubt.

## The disagreement, by hand

At one fraction bit, the raw value `-63` is `-31.5`.

```
floor(x + 1/2)         floor(-31) = -31
ties away from zero               = -32
```

They differ. On an unsigned domain they cannot: for `x >= 0` a tie at `x.5` goes to `x + 1` under both
readings, so the disagreement has no witness there at all. That is the same shape the blurb two sections
below already spells out for the retired word, where it says the two readings "differ on exactly the
rows where signedness is live".

So the tool explains the mechanism in one section and denies it in another, about a name that has the
mechanism.

## Three instruments, not one

The claim above is not this file's. Three seats measured it separately and none is cited by the others
for it.

`228` section 2.4 decides membership by exhaustive function equality against IEEE 754-2008 and finds
exactly one uncovered rule either way, and censuses twenty-five probe files across nine spellings for
one function. `229` finding 2 counts the disagreement at `2^(W-1-F)` values over `W` in `{4, 6, 8, 10,
12}`, zero unsigned and zero at `F = 0`. `233` section 8 gives the witness `p = -255, f = 1` at `-127`
against `-128`, and names `mock/tools/rounding-vocabulary/src/lib.rs:73` as the site that maps the
first family's spelling onto the name. That line is the `nearest-half-up` row of this table.

`233` landed on the trunk after this branch was cut, so it is read here rather than merged.

## What follows, and what does not

**The fifteen rewrites already made are not affected**, and this is the part worth stating plainly so
nobody re-opens them. `nearest-half-up` and `half_up` are two spellings of one ambiguous mode. The
ambiguity was in the row before the rewrite and is in the row after it, unchanged, so no region moved
and nothing was decided by the edit. The rewrite is faithful; the sentence claiming why it is faithful
is the thing that overreaches.

**The group it was filed under did not license it soundly, and that is a separate question from
whether it was right.** The blurb's stated warrant is that the mode is settled. For that row it is not,
so the reason given does not hold there. The rewrite reaches the right answer on the other warrant, the
one the blurb does not state.

**The ambiguity itself is not this round's to settle.** Which of the two readings `half_up` denotes is
a naming call. `228` declines it in those words and `229` declines it as well, both saying the factual
half is settled and the naming half is not theirs.

**And no registry row asks it.** Searched over `mock/registry/*.toml` with a positive control on a slug
known to be there. `question::which_tie_direction_an_unqualified_nearest_names` asks about `nearest`,
and its second option and its `unblocks` both refer to "the reading question that is still open" and to
"whether `half_up` denotes one operation or two" as though a row carried it. Nothing does. That is a
finding this round reports and does not repair, because filing a question row with options and a
decider is canon work rather than a tool edit.

## What the live corpus says, run rather than assumed

`cargo mock rounding-vocabulary` over the real registry reports 33 modes across 33 entries, 24 already
spelled as the canon spells them, and 9 needing attention: 2 retired, 2 underspecified, 5 outside the
six. **The alias group is empty.** All fifteen were rewritten, so the blurb under repair currently
prints for nobody and the repair is to a latent claim rather than to output on screen. It prints again
the moment a row is written with a prose spelling, which is the case it exists for.

Three entries name `half_up`, and one is
`law::fusing_a_multiply_add_preserves_the_answer_under_signed_wrapping` at `holds: in {floor, ceil,
half_up}` with `signedness: signed`, which is the row the rewrite touched. All three count as clean,
which is correct as a claim about spelling and is all the tool says about them.

## Out of scope

Whether the vocabulary needs a further name to separate the two readings. Whether `half_even` carries a
comparable split; `229` looked and found none that survives its test. Whether the tool should grow a
group for a ratified name that does not denote one operation. That last one is the change three seats
would support and the canon does not yet license, since the retired word's two readings are ratified in
`ruling::the_ambiguous_rounding_word_is_retired_for_six_explicit_names` and its retirement row while
`half_up`'s are panel evidence with no row, so the group has nothing in the canon to rest on until the
question above is filed.
