# Both sides dropped the same citations, and the merge has to keep both halves

The trunk and this branch each took the registry addresses out of the two published crate design
pages, separately and without knowing about each other. Twenty of the twenty-nine sites collide, so
git refuses the merge on four files and every conflict is two answers to one question.

The two are not the same work with different wording. The branch swept every body address to zero
and left the declaration block alone, which was its whole subject. The trunk did the same job as a
side effect of two other rounds, so it dropped some addresses, kept others as bare pointers, and
carried real content changes through the same paragraphs while it was there.

Where the trunk's paragraph carries a fact the branch's does not, the fact decides it. The phase
paragraph is the case that matters: the branch still says a `Phase` constructor cannot produce a
zero denominator, and the source says otherwise, because `Phase::of` is total and the refusal sits
on `Format::ADMITTED` instead. That correction landed on the trunk with the two paragraphs under it
that explain where the condition does sit, and those merged cleanly, so keeping the branch's opening
would leave the page contradicting itself three lines later.

Where the branch's paragraph is the citation-free one and nothing else separates them, the branch
decides it, because that is the round this branch is.

## What the fix is

Twenty conflicts resolved by hand, one at a time, each keeping whichever side carries more and
neither side taken wholesale. Four take the trunk: the phase paragraph and the two under it, the
blockquote the trunk had already replaced with prose above it, and two line-wrapping differences
where the text is otherwise identical. One is a genuine consolidation, the open `Slots` trait, where
the trunk's replacement paragraph is fuller than the branch's trailing clause and the branch's
opening carries no address. The rest take the branch.

The two rendered pages are generated, so they are not resolved at all. They are regenerated from the
resolved templates, which produces the page that is true now rather than either of the two stale
ones.

## What is out of scope

The declaration block at the top of each page still stays, on both sides and for the same reason as
before: a design declares the canon it relates to. Whether a required declaration belongs on a
published page is the open question it was, and it is not this round's either.
