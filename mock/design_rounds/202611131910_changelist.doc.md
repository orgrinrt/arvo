## CHANGE: IEEE 754 is one generic composition at the standard's parameters

Same file, a new subsection after the MATLAB ones.

`Interchange<A, P, EMAX>` is a format over an ambient `A`, a precision `P` and a largest exponent
`EMAX`, the parameters IEEE 754 states its formats in. Its quantum law, `ExponentRange<P, EMAX>`, is
the magnitude-indexed family from `emin - (p - 1)`, which is `2 - emax - p`, over `2 emax` magnitudes;
its slot range, `Significand<A, P>`, is symmetric at `radix^p - 1` and declared `p + 1` bits wide, since
the standard's significand is sign and magnitude. Each derived quantity is computed in an impl body, so
nothing sits in a bound. `Binary<P, EMAX>` and `Decimal<P, EMAX>` are aliases at the two ambients, and
`Binary16`, `Binary32`, `Binary64`, `Decimal32` and `Decimal64` aliases at the standard's table. The
value set of `Binary32` equals that of the shipped `Floating<25, -149, 254>`, the one slot the two
ranges differ on naming a value both hold.

## CHANGE: the rounding-direction map, and the attribute pair

Same file. `roundTiesToEven` is `HalfEven`, `roundTowardPositive` is `Ceil`, `roundTowardNegative` is
`Floor`, `roundTowardZero` is `TowardZero`, re-exported under the standard's names in
`standards::ieee754::rounding_direction`. `roundTiesToAway` is MATLAB's Round, the alias, and has no
mode. `Attributed<F, R, O>` names the signature a format and a rounding direction make, with the
overflow policy the consumer's parameter.

## CHANGE: where a result lands, and the refusals

Same file. A position is adapted at the finest magnitude whose rounded slot lands in the range, which
is the standard's rounding onto a floating format, the carry out of a binade included. `adapt` carries
no magnitude, so the parity suite composes that choice from `adapt` and `panic_on_overflow`; it is a
composition over the public surface like the Round alias, not a mechanism of the crate.

Refused and recorded: an infinity, which is the standard's answer past the largest finite member under
three of its directions and has no member here, so the overflow parity holds only where the standard's
answer is finite; the signed zero, which the value set does not distinguish; and binary128 and
decimal128, whose significands need 114 and 114 bits, past the slot ladder. Each is a test.

## CHANGE: what a parity arm asserts gains the planted mode

Same file, "What a parity arm asserts". Every arm carries a planted wrong mode or policy and a test
requires it to be reported. The IEEE expected side is the host's binary32 and binary64 conversion,
which Rust defines as round to nearest ties to even, the standard's published binary16 encodings, and
for decimal the digit rule the standard states, written in integers. The MATLAB suite gains a sweep of
every rounding method and both overflow actions against MathWorks' definitions as integer rules.
