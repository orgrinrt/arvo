# Src changelist: two tests that assert a constant against its own literal, and one that is deleted

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Round:** the door question closed, and three tests that cannot fail
**Scope:** `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_adaptation.rs`,
`mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_inventory.rs`,
`mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_coordinates.rs`

Three arms found by reading bodies rather than names. Two assert a constant against the literal its
own definition sets and one compares two computations to the same computation. None of the three can
fail for any implementation of the thing it claims to be about.

## CHANGE: `the_overflow_inventory_admits_a_member_this_crate_does_not_know_about` FROM reading the constant back TO running it through the map
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_adaptation.rs`
Verification: the arm calls `adapt` eight times under signatures carrying a foreign policy, and its
own control is `assert_ne!` between two foreign members naming different values, which an
`Adaptation` ignoring the declared policy fails.

The whole body was `assert_eq!(<DeclaredBound as Overflow>::POLICY, Policy::Clamp)`, four lines under
an impl setting `const POLICY: Policy = Policy::Clamp`. Its own doc said the body was incidental,
which is an accurate description of a test that cannot fail rather than a defence of one.

**What the name claims is that a policy declared outside this crate reaches the crate's machinery**,
and nothing ran it through `Adapt`, `overflow_of` or `adapt`. The arm adapts positions on both sides
of the window under a signature carrying the foreign policy and asserts each lands where the shipped
`Clamp` lands. **A second foreign member is declared naming the other value**, so the two decide
differently, which is the control: a map ignoring the declaration would give them one answer.

The wrapping answers are worked out by hand rather than by the expression the crate uses, `8` above
the window and `-9` below it over a span of thirty-two, so the arm does not agree with the code by
construction. The four foreign answers are asserted inside the declared window as well, because the
foreign path is subject to the same totality property as every other.

## CHANGE: `the_declared_width_is_read_rather_than_recovered` FROM four constants against their own literals TO a range whose bounds do not determine its width
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_inventory.rs`
Verification: `WiderThanItsSpan` declares four slots and thirteen bits, `declared_slot_width` returns
thirteen, `slot_count(...).ilog2()` is two, and the arm asserts the two differ.

The four assertions read `<Unsigned<13> as Slots>::WIDTH == Width::bits(13)` against a macro whose
body is `const WIDTH: Width = Width::bits($w)`. The file condemns that class further down, about the
cut before this one.

**A shipped range cannot separate read from recovered**, because its declaration and its bounds agree
by construction, so every shipped arm passes under either implementation. A range from zero to three
declaring thirteen bits is admissible, its span implies two bits, and the two answers differ. **The
control is the other direction**: over ten widths the two agree, so the arm is about which one comes
back rather than about the function refusing to answer.

**It also pins the asymmetry the obligation carries.** A declaration wider than its span is coherent
and admitted; one its width cannot address is not, which `WidthTooNarrow` already held from the other
side and which nothing said was a pair.

## CHANGE: `a_phase_and_a_fraction_do_not_stand_in_for_one_another` FROM asserting the two agree TO deleted
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_coordinates.rs`
Verification: `grep -c "a_phase_and_a_fraction_do_not_stand_in_for_one_another"
mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_coordinates.rs` returns zero, and
`tests/ui/a_phase_is_not_a_fraction.rs` is unchanged and still runs.

It built `Phase::of(1, 2)` and `Fraction::of(1, 2)` and asserted they agree about both numbers, which
is one computation compared against the same computation on two types that store what they are given.
Both storing it is asserted directly elsewhere, in each type's own denominator sweep, so it cannot
fail for any implementation either of them could have.

**The property in its name is a refusal and a refusal is a build failure**, which
`tests/ui/a_phase_is_not_a_fraction.rs` already holds: neither converts into the other, and the two
consts the phase replaced are gone rather than deprecated. **A tautological test is deleted rather
than improved**, and here there is nothing to improve it into, because the real claim is pinned and
the arm's own trailing comment said what it settled for instead.

The file's module doc loses the clause claiming it establishes that the two ratios are two
coordinates, says where that is established, and says what the deleted arm did instead.

## CHANGE: `an_extent_of_nothing_admits_no_index_at_all` FROM silent about the obligation TO naming it
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_coordinates.rs`
Verification: the arm's comment says a quantum law cannot carry that extent and the coordinate can
still be written, and the arm is unchanged otherwise.

The previous round gave `Quantum` an obligation refusing a law over no magnitudes, which could read as
making this arm unreachable. It is not: the extent is a coordinate a caller can build, the saturation
is a property of the type, and the obligation is about what a law may declare. Said in the arm because
a reader meeting the two would otherwise have to work out which one governs.
