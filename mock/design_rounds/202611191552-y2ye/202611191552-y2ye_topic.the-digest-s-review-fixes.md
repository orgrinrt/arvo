## What the review found, and where this round goes

- Finding 5. The digest counts its widths in `u16` (`Digest<const BITS: u16>`, the marker
  `Width<BITS>`, `Fnv1a<BITS>`, `Xxh3<BITS>`), while `Digest::bits` takes `const N: u32` and the rest of
  the stack counts bits in `u32` (`arvo_bits::Bits<const N: u32>`). The marker's name, `Width`, is also
  the name `arvo_format` gives its own width type, in a crate that depends on `arvo-format`. Ticket
  `arvo_has_two_width_vocabularies_u16_for_a_digest_and_u32_for` was filed for the first half. The
  digest takes `u32` throughout, and the marker becomes `Output<BITS>`, the width of a digest's
  output, with the sealed trait renamed to match. `Fnv1a` and `Xxh3` are bounded on
  `Output<BITS>: Carrier` (finding 7), so neither names a width the digest has no container for.
- Finding 2. "keeps the low `N` bits" is checked at four widths. It is a law over every `N` the two
  narrowable digests admit, so the test runs every `N` from 1 to 32 on `Digest<32>` and from 1 to 64 on
  `Digest<64>`.

  itself, and `the_high_bits_are_not_what_is_kept` asserts a property of one XXH3 value rather than of
  the cast. Both are deleted. The narrowing sweep carries its own planted case: a narrowing that kept
  the high bits instead, shown failing against it.

- Finding 8. The xor laws gain commutativity and associativity, at every width.
