## What is there now

The IEEE parity suites land a value on a binary or decimal format through `tests/ieee754/mod.rs`, which
walks every magnitude of the format from the finest, positions the value at each in the host's 128-bit
integer, and takes the first one at which `panic_on_overflow` holds the rounded position. Round
`202611132120-sknb` moved that composition into the crate as `land`, and kept the walk in the suites
beside a new test, `tests/the_landing_agrees_with_the_search.rs`, which checks that the two agree on
points drawn over each format's exponent range.

## What changes

With `land` shipped, the walk is a second copy of what the crate does, and the agreement test exists
only to keep the copy and the original in step. The suites' `land` becomes one call to the crate's
`land`, read back as a significand and the exponent of the magnitude it landed on. The parity suites
then check `land` itself against the host's IEEE arithmetic, every direction, over the same draws,
which is stronger evidence than agreement with a walk written from the same reading of the standard.
The agreement test is deleted with the walk.

One case is not a call to `land`: the overflow region. `land` lands nothing past the top magnitude,
under `arvo_landing_a_value_on_a_magnitude_indexed_format_is_a_call`, and the suites read the overflow
region as the completion's answer at the top magnitude, which is what a saturating IEEE format gives.
So where `land` returns nothing, the harness positions the value at the top magnitude alone and adapts
it there. That is one magnitude, not a search, and it stays until the canon question is answered.
