## Canon

The registry is silent on how wide an exact position's remainder is, and on the integer the exact steps
are formed in; both are design. `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path` is met as before:
every condition this round moves is still an obligation the build forces, and the accessors that now
answer with a maybe read a value back rather than validate one.

## CHANGE: the remainder is four limbs wide

`## Applying the adaptation, which is the second half of the composition`, the remainder paragraphs: `Remainder` is a numerator below a positive denominator,
both four 64-bit limbs wide, the width every exact step forms in, so what an operation holds out is a
step not formed in those limbs rather than a remainder too narrow for what was formed. The dither test
cross-multiplies a four-limb term by a 64-bit one into five limbs. `Remainder::of(num, den)` stays over
the host's unsigned 128-bit integer; the product, the quotient and the conversion build through
`Exact::at` over a remainder formed the same way in four limbs; `numerator` and `denominator` answer in
the 128-bit integer where it holds them and nothing where it does not.

## CHANGE: the division is Knuth's at the divisor's own limb count

`## Multiplication, the product of two members at a constant quantum`, the exact step: `D` stays in the four limbs and reaches `2^256 - 1`. The division is
by one limb where `D` is under `2^64`, otherwise Knuth's long division at as many limbs as `D` has, the
estimate corrected against `D`'s second limb, exact at two limbs, and at three and four one `D` added
back where the subtraction borrows. A numerator leaving the limbs is a step not formed, not a position
past the index.

## CHANGE: the held-out condition is a step not formed

The product's, the quotient's and the conversion's not-yet-admitted condition on `D` past `2^128 - 1`
becomes a step not formed in the four limbs: `D` or the phase's term past `2^256 - 1`, or the numerator
at the extreme members (the product's four corners, the quotient's four range-end corners with the
denominator at the two ends, the conversion's two ends). Each states why the extreme members bound
every other. Zero-phase readings: the product `e >= -255` at radix two and `e >= -77` at radix ten;
the conversion `max(-e_F, 0) + max(e_T, 0) <= 255`. The ticket stays
`arvo_s_exact_position_carries_no_remainder_denominator_past`, narrowed to that. The addition section's
sentence on the phase's remainder names the four-limb denominator.

## CHANGE: the shared ending names a step not formed

`## The exact step's ending, shared`: `D` from one up to `2^256 - 1`; a numerator past the limbs is a step
not formed, named apart from a position past the index; a corner is formed when its numerator is and
held when the index holds its slot.

## CHANGE: landing at a magnitude

`### The position at a magnitude`: a magnitude whose `D` or numerator passes `2^256 - 1` holds no
position, and `p den V` is bounded by `2^63 D` rather than by `2^191`.

## CHANGE: the open item on a public door

`## Open items`: the door for a remainder up to `2^128 - 1` is `Exact::at(slot, Remainder::of(num, den))`;
past it there is none, because the crate has no public four-limb integer, and that waits on a consumer
asking, with the door's other decisions. The sentence on the product past `2^128 - 1` becomes one on a
step past `2^256 - 1`.

## CHANGE: what the tests owe

The multiplication laws: the compile-fail pair turns on a phase whose step leaves the four limbs; the
edge sweep at radix two below `e = -127` to the last exponent the limbs form and one past it, against
the shared wide oracle; the four-limb integer's suite gains divisions by three and four limbs at every
normalisation with the add-back, the order, the scaled comparison past four limbs and the common
divisor, against the oracle. The conversion's compile-fail pair turns on `2^256 - 1`.
