## What the review found

The first review of `work/arvo-bits-tier` at `14d964bd` returned merge after fixes: the operations are right, the refusal has one hole, the tests pin less than the design says they do, and the generated structure documents are stale. This round takes every finding of that file. Each is decided below in the review's own numbering.

## Where the tier stands in the canon

The placement of the four traits in `arvo-bits` stands on `ruling::every_family_of_the_old_arvo_returns_and_the_shape_is_open`, ratified, which brings the bit contracts back in function and leaves their shape and their home open. It does not stand on `proposal::the_old_bit_contracts_crate_is_gone`, which is one expert and binds nothing, nor on the coordinator's assignment, which is a call and not a ratification. The proposal's `instead` names bit access on `arvo-bits`, and its `because` says `BitLogic` and `BitSequence` return with the masks over `arvo-bits`; this design agrees with both and rests on neither. The review is the first independent read that the placement is consistent with the canon; the next review pass is briefed without it and is the second.

## The decisions

1. The structure documents. `cargo mock` regenerates `docs/STRUCTURE.md` and its graph, and they are committed with the round. The generator's public API list naming `Bits` alone is mockspace's, filed as a ticket, not changed here.

2. `WIDTH` at a width outside one to sixty-four. `BitWidth::WIDTH` for `Bits<N>` forces `Bits::<N>::ADMITTED` before it gives `Width::bits(N)`, so reading the width of `Bits<0>` or `Bits<65>` is refused at build like every other door onto the type. Pinned by two refusal cases.

3. The refusal cases build. trybuild checks rather than builds a project with no case that must pass, so every refusal so far is pinned inside a `const` item, which check already refuses. The cases move into one `TestCases` with one case that must pass, which makes trybuild build. Beside the `const` cases go calls at run time: each of the six methods taking an index or an amount, through the inherent method in `main`, and each through a function generic over its trait. `shift_down` and `rotate_down` and `rotate_up` get cases of their own, so deleting the forced assertion from any one method turns a case red.

4. Every width. The sweeps over an index or an amount take every width from one to sixty-four and every index or amount below it, generated by one macro in `tests/common/` that walks the triangle of width and index once and calls back for each pair, with the amount a rotation down needs to equal the rotation up beside it. The thirteen-width list goes.

5. The width law. `width_of::<Bits<N>>() == Width::bits(N)` restates the impl and is deleted. In its place, the width a container gives back is the count of bits its full mask sets, `Bits::<N>::masked(u64::MAX).raw().count_ones() == WIDTH.count()`, at every width, evaluated in a constant through the tier, with decision 2's refusal pinning the widths outside.

6. The oracle for writing a bit. The expected value is built bit by bit, bit `I` from `to` and every other bit `j` from bit `j` of the value, the way `common::rotated_up` builds a rotation, rather than the impl's own mask and or.

7. What an implementor owes. The traits state the refusal as an obligation: an implementor refuses at build an index or an amount at or past its `WIDTH`, and the trait cannot enforce it, so the implementor's own refusal cases hold it. `ADMITTED` and `Within` stay private: they are how `Bits<N>` meets the obligation, and an outside container meets it by its own assertion over its own width, since nothing of `Bits<N>`'s carrier is its. `BitLogic` and `BitSequence` take `BitWidth` as their supertrait, like `BitAccess`, since `not` flips every bit of the width and a shift or rotation moves within it, so each speaks of a width a generic caller can read back. The design's sentence that `ADMITTED` needs no public visibility because only the type's own constructors force it is reworded to that.

8. The mutants in the design. The laws section names the five mutant flags and what each breaks, as `arvo-numeral` and `arvo-capacity` name theirs.

9. The mutant record. A new sketch runs the five mutants over the cases this round adds and keeps the lines naming which assertion failed, superseding `202611200230_bit-tier-mutants`, which stays as the record of its run.

10. Drift. The tier arm of the bench `bit-set` and the lowering note say that `meet` goes through a function generic over the tier and that `union` and `is_empty` are called on `Bits<64>` by name. The root design says `arvo-bits` builds on `arvo-format` and on notko. The two changelists of round `202611200157` name its topic's stamp, `202611200155`, as the round; they are locked and the round's id is `202611200157`, which this file records rather than amends. The crate readme comes down to the length the readme rule gives it.
