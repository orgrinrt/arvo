# The wrapping product keeps the floor's sign and parity

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Scope:** `arvo-format`'s wrapping product past the slot index, its verdict and its tests, and the
IEEE 754 harness points the same review raised.

The review of `work/arvo-format-base`, recorded in
`mock/research/202611140220_review-arvo-format-base-1/REVIEW.md`, found that round `202611140127`'s
wrapping product hands `adapt` the floor reduced onto the range, `lo` plus its residue modulo the count.
`adapt` rounds by reading the slot the position names: toward zero reads its sign, half to even its
parity on a tie. The reduced slot has neither of the true floor's, so a negative tie toward zero, and a
tie on an odd count under half to even, wrap to the wrong member. The tree said so already: `adapt`'s own
comment and the design's section on the position past the index say these two modes read the slot the
position names. The round's sentence that wrapping reads only the floor modulo the count was wrong.

Rounding a position adds 0 or 1 to its floor, decided by the remainder, the dither, the floor's sign and
its parity, and nothing else. Two floors that agree modulo twice the count and lie on the same side of
zero agree on all of those, and on the class modulo the count that wrapping reads. So the floor is
reduced modulo twice the count, into `[0, 2 count)` when it is not negative and into `[-2 count, 0)` when
it is, and handed to `adapt` with its remainder. An admitted range spans at most `2^64` slots, so the
representative is under `2^65` in magnitude, and `adapt`'s wrap arm reduces any slot in the index onto
the range. The `-lo` the old reduction formed, which overflows on a range starting at the index's
bottom, is gone with it.

The round admitted every formed format under wrapping and tested one corner of it, `UFixed<64, k>` at
zero phase under toward zero, where every remainder is zero. The tests owe the region admitted: all six
modes, a nonzero phase, signed and unsigned ranges, ranges at both ends of the index, odd and even
counts, against a reference that forms the position from the two values in a wide integer and reads no
step code. Saturation is shown refused at the unsigned 64-bit corner and clamping is not; it gets its
twin. And the signature's obligation has no verdict beside it, unlike every other obligation in the
crate: `is_multipliable_under` asks it without forcing it.

The IEEE harness points. The rule's claim that a dropped unit past the unsigned integer leaves under half
a unit fails at a significand of `-2^127` at radix two, a tie; no input is one, so the rule refuses it.
Most of the random draws below the smallest subnormal take the one stand-in remainder, and at radix ten
the exact unsigned band does not exist; the draws narrow to where the remainder is exact and the design
says the band is radix two's. Decimal64's sweep below the subnormal already runs through the shared
inputs, so the underflow suite keeps its pinned points and its control for decimal64 and drops the second
sweep. The crate summary's coverage sentence reads as covering MATLAB's `fi`, which has no subnormal; it
is scoped to IEEE 754.

The review also found that the body of commit `c920748f` names another branch and its commit. That
history is pushed and is not rewritten; it is recorded here and in the review's reading.
