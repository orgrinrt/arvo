# Topic: the lints can see the whole crate now, and arvo has 128 findings waiting

**Date:** 2026-07-27
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** every crate under `mock/crates/`, and the lint configuration in `mockspace.toml`
**Status:** owed work, filed deliberately without doing it

This topic exists to be in the way. Whoever opens arvo next will find it, and that is the point: the
work below is real, it is not small, and it was found while working somewhere else entirely. Filing it
and walking away is the honest move, but only if the note is loud enough to survive being inconvenient.

There is already an open topic here (`202607300100_topic.warm-and-precise-above-64-bits.md`). Two open
topics in one round is the normal shape, and they are unrelated: settle either first.

## What changed under us

Until now, mockspace's Rust lints only ever read each crate's `src/lib.rs`. Every module file was
invisible to them, so the gate reported clean over code it had never opened. Two of the twenty-eight
lints had noticed and grown their own loop; the other twenty-six had not, and nothing said so.

That is fixed upstream (mockspace PR #8): a dispatcher now runs every per-file lint once per source
file. No arvo change caused this and no arvo change can undo it. The next `cargo mock` here, once that
lands and the pin picks it up, reports what was always true.

## The three kinds

**Layer violations, and these are real.** Fifty-two `forbidden-imports` findings across seven crates,
almost all of one shape: a module file reaching for the umbrella `arvo` crate when its layer rule
forbids it. `arvo-tensor/src/array.rs` opens with `use arvo::USize;` while rule 10 says arvo-tensor
depends on arvo-storage, arvo-bits-contracts and arvo-numeric-contracts and nothing else. The crate
roots were clean, which is exactly why this was invisible: the import moved into the module files and
the lint was only ever looking at the root.

This is the part that deserves real work. A layer rule nothing enforces is a comment.

**Definition-site floats, and most of these are the lint being wrong.** Seventy-two `no-float`
findings: 53 in arvo, 15 in arvo-strategy, 4 in arvo-storage. The arvo-strategy ones are
`impl Sealed for f32 {}` and `impl_signed_identity!(-1.0, f32, f64)`, in the crate whose entire job is
to define the IEEE float strategy. Naming `f32` there is the definition-site exception the workspace
rule already grants: the width is the specification.

The resolution is a scope exemption in `mockspace.toml`, added at the lint's designed severity rather
than by lowering anything, and narrow enough to cover the crate that defines the strategy without
covering consumers that merely use one. The 53 in arvo itself have not been sorted and may not all be
the same case; assume nothing about them until each is read.

Adding that exemption is gate configuration, so it is the human overseer's call rather than an agent's,
per `mockspace-toml-edits.md`. Propose the exact diff; do not apply it.

**A handful of others.** `arvo-refit` has no definitions at all and `no-empty-crate` says so.
`arvo-strategy` exports eight where the guideline is about five. `arvo/src/ifixed.rs:426` has a
`try_from` returning `Result`, which is the std-trait-impl exception and should be confirmed as such
rather than changed.

## Do not follow one of these messages literally

`no-bare-result` currently says `use Result<T, LoimuError>`. There is no `LoimuError` in this
workspace; the name belongs to a downstream consumer and leaked into the lint's text. The same class of
leak was just fixed in `no-bare-vec`, which had been telling every consumer to reach for six types that
exist in no repository it governs.

So read the finding, not the suggestion. The finding is right that a bare `Result` in a public
signature is wrong here; the suggestion names something that does not exist. `notko::Outcome<T, E>` is
the vocabulary, and the std-trait-impl positions are exempt. Fixing the message is mockspace's work and
is filed there.

## Why this is not being done now

It was found from kolli, while fixing a lint. Doing it there and then would have meant a hundred and
twenty eight findings triaged inside an unrelated arc, in a repository nobody was working in, with the
sorting done by whoever happened to be passing rather than by someone holding arvo's layering in their
head. The layer violations in particular want that.

The cost of filing instead is that the next person here inherits it. That is the trade, made
deliberately and recorded so it is not rediscovered as a surprise.
