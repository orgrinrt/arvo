# The IEEE harness reaches below the smallest subnormal

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Scope:** the IEEE 754 parity harness in `arvo-format`'s test tree, its suites, and what the design
and the crate summary say the suites cover.

The IEEE 754 parity suites land a value by the test tree's own search over magnitudes, the finest one at
which the rounded position is in the slot range. The search builds the position in the slot index's
integer, so a value whose position at the finest magnitude needs a denominator past that integer has no
position, and the search answers nothing. The harness says so of itself, and says its inputs never reach
there: the sweeps start twenty exponents below the smallest subnormal. Binary64 at `2^-1400` and
decimal64 at `10^-480` are in that region, and the standard's answer there is ordinary: the smallest
subnormal under the direction away from zero on the value's side, and zero otherwise. The crate summary
says each format has a parity suite against the standard's own answers, which reads as covering it.

The search does not need the exact position there. A value below the finest step has a floor of 0 or
-1, and an undithered adaptation compares the remainder against zero and against a half and nothing
else. Where the scaled denominator still fits the host's unsigned 128-bit integer, the remainder is
exact. Past that the value is under half a step, because twice the numerator is below `2^128`, and a
remainder in the same open interval keeps every comparison. So the search lands every value below the
finest step, and nothing is left unanswered on that side.

The digit rule the suites compare against has a slip in the same region. Where the dropped unit is
past the slot index it answers that the dropped part is under half a unit, which holds only when twice
the value is under the unit. At radix two a significand from `2^126` up, two to the 127th dropped, is at
least half a unit. No input reached it. The rule reads the unit in the unsigned 128-bit integer, where
that case is decidable, and past it the claim holds.

The inputs gain the region: a far value, the significand bound, the first unit past the slot index and
one past that, the tie at half that unit where the index holds it (radix two only), and a sweep from
four hundred exponents below the smallest subnormal. The decimal and binary16 suites take them through
the shared inputs. Binary32 and binary64 read the host, whose conversion of an integer scaled that far
down is not one rounding, so below the smallest subnormal they read the digit rule in a suite of their
own, with the two reviewed points pinned and a wrong direction shown missing.

This is the test tree only. Nothing in the crate changes, and the design states what the suites now
cover.
