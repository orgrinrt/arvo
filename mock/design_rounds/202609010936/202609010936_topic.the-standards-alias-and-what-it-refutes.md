# Topic: the standards alias, and the adequacy test it is

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** a convention alias over the shipped primitives, and the parity arms that check it.

`obligation::every_standard_convention_expressible_as_an_alias_over_the_primitives` asks for each
industrial fixed-point and floating-point convention to be writable as a first-class alias over arvo's
own primitives, so a consumer reaches for the standard's own vocabulary and gets arvo underneath, with
a parity suite running the same computation through both and asserting the same answer.

It is the one obligation a ratified row reaches. `ruling::behaviour_is_stated_per_declared_signature_and_the_premise_dissolves`
ratifies `proposal::every_operation_arvo_declares_is_a_function_of_the_declared_width` by name, and
that proposal carries the typed edge to this obligation. Its `because` is the reason the obligation is
worth doing at all rather than a nice property to have:

> It is forced by the adequacy test rather than chosen. A convention that cannot be written as an
> alias over the primitives is a gap in the primitives, so the two standards refute a candidate rather
> than merely preferring one, and only the declared-width statement survives both.

So this round is not decoration over the floor. It is the instrument that tells the floor whether it
is adequate, and the useful outcome is as likely to be a refutation as a clean pass.

## Why this one and not another obligation

`ruling::the_next_unit_is_chosen_bottom_up` carries op's criterion: whatever is natural and settles the
most downstream questions at once, built strictly ground up, one tier at a time. The floor is built.
`arvo-format` ships the declared signature, `arvo-placement` the derivation, `arvo-strategy` the
binding, and this is the first thing that sits on top of all of it and reads back down.

`question::which_standards_the_alias_obligation_covers` says in its own note why the timing is now:

> the parity suite is a mandate with no artifact, and building it is work rather than a decision. It
> cannot start until this is answered

It is answered. `ruling::the_standards_bound_starts_at_two_and_reserves_the_rest` records op's call:
MATLAB `fi` and `fimath` and IEEE 754 are the core, and SystemC `sc_fixed`, Vitis `ap_fixed` and
Algorithmic C `ac_fixed` are reserved rather than excluded.

## What the bar is, which is lower than it first reads

`ruling::the_standard_is_parity_in_output_not_in_the_internals` is op's, and it bounds the work twice:

> just to note: the standard should be for parity in output, not so much the internals. as long as it
> matches the logic of the spec, but perhaps achieves it slightly different or unconventional way,
> that is fine.
>
> and we can leave each standard a few holes with a FIXME to fill later, to be honest, that's not the
> important thing to ship, it's a fundamentally sound, expressible and mathematically accurate and
> exhaustive framework

So a hole in a standard is a `FIXME` and is honest; a hole in the framework is a defect. This round
takes that literally: every place the convention cannot be expressed is marked and named rather than
worked around, and each mark is a finding about the floor rather than an apology.

## The three things that came out of reading the convention against the floor

Written here because they decide the shape of the doc changelist, and because two of them are findings
whether or not anything is built.

### MATLAB's `Nearest` has no mode in arvo, and the name that looks like it means something else

MathWorks documents six rounding methods. `Ceiling` is toward positive infinity, `Floor` toward
negative infinity, `Zero` toward zero, `Convergent` is nearest with ties to even, `Round` is nearest
with ties toward the greater absolute value, and `Nearest` is nearest with ties toward positive
infinity.

`ruling::the_ambiguous_rounding_word_is_retired_for_six_explicit_names` closed arvo's vocabulary at
`toward_zero`, `floor`, `ceil`, `half_up`, `half_even`, `stochastic`, and says nothing about what
`half_up` means. The shipped source picked one: `rounding.rs` documents `HalfUp` as "to the nearest,
and a tie goes away from zero", and `apply.rs` implements exactly that, returning the neighbour
further from zero on a negative tie.

That is MATLAB's `Round`. MATLAB's `Nearest` maps onto nothing arvo has, and the conventional reading
of the words "half up" is the one arvo does not implement.

Two open rows are about this and neither may be filled inside a design.
`question::is_the_rounding_vocabulary_complete_at_six` asks whether the vocabulary is short a name,
treating `away from zero` as a mode outside the six, which is the mode the code ships under the name
`half_up`. `question::which_tie_direction_an_unqualified_nearest_names` records that `half_up` "is
itself two operations under the reading question that is still open".

**So the source has already answered a question the registry holds open, in code, and nothing in the
registry records that it did.** The round reports it and does not resolve it. Four of the six methods
map cleanly and are what the parity arms cover; the two nearest-with-ties methods are marked.

### The width ladder stops well short of the convention's

MathWorks documents `WordLength` from 1 to 65535 for an unsigned `fi` and 2 to 65535 for a signed one,
and `FractionLength` from -65535 to 65535.

`slots.rs` implements `Slots` at widths 1 to 62 and the trybuild case pins the refusal above it. The
bound is stated in the diagnostic: the count of slots is two to the power of the width, and two to the
sixty-third does not fit the signed 64-bit integer a slot index is carried in.

So a `fi` at `WordLength` 64, which is an ordinary declaration a consumer writes without thinking, has
no arvo format at all. That is the adequacy test finding a gap in the primitives, which is what
`proposal::every_operation_arvo_declares_is_a_function_of_the_declared_width` says the standards are
for.

Worth stating precisely rather than as an alarm: the bound is on the admitted set of declared widths
and it comes from the carrier the slot index sits in. Nothing about an operation reads a carrier, so
`ruling::behaviour_is_stated_per_declared_signature_and_the_premise_dissolves` is not contradicted.
What is true is that the vocabulary of declarable widths is bounded by a carrier choice, and the
convention wants a thousand times more of it.

### A negative fraction length needs no forbidden feature, and the obvious spelling does

MATLAB's `FractionLength` is the negation of arvo's quantum exponent, so the alias would want
`Constant<{-F}>`, and an expression in a const generic argument position needs `generic_const_exprs`,
which is forbidden.

The wall is not real. Naming what the expression computes gives a `Quantum` implementor keyed on the
fraction length whose `BASE` is that negation, computed in an impl body where an arbitrary expression
is legal, and the alias then bounds on the contract rather than on the expression. Nothing in what is
being constrained changes; only the spelling does.

## What this round does not do

**It does not touch IEEE 754.** It is in scope by op's bound and it lands in its own round, because
half of its rounding surface sits behind the two open rows above and because the interchange formats
want the indexed quantum family rather than the constant one, which is a different reading of the same
floor.

**It does not settle what `half_up` means, does not add a seventh mode, and does not narrow any
predicate that names one.** Those are `question::is_the_rounding_vocabulary_complete_at_six` and
`question::which_tie_direction_an_unqualified_nearest_names`, both open, both the panel's.

**It does not widen the width ladder.** The gap is reported with the declaration that exposes it. What
to do about it is a question about the slot index's carrier and it is not this round's.
