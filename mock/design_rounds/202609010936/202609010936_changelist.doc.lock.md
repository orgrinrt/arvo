# Doc changelist: the conventions ship beside the points, because nothing else can implement `Format`

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Round:** the standards-alias round
**Scope:** one section in one crate design, and one paragraph in the root design.

## Comparison to deprecated changelist

The first cut of this round put the conventions in a crate of their own,
`arvo-standards`, on the reasoning that the ratified spine keeps the concept closed and the inventory
open, so a growing inventory carrying marked holes should not live inside the crate that defines the
closed concept. That reasoning still reads well and it is not what happened.

**The crate could not be built, and the reason is a finding rather than an obstacle.** Fifteen
positions in a hundred-line file were refused by `no-bare-numeric` and `arvo-types-only`, and every
one of them is a position the crate does not choose:

- Six are const generic parameters, `const W: u32` and `const F: i32`. That is the position op
  excepted in the same sentence that stated the rule, quoted on
  `obligation::a_primitive_for_every_position_a_bare_number_would_take`: no bare integer at an API
  position other than in const generics, and even there only where the alternative is painful. There
  is no alternative at all, since nothing the stack owns may sit in a const generic argument.
- Nine are associated consts whose type the implemented trait fixes. `Quantum` declares `BASE` and
  `SLOPE` as `i32` and `MAGNITUDES` as `u32`; `Format` declares `PHASE_NUM` and `PHASE_DEN` as `i64`.
  An implementor writes what the contract says.

**So no crate outside `arvo-format` can implement `Format`.** `arvo-format` is exempt from the numeric
lints under `[primitive-introductions]` because it introduces the numeric category, and the exemption
has been carrying more than its own description: the contracts are themselves spelled in the machine's
own types, so the open inventory the format spine ratifies is open in practice only to the exempt
crate. Nothing had implemented `Format` from outside before, so nothing had found out.

The escape routes were tried and none applies. A `SHAME.md.tmpl` entry does not key on either lint and
silenced nothing. Adding the crate to `[primitive-introductions]` would be a false architectural
claim, since it introduces no substrate; that config's own comment calls the list an auditable claim
rather than a permission list. Fifteen inline suppressions are what
`strict-by-design-quality-pressure` names as not the response.

**What is left is the honest locus.** A convention is an instance of the open inventory, which is what
`points` already is, and `arvo-format`'s own module documentation calls those "worked instances of the
open inventory rather than the inventory itself". The conventions ship beside them, and the finding is
recorded rather than dissolved: it is written into the design, and the floor round it belongs to is
filed as its own topic.

## What this changelist is

`obligation::every_standard_convention_expressible_as_an_alias_over_the_primitives` wants each
industrial convention writable as a first-class alias over arvo's own primitives, with a parity suite
running the same computation through both and asserting the same answer. Nothing in the tree answers
it.

So `arvo-format`'s design gains the section that says what a convention alias is, what the parity bar
is, and what happens where a convention names something the floor cannot express. MATLAB `fi` and
`fimath` are the first member.

## The canon this declares

`ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon`, ratified. Arithmetic on a format is an exact operation composed
with a named total adaptation, which is what makes a `fimath` an adaptation rather than a setting.
And the concept is closed while the inventory of admitted instances is open, which is what makes a
convention an admission rather than an amendment, and what puts it beside `points`.

`ruling::behaviour_is_stated_per_declared_signature_and_the_premise_dissolves`, ratified, which
ratifies `proposal::every_operation_arvo_declares_is_a_function_of_the_declared_width` by name. That
proposal carries the typed edge to this obligation and its `because` is the whole argument for doing
it: a convention that cannot be written as an alias over the primitives is a gap in the primitives, so
the standards refute a candidate rather than merely preferring one.

`ruling::the_standards_bound_starts_at_two_and_reserves_the_rest`, op's, for the scope.

`ruling::the_standard_is_parity_in_output_not_in_the_internals`, op's, for the bar, and for a standard
being allowed to ship with marked holes where the framework is not.

`ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path`, ratified, for why a declaration the floor cannot
express fails to build rather than being reported.

## The bar a parity arm has to meet

An arm cites the convention's own documented answer, never a value read back out of arvo. Where
MathWorks documents what `fi` returns, that number is the assertion and arvo is what has to agree. An
arm that computes both sides through arvo asserts that arvo agrees with itself, which passes for the
wrong reason and always passes.

**A published number is not always the value.** MathWorks prints five significant figures, so four of
its five worked examples print a rounding of the real-world value rather than the value. An arm
asserting equality against the printed number is wrong, and the first cut of this round's tests was
wrong in exactly that way and failed. What holds is that the value rounds to what is printed, and that
is what is asserted, in exact integers.

## What is deliberately not here

**IEEE 754.** In scope by op's bound and its own round. Half its rounding surface sits behind the same
two open rows, and its interchange formats want the magnitude-indexed quantum family, which is a
different reading of the same floor.

**Any change to the rounding vocabulary.** `question::is_the_rounding_vocabulary_complete_at_six` and
`question::which_tie_direction_an_unqualified_nearest_names` are open and are the panel's.

**Any change to the width ladder, or to what the trait contracts are spelled in.** Both are what the
round found. The second is a change to this crate's own design that takes the code under it, so it is
filed as its own topic rather than started here.
