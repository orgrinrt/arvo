# The agent instruction paraphrases `standing` and drops the clause that answers it

`mockspace.toml`, `proposal` namespace, field `standing`, defines the vocabulary:

> How many independent instances reached it, over the region they share.
> `one_expert`; `two_experts`, each deriving before reading the other;
> `three_or_more`; `cross_topic` where separate topics arrived at it without
> citing each other, which is the strongest thing this panel produces;
> `contested` where it is stated because somebody stated it and somebody else
> disagrees.

`mock/agent/MAIN.md.tmpl:49` renders that into the instructions every session
loads, as:

> `standing` records how many independent instances back the claim

Two things go missing. `reached` becomes `back`, so the field reads as counting
who supports a claim rather than who arrived at it. And `each deriving before
reading the other` is gone entirely, which is the ordering condition that
decides whether an instance counts at all.

What that cost: four panel seats argued whether a member who read another's
conclusion before writing its own contributes to a `standing`. Two more seats
were spent answering it, and both answered it by opening the schema and quoting
the clause. Nobody had opened it, because the paraphrase reads complete.

A grep over `mock/agent/` and `.claude/` finds the ordering clause zero times,
and finds this the only registry field either surface paraphrases. So the class
is one line, in the template and in the file generated from it.

The fix is to quote the vocabulary rather than summarise it. A field whose exact
wording decides an outcome is one a paraphrase cannot carry, and this one is
short enough that there was never a reason to try.
