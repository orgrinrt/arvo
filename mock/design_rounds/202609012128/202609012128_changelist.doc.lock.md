# Doc changelist: the identity is membership at zero over both coordinates

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Round:** the identity search runs over the magnitude, and the coordinates are types
**Scope:** `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`, `mock/crates/arvo-placement/DESIGN.md.tmpl`

Nine changes. Four are the trunk conflict consolidated by hand, and each keeps both sides: the trunk
states what the identity question is and this branch states what the coordinates are, and neither
sentence is about the other's subject. Three state contracts the source gains. Two take off the public
page what a stranger should not be reading there.

## CHANGE: the identity paragraph FROM a rule about the phase alone TO the existential over both coordinates
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the section states the existential over magnitude and slot, and
`grep -c "does not claim the general case" mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl` returns zero.

Both sides of the conflict here answer the same question and one of them is wrong.

The trunk says zero is in the set exactly when some admitted magnitude and some admitted slot cancel
the phase, so the existential runs over the pair. This branch says the deciding property is whether
the phase is a whole multiple of the quantum, adds that the answer is about magnitude zero, and
records the rest as a bound it does not claim.

**The trunk's is what the canon states.** `proposal::membership_of_the_representable_set_is_one_affine_predicate`,
ratified through `ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon`, gives the parameterisation as an affine slot
function, a quantum per magnitude and a phase. Membership quantifies over the pair, and the identity
question is that predicate asked at one value, so it quantifies over the same pair. A statement fixing
the magnitude has answered a different question and the two functions in this crate disagreed about
one thing, which is exactly what the branch's own paragraph one section earlier warns about.

So the paragraph states the existential, and the whole-multiple property becomes what it reduces to
in the constant family rather than the deciding property in general. **In the constant family the two
agree**, because there is one quantum and one magnitude, and that is the family the reduction was
measured in: `proposal::the_additive_identity_is_decided_by_the_phase_being_a_whole_multiple_of_the_quantum`
names its fraction width as the constant family's exponent. The magnitude-indexed family is where they
part company, and the design says which is which rather than leaving a reader to find out.

The two consequences the whole-multiple property alone does not carry stay named, because each is a
region a reader can gate on: a whole phase whose cancelling slot is outside the slot range leaves no
identity, and a phase with a fractional part at one magnitude is a whole number of steps at a higher
one where the quantum shrinks.

## CHANGE: the arithmetic FROM prose about divisibility TO the equation with the intercept cancelled
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the section carries `i * d * radix^(SLOPE * m) = -n` and states that `BASE` moves no
answer at any magnitude.

The design named a divisibility question and never wrote what is divided by what, so the source was
the only statement of the arithmetic and a reader checking one against the other had nothing to check
against.

Written out, membership at zero is `phase + i * quantum(m) = 0`. With the phase a rational in units of
the quantum at magnitude zero and the quantum an affine power law, dividing through by `radix^BASE`
and clearing the denominator leaves `i * d * radix^(SLOPE * m) = -n`. **The intercept leaves the
problem entirely**, which is a checkable claim rather than a decorative one: moving `BASE` alone must
move no answer at any magnitude, and the suite asserts it.

## CHANGE: the free-function list FROM the membership predicate alone TO the intermediate beside it
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the list names `cancelling_slot`, and the paragraph beneath says why it is named rather
than inlined.

`cancelling_slot` is the slot that cancels the phase at one magnitude, if an admitted one does.

**It is named rather than folded into the predicate**, because the identity question is a conjunction
and a law over a conjunction alone cannot say which half a failure came from. Naming it makes each
half assertable on its own, which is what lets the suite distinguish a phase no magnitude can cancel
from one whose cancelling slot is out of range.

The branch's own paragraph about the predicate being `contains` asked at a computed position stays and
gains the coordinate it was missing: the position is a slot **and** a magnitude, and working out only
the slot is what made the two functions disagree.

## CHANGE: the format contract FROM three obligations TO four
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the obligations section names `Format::ADMITTED` beside the three, with the condition it
refuses.

`Slots`, `Quantum` and `Ambient` each state what an implementor owes as a checked const with a
verdict-returning form beside it. `Format` is the fourth open contract here and had none.

**A phase denominator of zero names no position on the grid**, so the phase coordinate does not
denote and the membership predicate has nothing to be asked about. That is the same kind of condition
as a slot range that inverts or a step law over no magnitudes, and it is refused the same way rather
than being written in a doc comment and hoped for.

## CHANGE: the ambient domain FROM a radix carried without a bound TO a radix of at least two
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the obligations section states the radix bound and the two values it refuses, with the
reason each fails.

The cancellation divides by powers of the radix, and the search's termination rests on the product
growing. Neither holds below two: at one the step never changes with the exponent, so every magnitude
names the same value, and at zero the quantum is not a number at a negative exponent at all.

**Neither is a positional notation**, so the coordinate does not describe the thing it is a coordinate
of, and the contract refuses it rather than the predicates guessing at it.

## CHANGE: the phase coordinate FROM a normalised ratio TO the pair as it was written
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the section states that the phase holds the numerator and denominator as declared, that
divisibility and the quotient are independent of the denominator's sign, and that the zero denominator
is refused by the contract.

The phase was normalised to a positive denominator on construction, and two writable pairs have no
such normalisation inside the width the coordinates are declared in: a denominator of `i64::MIN`, and
a numerator of `i64::MIN` under a negative denominator. Both were read as a denominator of one with
the numerator kept, which loses the sign on one and nineteen orders of magnitude on the other.

**The invariant was not needed by anything.** `n mod d = 0` is independent of the sign of `d`, and so
is the quotient, and nothing else reads the denominator's sign. So the coordinate holds the pair as it
was declared, every read of it is taken one domain wider than the coordinates, and the one denominator
that names no position is refused by the contract rather than reinterpreted.

**It was not only a lost value.** The identity answer moves on both pairs: `i64::MIN` over `i64::MIN`
is a phase of one whose cancelling slot is minus one and in range, and `i64::MIN` over minus one is a
phase of `2^63` whose cancelling slot is `i64::MIN` exactly. Under the normalisation the first has no
identity and the second names no slot.

## CHANGE: the door section FROM an account of what this document used to say TO what the door carries
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: `grep -ciE "an earlier version of this document|until this round|nobody intended"
mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl` returns zero, and the section still states that the two
primitives are not the bound, with the ruling cited.

This template renders into `docs/FORMAT.md`, which is a page a stranger lands on. Three sentences on
it are about the document's own history and one is about what the crate used to get wrong: that an
earlier version named a two-type bound, that until recently every coordinate was a host integer, and
that this had a consequence nobody intended.

**None of them is actable.** A reader arriving at the crate wants the contract, and what an earlier
draft of the page said is not part of it. The load-bearing claims survive without the narration: the
two primitives are not the bound, the door carries the coordinate set spelled in types the stack owns,
deriving the coordinates from `Width` and `Bool` is arithmetically impossible at three of them, and
how many types that divides into is not settled here.

**The bare-primitive reason stays and is stated forward.** A crate outside this one has to be able to
write a format's coordinates, and every one of them is a number or a truth value the lints refuse
everywhere but here, so a contract spelled in host types is one no outside crate can supply without
turning a lint off. That is a statement about the contract rather than about a previous version of it.

only content is which body of work produced something.
