# Doc changelist: the tree goes, and no document describes it any more

**Date:** 2026-08-08. **Round:** nuking the tree for the canon.
**Source topic:** `202608082156_topic.nuking-the-tree-for-the-canon.md`.

## What this round does

Deletes every crate under `mock/crates` and empties the workspace member list, per op's instruction
and per the mutation order in `the-canon-design-code-chain.md`. The canon is being written; the tiers
beneath it are declared dead, and declaring them dead is what licenses the work.

## Doc-side changes

**None to the per-crate documents, because they go with their crates.** Each `mock/crates/*/` carries
its own `README.md.tmpl`, `DESIGN.md.tmpl`, `BACKLOG.md.tmpl` and `SHAME.md.tmpl`. Those are design
tier, they describe code that is being removed, and a design that survives its code becomes a claim
about a document that no longer exists. They are deleted with it.

**The root templates stay untouched.** `mock/DESIGN.md.tmpl`, `mock/PRINCIPLES.md.tmpl` and
`mock/WORKFLOW.md.tmpl` describe the workspace rather than a crate's contract. They will need
rewriting once the canon exists and designs are derived from it, and that is a later round with a
canon to derive from. Editing them now would be writing design ahead of the canon, which is the
inversion this whole exercise is correcting.

## What is deliberately not in this round

**No canon text.** The canon is being written in `mock/research/202608072330_the-numeral-canon-panel/`
under an explore-do-not-settle mode that op has extended to roughly the panel's first hundred files.
This round clears the ground; it does not build on it.

**No replacement crates.** There is nothing to put back yet, and putting anything back would be code
without a design, under a canon that does not exist.

## Lock criteria

- `mock/crates` is empty and `mock/Cargo.toml` declares no members.
- `mock/Cargo.lock` is gone, since it pinned the deleted members.
- `mock/benches` is untouched: its artifacts are committed evidence and the panel's doability claims
  cite them.
- Git history and `origin/dev` both still carry the full tree, so nothing is unrecoverable and
  consumers pinned to `dev` are unaffected.
