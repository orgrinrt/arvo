## What is there now

`land` names the finest magnitude at which a point's rounded position is in the slot range. Three
things in it are wrong or missing.

The phase is read in the wrong unit. `Format` states a member as `phase * radix^BASE + slot *
radix^step(m)`, so the phase is in units of the magnitude-zero quantum, and the landing subtracts it in
units of the step at the magnitude it is at. At every magnitude whose step is not `radix^BASE` the
position is off by `(p/q)(radix^(BASE - s) - 1)` slots. On `Indexed<-4, 6>` over `Signed<4>` with the
phase one half, `21/32` is slot 5 at magnitude one, a member, and `land` returns slot 4 off the grid.
The test oracle copies the same formula, so it agrees with the code and checks nothing.

The walk's start can be past the right magnitude. The bound rests on `radix^t >= 2^(t floor(log2
radix))`, which is false for `t < 0` when the radix is not a power of two, `10^-1 < 2^-3`. On a 64-bit
decimal range, `2^126` lands at step `10^20` and loses a digit.

Past the top nothing lands, under a FIXME calling it a canon question. The row above now answers the
part that was the canon's: wrapping is admitted exactly where the representable set is one arithmetic
progression, and saturation is defined on any bounded set and sends a value past an end to that end.
What saturation needs from the applied map at more than one magnitude it leaves to design.

## What changes

The position is formed from the membership predicate. With `t = power - s` and `b = BASE - s`, the
position is `num/den radix^t - p/q radix^b`, brought over one denominator by the smallest power of the
radix that clears both exponents, `b` left out where the phase is zero so a zero phase never forms a
power it does not need. The oracle in the tests is rewritten from the predicate, and a second test lands
every member of a phased format and checks each lands on the grid at the finest magnitude holding it,
which reads no position formula at all.

The walk's bound takes `ceil(log2 radix)` for a negative shift. A falling law with a nonzero phase is
walked from its finest magnitude, since there the phase is `p/q radix^b` slots with `b` positive and
grows past any bound on the value's own term.

Past the ends. The finest-grid-in-range rule reaches the nearest member only where the union has no
gap, and it has none where the slot range holds zero: then every magnitude's range holds every finer
one's, both ends of the union sit on the top magnitude, and a point no magnitude holds is past an end.
A slot range that does not hold zero can leave a gap between two magnitudes, slots 8 to 15 at quanta 1
and 4, where 20 rounds out of range at both; what each mode does between two members on different
grids has no single grid to read a tie or a dither on. `land` refuses such a format over more than one
magnitude at compile time, as not yet admitted, under `arvo_land_refuses_a_format_whose_slot_range_does_not_hold_ze`.

Under saturation and clamping, a point past an end lands at the top magnitude, and `adapt` pins it to
that magnitude's end, which is the union's. The position at the top is formed as at any magnitude; where
its numerator leaves the four limbs over a denominator under `2^128` it is provably past the index on
the side of the term that left, and it lands as the end on that side.

Under wrapping, the row is applied as a refusal at compile time from the declared signature, asked of
the set rather than of the slope. `is_one_progression` is that question: a constant quantum is one
progression, and a magnitude-indexed set is where each coarser magnitude adds at most its one next
member at each end of what the finer ones hold. That holds only degenerately, over at most two
magnitudes with slots within -1 to 1, so on a set that is not one progression wrapping is refused as
the row refuses it. On a constant quantum the landing past the range is its one magnitude's position
and `adapt` wraps it, as on any grid. On a magnitude-indexed one-progression set the wrap is in the
flattened progression, modulo its count of finest steps, and not in the top magnitude's slot range that
`adapt` reads; that is not built, and `land` refuses it at compile time under
`arvo_land_does_not_wrap_on_a_one_progression_set_over_severa`. The slope test `is_constant_family`
stays what it is, the axis the two families differ on, and the operations keep it: they refuse a
magnitude-indexed quantum because their exact step has no magnitude coordinate, which is a different
question from wrapping, and the sentences saying no row defines wrapping there are rewritten to say
what the row now says.

`Scaled` takes its denominator as `NonZeroU128`, so a point always names a ratio and building one
checks nothing, under `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path`. Coord's word on the zero
denominator, to leave the convention on this branch, was given while `Scaled` was new on the branch and
had no consumer outside it; `Remainder::of` and `Fraction::of` keep their convention as he said, under
`a_zero_denominator_in_arvo_format_s_ratio_constructors_reads`.

The IEEE suites land through `land` for the overflow region too, and read the region from whether the
landed position is in range.
