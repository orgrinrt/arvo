## The accumulator is one arm of an open question

`question::which_chain_carrier_ships` is open and its decider is the panel. Its first option reads
"The named accumulator, which carries the budget only". `Accumulator<S, T, CAP, C>` carries a
capacity and an exact sum, which is that option built. The crate design said the type answered
nothing and was not offered as the carrier, and the first reading called that ambiguous. The second,
taken from the registry before it opened the first, calls it misaligned: a shipped type is in the
shipped set, and a design round of one helper is not the panel deciding.

Two seats have now read it and neither found it aligned. The repository's own instructions say an
open question is not silence and may not be filled inside a design. So the type leaves the branch:
its module, its tests, its compile-fail pairs, its mutant and its section of the design. Removing it
decides nothing about the question. The source as it stood is at commit `a303d07b` of this branch,
under `mock/crates/arvo-numeral/src/accumulator.rs` and `tests/the_accumulator.rs`, and its closed
rounds and committed mutant run stay in the tree as the record, so whichever answer the panel gives
can start from it. The question to op, ticket
`does_the_name_accumulator_in_arvo_numeral_pre_empt_which_cha`, stays open and is bumped with this
outcome.

## `holds_index` is a membership verdict on outside data

`ruling::the_ingest_door_waits_on_a_known_use`, at `stated`, says a runtime membership door at
ingest is not blessed now and may be considered where a known use is shown. The round that added
the host door, 202611132039-hccc, rested on `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path` and
did not cite this row. `Num::holds_index::<H>(host)` returns whether a host integer names a member,
which is a membership verdict on a datum from outside. No use for it is known: `from_index` completes
by the policy and needs no verdict from the consumer, and the debug check inside it can ask the same
thing privately.

So the public function goes and the verdict stays as a private one the debug check asks. `from_index`
and `index` stay: the first completes and the second is refused at build where it would truncate,
and neither validates. This is one seat's reading of the row, and a second is owed. Removing a
function nobody is known to use is the side of that call that fills nothing in. A question ticket
goes to op for whether such a verdict is wanted.

## The oracle sweeps sample widths

The sweeps of `div`, `convert` and the fractional `mul` against the tests' own arithmetic stop at
narrow widths: the quotient at 8 bits and one fractional grid, the conversion at sources of 16 bits,
the fractional product at 6-bit grids. The bands already carry an edge lattice and seeded pairs for
widths 9 to 64. The three operations are swept on those too, at exponents other than zero among
them. The named divisors at widths 5 to 8 were all odd on the unsigned side, so no unsigned quotient
there landed on a tie and `half_up` and `half_even` were never told apart; an even divisor joins
each set.

The oracle computes in `i128`. A product of two 64-bit slots scaled by a positive exponent can leave
it, so the wide sweeps keep to cases the oracle can hold and say which they leave out.

## A test that asserts nothing

the rerun sets is present, so the binary has no test that passes by doing nothing.
