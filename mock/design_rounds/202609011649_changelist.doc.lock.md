# Doc changelist: what decides the identity, and the obligation one contract has and the other does not

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Round:** the identity turns on a whole multiple, and nothing holds the magnitude count
**Scope:** `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`

Two paragraphs state the claim `retirement::a_nonzero_phase_removes_the_additive_identity` retires,
and that retirement names the design tier as one of the places that corrects it. A third section
states what an outside implementor owes and describes one of the two contracts that state an
obligation.

## CHANGE: the phase paragraph FROM the phase deciding by being nonzero TO deciding by being a whole multiple
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: `grep -n "nonzero phase decides" mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl` returns
nothing, and the paragraph names both directions and the magnitude the answer is about.

The phase is a rational in units of the quantum at magnitude zero, so a phase of one whole quantum
shifts the grid onto itself and leaves zero on it at the negated multiple, and only a fractional part
takes it off. The half-step grid keeps everything the paragraph already claimed for it, narrowed to
where it holds: it is not closed under exact addition, it carries neither zero nor one when its
quantum is one, and it is not a monoid carrier.

**The paragraph says which magnitude it is answering about.** The divisibility decides membership at
magnitude zero, which is the magnitude the phase is stated in units of. Where the quantum shrinks as
the magnitude rises a fractional phase can still be cancelled higher up, and neither the retirement
nor `proposal::the_additive_identity_is_decided_by_the_phase_being_a_whole_multiple_of_the_quantum`
reaches that: the replacement's predicate names the fraction width as the constant family's exponent,
so under the omission rule it says nothing about a magnitude-indexed law. The design states the bound
rather than claiming the general case, and the code catalogues the rest as a red arm.

## CHANGE: the law list's phase entry FROM one direction TO both, with what the old test could not reach
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: `grep -n "A nonzero phase removes the additive identity"
mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl` returns nothing.

The entry claimed the removal and named the test asserting it. Every phase that test has ever passed
to the predicate is fractional, because `Biased` fixes its denominator at two and every numerator the
arm writes is odd, so the entry described a law asserted over half its own space. It states both
directions and says that a suite trying only fractional phases cannot see the other half.

## CHANGE: the implementor's obligations FROM the slot range alone TO both contracts that state one
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the section names the quantum law's obligation, and `grep -n "at least one magnitude"
mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl` returns a line inside it.

`proposal::the_concept_is_closed_and_the_inventory_is_open` is what the section already cites, and the
sentence it rests on is that admission is a check rather than a negotiation. Two contracts in this
crate state an obligation in prose. The slot range's is checked. The quantum law's, that a law ranges
over at least one magnitude, is a doc comment, which is a negotiation, and it is the one this
section's own reasoning already condemns: a check a later edit can delete while every test stays
green, except that here there was never a check to delete.

The section says what a quantum law owes, that it is checked the same way and at the same moment, and
that the same qualification holds about codegen against `cargo check`.

**It also says what the absence cost, because that is what makes the addition more than tidiness.**
`contains` conjoins the magnitude range, so under a law over no magnitudes nothing is a member.
`has_additive_identity` read the slot range alone, so it answered that the identity is present. Two
functions in one file disagreed about whether zero is in the set, and the one that disagreed with
`contains` is the one not derived from it.

## CHANGE: the membership neighbours FROM a flat list TO naming which of them is `contains` at a position
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the paragraph listing the free functions says that `has_additive_identity` is `contains`
asked at one computed position rather than a second reading of the coordinates.

One predicate decides membership, so a function answering whether one particular value is a member is
that predicate at a computed position. Writing it as its own conjunction over the same coordinates is
where the two got to disagree, and saying so in the design is what keeps the next reading of it from
growing a second copy.
