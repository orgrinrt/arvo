# The bit tier on `arvo-bits`

**Date:** 2026-11-20
**Round:** job `arvo-numeral-doors`, branch `work/arvo-bits-tier`. Overhauls `arvo-bits`.
**Source:** stack-coord's call of 19 November, 21:39 and 21:44, that the bit tier of the numeral
contracts' step 5 leaves that round and lands in a round on `arvo-bits`, with its impls there; the
shape arvo-contracts named for it (`orgrinrt/arvo work/numeral-contracts 646e9a18`,
`mock/research/202611192100_numeral-contracts-shape/READING.md`, the bit tier); loru's ticket
`arvo_dev_numerals_have_no_shift_mask_or_host_door_below_poin`.

## What asks for it

`ruling::every_family_of_the_old_arvo_returns_and_the_shape_is_open` is ratified and says every family
the old arvo carried returns in function and in feature, with the shape of its contracts and traits open.
The bit contracts are one of those families. `proposal::the_old_bit_contracts_crate_is_gone` says the old
crate's name does not return and puts its function here: "Bit access on `arvo-bits`". It stands at one
expert, so it binds nothing alone, and stack-coord's call says the same thing for this work.
`obligation::set_operations_over_a_fixed_size_bit_set` asks for intersection, union and an emptiness test
over a set of a size fixed at build, each lowering to one instruction. Loru's port of two byte formats
asks for shifts and masks so a field is read, aligned and written without dropping to the host's integer
at every site.

The current design of `arvo-bits` refuses all of this in two places. It declines a width accessor as a
second name for an `N` the caller already has in scope, because nothing erases `N`, and it calls `masked`,
`raw`, `cast`, `xor` and `halves` the whole public surface. The first reason stops holding once a
consumer writes a function generic over any bit container: inside it, `N` is gone, and the width is the
one thing the bound has to give back. A scheduler's read set and a compiler's artifact set are exactly
that kind of consumer. So the design changes, in its own round, rather than by impls placed in another
crate, which is what the contracts shape first tried and its own reading refused.

## Where the traits live

They are declared here and implemented here. The bit tier is the container's own work, and
`arvo-format` holds formats and states it is not a container, so declaring the traits there to sit
beside `Bool` and `Width` would put a container's contract into a crate that says it has none. Their
results are typed in `arvo_format::Bool` and `arvo_format::Width`, so `arvo-bits` now depends on
`arvo-format`. The old refusal of that edge weighed the format's whole surface against one constant;
the weight is compile time, paid once, and the tier needs the format's truth value and count of bits at
every predicate and at the width, which is more than one constant now.

`arvo-numeral` keeps its operations inherent and `arvo-contracts` implements its traits by calling them,
and this follows the same shape. Each operation is an inherent `const fn` on `Bits<N>`, and the traits
are `const` traits whose impls call it. A crate that names `Bits<N>` directly, `arvo-digest` among them,
calls the inherent method and needs no feature of the toolchain; a crate generic over the tier enables
`const_trait_impl`, which is on the vetted list.

## The four traits

`BitWidth` carries `const WIDTH: Width`, the width a generic bound has erased.

`BitAccess` reads and writes one bit: `bit::<I>(self) -> Bool` and `with_bit::<I>(self, to: Bool) ->
Self`, the index a const generic. An index at or past the width is refused at build, by a private
assertion forced inside the method, the shape `ADMITTED` already has here.

`BitLogic` is `and`, `or`, `xor` and `not` over two values at one width, each `Self`, and `is_empty(self)
-> Bool`. `not` is not in the obligation, and loru's alignment asks for it: rounding a length up to four
is a sum and a mask by the complement of three. The intersection test the obligation names is
`a.and(b).is_empty()`, and on a 64-bit carrier that is one `test`; the round records a disassembly of it
rather than asserting the count.

`BitSequence` shifts and rotates, both directions, by an amount that is a const generic:
`shift_up::<K>`, `shift_down::<K>`, `rotate_up::<K>` and `rotate_down::<K>`. Up is toward the high bit. A
shift drops what leaves the width, and a rotation brings it in at the other end, both within `N` bits
rather than within the carrier. An amount at or past the width is refused at build; zero is the
identity.

## What is not here

Nothing past sixty-four bits. The obligation names a 256-bit set, and the old masks family returns over
this crate and the capacity crate in its own step, as an array of `Bits<64>`; whether a 256-bit
intersection test still lowers to one instruction is the open half of that obligation's own `gap`.

No shift by an amount known only at run time. Loru's bit writer wants one. A total version, where an
amount past the width gives zero, is a choice between a comparison in the lowered code and the host's
shift that wraps the amount, and that choice is not this round's to make quietly; it goes to a ticket.

No host door for a numeral is added here. `arvo-numeral` already has one at every width and both signs,
`Num::from_index` and `Num::index` over a `HostInteger` (`arvo-numeral/src/host.rs:88`, `:147`), which
loru's ticket read only at the pointer-width points.

## What the tests owe

Every law against the host's own operation on the carrier, exhaustively at every width up to eight for
every value, index and amount, and at the widths above on the edge lattice and seeded values: `bit` reads
what `with_bit` wrote and nothing else moved; the logic matches the host's within the mask, `not` is an
involution, De Morgan holds, and `is_empty` is true exactly at zero; a shift matches the host's masked to
the width, a rotation by `K` up then down is the identity and a rotation by `K` up equals `N - K` down.
Each trait method agrees with its inherent method, through a function generic over the bound. An index or
amount at the width is refused at `cargo build`, through the inherent method and through the trait.
