The gate refusal that opened this round is

So the sentence the refusal said had no wording now has one, and this file derives the design from it.

## The shape, and why it is the shape

Read the ratified floor carefully, because the design falls straight out of it and the earlier
refusal misread which fork was live.

> every operation the design declares is a function of the declared width and never of the machine
> carrier [...] The footprint is nonetheless observable, through exactly one observation the design
> does not own, the host language's layout observation on a sized type, and that observation exists
> only where the value is the sole logical occupant of its allocation.

Both halves at once. The carrier has to be **nameable**, or the footprint is not observable and
`ruling::cold_is_for_cold_paths_and_cold_storage` is an intent about nothing. And no operation may
**read** it, or behaviour is stated over the container, which the MATLAB divergence refutes.

That is not a compromise between the two branches this round refused. It is a different object from
either: the carrier sits in the type and is absent from every operation's statement. So the design has
two layers that were previously being argued about as one.

**The declared signature** is what behaviour is stated over. It carries the format and the adaptation,
and nothing else, because the admission rule says an operation is admitted exactly when it is a
function of the declared signature.

**The placement** is what the carrier comes out of. It is derived rather than declared, it is keyed on
occupancy, and it produces one output at sole occupancy and three at shared, per the derivation
ruling. No operation consults it.

The two probes committed with the refusal measured this correctly and their reading changes. `p2`
showed a shape with no nameable carrier cannot express two footprints at one declared width, and that
is now a refutation of a shape the canon rejects rather than an open branch. `p3` showed two
placements at one declared width do not share a homogeneous container, and that is now the correct
behaviour rather than a cost of one branch: `dimension::occupancy` is declared, the split is real, and
at shared occupancy there is no per-element footprint to observe, so the column is keyed on the
placement and not on the element.

## What a format is as a type

`proposal::membership_of_the_representable_set_is_one_affine_predicate`, ratified through the format
spine, gives the parameterisation and the design copies it rather than inventing one: an affine slot
function, a quantum per magnitude, and a phase, of which integers, fixed point, scaled integers and
floats are points.

So a format's obligations are the coordinates of that predicate, and membership is one predicate
evaluated at them. The fixed-radix family is the constant-quantum case and the floating family is the
magnitude-indexed case; the ratified sentence says subnormals fall out of a maximum with no special
case, so the two are one concept and not two.

The phase is carried explicitly rather than assumed zero. That is the ratified row's own instruction
and it gives its reason: a nonzero phase decides whether the identity adaptation ever occurs and
whether the set carries an additive identity at all, so a design that hardcodes zero has silently
closed a case the canon leaves open.

`ruling::the_concept_is_closed_and_the_inventory_is_open`, through the spine, decides the mechanism:
the concept is a trait carrying those obligations, admitted instances are its implementors, and a new
numeral joins by supplying the obligations rather than by amending anything. A closed enumeration of
formats is the shape the canon refuses.

## Where the computation goes, given the forbidden feature

`generic_const_exprs` is forbidden and `min_generic_const_args` is what there is, so a derived
quantity may not be computed in a bound. Every derived quantity is therefore an associated item
resolved by an impl rather than an expression evaluated in a signature, which is the standing
instruction to express a refused computation as a trait contract.

This is not a workaround, and it is worth saying why, because it will look like one to a reader who
meets the trait ladder first. `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path` wants const-time
branching erased by monomorphisation. An associated item resolved per impl is resolved at
monomorphisation by construction, so the contract form is what the intent asks for and the const
expression is the thing that would have needed erasing.

## How the rounding modes and the overflow policies are carried

The six names are ratified and closed by
`ruling::the_ambiguous_rounding_word_is_retired_for_six_explicit_names`: `toward_zero`, `floor`,
`ceil`, `half_up`, `half_even`, `stochastic`. Both spellings of the retired word are gone. That is a
closed set because a ratification closed it, and it is the one closed enumeration in this design.

The overflow policy is a separate axis, `dimension::overflow_policy`, and it is not closed.

Both are carried as members of the adaptation, because
`proposal::arithmetic_on_a_format_factors_as_an_adaptation_of_an_exact_operation` is ratified and says
arithmetic is an exact operation in the ambient domain composed with a named total adaptation onto the
representable set, with the adaptation a first-class object with its own laws. First-class is the
operative word: the adaptation is a thing the design names, not a flag on an operation.

**A derived call, marked as derived.** `panic` is not an implementor of the overflow policy concept.
The ratified factoring requires an adaptation to be total and a panic diverges, so it cannot be a
member of the slot. `ruling::the_overflow_panic_is_permitted_and_bounded` reaches the same bound from
the other side: the panic is a debug-build behaviour, and what stands in its place on release is a
declared mode that lowers accordingly. So the panic is a property of how an adaptation is applied
under a build profile, and `dimension::build_profile` is the axis that carries it. Seat 221 reached
this independently and filed it as a finding against `dimension::overflow_policy`, which still lists
`panic` among its values; this design does not repair that row, and the dimension set is append-only
so the repair would be a note rather than an edit.

## How a strategy selects

`ruling::the_derivation_is_a_placement_and_the_operation_set_is_an_admission_rule` states the position
directly, and it is the part of that ruling this round needed most:

> the ladder is keyed on the placement objective and the strategy-to-objective map is the selector,
> which preserves the earlier two-expert result that a strategy name is a binding.

So a strategy is a binding from a name to an objective and to an adaptation selection. It is not the
key of the ladder and it is not a closed enumeration.
`ruling::the_strategy_set_is_not_closed_at_four` is at `open` and
`ruling::the_strategy_intents_are_not_clear_cut` is ratified and says the per-strategy statements are
not to be written down as clear cut and settled. A design shipping four markers with a behaviour table
against each writes exactly what that ratified row forbids, so this design ships the binding concept
and the presets as instances of an open inventory, with their contents stated as intents rather than
as rules.

`ruling::there_is_no_exchange_rate_because_there_is_no_generalisation` is why the selection is per
arm and per operation rather than a ranking: no rate exists between objectives, each arm carries its
own justification in words, and a win is taken however small the region it applies to.

## What this design does not decide, because the registry holds it open

Named rather than filled, which is the correction this round was given after the refusal: where the
registry holds a question open, say so and stop.

`does_warm_wrap_or_clamp` and `where_wrapping_lives` both have a dissolution reachable from ratified
rows at one expert, per `221_dolan_the_numeric_fundamentals.md` section 7, and neither is a shape this
design has to pick. Which mode a preset names is a defaults question about a binding, and the design
carries the axis rather than the value. So nothing here depends on either closing.

`is_the_derived_numeral_required_to_be_tightest`, `does_precision_count_the_sign_digit`,
`does_narrowing_compose`, `what_the_double_rounding_mechanism_is` and the dither rows are open and
none of them is reached by the skeleton this round writes. Where a later round needs one, it is a
canon question and not a design call.

## The standards bound, and what it changes here

`ruling::the_standard_is_parity_in_output_not_in_the_internals` is op's and it moves what the design
owes. Parity is in output, for the cases a standard covers, and reaching the result by a different or
unconventional route is fine so long as the logic of the spec is matched. The design does not owe the
standard's operation list.

His ranking of the two is the part that reaches this round's scope:

> and we can leave each standard a few holes with a FIXME to fill later, to be honest, that's not the
> important thing to ship, it's a fundamentally sound, expressible and mathematically accurate and
> exhaustive framework

So a hole in a standard is a marked `FIXME` and is honest, and a hole in the framework is a defect.
This round writes framework and marks nothing in it as a hole.

The fused multiply-add is the worked case and it lands where that ruling puts it: its result is
reachable by composing multiply and add everywhere except at signed saturating, measured with
controls, so it is not a required inventory member and is a required arm at signed saturating. That
is `law::the_fused_and_the_stepwise_multiply_add_denote_one_function` and it is recorded at one
expert. The skeleton does not ship the arm and does not foreclose it, because an arm gated on a
region is what the design is made of.

## The gate that certifies this round's source is untested, and that is this round's problem

Five lints have zero tests: `no_std_enforcer`, `no_alloc_enforcer`, `no_dynamic_dispatch`,
`no_runtime_grow`, `arvo_bits_traits_only`. Every canon-facing lint in the same directory carries
between eleven and twenty-three test markers, so this is not a repository without the habit; it is
five files that never got it, and they are the five that read `mock/crates/`.

They enforce the operating constraints this round's source is bounded by, so the first crate written
from this canon is gated by five untested checks. Two of them have real holes, and the holes are
measured rather than read off the source, in
`mock/research/sketches/base-primitives-gate/p4_the_untested_enforcers_have_holes.rs`, which
transcribes both predicates verbatim and carries a control that fails if the transcription is wrong.

`no_std_enforcer` matches `use std::` as a line prefix and therefore never sees a qualified path
expression, which is the ordinary way to reach the platform without an import. `std::process::exit(0)`
passes it, and so do `use std as platform`, a brace-first import, and an import behind a `cfg`
attribute on the same line.

`no_alloc_enforcer` looks for the literal `" String"`, a space then the word, against the untrimmed
line. So `String::from(..)` at column zero has no preceding space and passes, while the same
expression indented is caught, which makes the verdict depend on layout. An identifier beginning with
the word behind whitespace, `Stringify` or `StringBuilder`, is a false positive.

**That last sentence was wrong in this file's first cut and the probe is what corrected it.** The
claim written first was that any identifier ending in the word would fire, naming `CString`. It does
not: the token wants a space then `S`, and `" CString"` has a `C` there. The probe carries that as a
permanent negative so the overstatement cannot come back, and it is recorded here rather than quietly
fixed because the difference between the two claims is the whole reason to build the instrument.

A gate with holes reporting green over source that violates the constraint is the fabricated
correctness the test discipline exists to stop, and it would be this round's own source it fabricated
it over. The src changelist covers the tests for all five, which is why this round's source phase
carries both the crate and the lint suites. The other three are untested and unexamined, and nobody
has looked at them, which is its own finding rather than a claim that they are sound.
