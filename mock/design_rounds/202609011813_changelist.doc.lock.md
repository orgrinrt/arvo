# Doc changelist: the placement crate declares a format of its own, and why that is its law

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Round:** the open inventory is asserted where nothing can refuse it
**Scope:** `mock/crates/arvo-placement/DESIGN.md.tmpl`

The format contract's open inventory is a ratified clause and no crate outside `arvo-format` declares a
member of it, so the clause is asserted only where the bare-primitive pack does not read. The
placement crate is the natural place for the first one, because it consumes declared signatures and is
checked like any consumer.

## CHANGE: the laws this crate owes tests for FROM silent on foreign formats TO naming one
File: `mock/crates/arvo-placement/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the laws section names a format declared in this crate, and
`grep -c "impl Format for" mock/crates/arvo-placement/src/tests.rs` returns one once the source lands.

**The derivation reads a format it has never seen.** The crate's whole subject is a function from a
declared signature to a placement, and a signature is a format paired with an adaptation, so a format
this crate declares itself is the case that says the derivation is over the contract rather than over
the four points that ship beside it.

**Where it is declared is the point rather than an incidental.** `arvo-format` introduces the numeric
category, so the bare-primitive lints skip it whole, and a format declared there is written under a
rule that cannot refuse anything. This crate is read by the pack like every other consumer, so a
coordinate written as a host integer here stops the gate, and the declaration standing is the
assertion. `proposal::the_concept_is_closed_and_the_inventory_is_open`, ratified through
`ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon`, is the clause: admission is a check rather than a negotiation,
and a check nothing performs is a negotiation with whoever reads the config.

**It supplies all of them rather than reusing the shipped parts.** An ambient domain, a quantum law
and a slot range that nothing ships, plus the phase the format itself carries, which is the whole set
an implementor writes. Borrowing the shipped parts exercises the one coordinate `Format` demands and
says nothing about the nine underneath it.

## CHANGE: what this crate does not decide FROM silent on the format contract TO naming the boundary
File: `mock/crates/arvo-placement/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the section says the format contract's obligations belong to `arvo-format` and that this
crate declares an instance rather than restating them.

A format declared here is a member of somebody else's inventory, so what it owes and what refuses it
are stated where the contract is. This crate asserts that its own derivation reads such a member, and
nothing about the coordinates' obligations is repeated here where the two copies could disagree.
