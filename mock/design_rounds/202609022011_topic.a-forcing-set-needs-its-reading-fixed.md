# A forcing set has two readings, and comparing sets without fixing one produces a false sentence

## What is wrong

`arvo-format`'s design says two things about forcing sets that cannot both hold.

At `DESIGN.md.tmpl:687` it says `contains` "forces the quantum's obligation and
the slot range's". At `:749` it says the verbs that force each obligation "differ
per obligation and the sets are disjoint from the slot range's".

`contains` carries no `let () = <F as ...>::ADMITTED;` of its own. It is
`magnitude_in_range::<F::Quantum>(magnitude).and(slot_in_range::<F::Slots>(slot))`,
and those two carry the obligations. So the first sentence is true only if a verb
is counted as forcing what it reaches through a call, and the second is true only
if it is not. The document never says which it means, so a reader picks one and
the other sentence is false.

Both sentences are mine, from the round that closed at `202609021905`, and the
second replaced an earlier false one about the same paragraph. The replacement
was the round's own stated justification for the change, which is where a claim
gets written to fit the decision rather than the evidence.

## What the source actually does

Direct force sites for `Format::ADMITTED`, crate-wide, two of them:
`cancelling_slot` and `has_additive_identity`. Neither set is disjoint from
anything: `cancelling_slot` forces the ambient's obligation on the line after the
format's, and `has_additive_identity` forces the quantum's on the line after. A
verb belongs to as many sets as it has force sites, so "disjoint" was never the
relation to reach for.

The transitive reading has a further property the sentence flattens. Whether a
reached site is actually reached depends on the declaration as well as the verb:
`has_additive_identity` returns early when `cancelling_slot` answers `Isnt`, so
the same verb reaches the slot range's obligation for one format and not for
another. A transitive set is therefore a property of a verb and a declaration
together, which is why it cannot be compared per verb.

## What this round does

Fixes the reading in the design, states the two force sites rather than a
relation between sets, and pins the transitive case as a test, which is the half
that was missing: the round added four arms pinning the format's own set and
nothing touching how sets relate, so the false sentence had nothing to fail
against.

It also takes three smaller things in the same surface, all found in the same
review: provenance narration that renders into published documents, a duplicated
fixture that grew its own guard test, and an arm that cannot fail.

## Scope

`arvo-format` only. Doc side: `DESIGN.md.tmpl`. Source side: `src/width.rs`
module docs and `src/tests/obligations.rs`.
