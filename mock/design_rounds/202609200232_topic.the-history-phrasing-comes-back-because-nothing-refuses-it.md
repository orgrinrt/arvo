# Topic: the project's history keeps getting written into published prose, and a sweep is not what stops it

**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Governing canon:** `ruling::half_up_denotes_ties_toward_positive_infinity` is what the instance is
about; the class is a writing rule rather than a canon question.
**Where this came from:** finding 5 of the second review of pull request #150. The first review asked for
this at one site, the fix round fixed it there, and the same sentence shape came back three paragraphs
later in the same document in the same round. That is the second occurrence, which is what makes it a
class rather than a slip.

## What the sentence shape is

A published document says what this project used to do. "the rule this crate shipped before the canon
settled", which is gone, and "is why the crate once recorded a gap the alias does not have", which
replaced it. The shape is a true statement about the design with a clause bolted on saying how the
design got there, and the clause reads as helpful while it is being written because the writer has just
finished doing the thing it describes.

The statement always stands without it. What the doubled-denominator spelling cannot form is a fact
about the spelling; that this crate once recorded a gap because of it is a fact about last week.

## The sweep, and what it found

Run over `README.md`, the rendered `docs/`, `mock/*.md.tmpl`, `mock/crates/*/*.md.tmpl`, and every `///`
and `//!` under `mock/crates/*/src` and `mock/crates/*/tests`, for prose that dates a rule or says what
the project used to do. Ten hits in the templates and their rendered copies, four in doc comments.

Two are the class and are fixed in this round:

- `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl:1250`, rendered at `docs/FORMAT.md:1259`. The review's instance.
- `mock/crates/arvo-bits/DESIGN.md.tmpl:26`, rendered at `docs/BITS.md:35`. "An earlier draft of this
  design gave `Bits<N>` a dependency on `arvo-format`". The section it opens is worth keeping and says
  why the dependency would cost more than it buys; what a draft did is the part that is about last week.

Two are not the class, and saying which is the point of a sweep:

- `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl:117`, "incomparable with the one that used to be the join".
  That names a state of the lattice over one kind, not a past state of this codebase: adding floating
  points to the space is what stops the point being the join. It is a measured result and it predates
  this work.
- `mock/DESIGN.md.tmpl:97` and `:103`, the paragraph saying the consumers named in earlier versions used
  crates that no longer exist. Here the history is the statement rather than a clause on one. It exists
  to correct what earlier versions of that document promised, and it carries the removal date so a reader
  can check it. Rewriting it to drop the history would delete the correction.

The four doc comments are all outside it: `lib.rs:39` is "used to define", a different sense of the
words; `the_broken_maps.rs:319` names a deliberately planted wrong rule, where what the crate shipped
before the ruling is the fixture's identity; and the two in `tests/compile_fail.rs` are a test harness
explaining its own shape, in a directory rustdoc does not print.

## The decisions

[between sweeping again and writing a check] It is checkable, so it becomes a lint. The phrase list is
closed and short, the surface is the one `half-up-carries-its-note` already enumerates, and the two
legitimate uses are nameable. A sweep leaves nothing behind that refuses the third occurrence, and there
has already been a second.

**A new repo lint, `no-project-history-in-published-prose`.** It reads the published surface for a closed
list of phrases that date a rule or name a past state of this project, and reports each. The surface is
the templates that render into `docs/`, the root `README.md`, and rustdoc on published items; a crate's
`tests/`, the design rounds, the research tree and the registry are not it, for the same reasons the note
lint gives.

**The legitimate uses are carved out by naming them, not by weakening the phrase list.** Two entries,
each with the sentence it excuses and why, so a carve-out that stops matching its sentence is itself a
finding rather than a silent widening.

[between the phrase list and something that reads the sentence] The phrase list. The half_up reader is
what reading a sentence costs, it took two rounds to get right, and the thing being caught here is
already a fixed vocabulary: a writer reaching for this shape reaches for one of eight or nine words.
