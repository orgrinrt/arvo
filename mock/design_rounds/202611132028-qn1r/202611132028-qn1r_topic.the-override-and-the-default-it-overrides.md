## What the round is for

A consumer that holds a wrapping value and wants one sum of it to saturate has to write the whole
sibling signature out and then carry the value across by three doors,
`Num::adapted(Exact::on_grid(value.slot()))`. The capability is there and nobody would type it at every
site. This round gives the override a spelling, gives the crossing a name, and repairs the one place
where the default's mechanism would have closed a question that is still open.

It does not ship a default. Which adaptation the default names is the panel's, and the round stops
before it.

## The override

`WithOverflow<S, O>` is the signature `S` with its overflow policy replaced by `O`, and
`WithRounding<S, R>` the same for the rounding mode. Both are type aliases over
`Signature<S::Format, Adapt<_, _>>`, as the sketch has the first.

They go in `arvo-format`, beside `Signature` and `Adapt`. Every type they name is that crate's and
nothing in them is about a value, so the value crate would be holding a function over another crate's
types. `arvo-format`'s overhaul is another lane's; this is two aliases added beside what stands.

An alias and not a wrapper type. The overflow policy is no property of the value: it sits in the
signature, and a newtype around a value would be a second place for it. The alias is the signature
itself, so `Num<WithOverflow<S, Saturate>>` is the same type as the written sibling and every operation
and test that holds for one holds for the other with nothing transported.

The alias states no admission. A type alias carries no bound, so `Num<WithOverflow<S, O>, C>` owes
`WithOverflow<S, O>: Sole` where it is written, concretely by the compiler and generically by the
author. That is what the ruling on predicates asks for. The names `Saturating` and `Wrapping` are not
taken as aliases: the overflow axis is open and two names would be a closed list over it, and the
alias with the policy named covers every policy that will ever be admitted.

## The crossing gets a name

`under::<T>()` on `Num<S, C>` gives `Num<T, C>`, for `T: Sole` with `T::Format` equal to `S::Format`.
The member is the same and the signature it is held under changes.

It is a move of the carrier. The carrier type is derived from the format's slot family alone, and the
encoding is one member to one bit pattern per format, so two signatures sharing a format hold a member
identically. The compiler already knows the two carrier types are one type from the `Format` equality
in the bound, which was tried before the design was written: the body is the carrier handed over, with
no adaptation call. So the question the sketch left for a bench has no measurement to take. There is
no arithmetic in the body to lower.

The bound is what keeps it honest. `T: Sole` is written by the caller, so the admission is not
transported. The `Format` equality is what makes the slot a member of the target by construction; a
crossing to another format would have to round or complete, and that is `convert`, a later round's.
Naming a `T` over another format is a type error at the call.

Two shorthands sit on it for the per-case override at a value: `with_overflow::<O>()` gives
`Num<WithOverflow<S, O>, C>` and `with_rounding::<R>()` gives `Num<WithRounding<S, R>, C>`. Each states
the sibling's admission in its own where clause. They are for a concrete site. Code generic over the
signature takes the target as a parameter and calls `under`, since a where clause over a projected
sibling has to be repeated by every generic caller above it, which is the cost the sketch names.

The check `C` does not change in a crossing. It is an axis of its own and no part of the signature.

## The default's mechanism, and the one repair

The aliases already read their adaptation through `NamesAdaptation`, so that a strategy can name it
later and `A` can gain its default without breaking a consumer. That is the generic carrying a default,
minus the default.

As it stands the trait has one associated type and no parameter. So whatever names an adaptation names
one, for every format an alias could ever be over. A default that wraps integers and saturates
fixed-point quantities cannot be written against it, and that is a region the open question may land
on. The mechanism would have answered part of the panel's question by having no way to say the other
answer.

So `NamesAdaptation` takes the format: `NamesAdaptation<F: Format>`, with `Adapt<R, O>` naming itself
for every `F`, and each alias reading `<A as NamesAdaptation<its format>>::Adaptation`. A preset then
names an adaptation per format, by as many impls as it has regions, and one blanket impl where it has
one. It is done now for the reason the projection itself shipped early: an implementor written against
the one-parameter trait would be broken by the later one.

Nothing selects a default. `A` keeps having none, `Uint<8>` stays an error, and the line that gives `A`
a default lands with the panel's ruling. What the round shows instead is that the line is one line: the
consumer crate declares a preset of its own that names different adaptations for two formats, and an
alias of its own over `Uint` whose `A` defaults to that preset.

## What is proved

The crossing, through the value type at widths 1 to 8, both slot families: for every ordered pair of
the two admitted policies and every ordered pair of the five modes, every member crosses to the same
slot, equals what `Num::adapted(Exact::on_grid(slot))` builds, and crosses back to the value it left.
One named case shows the value that arrives adds under the policy it crossed into. A mutant,
`mutant-under`, sends the top of the range to the bottom in a crossing, and its red run is committed.

The sketch's two refusals as compile-fail pairs in the crate: a value under `WithOverflow<_, Clamp>`
beside one under `WithOverflow<_, Saturate>`, and a value under `WithRounding<_, Stochastic>` beside
one under `WithRounding<_, HalfEven>`. A crossing to a signature over another format, refused beside
one to a sibling.

The consumer crate, with no feature line, writes an override at a type and at a value, declares its
preset over two formats and its defaulted alias, and asserts what each gives.

nothing asked for it in this round.
