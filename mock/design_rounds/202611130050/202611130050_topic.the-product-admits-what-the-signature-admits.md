# Topic: the product admits what the signature admits

**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Round:** step AR3 of the roadmap, the product's second round, from review pass one of
`work/oracle-sub-neg` at `71856b84`.
**Canon:** `ruling::the_derivation_is_a_placement_and_the_operation_set_is_an_admission_rule`.

## What the last round got wrong

The ratified row says an operation is admitted exactly when it is a function of the declared
signature. The product of two members is one wherever the format has a constant quantum: two members
are `(phase + a) * q` and `(phase + b) * q`, and their product is the member at position
`(phase + a)(phase + b) * q - phase`, whatever the phase and whatever the radix. The last round refused
a phase and a radix other than two, and both refusals came from the job brief, which carries no
authority against a ratified row. The reason the design gave for the radix was also false: the
remainder of an exact position is a `Fraction`, which carries any positive signed 64-bit denominator,
and addition already carries thirds.

The last round also refused `UFixed<64, -k>` for every `k`, because the slot product was formed checked
in the index's own integer before the move. Its positions fit the index for every `k >= 1`. The
refusal was a property of the intermediate, not of the position.

## The step, in general

With the phase `n / d` in lowest terms, the radix `r` and the quantum's exponent `e`, the position is
`((n + a d)(n + b d) r^e - n d) / d^2` at `e >= 0`, and `((n + a d)(n + b d) - n d r^k) / (d^2 r^k)` at
`e = -k < 0`. Its denominator divides `d^2 r^max(-e, 0)`, so the remainder an exact position holds is
the one condition the phase and the radix together owe, and it has the shape of addition's: addition
asks that the phase's reduced denominator fit the remainder, and the product asks the same of
`d^2 r^max(-e, 0)`. At zero phase and radix two it is `2^-e <= 2^63 - 1`, the old `e >= -62`. At radix
ten it is `e >= -18`.

The numerator is formed in an integer four 64-bit limbs wide. A factor `n + a d` is at most about
`2^64 * 2^31.5` for an admitted range and an admitted phase denominator, so the product of two is under
`2^192`, and the move up by `r^e` stays exact until it leaves 256 bits, at which point the position is
past the index anyway. The slot is the floor of that numerator over the denominator, the remainder its
residue, reduced to lowest terms. No division appears except that one by the remainder's own
denominator, which the obligation bounds.

The position is bilinear in the two slots, so over a box of slots it is extreme at the four corners,
and whether every member product is one the index holds is decided by the four corner positions, each
rounded as far as one slot past where it sits when it is not on the grid.

## What stays held out, and why

A magnitude-indexed quantum. The product there is still a function of the signature, so this is a
placeholder and not a refusal by design: `Exact` and `adapt` carry no magnitude coordinate, and no canon
row says what wrapping means on a union of magnitude ranges, the same two things that hold addition
back. Ticket `arvo_product_over_a_magnitude_indexed_quantum_is_a_placehold`, cited by a `FIXME` at the
assert.

A phase whose denominator is zero names no member, and nothing is computed for it.

A remainder denominator `d^2 r^max(-e, 0)` past `2^63 - 1`, which no exact position carries, and a
corner position the index does not hold. The second is where the 64-bit ranges part now:
`Integer<64>` is admitted at a top position of `2^126`, `UFixed<64, k>` for `k >= 0` is held out at
`2^128` and past, and `UFixed<64, -k>` for `k >= 1` is admitted. The ticket
`arvo_s_product_over_the_unsigned_64_bit_range_needs_a_slot_c` is narrowed to `k >= 0`.

## The rest of the review

`negation_position` and `difference_position` force the addition obligation, or that
`difference_position` forces the slot range's: deleting any of those three lines leaves the suite
green, so each gets a compile-fail case with a twin. The product's oracle sweep stops at width eight
while the carry decision is made at widths nine to sixty-four, so an edge sweep over every admitted
width of both families, at whole and fractional exponents, goes beside it. The wrapping associativity
row's fails cell is `F > 0`, so every fraction width from one to `W` is crossed at each width rather
than one per family. The garbled clause in the subtraction section of the design, and the comment in
`subtraction.rs` past width 80, are fixed in passing.
