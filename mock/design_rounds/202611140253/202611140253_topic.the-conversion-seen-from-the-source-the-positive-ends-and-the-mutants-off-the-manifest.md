## Current state

The conversion rounds by the target's mode and completes by the target's policy, on the provisional
stamp `proposal::a_conversion_is_one_placement_onto_the_target_grid` carries. The design says a
conversion that read the source's mode or policy would disagree with the sweep under every source
signature. For the policy that is not so. That sweep crosses one step of exponent, minus one onto zero,
at one width and one range a side, and there no member leaves the target's range, so no policy is ever
consulted. Every other sweep fixes the source at `Adapt<TowardZero, Wrap>`. A conversion that saturates
wherever the source saturates and otherwise completes by the target's policy passes every test of the
crate, so the distinction the stamp decides has no test that fails on it.

The product's family of exponents is capped at one, and the quotient's and the conversion's run from
the deepest exponent to one. The format admits much more on the positive side: the product at
`Fixed<8, 40>`, the quotient at `Fixed<64, 5>`, the conversion from `Fixed<8, 20>` onto `Fixed<8, 0>`.
The design and the product's test say the family ends at the last exponent the format admits, which is
true at the negative end only.

The twelve mutants of the crate are cargo features of the published manifest, and each branches inside
a shipped `const fn`. Cargo unifies features across a build, so a crate anywhere in a consumer's build
that turned one on would change every other crate's arithmetic under signatures it never touched. That
is the argument the design itself gives against a feature for the debug check. Eight of the twelve are
this branch's; the four rung mutants are on `dev`.

`proposal::an_order_is_named_exactly_where_a_crossing_is_lossy` still says, in its note and its gap,
that whose reduction governs is the maintainer's to decide. `ruling::the_panel_finishes_the_canon_without_him`
gives every remaining canon question to the panel, and `question::whose_reduction_governs_a_lossy_crossing`
names the panel as its decider. The stamped proposal does not say which question it is a reading of.

## Changes proposed

The sweep under every source signature gains a second step, exponent zero onto minus one, on which the
widest members leave the target's range, and both pairs of ranges a side, so a member leaves the range on
both ends. Every source policy then meets every target policy where completion is consulted. A mutant that
completes by saturation wherever the source saturates is committed, and its red run shows the new sweep
catches it.

Each family of exponents ends at the format's own edge on both sides. The product's highest exponent is
the highest at which every product of two members is a position a slot index holds, the quotient's the
highest at which the remainder's denominator fits at the range's ends, and the conversion gets a sweep of
its own at the positive edge of either side: the source at its highest exponent onto exponent zero, and a
source at zero onto the target's highest. Each edge is computed in the tests' own arithmetic and held
against the format's verdict at every width, both ranges and the three radixes: admitted at the edge and
refused one past it.

The mutants leave the manifest. Each becomes a configuration flag of the compiler, `arvo_mutant_<name>`,
which only the build at the top sets, through `RUSTFLAGS`, and which no dependency can turn on for another.
The flags are declared to the compiler's configuration check in the crate's manifest, so a misspelt one is
reported. The four rung mutants move with the eight, since they are one mechanism.

The note and the gap of `proposal::an_order_is_named_exactly_where_a_crossing_is_lossy` say the panel
decides and point at the stamp, and the stamped proposal names the question it reads.
