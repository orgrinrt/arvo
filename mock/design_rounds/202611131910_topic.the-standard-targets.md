## What the sketch found

`mock/research/sketches/202611131910_ieee754-ties-to-even-through-adapt/` writes binary32 as the shipped
`Floating<25, -149, 254>` and asks the host for its `roundTiesToEven` answer at ties on both sides of
zero, a binade carry and the subnormal edge. Outcome WORKS for finite results, with no change to
`arvo-format`'s source. `adapt` carries no magnitude, so the caller picks it: the finest magnitude at
which the rounded position lands in the slot range, which is the standard's own rule of the nearest
member at the precision the binade allows. A carry out of a binade is the next magnitude answering. The
planted `half_up` is reported.

## What changes

The IEEE 754 interchange formats are declared as one generic composition, `Interchange<A, P, EMAX>`,
over the standard's own parameters: an ambient domain for the radix, the precision and the largest
exponent. Its quantum law is the magnitude-indexed family from `emin - (p - 1)` over `2 emax`
magnitudes, and its slot range is the significand, symmetric at `radix^p - 1`, because IEEE 754 is sign
and magnitude rather than two's complement. Binary and decimal are the same law at two radices, so
`Binary<P, EMAX>` and `Decimal<P, EMAX>` are aliases over it and binary16, binary32, binary64,
decimal32 and decimal64 are aliases over those, at the standard's table of parameters. Binary128 and
decimal128 have no format: their significands pass the slot ladder's 64 bits, which is the same bound
the MATLAB word length meets, and a compile-fail case records it.

The rounding-direction attributes are re-exported under the standard's names, four onto shipped
modes; `roundTiesToAway` is MATLAB's Round alias again and has no mode. The overflow policy stays the
consumer's parameter, because the standard's answer past the largest finite member is an infinity and
the format has no member there: that is a refusal recorded, not a policy chosen.

MATLAB's declarations move into `standards::matlab` beside `standards::ieee754`, unchanged. Each parity
suite gains planted wrong modes, and each planted arm is required to fail. The MATLAB suite gains a
sweep of every rounding method and both overflow actions against MathWorks' definitions written as
integer rules.

## Where the declarations live

Two options, with the evidence for each.

A module of `arvo-format`, `standards`, split into one submodule per standard. For: the module exists
and holds MATLAB already, the design section on the industrial conventions is in this crate's design,
and every coordinate a declaration names (`Quantum`, `Slots`, `Format`, the ambients) is this crate's,
so the declarations are instances of its open inventory sitting beside `points` the way the design
says. Against: T2 will overhaul `arvo-format`, and the standards are then inside the crate it moves.

A crate of its own, depending on `arvo-format` only. For: the parity suites would read the crate's
public surface and nothing else, which proves the "only from its own fundamentals" claim by the
dependency edge, and an overhaul of `arvo-format` would meet the standards as a consumer. Against: a
sixth crate is a decomposition change while the CLAUDE.md says what sits above the five is open, and
the existing MATLAB declarations would move crates in the same round.

Taken: the module. Every declaration here is built from public items already, so the dependency-edge
proof is available by reading the imports, and moving crates is not this round's to do. Recorded as a
provisional stamp for op on a question ticket.
