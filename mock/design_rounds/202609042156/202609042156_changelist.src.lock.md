# Source changelist: sweep every width, and give the widened lint its arms

## `crates/arvo-bits/src/tests.rs` (update)

The module header's defence of its sample goes, and the sample goes with it.

- A `mask_by_folding(n)` `const fn` that builds the mask by setting one bit at a time in
  a `while` loop, with no special case at the top width. It is the oracle every sweep
  below asserts against, and it is deliberately a different computation from the shipped
  `MASK`, which is a closed form with a branch. Two forms agreeing at a width is
  evidence; a second copy of the closed form would be a restatement.
- `the_folded_oracle_rejects_an_off_by_one_mask`, asserting the oracle disagrees with
  itself at every adjacent pair of widths, that it is not zero at one bit, and that it is
  `u64::MAX` at sixty-four. This is the arm that stops the sweeps passing against a
  broken oracle exactly as they pass against a correct one.
- An `every_width!` macro over the literals one to sixty-four, expanding four sweeps:
  that construction from the all-ones input lands on the oracle's bits at every width;
  that the drop law holds at every width over eight raw probes chosen to move different
  bits, including both alternating patterns, the top bit alone and both ends of the
  range; that masking an already-narrow value is the identity at every width; and that a
  cast from every width to every width lands on `raw & mask(M)`, all four thousand and
  ninety-six pairs, through a `cast_from::<N>` helper carrying its own inner macro over
  the same sixty-four literals.
- The probe array is a `let` inside the function rather than a `const`.
  `a-contract-coordinate-is-not-a-host-primitive` reads every file in this crate
  including the test module, with no exemption for tests, and it is right to: a const
  there is a position an outside implementor writes too. These are raw host inputs being
  fed to the one function that exists to take them, so they are a value in a function
  rather than a coordinate on a contract. The lint caught this itself.

The five existing tests stay exactly as they are. They name what they exercise and the
sweeps do not replace them; a sweep says a law holds everywhere and a named test says
which case somebody was worried about.

## `lints/a_contract_coordinate_is_not_a_host_primitive.rs` (update)

Three arms, in the `--- scope ---` section beside the one for a crate outside the list.

- `a_host_typed_const_inside_arvo_bits_is_reported_like_one_in_arvo_format`, building its
  fixture with `.with_crate_name("arvo-bits", "bits")` rather than going through `hits`,
  which is fixed to `arvo-format` and is why the widening had no coverage.
- `a_const_in_arvo_bits_spelled_in_the_crates_own_type_is_silent`, the half that stops
  the first passing vacuously. A lint firing on everything in a crate is not an exemption
  reaching that crate, it is a lint with no predicate.
- `the_exempt_list_names_the_two_crates_that_have_a_door_and_no_others`, pinning the list
  itself. Its own doc comment says a third entry is a decision needing its own round, and
  this is what makes a quiet fourth crate loud.

## What the control established, and what it costs to lose

`MASK`'s branch was changed from `N == 64` to `N >= 63`, making the mask wrong at exactly
one width and right everywhere else.

`construction_masks_to_the_oracle_at_every_admitted_width` and
`construction_drops_exactly_the_bits_above_the_width_everywhere` both fail and both name
`N = 63` in the message. The eight tests that were in the file before the round all pass.
Eleven passed, two failed.

So the sample was covering fifty-nine widths by assumption, and the assumption was the
one the crate's own source refutes. `MASK` was restored and the suite is thirteen
passing.

## Not in this changelist

`mockspace.toml`'s `[primitive-introductions]` entry. Whether the door should be
crate-wide or narrower is a question the canon does not settle in either direction, so it
is op's rather than a round's, and it is recorded in the topic file.
