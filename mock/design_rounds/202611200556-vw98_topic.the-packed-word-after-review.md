# The packed word after its first review

**Date:** 2026-11-20
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `arvo-bits`, the packed word of round `202611200533-wwxt`
**Source topics:** the review of `work/arvo-packed-word` at `0c8ffe54`, pass 1

The review found the word's build-time refusals open to a consumer, its unlisted-field sentence false,
its width refusals without their messages and its laws tested over one width. Decisions, the review's
findings by number:

1. Findings 1 and 2. `Fields` and `Holds` were public and unsealed, so a consumer's own list could claim
   any sum, cover and overlap and switch every refusal off, and the design named neither. They become
   unnameable: declared `pub` in a private module and not re-exported, reachable only as the bound on
   `Layout::Fields` and on `get` and `with`. The surface stays the four the design names. A case pins an
   impl of `Fields` by hand as refused.
2. Finding 3. `Holds` matches a field by place and width. Matching by marker type needs a type identity
   the crate has no way to compare without an index the caller would have to name. A marker at a listed
   field's place and width is that field under a second name and reads and writes the same bits, so it
   stands, and the design says so; a test pins the alias reading the listed field.
3. Finding 5. A width of zero or past sixty-four, and a field reaching past bit sixty-four, are refused
   with a message of their own before any shift, for a field and for the container.
4. Finding 8. A write masks what `low` gives to the field's span, so a value breaking `low`'s contract
   cannot reach another field.
5. Finding 4. The laws are also tested over a `Bits<64>` word: one field the whole width, and a `Bool` at
   bit zero, a 62-bit field and a `Bool` at bit sixty-three.
6. Finding 6. Two mutant flags, `arvo_mutant_bits_word_get` and `arvo_mutant_bits_word_with`, as the tier
   carries, with their red runs in a sketch.
7. Finding 7. The disassembly is rerun from a clean tree.
