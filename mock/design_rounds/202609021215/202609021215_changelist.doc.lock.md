## CHANGE: `arvo-strategy/DESIGN.md.tmpl` FROM naming what settled each contract TO stating it
File: `mock/crates/arvo-strategy/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: `grep -ciwE 'op|ratified|reviewer|panel' mock/crates/arvo-strategy/DESIGN.md.tmpl` returns zero, and every backticked identifier still resolves in source.

Twelve lines. The pattern throughout is a paragraph opening on a row's slug and
the fact that it is ratified, then the contract. The contract stays and the
opening goes. Three carry more than the framing: the strategy set being open is
a design fact and stays, said as a design fact; the preset cell called stale
under a restatement goes entirely, because a reader cannot act on it; and the
gap recorded rather than papered over becomes what the gap is.

## CHANGE: `arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl` FROM the same TO the same
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the same grep on that file returns zero, allowing for `round` and `op` appearing inside ordinary words the pattern does not match.

Seventeen lines, the largest of the three. Two of them are the sentences that
started this: a ruling's looseness described as ratified rather than tolerated in
the lead designer's own request, and a section opening on being derived and
marked as derived. Both go, and the loose bound stays with the reason it is
loose, which is a fact about the design.

One paragraph goes entirely rather than being rewritten. It said an earlier
version of this document had claimed something false and that the false sentence
appeared three times in two files. A reader cannot act on that and it tells them
the author was recently wrong about the subject they are being taught. What
replaces it is the thing the paragraph was guarding against: do not read the two
shipped shapes as the two that can exist.
