# The crate design documents say who decided, on a page that ships

The three per-crate design templates render into `docs/`, which is what a
stranger lands on. `documentation-writing.md` calls that tier 3: internal by
location and public by what it renders into, so the rendered half binds, and it
is explicit that a design file may not carry the lead designer's ratifications
or any other account of how the project came to know a thing.

They do, throughout. Measured across the three templates rather than the one a
reviewer happened to open: thirty-four lines carry `ratified`, `op`, `panel` or
`reviewer` as words. Not one of them is a stray. The house style of all three is
to introduce a contract by naming the row that settled it and saying it is
ratified, and only then say what it requires.

The technical content under that framing is fine and is not what changes. What a
reader wants from `arvo-format`'s design is that arithmetic on a format is an
exact operation in the ambient domain composed with a named total adaptation. It
is neither better nor worse for knowing that a ratified row says so, and it is
worse for the sentences it takes to say it, because the reader now has to hold a
registry vocabulary they cannot use.

The worst of it is not the word `ratified`. It is the sentences that report a
disagreement or a status: a preset cell called stale under a restatement, a
question described as open with the panel named as its decider, a count sitting
outside what was ratified, a paragraph opening on the fact that something is
derived and marked as derived. Each of those is a fact about where the project
is in its own process, addressed to a reader who is not in it.

## What stays

The registry slugs stay. `ruling::`, `proposal::`, `question::` and
`obligation::` render into the same `docs/` tree as `RULING.md`, `PROPOSAL.md`
and their siblings, so a reader of the published tree can follow one, and
dropping them would take a working cross-reference out to fix a leak that is in
the prose around it rather than in the reference itself.

Whether that rendered registry belongs in the published tree at all is a real
question and a wider one than this. It is not settled here and nothing in this
round assumes an answer.

## What replaces it

The contract, stated. Where a sentence today reads that a ratified row requires
something, it reads that the thing is required. Where a paragraph opens by
placing a claim on the provenance ladder, it opens on the claim. Where a status
is reported, it goes, unless what it says is a limit a reader has to work
around, in which case the limit stays and the process around it does not.

The one shape that survives intact is a genuine open choice the design leaves to
the consumer, because that is a fact about the design rather than about the
project. It gets said as what it is: this is not decided here, and here is what
that means for you.
