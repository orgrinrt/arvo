## The exact steps, from the same algebra addition's step comes from

A member of a format at slot `a` is `(phase + a) * q`. Every operation the brief names is a signed sum
of members, and a signed sum of members with signs `s_i` is

```
sum_i s_i * (phase + a_i) * q = (phase + (sum_i s_i * a_i + (sum_i s_i - 1) * phase)) * q
```

so its exact position is the signed sum of the slots plus `sum_i s_i - 1` copies of the phase. The
quantum and the radix drop out for the same reason they drop out of addition, which is the brief's
reason these steps belong in this crate rather than in one that can reach a carrier.

- Addition, `s = (+1, +1)`: `a + b + phase`, one copy. What `sum_position` computes now.
- Negation, `s = (-1)`: `-a - 2 * phase`, minus two copies.
- Subtraction, `s = (+1, -1)`: `a - b - phase`, minus one copy.

So the new steps are the addition step with a different signed sum of slots and a different count of
phase copies. `phase_parts` already takes the count of copies as its argument and has been asked only
at one; the new steps ask it at minus one and minus two, cached beside the one-copy parts so each is
settled once per format at monomorphisation, the same as the one-copy parts are now. The position is
then built the way `sum_position` builds its own, from the whole part added to the slot sum and the
reduced remainder as a `Fraction`, and that construction is moved into one private helper both use
rather than written three times.

## Admitted exactly where addition is, and why nothing further is needed

The brief asks for the new steps to be admitted exactly where `is_addable` admits addition. The
obligation addition forces has three conditions, and each has to be shown to cover the new steps, or a
format addition admits would leave the coordinate under negation or subtraction.

1. A constant quantum. The algebra above is stated at one quantum, so the condition is the same one.
2. A remainder the position holds. The reduced denominator of `k` copies of the phase divides the
   reduced denominator of one copy: `gcd(k * num, den)` is a multiple of `gcd(num, den)`. So wherever
   the one-copy remainder is held, the minus-one and minus-two remainders are held too.
3. Positions the slot index carries. Addition's condition is `2 * MIN + floor(phase)` and
   `2 * MAX + ceil(phase)` inside the index. A phase is a pair of signed 64-bit integers, so
   `|phase| <= 2^63`, and the condition then puts every member inside `(-2^126 - 2^62, 2^126 + 2^62)`.
   Negation's positions are `-a + floor(-2 * phase)` and one above it, with `|floor(-2 * phase)| <=
   2^64`, so they sit sixty bits inside the index. Subtraction's are `a - b + floor(-phase)` and one
   above it. Here the bound on each member alone is not enough: it allows `MAX - MIN` up to
   `2^127 - 1` exactly, and a phase of `-2^63` then carries the top position `2^63` past the index.
   What closes it is the slot range's own obligation, which bounds the span below `2^64` because no
   admitted width is wider than 64 bits. With it, `|a - b - phase| < 2^64 + 2^63 + 1`.

So negation needs nothing beyond addition's obligation, and subtraction needs the slot range's
obligation as well. `add` already forces that one, through `adapt`, so the composed operations `neg`
and `sub` are admitted at exactly the formats `add` is, and `is_addable` is the verdict for all three.
The exact step `difference_position` forces the slot range's obligation itself, because unlike
`sum_position` it is not exact without it; it is the one place where the exact step's admission is
narrower than `is_addable` says, and only for a format whose slot range is not admitted, which `add`
refuses anyway. The alternative, a separate `is_subtractable` verdict, would put a second name on the
same set of formats.

## Slots that are not members

Addition's step is total over every pair of slots: for a non-member there is no member sum to be exact
about, and the slot sum saturates once at the ends of the index's host integer. The new steps keep
that. Negation's slot sum is `floor(-2 * phase) - a`, one subtraction, which saturating arithmetic
makes exact up to the saturation. Subtraction's is `a - b + floor(-phase)`, three terms. Saturating it
step by step is the error `saturating_sum`'s doc already names, so it is saturated as one act: where
`a - b` fits, the whole part is added with one saturating add; where it does not, `a - b` left the
index on `a`'s side, and a whole part of the opposite sign is added to `a` first, which cannot
overflow, before one saturating subtraction of `b`. A whole part of the same sign as the overflow
leaves the answer at the end it was already past.

## Which verdicts exist for the new steps

The reach exists for both and is added for both. `negation_reach` covers `-MAX + floor(-2 * phase)`
to `-MIN + floor(-2 * phase)`, `subtraction_reach` covers `MIN - MAX + floor(-phase)` to
`MAX - MIN + floor(-phase)`, and the grid state of each is decided by the remainder of the copies it
adds, the way `addition_reach` decides its own: on the grid at no remainder, a tie at one half, off the
grid otherwise. The grid-state tail of `addition_reach` is extracted into a helper the three share.
Each carries no translation, for the reason `addition_reach` carries none.

The associativity verdict does not exist for either. Negation is unary, so associativity is not a
property it can have. Subtraction is not associative in the ambient rationals either:
`(a - b) - c` and `a - (b - c)` differ by `2 * c`. The absorption criterion decides whether reducing
onto the representable set breaks a law the exact operation keeps, and there is no law here for the
reduction to keep. The composed law the registry records, `(a + b) - c == a + (b - c)`, does hold in
the ambient rationals, and its count is reproduced as a test. A verdict for it in the shape of
`addition_is_associative` is a sufficient condition, the relocation law at the sum's reach under
translations `-MAX - phase` to `-MIN - phase` together with the relocation law at the difference's
reach under translations `MIN + phase` to `MAX + phase`, and it is left as a follow-up rather than
built in this round, since the brief names the verdicts of the addition section and that one is not
among them.

## The tests

Repeated per new step, from the addition section's set: the exact step is the denotation (values
computed with the quantum multiplied in, over exponents on both sides of zero, radix two and radix
ten, whole, half and third phases); totality over every admitted width of both families, at the edges
of the range and the ends of the coordinate, with the true answer where the true position decides it;
exactness where the exact position is a member; the identity where the format has one (`a - z == a`,
and `-z == z`); the reach is the positions the step produces; the steps evaluate at check time.
Commutativity does not carry over and is replaced by the laws that do: `a - a` is the position of the
value zero, `-phase`, at every member; negation is an involution on the exact positions; and the
difference is the sum with the negation, `difference_position(a, b)` equal to `sum_position(a, n)`
wherever `negation_position(b)` is the member `n` exactly.

New, as the brief asks: exhaustive equality of `neg` and `sub` with exact rational arithmetic, adapted
by an oracle that rounds and completes independently of `adapt`, at every width from 1 to 8 of both
slot families, every rounding mode, wrap and saturate, and the phases the shared list names. And the
registry's count: `(a + b) - c` against `a + (b - c)` at eight bits unsigned under saturation, whole
phase, diverges on 13,882,880 of 16,777,216 triples, which the row at `law.toml:196` records.
