# Topic: the ties-away alias, and the gap that was the test helper's

**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Governing canon:** `ruling::half_up_denotes_ties_toward_positive_infinity`, ratified on two expert reads
under `ruling::the_panel_finishes_the_canon_without_him`, which op ratified.
**Where this came from:** the review of pull request #150, whose first blocking finding is that the alias
gap the closed round `202609192215` recorded is false.

## What the canon says

The ruling's `says`: ties away from zero is a different operation on a signed domain and is not `half_up`.
"It is reached as an alias, `toward_zero(x + sign(x) q/2)`, which meets the standards bound as a result
rather than as a name. The two coincide on unsigned domains and wherever no tie is representable." So what
the canon asks of this crate is that the alias's result be reachable, not that some operation named the
shift exist.

## What the closed round wrote, and where it came from

`202609192220_changelist.src.lock.md` and the design it descends from say the alias can be formed "only
where twice the ratio's denominator fits the ratio's own integer or the denominator is even", and
`DESIGN.md.tmpl` at 1685 to 1686 concludes that "the alias is expressible over part of the domain and by
some callers only". Three sites pin that reading: the `ties_away` doc and the arm
`the_alias_cannot_be_written_at_a_denominator_past_half_the_fraction` in `tests/matlab_fi_parity.rs`, and
a FIXME in `src/standards.rs`.

The helper in that test builds the shift over a doubled denominator, `slot + (2n +- d)/(2d)`. That is one
way to write the shift and it is the way that stops earliest. Nothing about the surface requires it.

## What was measured

`mock/research/sketches/the-ties-away-alias/`, committed with its output. Two spellings of the alias from
the public surface alone, swept against `sign(x) floor(|x| + 1/2)` as an integer rule, over slots at both
ends of the index and around zero, denominators from one to eight and at both parities near `i64::MAX / 2`,
`i64::MAX - 1` and `i64::MAX`, numerators at both ends of each denominator and at its midpoint, under
`Integer<8>` and `Integer<64>` at `Wrap` and `Saturate`.

The shift, written without doubling, matches the oracle at every cell it forms. With an even `d` it adds or
subtracts `d/2` over `d` and moves the slot by one where the sum would leave the ratio's integer; with an
odd `d` no tie is representable and the alias is `half_up`. It fails to form in one region only:
`slot = i128::MAX`, `d` even, `n + d/2 > i64::MAX`, where the shifted position lies past the top of the
index and `Exact::between` cannot name it, because naming a position past the top takes a carry inside the
ratio and that numerator does not fit. No tie lies in that region.

The reading off the position matches at every cell, including positions whose ratio carries past either end
of the index: at a tie whose slot is below zero the alias is `floor`, and everywhere else it is `half_up`.
It reads `Exact::is_tie` and `Exact::slot`, both public, so it is available to a caller holding an `Exact`
it did not build, which is what the design said was impossible. Five planted wrong spellings are reported
by the same sweep, the doubled shift among them, which is unformable at 548 of 1196 cells rather than only
past `i64::MAX / 2`.

## The decisions

Taken from the review and the dispatching brief, which named the class rather than the instance.

[between pinning the gap as the test found it, and sweeping the public surface to find where the alias
actually stops] The sweep decides it. Every claim that states a limit of the alias is corrected or deleted:
the `ties_away` doc, the arm pinning `None`, the FIXME in `standards.rs`, the design's adaptation section
and its open item.

[between deleting the open item and narrowing it] Narrowed, and what remains is stated exactly: no
operation on an `Exact` forms the shifted position, and nothing needs one, because the alias's result is
what the canon asks for and that has a spelling everywhere. The one corner where the shift cannot be formed
is named with its region rather than as a limit of the alias.

[between adding an operation for the shift, adding a seventh mode, and adding nothing] Nothing is added.
The vocabulary is closed at six by a ratified ruling, and the alias is a composition a consumer writes.

## What the design owes after this

A sentence in the adaptation section saying the alias has a spelling at every position, one in the open
items saying what is actually missing and that nothing depends on it, and a test replacing the pinned
`None` with the sweep, carrying the planted wrong spellings as the controls that say the sweep can fail.
