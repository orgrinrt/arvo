# Topic: the magnitude lint is wrong in both directions, and why the shape of its reader is the cause

**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Governing canon:** `ruling::half_up_denotes_ties_toward_positive_infinity`.
**Where this came from:** the second blocking finding of the review of pull request #150. The lint
`half-up-is-not-a-magnitude-rule` runs at hard error on every gate, and it both misses prose that gives
`half_up` the magnitude reading and refuses prose that says something true.

## What the reader does today

`mock/lints/half_up_is_not_a_magnitude_rule/reading.rs` cuts a passage into clauses at `;`, at `.`, `:`,
`?` and `!` before whitespace, and at a blank line. Inside a clause it looks for a spelling of the name and
a reading from a fixed list, and it lets the pairing through when a negator sits anywhere before the later
of the two, when a marker anywhere in the clause says the clause is about another rule, or when the clause
holds two or more of `,`, `{` and `}` and the name and the reading fall in different items of it.

Both escapes are stated over the whole clause rather than over what the escaping word governs, and that is
the defect. A negator before the later of the two catches "unlike", "no" and "except" wherever they sit,
including in a fronted phrase about something else. Any two commas make a list, which swallows an ordinary
parenthetical and any sentence with a comma-separated aside in it.

## What the review measured

Six sentences that must fire and do not:

- "Unlike `half_even`, `half_up` sends a tie away from zero, as MATLAB's Round does."
- "With no dither to read, `half_up` sends a tie away from zero."
- "Except for the unsigned formats, `half_up` takes a tie away from zero."
- "`half_up` rounds to nearest and, for negative inputs, rounds the tie to the larger magnitude."
- "For `half_up` the tie at -2.5 goes to -3, away from zero, like Java."
- "`half_up` is nearest. Its tie goes away from zero."

Two that fire and must not:

- "The error of `half_up` is at most half a quantum in magnitude."
- "`half_up` bounds the error by `q/2` in magnitude at every sign."

## The decisions

[between adding the eight sentences as arms and changing how the reader binds] The reader changes, and the
eight sentences are the first entries of a corpus rather than the whole of the work. A word list patched
sentence by sentence is the shape that produced these.

**A clause is cut into segments at commas, brackets and braces, and an escaping word governs inside its own
segment.** A negator voids a pairing when it stands in the reading's segment before the reading with no
conjunction between the two, or in the name's segment before the name. So "Unlike `half_up`, the alias
sends a tie away from zero" stays silent and "Unlike `half_even`, `half_up` sends a tie away from zero"
fires, and the two differ by the name the escaping word governs rather than by where it sits.

**A list is two or more segments that are bare items.** A segment is a bare item when nothing is left of it
after the term, a conjunction, an article and the punctuation. Both the name's segment and the reading's
segment have to be bare items for the list escape to apply, so "The modes floor, away from zero, and
`half_up`" stays silent and "For `half_up` the tie at -2.5 goes to -3, away from zero, like Java" fires.

**A marker for another rule is split in two.** A qualifier of a thing, "planted", "wrong", "broken",
"defect", "mutant", "mutation", "old rule", voids only where it sits in the name's or the reading's segment,
so "`half_up` takes a tie away from zero, unlike the planted map" fires where it used to pass. A condition
on the whole clause, "formerly", "previously", "used to", "before the ruling", "no tie", "without a tie",
"away from a tie", "reaches no tie", voids the clause as it does today.

**A reading that is also ordinary vocabulary is read for what it describes.** "In magnitude" is a reading
only where a direction word sits in front of it, "up in magnitude", "larger in magnitude", "away ... in
magnitude". "Half a quantum in magnitude" and "`q/2` in magnitude" are error bounds and say nothing about
where a tie goes.

**A clause with no name of its own can take the previous clause's.** Where a clause opens with a pronoun
for the mode, "it", "its", "this mode", "the mode", "that mode", or continues the sentence before it with a
conjunction and a verb, the name of the clause before it carries in, within the same paragraph. That is
what "`half_up` is nearest. Its tie goes away from zero." needs, and it is bounded at a blank line so a
name does not leak down a document.

**Every sentence in the corpus has a near-twin on the other side.** Each sentence that must fire is paired
with one that must stay silent and differs by one thing, and each sentence that must stay silent is paired
with one that must fire. A corpus of one-sided examples is how a reader gets tuned into agreeing with
itself.

**The reader is mutated again once the corpus passes**, arm by arm as the previous round did, and the run
is committed beside the old one. A corpus a mutant survives is not testing the arm it names.

[on the registry exemption, from the review's second read] An answered question no longer has its whole row
skipped. Only the `answered` field is, along with `options` and `quote`, because `asks`, `note` and
`because` are prose somebody here writes and the lint reads prose.

## What this does not decide

Whether the word lists are complete. Nothing in a corpus of sentences says that a negator or a reading
missing from a list exists, and no mutation of the reader finds one either. That is the same limit the
previous round's sketch named, and it stands.
