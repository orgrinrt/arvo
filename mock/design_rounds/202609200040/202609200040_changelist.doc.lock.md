## Canon

`ruling::half_up_denotes_ties_toward_positive_infinity` governs, as it did in the closed round. What it
says about the alias is that ties away from zero "is reached as an alias,
`toward_zero(x + sign(x) q/2)`, which meets the standards bound as a result rather than as a name", and
what its `note` says about the name is that the note travels with it. Both are what the design now has to
agree with. No row changes in this round, and the registry is not edited.

## CHANGE: the adaptation section stops sending the alias to the open items
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`, "The adaptation is a first-class object", the paragraph
naming the alias.
The sentence "Whether a caller can write that alias against this crate is under the open items at the
end, and today the answer is only in part" goes. In its place: the alias has a spelling at every position
the surface constructs. At a tie whose slot is below zero it is `floor`, and everywhere else it is
`half_up`, which reads `Exact::is_tie` and `Exact::slot` and nothing else, so a caller holding a position
it did not build can write it. A caller that holds the slot and the ratio can write the shift itself,
adding or subtracting half the denominator over that denominator rather than doubling anything, with the
one corner it cannot reach named in the open items.

## CHANGE: the planted tie rule is described, not dated
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`, "The laws the applied map owes tests for", the paragraph
listing the broken maps.
"which is the rule this crate shipped before the canon settled what `half_up` denotes" goes, replaced by

## CHANGE: the standards section says where Round is written rather than that it is unwritable
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`, "The overflow map is total and the rounding map is not".
"the alias has no spelling in this crate yet, for the reason under the open items" goes. What replaces it:
Round has no mode, because the vocabulary is closed at six and ties away from zero is not one of them, and
the alias is a composition a consumer writes from the public surface. A `fimath` naming Round therefore
names no rounding mode, which is the vocabulary's closure and not a gap.

## CHANGE: the parity section carries the alias sweep
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`, "What a parity arm asserts, and what a published number
is".
A paragraph for what the alias arm asserts: both spellings of the alias against
`sign(x) floor(|x| + 1/2)` as an integer rule reading no arvo code, over slots at both ends of the index
and around zero, denominators at both parities from one to the ratio's own maximum, numerators at both
ends of each denominator and at its midpoint, under two formats and both overflow policies, with the
positions whose ratio carries past an end of the index included. Planted wrong spellings, `half_up`
alone, `toward_zero` where `floor` belongs at a tie below zero, zero read as negative, the shift always
upward, the shift composed with `floor`, and the doubled shift, each required to be reported, so the
sweep is shown able to fail.

## CHANGE: the third open item is the shift's one corner, not the alias
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`, "Open items", the third gap.
The claim that the alias is expressible over part of the domain and by some callers only goes, with the
sentence about twice the denominator. What stands in its place: this crate names no operation forming the
shifted position, and the alias does not need one. The gap that is real is `Exact::between` naming a
position past the top of the index only through a carry inside the ratio, so at `slot = i128::MAX` with an
even denominator and a numerator above `i64::MAX - d/2` the shifted position cannot be constructed. No tie
lies in that region. Where an operation on a position lives, if a consumer ever wants the shifted position
itself rather than the alias's result, stays undecided here.

## CHANGE: the root readme carries the note
File: `README.md`, the rounding paragraph under "What has settled".
One sentence after the retired word's: `half_up` sends a tie toward positive infinity at every sign, so a
tie at -2.5 goes to -2, and it is not the `HALF_UP` of Java or Python, which sends that tie the other way.
The root readme is hand-written rather than generated, which is why the closed round's sweep over the
templates did not reach it, and the source changelist lands the lint that would have.
