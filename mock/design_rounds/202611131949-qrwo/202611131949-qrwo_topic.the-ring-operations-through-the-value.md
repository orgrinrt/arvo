## What the tree holds

`Num::add` moves both carriers to the machine integer of the carrier's size, calls one function of
`rung.rs`, and moves the result back. Below the carrier's width the rung adds and then adapts onto the
declared width with `adapt_s8` and its seven siblings; at the carrier's width the machine's own wrapping
or saturating add is the answer. `Sole::ADMITTED` holds a value to a zero phase and a constant quantum,
which is where the sum of two slots is the slot of the sum.

The same admission makes the difference and the negation slot arithmetic too: at a zero phase
`difference_position` is `a - b` and `negation_position` is `-a`, both on the grid. So both are carrier
arithmetic followed by the same adaptation the sum uses, and nothing rounds.

The product is different in one respect. At a zero phase its position is the slots' product times the
quantum, `a b r^e`. At exponent zero that is the slots' product, on the grid, and only the completion
acts. At any other exponent the position is moved up by `r^e` or divided by `r^-e` with a remainder, and
at a negative exponent the rounding mode decides between two slots. The value crate has no rounding of
its own and is not to grow one: the six modes are `arvo-format`'s, in `round_slot`, private to it.

`arvo-format` refuses the product over the unsigned 64-bit range at exponents at or above zero, as not
yet admitted, because the corner `(2^64 - 1)^2` is past the slot index. Its ticket is
`arvo_s_product_over_the_unsigned_64_bit_range_needs_a_slot_c`, and the lane that overhauls the format
crate holds it.

## Decisions

### The difference and the negation are carrier arithmetic

`sub` and `neg` are inherent `const fn` on `Num`, dispatched like `add` onto rung functions written once
per carrier width. Signed, below the carrier's width, the machine's wrapping difference or negation is
exact and the rung's adaptation completes it; at the carrier's width the machine's wrapping or
saturating operation is the answer. Unsigned, a difference or a negation below zero wraps by the mask
under wrapping and is zero under saturation, which is not what the rung's unsigned adaptation gives a
wrapped bit pattern, so the unsigned saturating arms are the machine's `saturating_sub` and a constant
zero and do not pass through it.

The negation of an unsigned member is admitted, because the format admits it: its position is `-a`, and
the adaptation puts it back. Under wrapping that is the two's complement negation at the declared width,
under saturation zero. The value states no behaviour of its own, so it does not refuse what the format
computes.

### The product computes in the carrier where nothing rounds, and through the format where something does

At exponent zero, whatever the radix, the product is the slots' product and only the completion acts.
That arm is carrier arithmetic: under wrapping the machine's wrapping product followed by the rung's
adaptation, since the low bits of a product depend only on the low bits of its factors; under saturation
the machine's checked product, completed by the adaptation where it fits the machine integer and pinned
to the end on the product's sign where it does not. This is the arm integers take, the platform-width
ones among them.

At any other exponent the product is `arvo_format::mul` on the two slots, called as it stands. The
position there needs the radix raised to the exponent, a division with a remainder, and one of the six
rounding modes, and all of that has exactly one home. A second rounding in this crate, however fast,
would be the parallel arithmetic the plan forbids. What the audit found is true and stays true for this
arm until the door below exists: the format's step runs in a four-limb integer, and a fixed-point
product through it is slow. That is ticketed and marked in the source where the call is made.

Which arm an instantiation takes is a constant of it, read from the format's quantum, so one arm
survives monomorphisation as the rung dispatch does.

The product forces the format's own multiplication obligation as a constant of the instantiation, in
both arms. So `mul` on an unsigned 64-bit integer value is refused at build with the format's own
message until the format admits that range, and is admitted by that change alone with nothing edited
here. The carrier arm would compute a product there, and no oracle exists to call it right, so it does
not ship ahead of one.

### The verdicts

`fits_sub`, `fits_neg` and `fits_mul` return `Bool` and say whether the operation needs no completion.
The first two ask `slot_in_range` of the slots' difference and negation, as `fits_add` does of the sum.
`fits_mul` asks `arvo_format::panic_on_overflow` of `product_position`, so that at a fractional exponent
the verdict is about the rounded slot and cannot disagree with the map. Under `Abort` a debug build
asserts the verdict before the operation, with the message `add` uses; under `Quiet`, and in release,
the branch is a constant false.

### The multiplicative identity belongs to the format, and the value reads it

`Identity<Multiplicative>` is implemented for every `Num<S, C>`, and its constant asserts the format's
predicate first, as the additive one does. No such predicate exists in `arvo-format` today, and the
question it answers, whether some member of this format is one, is the format's. So it is added there
and not worked out in the value crate: `multiplicative_identity::<F>()`, which gives the slot of the
member that is one, or nothing. With the phase `n / d` in lowest terms, the radix `r` and the exponent
`e`, one is the member at slot `r^-e - n` when `d` is one and `e` is at or below zero, and that slot is
in the range. The function finds that candidate and then checks it with the product's own step at both
ends of the range, where the position is affine in the other factor, so the answer is the step's and
not a second derivation of it.

This is one of the two additions to `arvo-format` this round makes, both small and both named here. The
format crate's overhaul is another lane's.

### A signed fixed-point point

`points::Fixed<BITS, FRAC>` is the signed twin of `UFixed`: a constant quantum at exponent `FRAC` over
the signed range, at zero phase. `Biased<BITS, FRAC, 0>` is already exactly that format, and the format
crate's own reason for making `USize` an alias holds here word for word: through the `Format` contract
the two are indistinguishable, and a new type would carry a distinction the concept does not have. So
`Fixed` is an alias of `Biased` at a zero phase count. This is the second addition to `arvo-format`.

### The door at the container's width, as a proposal for the format lane

The lane that overhauls `arvo-format` writes a per-width adaptation that the value crate calls into.
Its shape is proposed here, from what this crate needs, and posted to the channel as
`briefs/arvo-door-shape.md` so that lane builds against it. It is a proposal: the format crate's design
is that lane's to write, and names are its to choose.

What is needed is the format's adaptation computed in a host integer of a ladder container's size
instead of in the 128-bit slot index and the four-limb integer, with the contract an equality to
`adapt`. Two functions per container size and sign, written once and expanded per width, all `const fn`:

1. Completion alone. `complete(x, width, policy)` takes an integer position on the grid, held in the
   host integer, and returns the member the overflow policy completes it to, in the same integer. For
   every signature whose slot range is the whole signed or unsigned range of `width` bits, with `width`
   at most the integer's, and every `x`, it equals
   `adapt::<S>(Exact::on_grid(Slot::at(x)), Dither::UNUSED)`. This is what `adapt_s8` and its siblings
   in this crate's `rung.rs` compute today, and those go once the door exists.
2. Rounding and completion. `adapt_ratio(numerator, divisor, width, mode, policy)` takes the position
   `numerator / divisor` in slot units, the numerator in the host integer of twice the size and the
   divisor a positive one of that size, and returns the member in the narrow integer. It equals `adapt`
   of the same rational position under the same mode and policy, for every deterministic mode. With
   the divisor a constant of the instantiation, a power of two lowers to a shift.

With the second, the fractional product becomes the machine's widening product and one call, the
quotient and the conversion of the later rounds become the same call with other numerators, and a host
integer comes into a value through the first. Until that lane lands, this crate calls what
`arvo-format` has today: `mul` on slots for the fractional product, and its own private adaptation for
completion.

## What is proved

Every pair of members of every signature at widths 1 to 8, both families, both policies and all five
deterministic modes, through the value type, for `sub` and `mul`, and every member for `neg`, against
`arvo_format::sub`, `mul` and `neg`. The rungs the same way as for `add`: widths 1 to 8 in every rung
and 9 to 12 in the three wide ones, against the oracle. The verdicts against `panic_on_overflow` on the
same pairs. The fixed-point product exhaustive at six bits, signed through `Fixed<6, -3>` and unsigned
through `UFixed<6, -3>`, all five modes and both policies, with a tie at a negative product named in
each mode. The wide bands on the edge lattice and the seeded pairs.

One mutant per operation, each a place the operation goes wrong first, behind a committed feature, with
its red run committed: the unsigned saturating difference pinned to the top instead of zero, the signed
saturating negation of the lowest member left where it is, and the saturating product pinned to the top
whatever its sign.

## Appended after the first lock: the identity's slot

The candidate above, `r^-e - n` where `d` is one and `e` is at or below zero, is wrong as a general
rule. The test written against a walk of the members' values found a format it misses: at a phase of a
half and exponent one the member at slot zero is `(1/2 + 0) * 2`, which is one. The member at slot `s`
is `(n / d + s) r^e`, so one sits at `r^-e - n / d` wherever that is whole, and the function forms
that over one denominator and takes it where the division leaves no residue. Nothing a value reaches
changes, since `Sole` holds a value to a zero phase. The doc changelist was unlocked to state it.
