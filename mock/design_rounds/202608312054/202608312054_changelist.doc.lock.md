# Doc changelist: the width bound, and the objective made to select

**Date:** 2026-08-31
**Round:** the width-bound round, opened on the reviewer's second pass over PR #28
**Scope:** two crate design documents. No new crate and no new document.

## What this changelist is

Two of the four blockers change what the design says, not only what the source does, so the design
moves first.

`arvo-format` gains a stated bound on the slot range and a stated width coordinate. `arvo-placement`
gains what the objective selects, which the document currently claims is keyed on the objective while
the source derives one answer for both.

## The canon this declares

`ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path`, ratified, which is what makes a width the
representation cannot carry a compile-time refusal rather than a runtime value.

`question::arbitrary_width_demands_in_the_canon`, whose second option leaves width families to design,
which is the authority for this design choosing its own supported range.

`ruling::cold_is_for_cold_paths_and_cold_storage`, ratified, which says the storage-minimising
objective bitpacks and is what the footprint arm is derived from.

`ruling::the_derivation_is_a_placement_and_the_operation_set_is_an_admission_rule`, ratified, for the
three outputs at shared occupancy that give the two objectives something to differ in.

## Files changed

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl` | states that the slot range carries the declared width as a coordinate rather than recovering it from a count, and states the bound above which a slot index cannot be represented and what happens there |
| `crates/arvo-placement/DESIGN.md.tmpl` | states what each objective selects, that they coincide at sole occupancy and differ at shared, and that the objective is a const parameter rather than an argument |

## The width bound, stated rather than assumed

The design supports declared widths of 1 to 62 bits. Above that a slot index does not fit the signed
64-bit integer the range is carried in, and the design refuses at compile time with a message naming
the width and the bound.

**62 rather than 63 or 64 because the count of slots is what runs out first**, and the count is
`2^BITS`. At 62 that is 4611686018427387904 and fits; at 63 it is 9223372036854775808 and does not.
The old code formed that count and wrapped it negative.

**The bound is a property of this representation and not a claim about arvo.** How wide arvo should
ultimately go is `question::arbitrary_width_demands_in_the_canon` and is open. What this design says is
what it supports and what it does outside that, which is what it owes.

## What is deliberately not here

**No answer to how wide arvo should go.** The open question stays open and the design states a bound
rather than a ceiling on the idea.

**No change to the rounding or overflow surfaces**, which this round does not touch.
