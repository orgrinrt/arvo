# the-remainder-is-carried-over-four-limbs

**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Round:** `202611132240-c5qc`, job `arvo-format`, job 7 of track T2 of the arvo triage.
**Tickets:** `arvo_s_exact_position_carries_no_remainder_denominator_past`, with
`arvo_has_no_public_constructor_for_an_exact_position_whose_r`.

## What is wrong

Every exact step past the sum forms its numerator in the four-limb integer and its denominator `D` in the
same four limbs, then narrows `D` to the host's unsigned 128-bit integer before it divides, because the
division and the remainder an exact position stores are both that wide. The narrowing is the only reason
the product refuses a format whose `d^2 r^max(-e, 0)` passes `2^128 - 1`, the quotient one whose
`d |n + b d| r^max(e, 0)` does at an end of the range, the conversion one whose `d_F V_F U_T d_T` does,
and `land` a magnitude whose `D` does. At radix two with a zero phase that is every product below
`e = -128`, and at radix ten below `e = -38`, formats whose members and products the four limbs hold
without trouble.

The second ticket asks for a public door to an exact position whose remainder passes a `Fraction`. That
door shipped after the ticket was filed: `Exact::at(slot, Remainder::of(num, den))` takes a residue over
an unsigned 128-bit denominator and is what every exact step builds through. The design's open item
still says no public constructor takes one, which is stale, and the remainder's two accessors return the
host's 128-bit integer, which a wider remainder cannot answer in.

## The shape

`D` stays in the four limbs instead of being narrowed. The floor division divides by a divisor of one to
four limbs: by one limb limb by limb as now, and otherwise by Knuth's algorithm D at the divisor's own
limb count, which generalises the two-limb division job 6 wrote. At three and four limbs the estimate's
correction against the divisor's second limb no longer sees the whole product, so a corrected estimate
can still be one too large, and the multiply and subtract borrows; the add-back step then returns the
divisor and lowers the quotient limb by one. At two limbs that step stays unreachable, by the argument
round `202611132142-jb7q` recorded.

The remainder an exact position stores becomes a numerator below a denominator, both in four limbs.
The rounding region reads three things of it: whether it is zero, which side of the midpoint it is, and
whether a dither is below it. The midpoint compares the numerator against `D` minus it, which cannot
leave the limbs. The dither is a 64-bit ratio, so the cross product is a four-limb value times a 64-bit
one on each side, held in five limbs. Reducing the residue to lowest terms uses the host's 128-bit
Euclid where both fit, which is every position the crate places today, and the four-limb division
otherwise.

`Remainder::of(num, den)` stays as the public door over the host's 128-bit integer. The two accessors
answer in that integer where the remainder fits it and say it does not otherwise, because a value in
four limbs has no public integer to be handed out in, and pretending it does would be a narrowing a
reader cannot see. So the second ticket closes for every remainder up to 128 bits and reopens past it:
a consumer forming its own position over a wider denominator has no door until the crate has a public
four-limb integer, which waits on a consumer asking for one, as the open item already says of the
door's other decisions. The ticket is bumped with that rather than closed.

What is left held out is a step whose numerator or denominator leaves the four limbs. With `D` free to
reach `2^256 - 1`, a member's numerator can leave the limbs while its position is inside the index, so
the crate stops reading a numerator past the limbs as a position past the index. A step names it apart,
and each obligation asks it of the extreme members (the four corners for the product and the quotient,
the two ends for the conversion), whose numerators bound every other member's because each numerator is
affine in each slot. The FIXME on each obligation narrows to that.

## What tests it

The division against the oracle in `tests/big.rs`, extended with a floor division by a four-limb divisor
written as a bit-serial search, over drawn dividends of both signs and divisors of two, three and four
limbs at every normalisation, plus a dividend and divisor that reach the add-back step, found by a search
against the oracle. The remainder's three readers against the oracle on
four-limb remainders. The product, the quotient and the conversion at formats whose `D` passes
`2^128 - 1` against their oracles, and `land` at a binary format whose finest magnitude takes a
denominator past it. Mutants: the add-back removed, the dither cross product cut to four limbs, and the
numerator's leaving read as a position past the index again.

## On a branch without `land`

The round is carried onto a branch that holds the format without `land`. What it says of landing at a
magnitude, and the test of `land` at a binary format whose finest magnitude takes a denominator past
`2^128 - 1`, stay with `land` and are not part of the round here.
