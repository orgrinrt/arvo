# Topic: an exact-width bit container

**Date:** 2026-09-04
**Scope:** new crate `arvo-bits`; `mock/Cargo.toml`; `mock/DESIGN.md.tmpl`; `mock/PRINCIPLES.md.tmpl`;
`mockspace.toml`; `mock/lints/a_contract_coordinate_is_not_a_host_primitive.rs`
**Obligation:** `obligation::an_exact_width_container_a_consumer_can_alias_and_pin`
**Governing rung:** `ruling::ingest_is_the_consumers_and_the_c_abi_is_where_it_ends_up` (`stated`)

## What is asked

hilavitkutin's content hash is a 28-bit value in its runtime API and a bare 32-bit value on disk, the
on-disk width pinned by a serialisation format rather than chosen. The obligation states the need
crate-agnostically: "a container of an exactly declared bit width that a consumer can name, alias
under its own name, and convert to a fixed wider width at a boundary it controls." The consumer prices
the conversion at one masked cast and treats that cost as part of the need.

## The ruling this sits under, read in full before anything else

`ruling::ingest_is_the_consumers_and_the_c_abi_is_where_it_ends_up` carries op's own words: helpers for
casting and converting may exist, and arvo may not use them in place of the consumer, "it's not
conventional to any library ever." Its own `note` records that op was asked to bless the row's `says`
field and declined in terms ("I need elaboration on the Ingest vs C ABI... I can't bless it or comment
on it"), and that a panel question, `question::what_the_ingest_row_claims_and_what_turns_on_it`, now
sits open asking what the row concretely constrains. That question is unresolved (`cargo mock coverage
question` still lists it at `nothing`) and its `decider` is `panel`, not one dispatched round. I am not
resolving it here.

What I can do without resolving it: build a container whose construction and every conversion are
total (never reject, never validate, never ingest on the consumer's behalf), so the design is correct
under all four of the question's candidate readings simultaneously.

- Reading 1 (constrains the surface: no arvo entry point calls a helper for the consumer): satisfied,
  because nothing in this crate calls its own constructor or cast automatically; the consumer calls
  both explicitly at their own boundary.
- Reading 2 (a refusal against a validating constructor, restating the never-a-runtime-check intent):
  satisfied vacuously, because there is no validating constructor here to refuse. Construction masks;
  masking cannot fail.
- Reading 3 (an adequacy demand on arvo's shapes): satisfied, because one generic type over a `u32`
  const covers every width from one bit to sixty-four.
- Reading 4 (two separate claims, C ABI and helper usage): both held regardless of whether they are one
  row or two.

`predicate = ["question: unresolved, decider = panel", "design: compliant under readings 1-4 of the open options", "runtime checks introduced: zero"]`

## Existence: yes, and the reason is a hole rather than a preference

`arvo-format`'s own crate doc: "A format here is not a container... There is no machine carrier in this
crate and none is reachable from it... Where the bits go is `arvo-placement`." That sentence is about
not conflating a `Format` marker (which describes a value *set*) with a value. `arvo-placement`'s own
crate doc: "Every width here is `arvo_format::Width` rather than a host integer. This crate does not
introduce the numeric category and is checked for that," and everything it derives (`Placement`) is
metadata, three `Width`s and an `Occupancy`, never an actual stored value. Neither of the two existing
crates below `arvo-strategy` is chartered, by their own stated words, to hold a runtime value at a
declared width. `arvo-format` explicitly disclaims it; `arvo-placement` is checked against it. This is
not an inconvenience I am routing around, it is a gap between what the obligation asks for and what
either crate's own docstring says it is for.

## Locus: a new crate, `arvo-bits`, and the naming is not the reason

The obligation's `quote` names `arvo_bits::Bits<28>`, from `hilavitkutin/mock/crates/hilavitkutin-persistence/DESIGN.md.tmpl:67-69`
(verified directly, not taken on the obligation row's word for it). I went looking for whether that
name was load-bearing evidence for locus and found it is not: `mock/DESIGN.md.tmpl`'s own "Consumers"
section says outright that "the consumers named in earlier versions of this document, hilavitkutin and
vehje, used crates that no longer exist. Those crates were removed on 2026-08-08." A closed, pre-reset
topic file, `mock/design_rounds/202604201338/202604201338_topic.arvo-hash-new-crate.md`, is that dead
architecture's own record: it designed `Bits<const N: u8>` in an `arvo-bits` that shipped `BitWidth`,
`BitAccess`, `BitSequence` contracts and much more besides, and an `arvo-hash` crate on top of it for a
`ContentHash` alias, with `HashAlgo`/`Hasher` traits. hilavitkutin's citation and that old topic file
are the same historical naming surfacing twice, not two independent confirmations; per
`expert-dispatch-defends-the-canon.md`, agreement between unratified, dead-source artifacts is shared
drift and not corroboration, and I am naming this rather than quietly leaning on it.

What is genuinely live and independent of that dead tree: `mockspace.toml`'s `[primitive-introductions]`
config and the external, currently-fetched `mockspace-extra-lints` pack (compiled fresh in this
session's `cargo mock query`, at ref `81d69141` on `dev`) both still carry the concept. The lint pack's
own category table, in `src/util.rs`, documents `numeric` as covering "Numeric + bool wrappers (UFixed,
USize, Bool, **Bits**, ...)" with typical introducers "arvo, arvo-bits, arvo-hash", and its test suite
exercises `arvo_types_only_skips_numeric_introducer` as a live behaviour. That is current, shipped
machinery built to accommodate exactly this shape with zero lint-pack changes, not dead-tree residue,
and it is a real (if secondary) reason the name costs nothing to keep. The actual reason for a new crate
is the hole above: neither `arvo-format` nor `arvo-placement` can honestly hold this without
contradicting a sentence its own doc already states.

I am not resurrecting the old crate. The new `arvo-bits` here is far smaller: no `BitWidth`/`BitAccess`/
`BitSequence` contracts, no `arvo-hash`, no hashing traits, no alias. Those are either not asked for by
this obligation or are the consumer's own job ("alias under its own name" is the consumer aliasing it,
not arvo pre-aliasing it for them).

## Dependency direction: zero workspace dependencies, and this was a live decision inside the round

First draft of this design had `arvo-bits` depend on `arvo-format` for `Width`, to give the container a
`WIDTH` accessor in a vocabulary the rest of the stack already owns. Reconsidered against
`mock/DESIGN.md.tmpl`'s own stated principle: "Trait contracts, not concrete types. Sub-crates generic
over trait bounds so downstream code picks its own concrete instantiations... small transitive
dependency footprint per consumer." hilavitkutin wants one type, `Bits<28>`. Pulling in `arvo-format`
for it would drag `Format`, `Ambient`, `Quantum`, `Rounding`, `Overflow`, `Adapt`, none of which this
container touches, into a downstream Cargo.toml for the sake of one associated const. And the const
itself is redundant: nothing this crate ships erases `N`, there is no `dyn` and no type-erased context
anywhere in the design, so a caller generic over `N` already has `N` in scope directly and gets nothing
from a `Bits::<N>::WIDTH` mirror of it. Dropped both: no dependency on `arvo-format`, no `WIDTH`
accessor. `arvo-bits` is a zero-workspace-dependency leaf crate, structurally parallel to `arvo-format`
rather than built on it, and it needs its own primitive door for the same reason `arvo-format` has one:
it cannot express a value of an exact width in types that do not exist yet.

## The container

```
Bits<const N: u32>(u64)
```

`repr(transparent)`. `N` is bounded to `1..=64` by a private `ADMITTED: ()` const, the same
codegen-forced-assert pattern `arvo_format::slots::Slots::ADMITTED` already uses (`assert!` inside a
`const` item, forced by every function that reads the range, refusing at `cargo build` rather than at
`cargo check`, per the same reasoning `slots.rs` already states in full). `masked` and `cast` force it;
nothing here needs it to be `pub`, since there is no outside implementor of anything, only a concrete
type.

I deliberately did not bound `N` through `arvo_format::slots::Slots`/`Unsigned<N>` even though that
trait already states almost the same bound. `Slots` is admitted at `1..=62`, and the reason is a
different overflow: a signed 64-bit slot *index* cannot address `2^63` positions. `Bits<N>`'s bound is
about `u64` *storage* capacity, which runs out at `64`, not `62`. Reusing `Slots` would have silently
cost this container two widths, sixty-three and sixty-four, that it can represent and has no reason to
refuse. Two bounds that read alike are not the same claim, and conflating them would have been exactly
the kind of duplicated-rule pile this workspace's own discipline names and rejects.

```rust
impl<const N: u32> Bits<N> {
    const MASK: u64 = if N == 64 { u64::MAX } else { (1u64 << N) - 1 };

    pub const fn masked(raw: u64) -> Self { .. }   // total; never rejects
    pub const fn raw(self) -> u64 { .. }            // the unwrap door
    pub const fn cast<const M: u32>(self) -> Bits<M> { .. }  // total in both directions
}
```

`masked` is the whole of construction: bitwise AND against `Self::MASK`, so it is total by
construction and there is nothing for a caller to get wrong that the type refuses at runtime. `raw` is
the "unwrap door, declared as one" pattern this codebase already uses for `Width::count`, `Slot::index`,
`Bool::get`. `cast<M>` is `Bits::<M>::masked(self.raw())`: one operation covers both directions, because
widening (`M > N`) and narrowing (`M < N`) are the same masking arithmetic at a different bound, and
splitting it into two named operations would be a second rule for the same fact. Widening never drops a
bit because the wider mask is a superset. Narrowing drops bits above `M` deliberately, which is what
"masked cast" already meant in the consumer's own words and is not a defect to guard against.

Derives: `Clone, Copy, PartialEq, Eq, PartialOrd, Ord, Hash, Debug`, matching `Width`/`Slot`'s own set
plus `Hash`, which hilavitkutin's own design needs for content-hash table lookups.

`predicate = ["N: any u32 in 1..=64", "construction: total, never rejects", "cast: total in both directions, narrowing is lossy by declared intent", "backing: u64 uniform, chosen for the scalar-identity use named by the obligation rather than for storage density, which is placement's concern for whoever embeds this later"]`

### Considered and dropped

- **`TryFrom<u64>` / `notko::Boundable`.** Both are shaped around rejection. Op's own words in the
  ruling are that arvo does not own a validating constructor for ingest; shipping one here, even for an
  in-memory value rather than untrusted bytes, would be building the exact door the ruling's `note`
  records him declining, under a different name. Dropped.
- **`WIDTH: Width` accessor tying the crate to `arvo-format`.** Dropped above; redundant given no type
  erasure exists anywhere the design reaches, and costs a real dependency for it.
- **Deriving `N`'s bound from `arvo_format::slots::Slots`.** Dropped above; different overflow bound,
  would have cost two widths for no reason.
- **Reproducing the pre-reset `arvo-bits`'s `BitWidth`/`BitAccess`/`BitSequence` contracts, or an
  `arvo-hash` crate with `HashAlgo`/`Hasher` traits.** Not asked for by this obligation. hilavitkutin's
  own `ContentHash` alias is theirs to write once this container exists; nothing here should pre-empt
  it, matching "alias under its own name" literally.
