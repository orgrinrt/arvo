the representation by a broad bench after the requirements are named. Both rest on an understanding of
what the competing formulations actually disagree about, and this round got that wrong twice in
opposite directions: first by citing one side without noting a fork exists, then by asserting the fork
was terminological drift. Both errors came from working off summaries. The article is the primary
source that settles it.

## What Lengyel disagrees with, in his own words

His target is what he calls the dual construction model, associated with the SIGGRAPH 2019 course.
Three substantive disagreements, quoted:

**Dimensional assignment.** Their model requires that "points be represented by trivectors and planes
be represented by vectors. This is backwards." His position: "In 4D projective space, points should be
one-dimensional, and planes should be three-dimensional."

**Wedge and antiwedge.** "Their model requires that ∧ corresponds to a dimension-decreasing meet
operation and ∨ corresponds to a dimension-increasing join operation, but in the Grassmann algebra
used by the rest of the world, the meanings are the other way around."

**Normalisation.** "in the dual construction model, its the components that don't involve e₄ that must
have a magnitude of one", against his own requirement placed on the components that do involve it.

His summary judgment is that the other approach requires things to be "flipped upside down or turned
inside out", and that the geometric antiproduct approach avoids this, making it "the right way to go".

## What the companion sites add

`rigidgeometricalgebra.org` organises the sixteen basis elements of the four-dimensional algebra by
**grade** and **antigrade**, counting full and empty dimensions respectively, and states the symmetry
directly: vectors, bivectors and trivectors "have dimensions one, two, and three when we count the
black bars" and "three, two, and one when we count the white bars".

It also rejects standard nomenclature on a point that matters for how the algebra is taught rather
than only for how it computes: "We eschew the term pseudoscalar due to its portrayal of the element 𝟙
as different from and perhaps somewhat less significant than the element **1**. It is not." Scalars
and antiscalars are treated as equally fundamental duals.

Its claim against the alternatives is one of generality rather than of error: RGA "completely subsumes
conventional models that include homogeneous coordinates, Plücker coordinates, quaternions, and screw
theory".

## What this does not settle

Nothing here says which convention arvo takes. That is D41's question and D41 answers it by naming
requirements and then benching broadly, with these two as real members of a wider matrix rather than
as the whole of it. The reason this file exists is that the disagreement is substantive, so the choice
has consequences for which operations are cheap and how normalisation behaves under repeated
composition, and neither side can be evaluated from a summary of it.

The other side of the fork wants the same treatment before the bench is designed. The bivector.net
convention's primary material (Gunn, de Keninck, Dorst, Roelfs, and the de Keninck and Dorst 2022
exponential-logarithm-normalisation paper the research pass named as the state of the art for the
general-dimension case) is not linked here yet and should be, at the same standing.
