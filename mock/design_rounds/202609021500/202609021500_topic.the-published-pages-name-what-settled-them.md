# The two published crate design pages name what settled them

`mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl` and `mock/crates/arvo-placement/DESIGN.md.tmpl` render
into `docs/FORMAT.md` and `docs/PLACEMENT.md`, which is a tier 1 surface: a stranger landing on the
repository reads it, and it may carry what the crate is and how it works and nothing about how the
project came to know it.

Both pages carry registry addresses in the body prose, outside the declaration block at the top.
Counted on the templates, in the body only:

| Namespace | `arvo-format` | `arvo-placement` |
|---|---|---|
| `ruling::` | 8 | 6 |
| `proposal::` | 5 | 1 |
| `question::` | 5 | 0 |
| `dimension::` | 2 | 1 |
| `obligation::` | 1 | 0 |

Twenty-nine in total. A `ruling` is something op has stated, so a sentence citing one is a sentence
telling a stranger that a person they have never heard of settled this and when. A `proposal` is a
claim the panel established and op has not seen yet, so citing one publishes the fact that there is
a panel and that it has a queue. A `question` carries whose call the answer is.

The prose around them is the larger half of it. The pages explain the tier discipline outright:
"Derived, marked as derived, from two ratified rows that agree", "which is what makes it a
derivation rather than a new decision", "Two instances establish what they both reached and never
their union, so the count sits outside what was ratified", "One instance, named here as open rather
than adopted, because a design adopting a one-expert proposal has decided a canon question in the
tier below where it belongs". None of that is about the format or the placement. It is about how
this project decides things, addressed to a reader who does not know there are tiers and has no way
to find out.

Two paragraphs are bookkeeping with no contract in them at all. One records that
`dimension::overflow_policy` still lists a value this design disagrees with, and that the repair
belongs on that row rather than here. The other says an earlier version of the page made a false
claim, and that it is recorded rather than replaced. A reader who never saw the earlier version now
has two designs to hold to understand one, and learns that the page was recently wrong about the
thing it is teaching.

Op stated the rule directly, four minutes after a ratification, when the markers were found in
another repository's design files:

> also remember that the design files *may not* contain any internal process details or leaking
> internal stuff otherwise, such as my ratifications etc. Those belong somewhere else entirely

## What the fix is

Every passage states the contract and drops what settled it. The substance survives: where taking a
citation out would take a fact with it, the fact stays and the address goes. A ruling that says
never a runtime check and one lowered path becomes the design saying so, which is what it means to
the reader either way.

The two bookkeeping paragraphs go entirely, because there is no contract under them.

## What is out of scope

The declaration block at the top of each page, headed "Canon this design declares", stays. Every
design document has to declare the canon it relates to, and that rule is not being relitigated here.
Whether a required declaration belongs on a published page at all is a separate question and is not
this round's.

`arvo-strategy/DESIGN.md.tmpl` is not touched. It carries the same defect and worse, and it does not
render into `docs/` at all, which is a finding of its own and wants its own round.
