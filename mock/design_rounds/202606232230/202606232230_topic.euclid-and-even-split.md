# Topic: euclidean division + even-split traits (fixed-point)

**Date:** 2026-06-23
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** arvo-strategy (inner contract), arvo (outer traits)
**Source:** consumer need from ikiuni-aggregation (exact-conserving N-way split of a `Quantity`); op design-talk

## Motivation

A consumer (ikiuni's Individuation/Aggregation Reconstruction) needs to split a fixed-point quantity into N
shares that sum back to the original exactly (no quantity created or destroyed at the LOD crossing). Today
the consumer reaches into `to_raw()` / `from_raw()` and does the integer euclidean division itself: a
substrate leak. The arithmetic belongs in arvo. arvo also lacks euclidean division on its fixed-point types
generally (it has `Add`/`Sub`/`Mul`/`Div` but no `div_euclid`/`rem_euclid`).

## The leak, concretely

`mass.to_raw()`, then `raw.div_euclid(n)` / `raw.rem_euclid(n)`, then `from_raw(...)` per share, in the
consumer. The raw container type is arvo's to own; consumers should never unwrap it for arithmetic arvo can
express.

## Design (op-confirmed): small separate traits + blanket impls over an inner per-width contract

Not folded into `IArith`/`UArith`. Three independent outer traits, each separately importable and prelude-
exported, blanket-impl'd over the fixed-point types. The blanket impls bound on the existing strategy
arithmetic plus a new small inner contract that supplies the per-width concrete capability (the same shape as
the array-length `Capacity` contract: a contract for the specific capability, concrete impls handle the
per-width side, outer blanket bounds on it). This tames the trait-solver overflow that a fully-generic
container-from-count would otherwise hit.

Inner contract (arvo-strategy, alongside `IArith`/`UArith`, macro-impl'd per strategy x width where the
container is concrete):
- `ScalarEuclidRaw<const N>` (signed) / `UScalarEuclidRaw<const N>` (unsigned): `div_euclid_scalar` /
  `rem_euclid_scalar` on the raw container by an integer count (ULP granularity); plus the whole-unit
  `div_euclid_whole` / `rem_euclid_whole` raw ops (fixed-point divisor, quotient floored to whole units,
  needs FRAC). The concrete container makes `n as $container` and the `<< FRAC` shift trivial.

Outer traits (arvo, blanket-impl'd over `IFixed<I,F,S>` / `UFixed<I,F,S>`):
- `ScalarEuclid`: `div_euclid_scalar(self, n: USize) -> Self`, `rem_euclid_scalar(self, n: USize) -> Self`.
  The ULP-accurate division by an integer count. Bound on the inner contract.
- `EuclidDiv`: `div_euclid(self, rhs: Self) -> Self`, `rem_euclid(self, rhs: Self) -> Self`. std-parity
  (divisor is `Self`), quotient floored to whole fixed-point units (the coarse but conserving split). Bound
  on the inner contract's whole-unit ops.
- `EvenSplittable`: `split_evenly(self, n: USize) -> EvenShares<Self>`. A no-alloc iterator yielding exactly
  `n` shares that sum to `self`, the `self_raw % n` remainder distributed one ULP to the first `r` shares by
  index, deterministic. Built on `ScalarEuclid` (base + remainder). The consumer collects.

Both euclid variants ship so the consumer chooses (ULP-accurate vs whole-unit); rustdoc on each names the
difference. `EvenSplittable` is the nice utility; it stays a separate trait so it is pulled in only where
needed.

## Why both euclid variants

The accurate (ULP) split is as-equal-as the representation allows (100/3 -> 33.334 / 33.333 / 33.333). The
naive whole-unit split gives whole-unit shares (34.0 / 33.0 / 33.0); both conserve, the consumer picks per
need. Neither is "wrong"; they are different granularities, documented.

## Determinism / no_std

Pure arithmetic; deterministic. `#![no_std]`, no alloc: the split returns a lazy iterator (a small struct
holding the two precomputed share values + counts), never a Vec. Internals use the container raw (arvo owns
it); the consumer surface is raw-free.

## Scope

Signed (`IFixed`) and unsigned (`UFixed`), both. Tests cover conservation, determinism, the remainder rule,
negative mass (signed), and the whole-unit variant. Out of scope: float (`FastFloat`/`StrictFloat`) euclid,
a later round if needed.
