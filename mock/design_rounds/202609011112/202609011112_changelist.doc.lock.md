# Doc changelist: say why the declared widths are spelled with a machine integer

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Round:** the one position the language refuses to repair
**Scope:** `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`

## CHANGE: the two-primitives section FROM stating the door TO stating what the door cannot reach
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
Verification: the refusal compiled on the pinned toolchain and is landing as a `trybuild` case in the
src changelist of this round, so the paragraph and the test say the same thing and one of them fails
loudly if the other stops being true.

The section says `Width` and `Bool` are here because the crate introduces the numeric category, and that
the door is kept narrow. It does not say what happens at the one position where the narrow door cannot
be used at all, and a reader looking at `Constant<const EXP: i32>` or `Signed<const BITS: u32>` right
after reading that paragraph is owed the reason.

The reason is the language rather than a preference. A const generic parameter takes an integer, a
`bool` or a `char`, so `Width` is refused there for what it is and not for what it holds, and the
diagnostic names a compiler feature as the way through. Taking that way through would put the feature on
every crate that declares its own format, which is a cost this crate is not in a position to pass on.

One paragraph, placed after the narrow-door sentence, saying that and pointing at where the refusal is
kept. Nothing else in the section moves: the two types, their unwrap accessors and the observation
surface are unchanged, and the paragraph makes no claim about whether any other position here should
carry a bare integer.
