# Topic: the remainder carries a 128-bit denominator

**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Round:** step AR6 of the roadmap, its first round, on `work/oracle-fraction-wide`.
**Canon:** `ruling::the_derivation_is_a_placement_and_the_operation_set_is_an_admission_rule`,
`ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon`, `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path`.

## What is short

The remainder of an exact position is a `Fraction`, a signed 64-bit numerator over a positive signed
64-bit denominator. A quotient by a member of a 64-bit format has a remainder over that member: the
unsigned range reaches `2^64 - 1` and the signed range `2^63`, and neither is a positive `i64`. So the
next step of the oracle, the exact quotient, has no position to land at for the divisors a 64-bit
format declares, before any phase or quantum enters.

The same shortness already holds two things out. The sum over a phase whose fractional part reduces to
a denominator of `2^63` is refused, and the product whose remainder denominator `d^2 r^max(-e, 0)`
passes `2^63 - 1` is held out under a placeholder, both under the ticket
`arvo_s_exact_position_carries_no_remainder_denominator_past`. The open items name the widening of the
remainder, and of what `adapt` reads from it, as what admits them.

## The stance: the remainder is its own coordinate, unsigned and 128 bits wide

The remainder an `Exact` holds is always in `[0, 1)`. It needs no sign, and the caller's ratio and the
stored remainder are two different things that the type has been using one coordinate for. So the
stored remainder becomes a crate-private coordinate of its own, a numerator below an unsigned 128-bit
denominator, and `Fraction` stays what a caller hands `Exact::between` and `Dither::at`, unchanged.

Why 128 and not 64. At 64 bits the quotient by a 64-bit member at zero phase and exponent zero fits,
and nothing else gains: the product at radix two moves from `e >= -62` to `e >= -63`, a phase's
denominator squared still passes it past `2^32`, and the quotient at a phase or at a positive exponent
needs `d |n + b d| r^e`, which passes 64 bits at a phase of a third over a 64-bit range. At 128 bits
the product admits every phase at a non-negative exponent, since `d^2` is at most `2^126`, the radix
two exponent reaches `-127` and radix ten `-38`, and the quotient by any 64-bit divisor has a
position at every phase whose denominator times the divisor's span stays under `2^128`. A third
widening later is the cost the narrower choice would carry.

What the wider coordinate costs. The rounding region compares the remainder against the midpoint and
against the dither. The midpoint test becomes `num` against `den - num`, which cannot overflow. The
dither test cross-multiplies a 128-bit term by a 64-bit one, which is formed as a 192-bit product in
two words of the host's unsigned 128-bit integer, inside the one function that reads it. The product's
one division becomes a division of four limbs by a divisor of up to two limbs; the one-limb path stays
for a divisor under `2^64` and a bit-serial path takes the rest, both exact and both `const`.

## The compromise

A position whose remainder passes what a `Fraction` carries is reached only through an operation's
exact step. `Exact::between` takes a caller's `Fraction` as before, and no public constructor takes a
128-bit remainder. A consumer computing its own exact position with a wide remainder, a 64-bit divisor
of its own for example, has no door to hand it in by. That is additive later, as a constructor beside
`between`, and it is not added before a consumer asks for it, because the shape of that constructor,
whether it normalises a carry and how far past the index a carry may land, is a decision of its own.
It is a ticket, `FIXME` cited where the constructor would go.

## What the widening changes, and what it does not

The four properties of `Fraction::of` and every test of them stay as they are, since `Fraction` is not
changed. `Exact::between` stores the caller's remainder in the wider coordinate exactly, and a
remainder compares equal to the `Fraction` it was built from, so every test comparing a stored
remainder with a `Fraction` reads as before.

Addition's remainder condition loses its second half. A phase denominator is at most `2^63` in
magnitude, so its reduced remainder always fits, and the only refusal left is a phase that does not
denote, which is a refusal by design. The placeholder and its `FIXME` go, and the compile-fail fixture
for the `2^63` phase turns into a pair: a zero denominator refused, and the `2^63` phase admitted.

The product's denominator condition moves from `2^63 - 1` to `2^128 - 1`, under the same ticket,
narrowed. The four-limb integer moves out of the product's module to the crate, because the rounding
region does not need it but the next round's quotient and conversion do, and three private copies of
one wide integer is the failure the reuse rule names. The edge oracle `Big` moves beside `Ratio` in
the shared test module for the same reason.
