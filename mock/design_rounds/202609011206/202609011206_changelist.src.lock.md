## CHANGE: `half_a_display_unit` is named for what it holds
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/tests/matlab_fi_parity.rs`
Verification: the binding is `1i128 << $f`, which is a whole display unit in the scaled space, and the
halving happens as a `* 2` on the left of the comparison. Every arm still passes and none of the
arithmetic moves.

The assertion is correct and the name is not. It matters more than a name usually does because the bound
is exactly tight on one arm, `fi_pi_signed_eight` at `f = 5`, where the comparison is `32 <= 32`. So the
`<=` is load-bearing, and a reader who trusted the name would conclude there is a bit of slack that is
not there and would relax something.

## CHANGE: the tightness the name hid becomes an arm of its own
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/tests/matlab_fi_parity.rs`
Verification: turning the comparison into `<` fails on `fi_pi_signed_eight` and on nothing else in the
file, measured both ways round.

The bound was established as attained by computing it by hand while checking the name, and a hand check
that answers and then goes away leaves the next reader to redo it, differently. So the arithmetic is
written down: `101 * 10000 = 1010000` against `31562 * 2^5 = 1009984`, a difference of 16, doubled to 32,
against a whole display unit of 32. Equal.

That is what makes the `<=` load-bearing rather than conservative, and it is the fact the old name
obscured in the direction that would have got it relaxed.
