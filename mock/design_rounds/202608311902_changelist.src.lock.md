# Src changelist: the three base primitive crates, and what the gate made the design say

**Date:** 2026-08-31
**Round:** the base primitive design round, resumed after its canon-gate refusal
**Scope:** three new crates under `mock/crates/`, one `[primitive-introductions]` correction in
`mockspace.toml`, and a rename the documentation gate forced.

## Comparison to deprecated changelist

`202608311902_changelist.src.deprecated.md` was the first src changelist of this round. It was
deprecated by `cargo mock unlock` rather than by a change of mind: the source it described landed and
passed the commit gate, and then the push gate reported eighteen public types the design documents
never named. Fixing that is a `*.md.tmpl` edit, which belongs to DOC, so the round went back a phase
and this changelist replaces the one that could not have known.

Section by section, against the deprecated file.

### What lands

**Unchanged in substance, extended by two things the gate forced.** The three crates and their
decomposition are the same. Added since: the `[primitive-introductions]` correction, which is the
finding of the source phase, and the carrier rename.

### Two derived calls in the source, marked as derived

**Unchanged, both of them, and both still marked as derived.** The type-level coordinates being

### What is deliberately not here

**Unchanged, with one item moved rather than dropped.** The chain, the strategy object's component

**Moved**: the five lint test suites. The deprecated changelist promised them under "what lands". They

### How this changelist expects to be checked

**Sharpened from the deprecated version's "green at strict" into exact counts**, so the claim is
checkable rather than an assertion that things are fine. The numbers are at the end of this file.

## What lands

### The three crates

`arvo-format`, `arvo-placement`, `arvo-strategy`, implementing the locked doc changelist.

`arvo-format` carries the affine predicate as a trait contract with its coordinates as associated
items, the six rounding modes as a closed enumeration, the overflow policies as an open one, the
adaptation as the pair of those two, the declared signature as the pair of a format and an adaptation,
and the admission rule as a trait an operation implements by naming a signature and nothing else.

`arvo-placement` carries the occupancy axis and the two derivation arms.

`arvo-strategy` carries the binding, four presets, and their intents.

### The `[primitive-introductions]` correction

**This is the finding of the source phase and it is worth reading before the rest.**

`mockspace.toml` declared eight crates as introducing the numeric category, which is what exempts a
crate from the bare-primitive lints so the stack's own primitives can be defined somewhere.
**Seven of those crates were deleted on 2026-08-08 and do not exist.**

The eighth was worse than stale. It read `arvo-strategy`, which is also the name of a crate this round
creates, so a brand-new crate inherited a deleted crate's exemption and went unchecked by the numeric
lints with nothing anywhere saying so. It was not caught by noticing; it was caught by the other two
crates failing 181 times while this one failed only on static strings.

The table now names `arvo-format` alone, which is the crate that defines what a number is here.

**A stale name-keyed exemption is silent by construction**: it fails open, it fails only for the crate
whose name happens to match, and the gate reports nothing at all. Any config keyed on crate names
carries this, and the repair is that the names get checked against the tree rather than trusted.

## CHANGE: the carrier types FROM `C8`, `C16`, `C32`, `C64` TO `Carrier8`, `Carrier16`, `Carrier32`, `Carrier64`
File: `mock/crates/arvo-placement/src/lib.rs`
Verification: `grep -c 'pub struct Carrier8' mock/crates/arvo-placement/src/lib.rs` returns one, and
`grep -c 'pub struct C8' mock/crates/arvo-placement/src/lib.rs` returns zero.

**Forced by a lint defect rather than chosen, and the rename is right anyway.**

`undocumented-type` requires every public type to be named in a design document. Its doc-side
extractor, `is_type_name`, accepts a word only if it contains at least one lowercase letter, while its
source-side collector takes every `pub struct` regardless. So a type whose name is capitals and digits
is collected from source, can never be collected from a document, and is reported as undocumented no
matter what any design says about it. `C8` and its three siblings sat exactly there, and the four were
unsatisfiable rather than undocumented.

The rename satisfies the gate honestly, because `Carrier8` genuinely is named in the design now. It is
also the better name: `C8` is terse to the point of opacity, and the type is a carrier.

**The lint defect is reported rather than worked around silently.** It is in
`lint-rules/src/undocumented_type.rs` in mockspace, the asymmetry is between `is_type_name` and
`collect_source_type_defs`, and a repair belongs to whoever owns that crate.

## Two derived calls in the source, marked as derived

**The type-level coordinates are primitive-typed, and they have to be.** A const generic parameter and
an associated const must have a primitive type; the language gives no alternative. So the operating
constraint is read as binding value positions, and `arvo-format`'s value positions carry `Width` and
`Bool` rather than host integers. Flagged rather than assumed: if a later round decides otherwise, it
is these crates' signatures that change.

**The quantum law is affine in the magnitude**, carried as a base and a slope rather than as a
function. The ratified proposition says affine, so the two families differ in one coordinate: the
slope is zero for the constant-quantum family and nonzero for the magnitude-indexed one. This is what
lets membership be a free `const fn` over a format's associated items instead of a method the trait
would have to make const, which needs a feature nobody sanctioned.

## What the tests establish, and the one that is red

The width sweeps run every width from one to sixty-four rather than the powers of two, because the
whole point of a declared width is that thirteen and twenty-seven are as ordinary as sixteen. The
adaptation matrix is the full six by three cross product and asserts its own count so a skipped row
would fail. Every inventory carries a member declared inside the test module, which is what makes
"the set is open" a checked claim rather than a sentence.

**`the_carrier_is_not_a_function_of_the_access_width` is red on purpose**, marked with its reason. The
design states the independence of carrier and access in both directions, measured under both packing
rules. This crate ships one packing rule, and under it every access width reaches exactly one carrier,
so one direction holds and the other does not. The assertion stands at the intended behaviour and is
not narrowed to pass; its sibling asserts the direction that does hold, found by sweeping the ladder
rather than by picking a pair.

**The first cut of that sibling picked widths 9 and 13, which share a carrier and also share an
access.** It failed for the right reason and established nothing, which is why the passing version
sweeps rather than chooses.

## What is deliberately not here

**No arm.** An arm carries a predicate over a region and its own justification in words, and none has
a measurement behind it yet.

**No overflow mode bound to a preset**, because `does_warm_wrap_or_clamp` is open in the registry and
the ratified row forbids writing the per-strategy statements as settled.

**No chain and no strategy-object component count**, both out of the round's scope.

**No repair to `dimension::overflow_policy`**, which still lists `panic` among its values while the
ratified factoring requires an adaptation to be total. The dimension set is append-only, so the repair
is a note on that row and belongs to whoever next touches the axis.

## What is owed and did not land in this round

**Test suites for five lints that have none**: `no_std_enforcer`, `no_alloc_enforcer`,
`no_dynamic_dispatch`, `no_runtime_grow`, `arvo_bits_traits_only`. Two have measured holes, recorded
in `mock/research/sketches/base-primitives-gate/p4_the_untested_enforcers_have_holes.rs`, which
transcribes both predicates verbatim and carries a control that fails if the transcription is wrong.
The other three are untested and unexamined, which is a separate statement from saying they are sound.

**A second lint defect found while working and not repaired**: the `no-std` lint reads only the first
thirty lines of a crate root, so a crate with a long module document fails despite carrying the
attribute. `arvo-format` hit it, and the attribute moved above the module document, which is fine and
is not a fix.
