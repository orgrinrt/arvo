# The primitives a consumer names instead of a bare number

Five of the sixteen consumer-stated needs are one feature, and nothing in the
tree reaches four of them. The fifth is reached by a ruling that binds as
direction rather than as canon. This topic is where that feature gets stated;
it decides nothing that two independent readings have not settled.

## What asks for it, in the asker's own words

`mock/registry/obligation.toml` is read from outside the canon on purpose, so
the enumeration comes from what a consumer said it needs rather than from the
canon agreeing with itself. `cargo mock obligation-coverage` reports what
reaches each, derived rather than written down, and it currently answers
`nothing` for ten of the sixteen.

Four of those ten, plus one at `stated`, are the same feature:

| obligation | consumer | reached by |
|---|---|---|
| `a_primitive_for_every_position_a_bare_number_would_take` | any, and the whole stack by rule | nothing |
| `a_platform_sized_unsigned_integer_at_an_api_position` | hilavitkutin | nothing |
| `debug_output_from_every_numeral_shape` | any | nothing |
| `the_unstable_machinery_does_not_reach_a_consumer` | kolli, and every crate in the stack | nothing |
| `an_exact_width_container_a_consumer_can_alias_and_pin` | hilavitkutin | `ruling::ingest_is_the_consumers_and_the_c_abi_is_where_it_ends_up`, at `stated` |

The widest of them is the first, and it is the one that makes the others
urgent rather than merely unmet: the stack's own rule forbids a bare host
integer at a public position, and there is no primitive to write there instead.
A consumer held to that rule cannot comply with it today.

## What is already built, and why it does not answer this

The format contract's ten coordinates are spelled in types the stack owns
already. `Width`, `Slot`, `Exponent`, `MagnitudeCount`, `Radix`, `Bool` and
`Phase` cover them, and `ruling::the_numeric_door_carries_the_coordinate_set_and_the_two_type_bound_is_not_canon`
ratifies that the door carries the coordinate set spelled that way.

**Those are the format's coordinates and not a consumer's API surface.** A
consumer writing a function that takes a length, or a struct that holds a
28-bit content hash, is not declaring a format and has no coordinate to reach
for. The obligations above are stated from that side, and nothing there is
answered by a type whose meaning is a position in a numeral's declaration.

**The const generic parameter positions are still host integers**, at
`quantum.rs:348` and `:360` and `slots.rs:260` and `:263`, and that is the one
position seat 238's proposal named as legitimate. It is recorded as a proposal
and is not settled here.

## What this round may not decide on its own

**Where these primitives live, and how many types they are.** The ratifying
ruling is explicit that the count is outside what it settles, because its two
seats disagreed: one answers a type per coordinate, the other more than two and
fewer than ten. It says so in its own words, that two instances agree about the
intersection of their claims and never the union.

So the locus and the count go through two independent readings before anything
is written against them, and the coordinator gates. The canon is silent on
where a consumer-facing primitive lives, and silence is not permission.

**What is not open**: that the primitives are owed, which the obligations state
from the consumer's side; and that a bare host integer at a public position is
refused, which the stack's rule already enforces and a lint in this repository
already checks.

## The correction this round carries with it

`ruling::the_numeric_door_carries_the_coordinate_set_and_the_two_type_bound_is_not_canon`
says a design correction is owed and is not that ruling's to make. The design
tier changes before the code under it does, so both halves belong here:

- `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl` states the two-type bound as the
  crate's own design, and it is known to be wrong.
- `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/width.rs:14` and `:16` state the same bound from
  the other side, and the source kept the sentence the design has given up.

**Both are in scope because they are the same claim**, and fixing the design
without the source leaves a reader who trusts the source holding a bound the
canon refuses.

## The test-gate finding that travels with the same ruling

`the_format_inventory_admits_a_member_this_crate_does_not_know_about` is the
only test asserting the ratified open-inventory clause, and it sits inside the
crate-wide exemption, so it asserts that clause in the one crate where the rule
it is about cannot refuse anything. Its `Ternary` also borrows the crate's own
ambient, quantum and slot range, so it writes two of the ten coordinates rather
than all of them.

A test that cannot fail for the reason it exists is the shape the test gate
refuses, and this one is narrow twice.
