# The sentence above the four properties says the wrong thing pins

`Fraction::of` has two regions. The four properties stating what the constructor
is are exact and hold over every pair. The one-line gloss above them, in
`apply.rs` and in the crate's `DESIGN.md.tmpl` table, is not.

Both say the magnitude pins to the largest the coordinate carries. Take
`of(3, i64::MIN)`. The answer is `-3/i64::MAX`, whose magnitude is `3/(2^63-1)`,
a hair larger than the named `3/2^63` and nowhere near the largest magnitude the
coordinate carries. Nothing about the answer's magnitude is at any maximum.

What pins is the operand that would have to be `2^63` to write the exact form.
In this pair that is the denominator; where the signs fall the other way it is
the numerator. Either way one operand leaves the type and takes the nearest
value the type holds, which moves the answer's magnitude by a relative
`1 / i64::MAX` in whichever direction that operand sat.

The properties below already say this correctly, so nothing about the behaviour
is in question and no source changes. What changes is the sentence the next
round will reason from, since a reader who takes the gloss at face value has the
wrong model of which of the two operands is the one that moved.

A test comment carries a related defect. It introduces an assertion as a control
for "a real tie at a denominator whose doubling also leaves the type", and no
such case exists: a tie is `2*num == den`, so a numerator large enough to
overflow on doubling forces a denominator past `i64::MAX`, which the coordinate
cannot hold. The assertion under the comment is a whole number rather than a
tie, and the arms carrying the real content sit above and below it. The
impossibility is worth stating, because it is the more interesting fact and
nothing currently says it.
