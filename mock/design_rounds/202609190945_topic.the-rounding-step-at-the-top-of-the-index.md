# Topic: the rounding step at the top of the slot index, and what the suite never fed

**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `arvo-format`'s applied map (`round_slot`, `complete_slot`, `adapt`, `panic_on_overflow`),
the design sections that describe its intermediate, and the test arms the platform-width round left
unreached. Same branch as round `202609190825`, `feat/platform-width-types-on-a-wider-slot-range`.
**Source:** the review of that branch at `6782c3aa`, read as claims and checked against the code.

## Authority

`ruling::a_platform_width_type_is_a_target_bound_member_of_the_format_family` governs the widening this
round repairs. The applied map's own contract is the design's, `crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`,
"Where the exact step happens" and "The index's host integer": the map is total with no check on the
value path, and the exact step happens in a domain that holds it. `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path`
rules out a guard as the repair.

## What is wrong

Round `202609190825` widened the slot index to `i128` and wrote the rounding step's slot above as
`down.saturating_add(1)`. Before the widening the step ran in `i128` over an `i64` index and was exact.
After it, at `down = i128::MAX` the step names one past the index, the saturation returns `i128::MAX`
instead, and two answers go wrong for any range an outside implementor places at the top of the index,
which `is_admissible` accepts (`AtTheTop` in `tests/the_inventory/the_range_obligation.rs`):

- under `Wrap`, `Ceil` on a position between `i128::MAX` and one past it returns the wrap of
  `i128::MAX` where it owes the wrap of one past it, which is the range's lowest slot when the range
  ends at `i128::MAX`;
- `panic_on_overflow` compares the saturated slot against the range, finds it inside, and reports a
  position that rounds past the range as one that does not.

The round's own arm `rounding_past_the_top_of_the_index_saturates_there_and_wrapping_pays_for_it`
asserted the wrong wrap answer and asserted that the right one was not returned, so it pinned the
defect green. `AtTheTop` was admitted and never adapted, which is why nothing else caught it.

The design disagrees with itself about it. The section on the index's host integer lists what
saturates (positions for non-members, chains of carries) and does not list the rounding step, then
says the wrap is exact at every position the integer holds. The older section on where the exact step
happens says the step's intermediate exists to be wider than a slot and that widening removes the case
rather than catching it. The code did neither.

## The repair, and why this shape

The rounding step does not need a wider integer. Every mode's answer is either the slot at or below the
position or the one above it, so the step is `down` plus one bit saying whether to step. Carried as that
pair, it names every position from `i128::MIN` to one past `i128::MAX`, one more than the index holds,
which is exactly the reach rounding needs. The completion then works from the pair:

- in range when `down >= lo` or the step lands on `lo` (`step && down + 1 == lo`, where `down + 1` is
  only formed when `down < lo`, so below `i128::MAX`), and `down <= hi` without stepping past it
  (`!(step && down == hi)`);
- wrap as `lo + ((down mod span) - (lo mod span) + step) mod span`, each reduction below the span and the
  span at most `2^64`, so every intermediate stays inside the index;
- saturate to `min` when `down < lo` and to `max` otherwise, which is unchanged, because an out-of-range
  pair with `down < lo` can only lie below the range.

`panic_on_overflow` asks the same in-range question of the pair. Addition's verdict reads the offset one
rounding adds, which is the step bit read as zero or one. A signed 256-bit carrier and a checked step
that refuses at the top were both considered. The first is a wider integer for one bit of information;
the second is a check on the value path, which the design and the ruling above refuse.

What still saturates is what the design already lists: a carry out of `Exact::between` past the index's
end, and positions computed for operands that are not members. Those are positions that were never
representable, so the map's input is already the saturated one. The applied map itself, from an `Exact`
to a slot, is exact everywhere.

## What the suite owes for this

- the top-of-index arm in `apply/tests/the_edges.rs` rewritten to the right answers, with the true
  one-past-the-top position wrapping to the range's lowest slot;
- `adapt` and `panic_on_overflow` over `AtTheTop`, every mode, both off-grid positions next to the top,
  against hand-worked answers, red before the repair;
- the same at `AtTheBottom`, since the step at the bottom never leaves the index and is the control.

## The other arms the platform-width round left unreached

`the_range_obligation.rs`'s `EmptyRange` is inverted as well as zero-width, so deleting the `WIDTH >= 1`
condition from `is_admissible` leaves the suite green. A range at `MIN = MAX = 0, WIDTH = 0` fails only
that condition.

The 16-bit arms in `tests/the_platform_width.rs` and `arvo-placement/src/tests.rs`, and the alias arm at
a 32-bit pointer, have never been compiled by anything a gate runs. A test harness needs `std` and no
16-bit target ships one, so the 16-bit test arms cannot be compiled at all today; the 16-bit library can,
at `msp430-none-elf` with `-Zbuild-std=core`, and the 32-bit test arms can at `i686-unknown-linux-gnu`.
A tool or lint that shells out to `cargo check` at two foreign targets from inside the lint pack runs a
nested cargo on every gate and needs `rust-src` and two installed targets on every machine. So this round
catalogues it rather than gating it: the unreachable arms carry a `// FIXME:`, and one ignored catalogue
test runs both checks when asked, so the check exists as code and fails when a target stops compiling.
