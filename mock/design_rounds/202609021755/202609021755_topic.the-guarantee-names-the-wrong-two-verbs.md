# The guarantee names the wrong two verbs, and an override is not a bypass

Round `202609021755`. A fix round on `arvo-format`, carrying the findings a review
raised against the round before it, `202609021714`.

## What is wrong

The previous round replaced a false guarantee with a checkable one and then lost the
measurement while writing it down. Three defects, each in the same sentence family.

**The two forcing sites are named wrong.** `DESIGN.md.tmpl` and the rustdoc on
`Format::ADMITTED` both say the obligation is forced at `apply` and the identity
search. `apply` is a module rather than a verb, and the obligation forced inside it
is `Slots::ADMITTED` at `apply.rs:355`. The two functions that force
`<F as Format>::ADMITTED` are `cancelling_slot` at `format.rs:325` and
`has_additive_identity` at `format.rs:401`, which `grep -rn "as Format>::ADMITTED"`
returns and nothing else. The round's own doc changelist had it right and the shipped
prose did not, which is why nothing caught it: the sentence the round exists to make
checkable names a site forcing a different obligation and drops one that forces this
one.

**The `Slots` clause is wrong in the same way.** "`Ambient`, `Quantum` and `Slots`
each force their own beside those" is false for `Slots`, which is forced at
`slots.rs:306`, `:317` and `:324` and at `apply.rs:355`, none of them beside the two
`Format` sites.

**An override is not one of the three routes.** The design lists an implementor
writing an empty `ADMITTED` over the default as reaching a value without passing a
forcing site. It passes the forcing site; the site does not refuse, because what it
forces is the implementor's own constant. So the restated guarantee is false in the
same direction as the sentence it replaced: it holds over those verbs only for
implementors who do not override, and a guarantee with that shape is not one.

## What this round does

- States the two verbs by name in both places, and states where `Slots` is actually
  forced rather than asserting it sits beside them.
- Reclassifies the override: two routes reach a value without passing a forcing site,
  and a third passes one and defeats it there. The distinction is what a reader needs
  to know which of the three a fix would have to close differently.
- Moves the override test arm off `contains`, which forces nothing, onto
  `has_additive_identity`, which does. Routing an override through a verb that forces
  nothing makes the two shapes the same call, so the arm cannot observe whether the
  override did anything, and it is the arm that would have caught the defect above.
- Adds the arm nothing carried: which verbs force. Three arms pin the routes that do
  not, and the claim that was wrong is the positive one, which no test contradicted.

## What it does not do

**It does not close any of the routes.** Whether the design should is open and belongs
to this tier, and `ruling::the_canon_does_not_police_what_shape_a_law_takes` licenses
leaving it open. Two proposals for closing it disagree and two disagreeing proposals
are not a decision.

## Scope

`arvo-format` only. One crate, because it is a fix rather than a feature.
