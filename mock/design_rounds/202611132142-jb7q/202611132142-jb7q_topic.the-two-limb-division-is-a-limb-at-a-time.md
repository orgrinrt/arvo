# the-two-limb-division-is-a-limb-at-a-time

**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Round:** `202611132142-jb7q`, job `arvo-format`, job 6 of track T2 of the arvo triage.
**Ticket:** `arvo_format_s_four_limb_division_by_a_two_limb_divisor_is_bi`.

## What is wrong

`Wide::floor_divided` divides the four limbs by a divisor of one limb limb by limb, and by a divisor
from `2^64` up one bit at a time: 256 rounds of a shift, a compare and a subtract on the host's 128-bit
integer, whatever the dividend. Every exact step that places a numerator over a `D` past `2^64` takes
that path, which at radix two is every product at an exponent below `-32`, the quotient by a 64-bit
member, the conversion between grids far apart, and now `land` at every magnitude it walks with a wide
denominator. The design says so in the multiplication section: "one bit at a time otherwise".

## The shape

The schoolbook long division by two limbs, Knuth's algorithm D at `n = 2`. Both operands shift left
until the divisor's top bit is set, which costs a fifth limb on the dividend. Each of the three quotient
limbs is estimated from the running remainder's top two limbs over the divisor's top limb, an estimate at
most two too large, and corrected against the divisor's low limb. At `n = 2` that test compares the whole
product with the whole three-limb partial remainder, so the corrected estimate is the quotient limb and
the multiply and subtract after it never borrows: algorithm D's add-back step is unreachable at two limbs
and is not written. The remainder is the last two limbs shifted back down.

The argument for the missing add-back, since a reader expecting algorithm D will look for it. Write the
running remainder `u2 u1 u0` and the divisor `v1 v0`, and `rhat = u2 u1 - qhat v1` as the loop keeps it.
`qhat (v1 B + v0) <= u2 u1 u0` is `qhat v0 <= rhat B + u0`, which is the loop's test, so a `qhat` it
passes is not too large, and it starts no smaller than the quotient limb and only falls. Where the loop
stops early because `rhat` reached `B`, `rhat B + u0` is at least `B^2` and above any `qhat v0` with
`qhat < B`, so the test would have passed. And the estimate cannot stop at `B` or above: the running
remainder is below `B` times the divisor, so `u2 <= v1`, the first estimate is at most `B + 1`, and each
pass that leaves it at `B` or above has `rhat < B` and goes round again.

## What tests it

The division against the shared oracle in `tests/big.rs`, whose division is another algorithm, over
drawn four-limb dividends of both signs and divisors at every normalisation shift, the two ends of the
two-limb range, and dividends built as a quotient limb of `B - 1` times the divisor plus the largest
remainder, where the estimate is corrected. Each quotient is also multiplied back. The mutant is the
correction loop taken out.
