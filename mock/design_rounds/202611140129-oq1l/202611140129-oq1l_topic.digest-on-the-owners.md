# Topic: the digest rebuilt on the operations its owners already have

**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Round:** `202611140129-oq1l`, job `arvo-digest`, ticket `arvo_takes_xxhash_rust_for_its_digest_and_allows_bsl_1_0`.
**Source topics:** round `202611140014-l3t2`, closed.

The first round put a host door of its own into `arvo-numeral` (`Byte`, `Bytes`, `Word`, `HostWord`,
`At`) and built the digest on word arithmetic written there for it. Coord ruled that route out: the
digest is built on the operations the owning crates already have, and what is missing is added at its
owner. This round replaces the host-door digest with that one, and removes what the first round added
wherever it is replaced.

## D1. Who owns what the digest needs

Coord, verbatim: "FNV-1a on `Num::mul` with wrap and a new `Bits::xor` in arvo-bits; `Bytes` in
arvo-bits beside Bits, its host door through the introducing crate that owns it; no file of your own
named `host.rs` in arvo-numeral: extend arvo-values' `host.rs` (its `from_index`/`index` door) with
what the digest needs ... 128 bits as two `Bits<64>` halves, unless the design of arvo-bits says
otherwise." And: "Build what builds (Fnv1a<32>, Xxh3<64|128>, Bytes, Bits::xor, the two-halves
128-bit digest), with Fnv1a<64> and the switched Digest<64> each under a FIXME citing a new ticket".

The intent: a byte, a word and a product each have one owner, and the digest reaches them through it.
Settled as follows.

1. The wrapping product is `arvo_numeral::Num::mul` on `Uint<32, Adapt<TowardZero, Wrap>, Quiet>`. A
   value crosses between the numeral and the container through arvo-numeral's existing
   `from_index`/`index` door, with the host type inferred from `Bits::masked` and `Bits::raw`, so the
   digest spells no host integer. Nothing is added to arvo-numeral, and the first round's section of
   its `host.rs` goes.
2. A byte is `arvo_bits::Bits<8>`. There is no separate byte type.
3. Xor is `Bits::xor`, at one width, total, a bit operation and not arithmetic: it never carries, so
   it does not make the container a numeral.
4. `Bytes<'a>` lives in arvo-bits beside `Bits`: an introducing crate, so it owns the door for the
   host's byte slice (`of`, `host`). `split_first` hands out a `Bits<8>` and the rest, as a
   `notko::Maybe`. notko is the stack's foundation and not a workspace crate, so arvo-bits keeps its
   zero workspace dependencies; it gains notko as its one dependency.
5. 128 bits is `[Bits<64>; 2]`, low half first, split from the host's 128-bit integer by
   `Bits::<64>::halves`, a door in arvo-bits, the crate that introduces the container.
6. A length predicate for the switch is not added now: the switched digest waits (D2), and the
   predicate is designed with it.

## D2. What waits on arvo-format

`Uint<64>` multiplication is refused at build, because arvo-format does not admit the wrapping
product over the unsigned 64-bit range. Ticket `arvo_format_admits_the_wrapping_product_over_the_unsigned_64`
(8797ef3e7954) tracks it, and arvo-format's lane takes the admission. Until it lands, `Fnv1a<64>` and
the switched `Digest<64>` are not in the crate, each named by a `FIXME` citing that ticket, and the
`digest-crossover` bench is parked with them. When the admission lands, `Fnv1a<64>` is the same
walk at 64 bits, the switched digest and its crossover come back, and the bench runs on the shipped
code. The branch is ready only then.

## D3. The digest's carriers

`Digest<BITS>` holds `Bits<32>`, `Bits<64>` or `[Bits<64>; 2]` through `Carrier`; `raw` returns that.
`bits::<N>()` on `Digest<32>` and `Digest<64>` is `cast::<N>()`, refused at instantiation when `N`
is wider than the digest.
