# Doc changelist: the width walk covers the verdict, the phase parts settle at compile time

**Date:** 2026-09-11
**Round:** review fixes on the admitted-addition round (branch `feat/admitted-addition-on-a-declared-signature`)
**Scope:** one wording fix in `crates/arvo-format/README.md.tmpl`; everything else is test-only or an internal
const-hoist. No design surface moves.

## `crates/arvo-format/README.md.tmpl`

"Floating point formats are refused for now, because the applied map has no magnitude coordinate to land a
sum at" states a refusal as temporary when the design section it summarises, "What this section does not
build", gives no timeline and no dependency that would lift it: two things block the magnitude-indexed
family, one a change to `Exact` and `adapt` this crate does not own and one an open canon call on what
wrapping means over a union of ranges. Drop "for now" and say what is refused and why, matching the design
section rather than promising a date the design never sets.

## Everything else is test-only or an internal hoist, and none of it is a doc change

The associativity verdict's own section, "Where the adapted sum is associative", already claims what the
new tests check: a licensed cell never diverges, and at a whole phase a refusal always names a triple that
diverges, "the witness one past the top" or "the witness one past the bottom", over any range of two slots
or more. The existing suite reproduces that claim only over the test-local windows inside `[-4, 4]` and
widths up to six bits, brute force by construction (the `Table` type this file's own tests share caps at
256 members). The obligation's own arithmetic in `saturated` and `completion_at` narrows a value one domain
wider than a slot into the slot coordinate, and the narrowing only has anything to saturate near the top of
the admitted width, sixty-two bits. Nothing in the suite reaches it.

The fix is test-only: a new arm over `dispatch::every_width`, the same walk `the_operation.rs` already uses
for totality, covering both slot families at every admitted width from one bit to sixty-two. At a whole
phase, a refused cell gets the witness the design names, built from the reach the obligation already
computes (one past the top, or one past the bottom, whichever the range affords) and the operand that
demonstrates it, asserted to diverge through the real `add` function rather than assumed. A licensed cell
gets the same edge-drawn sample `the_operation.rs`'s totality test already uses, asserted never to diverge.
The same two widest formats `the_operation.rs`'s fractional-phase totality test already carries,
`Signed<62>` and `Unsigned<62>` at a third and a half, get the licensed-cell half of the same check, since
the design's "a licensed cell never diverges" claim is not stated as a whole-phase-only claim and the suite
never exercised it past eight bits either.

Two more are test hygiene on the existing suite, both test-only: the fractional-phase invariance test in
`the_verdict.rs` asserts every count equals the first without ever pinning what the first is, so a map that
stopped diverging entirely would pass silently; pin it. `the_operation.rs`'s totality test already covers
the widest ranges under a fractional phase; nothing here changes there.

One is an internal, non-observable hoist: `phase_parts`, the euclidean division and the reduction it runs,
depends only on the format `F` and is recomputed on every call to `sum_position`, `addition_reach` and
`addition_is_associative`. It moves to an associated const the same three call sites read, settled once at
monomorphisation rather than recomputed per call, per the workspace's const-first discipline. The signature,
the obligation, and every verdict the section states are unchanged; only where the arithmetic runs moves.

No row in the registry needs a new citation for any of this. The associativity section states its claim
over any format meeting the admission rule, with no width bound in the text; "every admitted width" is the
totality section's own phrase, two sections down, for the property the totality test already walks with
`dispatch::every_width`. The gap this round closes is that the associativity suite never walked the same
set; nothing in the design moves. The const-first discipline is a workspace rule rather than a canon row.
