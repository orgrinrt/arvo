# Topic: a locked changelist cannot be edited, so what it got wrong is said in the next round

**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Governing canon:** `ruling::half_up_denotes_ties_toward_positive_infinity`.
**Where this came from:** findings 2, 3, 6 and 7 of the second review of pull request #150. None of them
is about what the crate computes. They are about the record of how it got there and about two arguments
that rest on something nothing measures.

## The two verification claims that are not true at head

`mock/design_rounds/202609192215/202609192220_changelist.src.lock.md` offers two claims as verification
and neither holds now. Both were re-run against the tree at `fc71ffb1`:

- `:156`, "Each of 29 arms broken in turn fails the suite, recorded in
  `mock/research/sketches/the-half-up-lint-mutants/`". That sketch's README says 28, and says the run is
  of the reader as it stood at `b8a7ce4b`, which is not the reader in the tree. Round 2 fixed the README
  and said nothing about the changelist.
- `:54`, "`grep -c \"neighbour\"` in `rounded` returns zero". `fn rounded` runs from
  `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/apply/tests/the_oracle.rs:167` to `:208`, and `:185` carries the word in a
  comment on the `HalfEven` arm. The claim was true of the function when it was written and the arm that
  carries the word landed in the same round.

A locked changelist is immutable and that is the right rule, so neither is edited. What is missing is a
place a reader meets the correction, and the only one the flow offers is the next round's record.

Two more claims in the same file have gone stale in the ordinary way rather than being wrong, and are
recorded for completeness rather than as defects: `:159` says the lint suite passes forty tests, where it
now passes sixty-nine, and `:19` says a grep names only the note's statement, which it still does.

## The registry edits no changelist declares

Commits `5e64c2ae` and `ffcf3c44` each edit the `note` of a ratified `ruling` row. Both landed between
the close of round `202609192215` and the open of round `202609200040`, so no changelist on this branch
declares either, and `202609200040_changelist.doc.lock.md` states that the registry is not edited in that
round. That sentence is true of its own round and leaves the branch's record saying the registry was
untouched while the branch edits it three times, counting `fc71ffb1`.

The phase gate does not cover `mock/registry/`, so nothing refused it, and the edits are non-invalidating
so they nuke no design. What is owed is the declaration.

## Two arguments resting on something nothing measures

**`mock/lints/half_up_carries_its_note.rs:46-56`.** The lint excuses the documents that render from the
registry, on the argument that none of them tells a reader what the name means. That is a claim about
live data and nothing instruments it. It holds today and the review found it holding barely: one of those
documents names the mode three times and states the denotation twice, and clears only because both rows
citing it name the ruling by slug in the same sentence. A carve-out resting on a property of the corpus
is checkable against the corpus, and the gap that argument leaves is where the first review's blocker 4
and the second review's blocker 1 both landed.

**`mock/crates/arvo-format/tests/the_ties_away_alias.rs:58`.** The rounding half of the oracle has two
statements of itself and they are cross-checked; `complete` has one. A wrong `complete` would not pass
quietly, because the alias arms would fail while the planted controls still passed, so the severity is
low and the asymmetry is real: it is the one part of the oracle nothing double-states.

## The decisions

**The corrections go in this round's doc changelist, under a heading that says what they are**, naming
each voided claim by file and line and saying what is true at head instead. Rounds are read in order and
that is the only surface the flow gives a reader of a closed round.

**The registry edits are declared here**, as the three commits they are, with what each changed and why
none invalidates a design.

**The note lint's carve-out gains an arm over the real `docs/` tree**, asserting the property the
argument rests on rather than stating it in a module doc. The carve-out stays; what changes is that it
becomes checkable, and a document that starts telling a reader what the name means becomes a failure
here rather than a blocker in the next review.

**`complete` gains a second statement and a control**, written the way `brute_step` is written against
`oracle`: a rearrangement that could be rearranged wrongly, cross-checked over the same domain the alias
sweep walks.

## What is handed back rather than done

The review's finding 8 is about the squash subject of the pull request, which wants
`` fix: round `half_up` ties toward positive infinity `` with the identifier backticked. Nothing in a
round can set that. It is the merge's to do, and it is recorded here so it is not lost.
