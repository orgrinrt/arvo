# Topic: hashing is an application, and it distributes along two axes

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Status:** op's call on the shape. The crate name and the sequencing are the agent's, delegated by
op, with the mathematics sourced inline.

`arvo-hash` was agenda item 9: it appears nowhere in D2's crate graph, while every other domain in
this restructure either split into contracts and concretes or was decomposed into its constituent
families. op declined to answer it as a placement and asked three questions instead: does hashing fold
into some other domain under its mathematical semantics, is there a name for that, and does the bigger
grouping make the contracts split more sensible.

The answer to the first is that it folds into **two** domains, along different axes, and they are not
in competition.

## D42. Both axes, and each takes what belongs to it

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** Distribute along both, and distribute correctly according to what the
mathematical field itself establishes rather than according to what is convenient.

**By machinery, everything under hashing is finite-field and modular arithmetic.** This is not an
analogy. Verified from the sources rather than recalled:

| Construct | Arithmetic | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| FNV-1a | XOR, then multiply by a prime where "the multiply returns the lower n bits of the product" | the ring ℤ/2ⁿℤ |
| CRC | polynomial division where "coefficients are elements of the finite field GF(2)" | GF(2)[x] |
| Universal hash families | constructed over finite fields | field arithmetic |
| LCG and xorshift generators | the same modular and GF(2)-linear maps | as above |

A finite field is an algebraic structure, so **its contracts belong in `arvo-algebra-contracts`
regardless of what happens to hashing**. `Ring`, `Field` and `FiniteField` join the ladder there
alongside `Semigroup` and `Monoid`; GF(2ⁿ) concrete arithmetic goes to `arvo-algebra` as its sibling.
That placement stands on its own merits and would be right even if arvo never hashed anything.

**By purpose, hash and PRNG and noise are one family.** They are deterministic functions whose output
is statistically indistinguishable from random. Coherent noise of the Perlin and simplex kind is built
directly on integer hashing, so the family is not three neighbours but one construction wearing three
names. This is the grouping consumers actually reach for: hilavitkutin wants hashing, ikiuni wants
noise, and both want the same underlying mixing.

That is also the grouping that makes the contracts split worth doing, which was op's third question.
Splitting `arvo-hash` today would produce a contracts crate holding `ConstHash` alone, which is
ceremony. A contracts crate holding a hash contract, a generator contract and a noise contract is a
real surface.

## The name, and why not the shorter ones

**Agent call, delegated.** op proposed `arvo-pseudorand` and asked for a read on whether something
shorter is still accurate. It is the right name, and the shorter candidates each lose something
load-bearing.

`arvo-rand` is the obvious shortening and is actively wrong. arvo is `no_std` with no entropy source,
so every one of these functions is deterministic and reproducible, and that is the property consumers
depend on. The **pseudo** is not padding: it is the guarantee. Dropping it names a thing arvo does not
and cannot provide.

`arvo-noise` is one member standing for the family. `arvo-mixing` names the operation rather than the
field, and would not tell a reader that generators belong there.

**One honesty caveat the crate documents rather than hides.** "Pseudorandom" is precise for
generators, where pseudorandom generators and pseudorandom functions are the field's own terms. A
non-cryptographic hash like FNV-1a is a mixing function with good avalanche behaviour, not a proven
pseudorandom function. The crate carries that distinction in its own documentation and claims no
cryptographic strength anywhere, because a consumer who reads the crate name as a security property
would be wrong in a way that matters.

## Sequencing, so the split is not ceremony

`arvo-hash` becomes `arvo-pseudorand`, holding what it holds today. The generator and noise families
land there when they are built, and **the contracts split happens at that point**, when there are
three traits to hold rather than one. Doing it now produces exactly the near-empty crate that the
alternative to op's answer objected to, and `arvo-refit` in D28 is this round's worked example of what
a crate created ahead of its contents becomes.

When the split lands, `Hash<N, S>` moves to the contracts side with `ConstHash`. Under
`arvo-bridge-home-rule.md` the trait lives where its return type is reachable, and today that return
type is declared in `arvo-hash` itself, which is the only reason the question looked settled.

The dependency direction is fixed by the first half of this decision: `arvo-pseudorand` depends on the
algebra crates for the field and ring arithmetic it is built out of, rather than carrying its own copy
of modular reduction.

## Decisions

D42 is op's, including the instruction to distribute by mathematical fact rather than convenience. The
crate name, the caveat about non-cryptographic hashes, and the sequencing of the contracts split are
the agent's under delegation.
