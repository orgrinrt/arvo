# Research: arvo's strategy axes measured against the field

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Phase:** TOPIC (research artifact, no decisions)
**Answers:** D46 of `202607292300`, which sent the conditional-law finding to research rather than
picking a mechanism for it.

D46 asked three things: whether existing work on dispatch strategies for numeric primitives exists,
whether arvo is doing it wrong, and whether there are more axes. All three have answers, and the round's
framing of the underlying finding turns out to be wrong on the part it called unsolved.

## Answer to the first: the work exists, extensively, and one instance is an IEEE standard

Four independent industrial systems parameterise fixed-point arithmetic, and they agree on the
decomposition to a degree that makes it the field's settled answer rather than one design among many.

| System | Type | Parameters |
|---|---|---|
| SystemC, IEEE 1666 | `sc_fixed<W, I, Q, O, N>` | word length, integer length, quantization mode, overflow mode, saturation-bit count |
| AMD Vitis HLS | `ap_fixed<W, I, Q, O, N>` | same five |
| Siemens Algorithmic C | `ac_fixed<W, I, S, Q, O>` | width, integer bits, signedness, quantization mode, overflow mode |
| MATLAB Fixed-Point Designer | `fi` plus `fimath` | word length, fraction length, signedness, `RoundingMethod`, `OverflowAction`, `ProductMode`, `SumMode` |

Two formal treatments cover the same ground. "Modeling SystemC Fixed-Point Arithmetic in HOL" states
its theory "included the parameters representing the overflow, rounding mode, and the number of
saturation bits". "An SMT Theory of Fixed-Point Arithmetic" formalises sorts as `FixedPoint<tb, fb>`
with rounding-mode and overflow-mode constants as operation arguments, and notes that earlier
bit-vector formalisations "do not formalize rounding and overflows", which is the gap it closes.

Boost.SafeNumerics is a fifth data point from a different angle: it carries `PromotionPolicy` and
`ExceptionPolicy` as separate axes, so the width-growth question and the error-reporting question are
independently settable there.

## Answer to the third: yes, there are more axes, and one of them is missing entirely

The axis arvo does not have:

| Axis | SystemC | Vitis | MATLAB | SMT theory | arvo today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| total width | `W` | `W` | `WordLength` | `tb` | `N` on `Bits<N>` |
| radix point | `I` | `I` | `FractionLength` | `fb` | `I` and `F` |
| signedness | type choice | type choice | `Signed` | sort choice | `Sign` marker |
| **rounding / quantization** | `Q`, 7 modes | `Q` | `RoundingMethod`, 6 values | `RNE`, `RTZ` | **absent** |
| overflow | `O`, 5 modes | `O`, 5 modes | `OverflowAction`, 2 | `SAT`, `OVF` | `OverflowPolicy`, 2 |
| intermediate precision | | | `ProductMode` / `SumMode`, 4 each | | implicit inside `Strategy` |
| saturation-bit count | `N` | `N` | | | absent |

**Rounding mode is the missing axis, and it is not a minor one.** Every system above treats it as
co-equal with overflow, and SystemC enumerates seven modes: `SC_RND` (to plus infinity), `SC_RND_ZERO`,
`SC_RND_MIN_INF`, `SC_RND_INF`, `SC_RND_CONV` (convergent), `SC_TRN` (truncation, the default), and
`SC_TRN_ZERO`. MATLAB's six are Nearest, Ceiling, Convergent, Zero, Floor and Round. arvo has no
vocabulary for any of this, so every narrowing operation it performs has an implicit, undocumented
rounding behaviour that a consumer cannot select or even name.

**The overflow axis is also under-populated.** arvo has two values where SystemC and Vitis have five:
`AP_SAT`, `AP_SAT_ZERO` (force to zero on overflow), `AP_SAT_SYM` (symmetric saturation), `AP_WRAP`, and
`AP_WRAP_SM` (sign-magnitude wrap). Three of the five have no arvo equivalent.

**And `Strategy` is a preset over several axes rather than an axis.** `Hot`, `Warm`, `Cold` and
`Precise` each bundle a container-width policy, an overflow policy and an intermediate-precision
policy. MATLAB separates that last one explicitly as `ProductMode` and `SumMode`, each with four values
(FullPrecision, KeepLSB, KeepMSB, SpecifyPrecision), and FullPrecision is exactly what D34 established
`Warm` must mean at every width. arvo currently has that decision baked into a marker rather than
selectable.

## Answer to the second: partly, and the specific way matters

The axes are justifiable in principle, which was op's reading, and the field's own decomposition
confirms it: width, radix point, signedness, rounding and overflow are the right axes to have. The
defects are that one axis is missing (rounding), one is under-populated (overflow), and one is
conflated into a preset (intermediate precision inside `Strategy`).

This is a different conclusion from "the split is wrong". The split is right as far as it goes and stops
too early.

## The conditional-law finding is settled in the literature, and the round mis-recorded it

`202607281220`'s addendum recorded the finding as "a shape nobody has solved" and reported that no
literature exists on laws conditional on a second type-level parameter, searched across Haskell, Scala,
Rust and Agda.

The mathematical half is not open at all. The SMT theory states it directly:

> "Note that addition is commutative under both methods, but only addition using the wrap-around method
> is associative."

An independent source states the complementary half:

> "For unsigned integers, clamping preserves associativity of addition and multiplication, since
> saturation occurs only at a single boundary and cannot be [walked back]."

Both match a derivation that needs no source. Unsigned saturating addition is `a ⊞ b = min(a + b, M)`,
so `(a ⊞ b) ⊞ c = min(min(a+b, M) + c, M) = min(a+b+c, M+c, M) = min(a+b+c, M)` because `M + c >= M`,
and the other grouping gives the same. It is truncated addition, a commutative monoid.

**The precise characterisation, which is sharper than either the round or the sources state it:**
saturating addition is associative exactly when the operands are one-signed. The cause is two-sided
clamping, not asymmetry. Once a value can be clamped at one bound and then moved away from it, the
clamped information is lost and the grouping matters.

**So symmetric saturation does not fix it**, which is worth stating because `SC_SAT_SYM` and
`AP_SAT_SYM` look like they should. Symmetric saturation clamps signed values to `[-M, M]` rather than
`[-M-1, M]`, and `(127 + 1) + (-1) = 126` while `127 + (1 - 1) = 127` still holds. `SAT_SYM` exists for
DSP reasons (symmetric range, no asymmetric negative extreme), not for algebraic ones.

**What is genuinely open is only the encoding question**, which the earlier pass conflated with the
mathematical one. How to express in Rust's type system that a law holds for one instantiation of a
parameter and not another is a separate problem from knowing which laws hold, and the second is
answered. On the encoding, the proof-assistant world carries the closest precedent: Lean declares
associativity, commutativity and idempotence as separate law-bearing classes attached to an operation
rather than as prose attached to a structure, which is the shape of a mechanism rather than a borrowed
solution.

## What follows for the round, stated as findings rather than as decisions

- A rounding-mode axis is owed. It is absent, the field considers it co-equal with overflow, and its
  absence means arvo's narrowing behaviour is unspecified rather than chosen.
- The overflow axis wants the three missing values considered on their merits: saturate-to-zero,
  symmetric saturation, and sign-magnitude wrap.
- `Strategy` wants examining as a preset. If `Hot` and `Warm` are bundles over (width policy, overflow,
  intermediate precision), then the bundle is a convenience and the axes underneath it should be
  nameable. D34 already prised one of the three apart by necessity.
- The law question needs no research, only an encoding. Associativity holds under wrapping and under
  one-signed saturation, and fails under two-sided saturation. That is a fact to encode, not a gap.

None of the above is a decision. The axis set is op's call, and the changes implied here are larger than
the round's remaining scope.
