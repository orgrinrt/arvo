# Topic: `bitfield!` becomes its own crate

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Status:** one op call.

## D25. `bitfield!` moves to `arvo-bitfield`

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** Its own crate, rather than following its expansion target into
`arvo-bits` or staying beside the rest of what the facade declares.

**The reason is what it becomes, not what it is.** As a declarative macro it would sit reasonably in
`arvo-bits`, since it expands to `Bits` accessors and never mentions `UFixed`. But a macro crate can
later be a **proc-macro crate**, and that opens attribute and derive macros: `#[bitfield]` on a
struct, `#[derive(...)]` for the accessor surface, and whatever else the convenience layer wants. A
proc-macro crate cannot be the same crate as an ordinary library, so putting the macro where it would
have to move from is choosing a migration later instead of a crate now.

It also matches the granularity settled for kirjo: a consumer reaching for bitfields compiles the
bitfield surface and nothing else.

**Consequences to carry into the changelist.** `arvo-bitfield` is the workspace's first crate that may
become a proc-macro crate, which interacts with two existing rules. `no-alloc-no-std-framing.md`
grants proc-macro crates a configurable exemption, since their source runs in the compiler host and
does not ship with the consumer binary, and `mockspace.toml` declares `proc_macro_crates` for exactly
that. Neither applies while it stays declarative; both apply the moment it does not, and the switch is
the point at which `proc_macro_crates` gains its first entry.

The facade re-exports it, as it does everything else.
