# Topic: arvo-shape, the two-dimensional primitives

**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** naming the crate and what it owns. No work is done in this topic and none is planned yet.
**Origin:** the first design round of `hökkeli`, the application shell, which needed shapes and found
that arvo has none.

## Why this is opened

arvo has no two-dimensional geometry at all. There is no `Point`, no `Extent`, no `Rect`, no `Size`
and no `Affine` anywhere in the workspace, which was confirmed rather than assumed. Three consumers
want them, and today each either does without or writes its own.

The consumers, named because a primitive with no named consumer is a guess:

- **`hökkeli`**, the application shell, working in pixel space.
- **`kolli`**, whose `kolli-layout` already computes positions in integer terminal cells and states
  that "terminal geometry is arithmetic: it computes positions and never draws".
- **`ikiuni-renderer`**, whose `-ui` crate composites in screen space.

## Why arvo rather than a crate of its own

Because arvo is **already free of hardcoded scalars**, and that is the property the whole thing turns
on. arvo works on integers as well as fixed point and carries its own float abstractions, and an
integer is fixed point with `F = 0`. A shape declared over arvo's ops abstractions is therefore
usable in pixels, in fixed point and in terminal cells without knowing which it is in, and without a
single scalar being named in its definition.

That is worth stating against the alternatives, because the two obvious ones in the wider ecosystem
both fail exactly here. `kurbo` welds `f64` into `Point` itself, and its only float abstraction is a
sealed trait that chooses between `std` and `libm` implementations of `f64` methods, so it cannot be
implemented for any other scalar. `taffy` teases genericity with `Size<T>` and `Rect<T>` while
hardcoding `f32` through every one of its algorithm sites. Neither can serve a consumer measuring in
terminal cells, whatever is done to it.

## What the crate owns

The fundamental contracts for shapes, the primitive contracts, and concretes where any are needed.
**The numeric and primitive side only.** Points, extents, rectangles, affine transforms, and the
curve primitives a themed interface needs to describe a rounded corner. Declared over arvo's ops
abstractions throughout, so the crate names no scalar.

## What the crate does not own

Layout. Arranging things relative to one another, adapting across phone, desktop, television and
widescreen, and the constraint and flow models that go with it, all live in **`lato`**, a separate
crate on arvo and notko. The split is by what a crate declares against what a crate uses: **arvo
declares the shapes for use, and lato declares the things that use them.**

Layout is policy over shapes rather than arithmetic on them, which is a different axis from
everything arvo holds today, and it is the axis along which arvo would become a monolith if it
absorbed this.

## Status

Named, not scheduled. The work begins when `hökkeli` and `lato` reach the point of needing it, and
this topic exists so that the naming and the boundary are settled before either one improvises a
local version.
