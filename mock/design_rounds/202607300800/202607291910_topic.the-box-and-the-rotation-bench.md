**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Status:** two agenda items closed. The `Rect` call and the bench instruction are op's. The general

## D40. The box is `Orthotope<N>`, with `Rect` aliasing rank 2

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29):** `Rect` is an alias for the two-dimensional case, not a distinct type.
The general type is rank-generic and `Rect` is how spatial code says rank 2.

**The general name is the agent's call, delegated.** op asked whether `KCell` could work, on the
grounds that it is shorter and more memorable, and asked for a domain read on whether it is too niche
or carries a different flavour. It carries a different flavour, and the direction is unhelpful.

The literature, fetched rather than recalled: **hyperrectangle** is the name Wikipedia leads with,
"also called a box, hyperbox, k-cell or orthotope", and equal edges make it a hypercube instead. So
all five are genuinely names for this shape and the choice is about collisions, not correctness.

**`KCell` collides twice, and both times toward the wrong shape.** In algebraic topology an n-cell is
"homeomorphic to the open unit ball B^k", so "cell" already names a **ball**, which is the opposite of
a box and the more widely known meaning by a wide margin. Separately, "cell" is what every simulation
and graphics codebase calls a grid element, and both arvo's consumers will want that word.

It is also more niche than its position in the synonym list suggests. Wikipedia's Heine-Borel article,
where a closed box in ℝⁿ is exactly the object under discussion, calls it an **n-box** and never says
k-cell. The term is house vocabulary from Rudin's analysis text rather than shared usage.

**`Orthotope` collides with nothing and is transparently constructed**: *ortho*, right-angled, plus
*tope* as in polytope. It is "the right-angled polytope", so once polytope is known the word decodes
itself, which answers the memorability half of op's question in its favour rather than against it.

`Box` is out on sight, since it reads as `alloc::Box` to every Rust reader. `Hyperrectangle` is
correct and recognisable but long, and its prefix reads oddly at the ranks where the type is still
perfectly valid.

So: `Orthotope<N>` general, `Rect` at rank 2, `Cuboid` available at rank 3 if wanted.

**On `Volume`, which op raised as the descriptive English word.** It names the right thing in the
wrong slot. The n-dimensional generalisation of length, area and volume is the **Peano-Jordan
measure**, and "some authors prefer to use the term *Jordan content*", defined on exactly this shape
as the product of its interval lengths. Volume is what an orthotope **has**, so it is the name for the
measure method, not for the type.

## D41. The rotation representation is settled by a broad bench, after the requirements are named

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** Bench it, in the sense D11 established, but not before naming what is
actually being benched for. The requirements and constraints get identified and written down first,
and then the bench matrix covers as many variants as can be thought of rather than the two the round
happened to name. The most inclusive and extensive matrix is the right one.

That ordering matters because the round had reduced this to a binary, and the binary was wrong.

### The correction: the fork is smaller than this round has been claiming

**Agent correction, and it is a correction to the agent's own repeated claim.** The round has been
carrying, from `202607281220` forward and into `202607291730`, that projective geometric algebra has
"a live unresolved split" between the bivector.net convention and Eric Lengyel's rigid geometric
algebra, "differing on dimensional assignment, on what wedge and antiwedge mean, and on
normalisation". Neither primary source carries that framing.

Wikipedia's plane-based geometric algebra article cites Selig, Dorst, Gunn, Lasenby **and** Lengyel
together as one body of work, mentions only "terminology variations regarding duality definitions",
and does not present RGA as a competing framework at all. Lengyel's own site states that RGA
"completely subsumes conventional models that include homogeneous coordinates, Plücker coordinates,
quaternions, and screw theory", which is a claim of generality rather than of disagreement. Its stated
distinguishing features are the grade and antigrade symmetry and a refusal of the word "pseudoscalar"
on the grounds that it portrays the element as "different from and perhaps somewhat less significant"
than the scalar.

There is a real structural and terminological difference. "Two camps with an unresolved split" came
from an unratified research memo produced inside this round, and it survived because later documents
cited it rather than the sources. This is the shape the workspace rules describe: agreement between
unratified artifacts is shared drift, because each one copied the previous one's framing.

### What this means for the bench

The correction supports op's instruction rather than undercutting it. If the two PGA formulations
differ mainly in terminology and dual conventions, then benching only those two would measure almost
nothing, and the variants worth measuring are elsewhere. The matrix wants the actual competing
representations of rotation and rigid motion, which include at least rotation matrices, quaternions,
axis-angle, Rodrigues parameters, the Cayley transform, dual quaternions for rigid motion, Lie-algebra
exponential maps on so(3) and se(3), and the geometric-algebra rotors under both conventions.

The requirements to name before any of that is built, since they are what the numbers get read
against: fixed-point representability of each form, which operations must be cheap (compose, apply to
a point, invert, interpolate, renormalise), how each degrades under repeated composition at finite
precision, storage width, and whether the degenerate metric is needed at all for arvo's cases or only
for ikiuni's.

Per `bench-in-bench-harness-never-sketches.md`, this is bench-harness work in `mock/benches/` with
committed artifacts, not a sketch.

## Correction: the fork is real, and the correction above was the error

**Appended 2026-07-29, same day, before any work was built on it.**

The section above claimed the round's "live unresolved split" framing was unsupported drift from an
unratified research memo. That claim is wrong, and the memo was right. Retracted in full.

The evidence it rested on was two shallow fetches: Wikipedia's PGA article, which cites the authors
together, and the main page of Lengyel's RGA wiki, which states a subsumption claim rather than a
disagreement. Neither mentions a dispute, and absence of mention in an encyclopaedia summary and a site
front page was treated as evidence of absence. It is not.

Lengyel's own writing disagrees explicitly, and on precisely the three axes the memo named. Against
what he calls the dual construction model, associated with the SIGGRAPH 2019 course:

| Axis the memo named | Lengyel's stated position |
|---|---|
| Dimensional assignment | their model requires "points be represented by trivectors and planes be represented by vectors. This is backwards", where "points should be one-dimensional, and planes should be three-dimensional" |
| Wedge and antiwedge | "Their model requires that ∧ corresponds to a dimension-decreasing meet operation and ∨ corresponds to a dimension-increasing join operation, but in the Grassmann algebra used by the rest of the world, the meanings are the other way around" |
| Normalisation | "in the dual construction model, its the components that don't involve e₄ that must have a magnitude of one", against his own requirement on the components that do involve it |

He concludes the other approach requires things "flipped upside down or turned inside out". That is a
substantive disagreement about the algebra, not a terminology variation.

**The lesson is about how the distrust rule was applied, not about whether to apply it.** Unratified
research is presumed wrong where it conflicts with the canon. This memo conflicted with nothing in the
canon: it reported a fact about external literature, checkable against that literature. Treating
"unratified" as licence to overturn it on weaker evidence than it was built on inverts the rule, and
doing so in front of op with two fetches behind it was worse than leaving the claim alone.

**Nothing in D41 changes.** Naming the requirements first and benching broadly is still right, and is
now better motivated: the two conventions differ in ways that change which operations are cheap and how
normalisation behaves under repeated composition, so they are genuinely worth measuring against each
other rather than being near-duplicates. The wider matrix listed above still stands, with these two as
real members of it rather than as a distinction not worth the bench.
