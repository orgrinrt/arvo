# Topic: `Identity` is algebra, and `Bounded` is the shape `Identity` already replaced

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Phase:** TOPIC

## D23. `Identity` and `SignedIdentity` move to `arvo-algebra-contracts`

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** Already called in substance and recorded here because it had not been
written down. The identity is algebraic structure: `Monoid<Op>` is `Identity<Op> + Combine<Op>`, and
`202607281547` created `arvo-algebra-contracts` to hold exactly that ladder. Keeping the identity in
the strategy-marker crate splits one concept across two crates and leaves the algebra naming its own
foundation from elsewhere.

**What follows it.** `Additive` and `Multiplicative` are the operation markers `Identity<Op>` is
parameterised by and have no meaning apart from it, so they move with it. The `OneRepresentable<TAG>`
witness and `tag_one_representable` exist solely to remove `Identity<Multiplicative>` from a purely
fractional type, so they move with it too. What stays in `arvo-strategy` is the strategy axis itself:
`Hot`, `Warm`, `Cold`, `Precise`, `Strategy`, `Resolve` and the container projection.

## D24. `Bounded` moves with it, and is a candidate for dissolving entirely

**Decision (agent, on op's explicit delegation, 2026-07-29).** op called `Identity` and said `Bounded`
felt like a property of it, while stating they are not a mathematician and delegating the call. This
is that call, with the reasoning shown so it can be overturned on sight rather than taken on trust.

**`Bounded` is algebra, and op's instinct is exactly right.** `MIN` and `MAX` are not merely
representational limits; they are identity elements. For the lattice operations,
`max(x, MIN) == x` and `min(x, MAX) == x`, so `MIN` is the identity of `max` and `MAX` is the identity
of `min`. A type with a least and a greatest element is a **bounded lattice**, and its bounds are
precisely its two identities. So `Bounded` moves to `arvo-algebra-contracts` alongside `Identity`.

**And the finding this exposes.** `202607281547` records why `Identity<Op>` has one constant with the
operation as a parameter:

> The earlier shape carried `ZERO` and `ONE` together, which named two instances of one concept after
> their values and forced a type to have both or neither.

`Bounded { MIN, MAX }` is that exact shape, unreformed. Two constants, named after their values,
forced to arrive together, where they are two instances of one concept under different operations.
The same crate already anticipates the replacement:

> `Identity<Min>`, `Identity<Max>` and `Identity<BitOr>` each have their own correct element and
> arrive as new impls rather than as a third and fourth constant on this trait.

**So `Bounded` is `Identity<Max>` and `Identity<Min>` wearing one name**, and the argument that
produced `Identity<Op>` applies to it verbatim. Dissolving it would also buy the absence property the
same way: a type with no least element simply has no `Identity<Max>` impl, rather than a `Bounded`
impl carrying a `MIN` that is wrong.

**Why this is recorded as a candidate rather than done.** `Bounded` has a second, non-algebraic
reader: saturating arithmetic clamps to the representational limits of the container, and that is a
fact about the representation rather than about a lattice. For every arvo numeric type the two
coincide, which is why the conflation survived. Whether the saturating path should name
`Identity<Min>` and `Identity<Max>`, or whether a separate representational-limits contract is owed,
is a real question and is not settled here.

**The immediate call stands regardless of how that resolves:** `Bounded` is algebra and moves with
`Identity`. Whether it survives as its own trait afterward is the follow-up.
