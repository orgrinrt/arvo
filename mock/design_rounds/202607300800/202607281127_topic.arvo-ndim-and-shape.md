# Topic: arvo-ndim and arvo-shape, the split and what each would contain

**Date:** 2026-07-28
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** working through op's proposed split. What would go in each crate, what has to be written,
what has to port. No decisions beyond op's own steer, and no work.
**Companion:** `202607281126_topic.what-arvo-ships-toward-n-dimensions.md`, the research this rests
on.

## op's steer, recorded as given

> perhaps add an `arvo-ndim` crate along with shape, and reserve shape for things using the
> `arvo-ndim` to express spatials (`Point<N: Dim>`, `Rect<X: Into<Point<2>>, Y: Into<Point<2>>>` or
> something like that etc. more whatever makes sense)

and, on what the abstractions must serve:

> They should work for theoretical math as well as real spatial concrete ariths.

Everything below works through that steer. Where the steer is under-determined, the alternatives are
laid out rather than resolved, and the resolution is reserved for the talk.

## The split, as the steer draws it

**`arvo-ndim`** owns dimensionality as such: rank as a parameter, the machinery for carrying a
capacity per axis, and whatever names a shape without putting a const expression in type position.
Nothing in it is spatial. A point in a 17-dimensional parameter space and a point on a screen are the
same construction at different ranks, and `arvo-ndim` is where that sameness lives.

**`arvo-shape`** owns the spatial reading of it: the semantic aliases, the types a renderer or a
layout engine names, and the operations that only make sense when the axes are space. It uses
`arvo-ndim` and adds no dimensional machinery of its own.

This is the same relationship, one layer down, that D10 drew between arvo and `lato`: one crate
declares a vocabulary, the next declares things that use it. It also matches how the ecosystem
outside arvo splits, where a geometry crate is separate from any numeric library.

## What `arvo-ndim` would have to contain

**A per-axis capacity.** The single most consequential gap. `Matrix<W, C>` is square, one capacity
serving both axes, which is right for an adjacency matrix and wrong for a rectangle. Anything
spatial needs extents that differ per axis, so `arvo-ndim` has to carry a sequence of capacities
rather than one.

The shapes available, each with different consequences and none of them chosen here:

- **A type-level list of capacities.** `Cons<Dim<3>, Cons<Dim<4>, Nil>>` for a 3 by 4 shape. This is
  the shape hilavitkutin already uses for access sets, and the workspace vocabulary already calls it
  a cons-list. It carries rank implicitly as list length and generalises to any extent per axis. It
  is also the most verbose at the call site and needs a length-fold to recover rank as a number.
- **Recursive nesting, as today.** `C::Array<C::Array<T>>` extended by induction. Requires no new
  concept and is what already works, but a function generic over rank cannot easily be written
  against it, which is the thing that is actually missing.
- **A capacity plus a rank marker.** Simple when every axis has the same extent, which covers a point
  in N dimensions but not a rectangle, so it likely fails the spatial case it exists for.

**A way to be generic over rank.** The concrete thing missing today: a `const fn` or a trait method
that works for rank 1, 2 and 3 without three implementations. This is what makes the abstraction
worth having, and it is the part with no worked example anywhere in arvo.

**A const half.** `Capacity` and `ConstCapacity` had to split, because `Capacity::from_fn` takes a
closure and closures are not const-callable. Any rank abstraction inherits that split, and the
question of whether it is one trait pair or something that avoids the split is open.

**And nothing else.** In particular not broadcasting, reshaping, or transpose chains, which
`arvo-tensor` explicitly refuses as "not aligned with arvo's role". `arvo-ndim` naming rank makes
those easier to write, which is exactly why the refusal has to be restated where the temptation is
strongest.

## What `arvo-shape` would have to contain

**The semantic aliases.** loimu already designed this table, over these exact arvo primitives, and it
is the part most worth taking rather than re-deriving. Recorded here as prior art rather than as a
proposal:

| Alias | Backing | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `Coord` | `IFixed<16, 16>` | Spatial coordinate, signed, sub-pixel |
| `Dimension` | `UFixed<16, 16>` | Extent, unsigned |
| `Factor` | `IFixed<2, 14>` | Scale, opacity, blend, range -2 to 2 |
| `Angle` | `IFixed<16, 16>` | Rotation in radians |
| `ColorChannel` | `UFixed<0, 8>` | Colour component, 0 to 1 |
| `GradientPosition` | `UFixed<0, 16>` | Position along a gradient axis |

Two warnings attach. `ColorChannel` as `UFixed<0, 8>` is the purely fractional configuration, which
is the case where arvo's own `Identity` could not represent one, since the raw encoding of one is
`1 << F` and at `I == 0` the container is exactly `F` bits wide. And loimu **abandoned** this whole
table in favour of `kurbo` and `peniko`, with no recorded reason, which is the open question flagged
in hökkeli's research at `mock/research/202607281058_loimu_research/03-geometry-already-designed.md`.

**The spatial types.** `Point`, an extent, an axis-aligned box, a transform. Over `arvo-ndim`, a
point in N dimensions is a rank-1 shape of `Coord`, and a box is two points or a point and an extent.

The steer sketches `Rect<X: Into<Point<2>>, Y: Into<Point<2>>>`, which reads as a rectangle
constructible from anything convertible to its corners. Worth noting that the same construction at
rank N is an axis-aligned bounding box, and that naming it `Rect` fixes it at rank 2 in the reader's
mind while the type does not. Whether the rank-2 case gets its own name over a general box, or
whether the general name is used throughout, is a call for the talk.

**What is genuinely spatial and not dimensional.** Rotation is the sharp example: an angle is a
scalar at rank 2, a three-component thing at rank 3, and a bivector in general. Anything that behaves
like that belongs in `arvo-shape`, because it is where the axes stop being interchangeable.

**Colour is the boundary case.** loimu put `Color` and `GradientStop` in its geometry crate. Colour is
a bounded vector space and nothing about it is spatial, so it is a genuine question whether it
belongs in `arvo-shape`, in a crate of its own, or nowhere in arvo at all.

## What has to port, either way

From `202607281126`, the consumers that would be re-read:

| Crate | Holds | Relationship to a rank abstraction |
|---|---|---|
| `arvo-tensor` | `Array` rank-1, `Matrix` rank-2 square | The instantiations. Port onto the general form |
| `arvo-bitmask` | `Mask<W>` rank-1, `BitMatrix<W, C>` rank-2 | Same stop, over bits. Whether it shares the abstraction is open |
| `arvo-sparse` | `Csr<C>` | Rank-2 sparse. Probably unaffected, worth checking |
| `arvo-spectral` | Consumes and re-exports `Matrix` | Follows `arvo-tensor` |
| `arvo-graph` | Consumes `BitMatrix` | Follows `arvo-bitmask` |
| `arvo-comb` | Const-generic structures | Follows whichever it binds |

The consolidation precedent is established and points one way: `cap_size` was duplicated in
`arvo-bitmask` and `arvo-spectral` and was pulled into `arvo-tensor` as the single home. This family
converges rather than replicating.

## The constraint that governs every option

Restated because it is the one that turns a design into an internal compiler error rather than a
compile failure:

> the round-trip `cap_size(cap(N))` overflows the well-formedness evaluator and ICEs the moment a
> generic consumer threads the capacity through its own generic code.

Any scheme that computes a shape in type position, a product of extents, a stride, a flattened
length, walks back into it. `Capacity` exists precisely to keep `[T; N]` in plain min-const-generics,
and an N-dimensional abstraction is exactly the kind of thing that wants to multiply extents together.

## Open questions for the talk

1. Is `arvo-ndim` a new crate, or is this what `arvo-tensor` becomes? The foundation already lives in
   `arvo-tensor`, and the consolidation precedent points there rather than to a new crate.
2. Does the rank abstraction span values and bits, or is it stated once per element domain?
3. Which per-axis capacity shape: a cons-list, recursive nesting, or a capacity plus rank?
4. Does `arvo-shape` keep the `Rect` name at rank 2, or name the general box?
5. Does colour belong in `arvo-shape` at all?
6. Is loimu's alias table adopted, and what was the reason it was abandoned there?
7. What is the const surface, given `Capacity` and `ConstCapacity` had to split?

Question 6 is the one that cannot be answered by research and is worth asking first.
