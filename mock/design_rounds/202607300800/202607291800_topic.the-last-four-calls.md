## D34. Strategy is a mapping of mathematical intent, so the band follows from what Warm promises

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** The question of what `Warm` and `Precise` do between 65 and 128 bits is
not settled by which document is older or by what currently compiles. A strategy marker is the mapping
of an intended mathematical concept onto the arithmetic a machine actually performs, so the answer
comes from what a consumer, a mathematician among them, is entitled to expect from the marker they
named.

**What Warm promises is headroom.** Its native ladder is 2x-logical: `container.rs:205` maps tag 0 to
`u16` for widths up to 8, tag 3 to `u128` for widths up to 64. A 64-bit logical value sits in a
128-bit container so an intermediate product is exact rather than wrapped. That is the whole content
of the marker.

**The promise stops at 64 without saying so.** `BitsContainerFor for Warm` passes
`bytes_for_u16(N)`, the byte-exact width (`container.rs:270`), and the wide bucket is
`WideBits<BYTES, A1>` (`container.rs:238`). So:

| Logical width | Warm container | Bits available | Headroom |
|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | `u128` | 128 | 2x |
| 65 | `WideBits<9>` | 72 | none |
| 128 | `WideBits<16>` | 128 | none |

The container **shrinks by 56 bits** as the logical width grows by one, and every width past 64
carries a marker whose meaning no longer holds. Nothing warns; the type still reads `Warm`.

**Derived (agent), from op's principle plus the table above.** Neither shape the earlier topic
proposed is right. Refusing the band would deny a mathematician a width the storage can serve.
Allowing it as it stands keeps a marker that has quietly stopped meaning anything. The resolution the
intent forces is that **Warm and Precise carry 2x-logical at every width**, so their wide bucket is
sized from twice the logical width rather than from the logical width.

Mechanically this is small, because the strategies already dispatch through separate impls with
separate tag functions. Warm and Precise pass a second const fn (`(2 * N).div_ceil(8)`) where they
today pass `bytes_for_u16(N)`; Hot and Cold are untouched, since byte-exact is what their markers
promise. `Warm` at 65 becomes `WideBits<17>` (130 bits), and at 200 becomes `WideBits<50>`.

This also corrects the design document, whose "Above 128 bits, Warm uses `WideBits<{bytes_for(N)}>`"
is the same drift stated at a different width, and it retires the `on_unimplemented` note's advice to
"choose Hot or Cold explicitly", which was never reachable. Three artifacts disagreed because none of
them was derived from what the marker means.

The test the earlier topic called absent is now specific: at a width in the band, assert that the
resolved container is at least twice the logical width, for Warm and for Precise. That is a property
of the marker rather than of one width, so it belongs over the whole matrix.

## D35. `BitMatrix` implements the n-dimensional contracts and is not bespoke

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** `BitMatrix` is not a special case wanting a bespoke rename. It is a
thing that implements the n-dimensional contracts, and once it does, it is generically implementable
over arbitrary extents for all of its dimensions.

That dissolves the naming question rather than answering it. `BitMatrix<W, C: Capacity>` reads as
"64 by 100" for a 64-bit row with 100 rows only because `W` and `C` are two unrelated ad-hoc
parameters. Under the shape contracts the axes are extents and are named as such, and the per-axis
reading follows from the abstraction instead of from a parameter letter.

**This does not overturn D8.** D8 said the shape vocabulary is available to bits and that neither
bits nor sparse is obliged to take it, and its worked example was precisely this case: a bit shape
wants `[Mask<4>; 3]` rather than `[[bool; 4]; 3]`. Availability is not refusal. The shape names the
axes; the **projection** is what differs, packing the innermost axis into words. `BitMatrix` takes
the contract and keeps the bit projection, which is exactly the arrangement D8 described.

## D36. UNORM shows the identity impl is incomplete

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** arvo takes UNORM, or something to the same effect. The finding is not
really about where a colour encoding is filed: it is that **arvo's identity is incomplete**.

`UFixed<0, F>` having no multiplicative identity was established as a fact about the type. It is
actually a fact about one **encoding** of that type. Divide by `2^F` and the span is `[0, 1)` with no
representable one, which is arvo's encoding and where `OneRepresentable` correctly refuses. Divide by
`2^F - 1` and all-ones lands exactly on 1.0, which is the Direct3D UNORM encoding, and the identity
exists.

So the encoding is a property the type does not currently carry, and `Identity<Op>` resolves against
an encoding it never named. Fixing that is what places UNORM: it stops being a foreign type needing a
home and becomes a second instantiation of machinery arvo already has, once the machinery admits that
the encoding is a parameter.

The shape of the fix is open. It is the same question `202607291710` raised for NaN: a property that
was assumed universal turns out to be a typestate the value should carry. Both are recorded as
directions, and the round should not invent an answer to either in passing.

## D37. `ConstDefault` is not an arvo concern at all

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** It goes to **notko**. None of the three candidates the round argued
over was right, because all three assumed the trait belongs somewhere in arvo.

notko is the standard library of this stack. A const-callable `Default` is a construction convention
with no numeric, bit-level or algebraic content: it says a type has a sensible starting value, which
is the same kind of statement as `Boundable` and `NonZeroable`, and it is wanted by any consumer of
the stack rather than by arithmetic specifically.

This retires the argument recorded in D27 and in `arvo-bridge-home-rule.md`, which placed it in
`arvo-storage` "for organisational consistency" with the predicate bridges. Proximity was the whole
of that reasoning, and proximity is what D27 set out to undo. The bridge-home rule's own test agrees
once the assumption is dropped: the trait returns `Self`, reaches nothing arvo-specific, and so has no
reason to sit above notko.
