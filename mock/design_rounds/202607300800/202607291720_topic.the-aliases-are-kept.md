# Topic: the ergonomic aliases are kept, rehomed and migrated

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Status:** one op call, overturning a proposal to delete them.

## D31. All four aliases survive

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** `Fixed`, `Signed`, `Uint` and `Int` are kept, rehomed to
`arvo-numeric` as D2 already plans for the types they alias, and migrated off `generic_const_exprs`
under the cure D2 settled.

**Deleting them was proposed on the grounds that they are barely called**: `Fixed` and `Signed` appear
only inside two test comments, and `Uint` and `Int` have one use between them outside their own file.
That was the wrong measure. They were written for a reason, and a surface being unexercised today says
more about how young the consumer set is than about whether the surface is wanted.

**And the naming axis they express is one the stack will want as a bound.** `Signed` and `Uint` name
the signedness axis; `Fixed` names the fixed-point form. Those are exactly the distinctions a future
consumer wants to bound on, whether they end up expressed as aliases or grow into something a bound
can name. Deleting them now discards the vocabulary and the reasoning that produced it, and the
migration cost is small next to re-deriving both later.

## What migrating them means

They are one of the three GCE sites, per `202607291600`:

```rust
pub type Uint<const N: u16, S = Warm> = UFixed<{ ibits(N) }, { fbits(0u16) }, S>;
```

The computation is a **wrapping**, not arithmetic: `ibits` lifts a bare `u16` into the `IBits`
newtype. So the alias exists to spare a consumer writing the newtype at every call site.

The cure is D2's, the same one `Capacity` already applied: nothing computes in type position. The
sketch at `mock/research/sketches/202607291400_const-args-under-min-gca/` proves two gate-free
expressions of the shape, an impl table projecting the answer as an associated type, and the parameter
ceasing to be a const at all. Which of the two the aliases take is an implementation choice inside the
migration rather than a separate decision, and the constraint on it is that `Uint<13>` must still read
as `Uint<13>`, since that spelling is the entire point of the alias.
