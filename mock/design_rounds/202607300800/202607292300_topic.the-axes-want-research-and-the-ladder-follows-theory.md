# Topic: the strategy axes want research, and the ladder follows mathematics rather than precedent

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Status:** four op calls. Two of them reject the framing they were put in, which is the substance in
both cases and not a preamble to it.

## D46. The conditional-law finding is a research question, not a mechanism to pick

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** Three mechanisms were offered for expressing a law that holds under
`Wrapping` and fails under `Saturating`. None is chosen, because choosing one accepts a premise that
has not been checked.

**What is not in doubt:** different strategies having different behaviour comes with the territory,
and the type system making those failures prominent at compile time, rather than silent at runtime, is
the point of the design rather than a problem in it. The axes are justifiable and good in principle.

**What is in doubt is whether the axes are split correctly.** The finding that signed saturating
addition is not associative while wrapping addition is a full abelian group may be reporting something
about arvo's axis decomposition rather than only about saturation. The round is not entitled to
encode a workaround for it before knowing that.

Three things to establish, and the research is the deliverable:

- **Existing work on dispatch strategies for numerics and mathematical primitives in general.** The
  pass that produced the finding searched for laws conditional on a second type-level parameter and
  found none across Haskell, Scala, Rust and Agda. That is a narrower question than the one that
  matters. The broader literature on how numeric behaviour is selected and parameterised has not been
  searched.
- **Whether arvo is doing it wrong.** Explicitly on the table. The current split into overflow policy,
  container width and precision may be the wrong decomposition of the same space.
- **Whether there are more axes.** The two `OverflowPolicy` instances and the four strategy markers
  may be an incomplete covering of what a consumer actually chooses between.

Recorded as an open research task rather than an open design question, because the answer decides the
mechanism and no mechanism should be built first.

## D47. The ladder mirrors the mathematics, and other languages' retreats are not evidence about arvo

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** The algebraic ladder goes as deep as the theory does. Not as deep as a
named consumer forces, and not truncated to where other ecosystems stopped.

**The two cautionary data points are rejected as decision inputs.** NumHask collapsing Ring, Field,
Distributive and Module to type synonyms, and `alga` being succeeded by the narrower `simba`, are facts
about those projects and the machinery they had. They are not measurements of what is possible here.
This round has already demonstrated the pattern: things that were problems in Haskell and Scala have
repeatedly not been problems here, established by sketch and by bench rather than by argument, and the
compile-time-cost warning transferred from `shapeless` and `frunk` was already found not to apply
because recursive chain depth and flat breadth are different costs.

**The reasoning, in op's terms:** arvo embraces typestate and is already expressive with it, so the
answer is to lean on that harder and make the ladder work the way theory expects, rather than the way
languages without that machinery have had to settle for. Breaking the mould is the identity of this
work. Stopping short because others tried and retreated is the opposite of it.

**The obligation that comes with the licence, and it is not optional.** Rejecting precedent is
permitted precisely because the alternative to precedent here is evidence. Every rung that goes in is
sketched and benched to prove that productivity and efficiency both hold. That is the standard the
earlier refusals of borrowed conclusions were held to, and it is what makes this a different claim
from the one `alga` made.

So: lean on the theory and on contemporary directions in it, and reject tradition and existing
practice in this domain as a source of limits. Prove the result rather than assuming either outcome.

## D48. Width stays a const parameter publicly and lowers to typestate internally

**Decision (op, 2026-07-29).** `Bits<13, Hot>` keeps reading as `Bits<13, Hot>`. The `Wid<N>`
typestate the container-projection sketch introduced is an internal representation, and the lowering
happens inside rather than at the consumer's call site.

This matches the constraint D31 placed on the ergonomic aliases, that `Uint<13>` must still read as
`Uint<13>`, and for the same reason: the spelling is the surface consumers were given, and a migration
that changes it is charging every call site for an internal restructuring.

It unblocks the changelist, which was waiting on this to know what the migration's public delta is.
The answer is that there is none.

## D49. Determinism is not an unnamed property, it is what the design already guarantees

**Correction (op, 2026-07-29).** The research pass recorded deterministic reproducibility across
compilers and targets as a property arvo's vocabulary does not name, and treated both it and
constant-time execution as gaps. That framing is wrong for the first of them.

**Why fixed point is reached for here is accuracy together with demonstrable, proven determinism,
which floats can never provide.** That is the reason the substrate exists, not a quirk of it and not a
cheap trick, and it is not something to bolt on as a newly named axis.

**And the strategies do not compromise it.** Typestate is what makes sure of that. The behaviour is
determined by the exact shape and type a consumer chooses, so determinism holds for everyone who
chooses the same things. A consumer choosing differently also gets determinism, disjoint from the
other strategies rather than absent. Two consumers on different strategies agreeing is not a promise
arvo makes or needs to make, and reading their disjointness as non-determinism is the error in the
framing.

**Constant-time execution is untouched by this.** op's answer addresses determinism; the
constant-time half of that research finding has no answer recorded, and this file does not invent one.
It remains what the pass said it was: unnamed, and in tension with
`arvo-always-optimal-internals.md`, since the fastest path and the constant-time path differ by a
data-dependent early exit.

## Decisions

All four are op's. D46 and D49 reject the framing of the questions they answer, and in both cases the
rejection is the decision rather than a preface to one. D47 carries an obligation (sketch and bench
each rung) that is part of the decision and not a caveat on it.
