## 1. The lint is a substring test

`mock/lints/the_platform_width_points_read_the_pointer_width.rs` accepts an alias whose line contains
`usize::BITS` anywhere. So `pub type USize = UFixed<64, 0>; // was usize::BITS` passes, and so do
`UFixed<{ if usize::BITS < 32 { 32 } else { usize::BITS } }, 0>` and `ISize` spelled over `UFixed`.
The first two also pass every host test on a 64-bit host. The other way round, a correct alias whose
right-hand side rustfmt wraps onto a second line is a finding, because the lint reads one line.

The lint reads each alias's declaration up to its `;`, across lines, strips comments, removes
whitespace, and compares the right-hand side exactly: `UFixed<{usize::BITS},0>` for `USize` and
`Integer<{usize::BITS}>` for `ISize`. A test per bypass above, and one for a wrapped correct alias.
It declares `per_file() -> false`, which mockspace's `CrateLint` carries for a lint judging the crate
as a whole (`lint-rules/src/lib.rs`, the dispatcher hands such a lint the context once), in place of
comparing `ctx.source` against the first of `all_sources`.

## 4. `Exact::between` saturates past the ends of the index

The carry a remainder moves into the slot is at most `2^63` either way, and where the sum leaves the
index the constructor pins the slot and keeps the remainder. `between(MIN, -7/4)` stores `MIN + 1/4`,
so `Floor` over a range at the bottom calls it in range and adapts it onto `MIN`, where the position
named floors to `MIN - 2`, which is out of range and wraps to `MIN + 254`. The debug verdict says in
range for a position that left it, and the release wrap answers for a different position. Both are
what I18 rules out.

A flag saying the slot was pinned is not enough, and it fails at one place. `between(MIN, -1/4)` names
`MIN - 1/4`, and `Ceil` takes it onto `MIN`, in range. Pinned with the remainder kept, the stored
position `MIN + 3/4` ceils to `MIN + 1`, and a flag reading "pinned" would call it out of range.

What decides every answer past an end is how far past, so the constructor carries that: the slot pinned
at the index's end, as now, and beside it the distance of the named slot past that end, a signed count
that is zero wherever the index holds the sum. The distance is at most the carry, so it fits the
fraction's own 64-bit integer. Every region reads the pair with nothing wider than the index:

- the sign-dependent modes read the named slot's sign, which is the end's; half-even reads its parity,
  the pinned slot's parity plus the distance's;
- the in-range question is false past the top, and past the bottom it holds only where the named slot
  is one under the index's lowest, the rounding steps up, and the range starts at the index's lowest;
- the wrap reduces the pinned slot, the distance and the lowest slot separately, every one under the
  span, and adds the step before reducing once more;
- saturation pins to the end the position is past.

`Exact::slot()` still answers the pinned slot, and says so.

The other sites that construct an `Exact` do not reach this. `sum_position` hands the constructor a
remainder already under its denominator, so its carry is zero. It does saturate `a + b + phase` itself
at the index's ends, and the verdict is right there: `sum_is_carried` doubles each end of the range in
the index's own integer, so every range addition admits lies within `[-2^126, 2^126)`, a saturated
position sits at an end of the index at least `2^126` from it and on the side the named one is, and
only an operand outside the range gets there. What the wrap answers for that position is not the
named one's, and the design already says so and prices it as the whole of what the saturation costs,
for operands that are not members. That stands, and a test asserts the verdict beside it.
`addition_is_associative` hands the constructor a remainder under its denominator too.

The carry test in `apply/tests/the_top_of_the_index.rs` asserts the saturated answers today. It is
rewritten to the named positions first and shown failing. A law beside it feeds positions past each
end of the index through the carry, to every range at an end against a reference well inside, over
all 18 signatures.

## 5. The translation law at a span that is not a power of two

Spans 256 and `2^64` only. Two pairs are added, `TopOf200` against a reference at `[1002, 1201]` and
`BottomOf200` against `[-1998, -1799]`. Each reference's lowest slot is two modulo 200, off a multiple of
the span and off the residue of the range it is compared with, and each shift is even. Shown to fail
with the wrap reduction broken twice: reduced from zero, and the lowest slot reduced by 256 in place of
the span, which only a span of 200 can see.
