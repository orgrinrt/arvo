## What this round is for

arvo holds a format, a placement and a strategy binding, and no value. Nothing a consumer can put in a
field, pass to a function or return holds a number of a declared signature. This round adds the crate
that does, on the integer points: a value of a signature whose sum of two members is on the grid, held
alone in the carrier the placement derives, with its sum, its comparison and its doors.

The ratified rows license the type and say what it may read. The container ruling places the
footprint's one observation at sole occupancy, "where the value is the sole logical occupant of its
allocation", and binds every operation the design declares to be "a function of the declared width and
never of the machine carrier". The spine factors every operation as "an exact operation in the ambient
domain composed with a named total adaptation onto that set", so the value's sum is defined as the
oracle's sum: `slot(a.add(b)) == add::<S>(slot(a), slot(b), Dither::UNUSED)`, which arvo-format
already ships. The second read at `mock/research/202611122300_value-stance-second-read.md` argues both
readings and files them as proposals at one expert; this round builds on them and does not promote them.

## The crate

`arvo-numeral`, a new member under `mock/crates/`, depending on `arvo-format` and `arvo-placement`
and adding nothing to either. The roadmap named it as a working name. It stays: a numeral is what the
canon calls a value of a format, and the crate holds that and nothing else.

## How the value holds its carrier

The sketch at `mock/research/sketches/202611122300_value-over-derived-carrier/` built five arms and
names arm B the one to build on, because it is the only arm where code generic over a signature bounds
it by `Sole` and nothing more, with no feature line in the value crate or in the consumer. This round
takes arm B as it stands:

1. A map from each slot family the ladder admits, `Signed<W>` and `Unsigned<W>` for `W` from 1 to 64,
   to its ladder carrier, one row per width, each row a check-time assertion that the carrier's size is
   what `arvo_placement::derive_sole` derives for that family's canonical point. Width 0 and 65 have no
   row and are refused where a value is named.
2. `Num<S: Sole>(S::Repr)`, field private, `repr(transparent)`.
3. Each operation a `const fn` written once, which moves the bits to the machine integer of the same
   size and signedness, calls that rung's function, and moves them back. The move is a bit copy, the
   crate's one `unsafe`, bounded on a sealed `Bits` that admits the plain machine integers only, so
   every bit pattern of both sides is a value, and guarded by an assertion on equal size that folds
   away on the arm taken. Its precondition is in the type.
4. The rung arithmetic once per carrier width, `#[inline]`, expanded by one macro.

The sketch found one waste in its own reading: at a width below the carrier two members sum without
leaving the carrier, so the saturating machine add's overflow test is dead there. The rung's `add`
here takes the wrapping machine add followed by the adaptation below the carrier width, and the
machine's own wrapping or saturating add at the carrier width.

## What a value signature admits

`Sole` is implemented once, over every declared signature whose slot family is on the map, whose
overflow policy is a value policy and whose rounding mode is deterministic. Its `ADMITTED` constant
asserts that the format has a constant quantum and a zero phase, which is where the sum of two slots is
the slot of the sum, and every door forces it.

The overflow policies admitted are wrapping and saturating, each under two markers described below.
`Clamp` is refused: with no bound to carry it is saturation under another name, which arvo-format's
own completion says. The rounding modes admitted are the five deterministic ones; `Stochastic` reads a
dither and a value has nowhere to carry one, so it is refused at the bound, not at run time.

## The overflow assertion, and the second marker of each pair

`ruling::the_overflow_panic_is_permitted_and_bounded` puts a debug panic on "what cannot be caught at
compile time through the typestate, on debug builds, in every strategy", and its ratification asks it
be read loosely and sharpened "as we find the boundaries and real constraints". The names page ships
both readings at once, and this round does: each overflow policy has two markers over one policy
constant.

The plain spellings are arvo-format's `Wrap` and `Saturate`, and under them leaving the range in a debug
build asserts, in every operation and every door that adapts. This is the roadmap's default for its
call 5 and the row's letter.

The second markers are `Wrapping` and `Saturating`, declared here, implementing arvo-format's open
`Overflow` with the same `POLICY`. Under them completion by the declared policy is the meaning of the
operation, and nothing asserts in any profile. The precedent is the host's own: `core::num::Wrapping`
and `core::num::Saturating` never panic in a debug build, and that is what their names have meant to
every reader of Rust. The declaration is in the type, never in a build flag, which is what
`ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path` leaves open.

The assertion never lives inside the total operation. It asks a `const` verdict, `fits_add`, the way
arvo-format's `panic_on_overflow` is asked, and only under an asserting marker and `debug_assertions`;
in a release build and under the second marker the branch is a constant and clears at lowering. The
ingest doors ask arvo-format's own `panic_on_overflow` of the position they adapt. Every exhaustive law
of this round runs under the second markers; one test per policy shows through the verdict that the
first marker would assert, with no `#[should_panic]`.

Compromise stated: a consumer writing `Wrap` gets a debug abort on a sum that wraps. That is the
ratified row's letter and the roadmap's default, and the marker that turns it off is one word away.

## The doors and the operations

The names are the names page's (`briefs/fixup/pl0-arvo-value-names.md` on the kaski channel), each a
`const fn` taking `self` by value:

- `constant::<V>()`, `V` a slot index as a bare `i128` const parameter, refused at build with E0080
  where it is not a member, by an inline `const` assertion.
- `adapted(exact)`, ingest: `adapt::<S>(exact, Dither::UNUSED)`, the format's own adaptation.
- `from_raw(raw)`, total: `adapted` of the on-grid slot the bits name, through the rung's adaptation.
- `slot()` and `raw()`, the unwrap doors, declared as such.
- `add`, equal to the oracle; `fits_add`, the verdict.
- `eq` returning `Bool`; `cmp` returning `Ordering`; `lt`, `le`, `gt`, `ge`; `min` and `max`. The slot
  map is monotone at a constant quantum, and the carrier's order is the slot order because the carrier
  is signed exactly where the slot family is, so these read the carrier.

`Num<S>` implements `Clone`, `Copy`, `PartialEq`, `Eq`, `PartialOrd`, `Ord`, `Hash` and `Debug`, and no
operator trait, no `Default`, no `From` of a host integer.

Sub, neg, mul, the exact accumulator and `Lane` are the next step's (AR7a), and nothing here presumes
their shape.

## Identities

`Identity<M>` with one constant, and `Additive`, both new here; the call shape is
`<T as Identity<Additive>>::IDENTITY`. `Num<S>` implements it for every `S`, and the constant asserts
`has_additive_identity::<S::Format>()` before anything else, so on a format without one it is refused
as a failed constant with a message naming the missing identity. That answers the names page's open
point: the refusal is a failed constant, not a missing impl.

Within what `Sole` admits, zero is always slot zero and always a member, so the refusal fires only on a
format the admission would refuse as well; the pair below uses a half-step phase, which has no identity,
against a zero phase, which does.

The names page asked that associativity stay a verdict and never become a marker beside `Identity<M>`.
`proposal::a_law_stated_as_an_author_written_marker_is_checked_by_nothing` is the row behind that, and
`Identity<Additive>` here is computed from the format's own predicate rather than declared by an author,
so it is that row's repair and not its failure. Associativity stays arvo-format's
`addition_is_associative`. `Multiplicative` waits for the product on values.

## Aliases

`Uint<BITS, A>` over `UFixed<BITS, 0>`, `Int<BITS, A>` over `Integer<BITS>`, `USize` and `ISize` at the
pointer width under `Adapt<TowardZero, Wrap>`, and `Bool` re-exported from arvo-format. The const
parameters are bare integers, as `obligation::a_primitive_for_every_position_a_bare_number_would_take`
quotes op allowing. `USize` and `ISize` carry two more doors, `from_host` and `host`, the only places a
host `usize` or `isize` stands in a signature here, since their carrier is the ladder's and differs per
target.

## The proof

Equality with the oracle for `add`, every signature at widths 1 to 8 exhaustively, both slot families,
both policies, all five modes, through the value type; the same widths computed in each of the four
rungs against the oracle, which is the derived carrier checked against the 64-bit one; widths 9 to 64
on an edge lattice (range ends and their neighbours, zero and its neighbours, every single-bit value)
plus seeded pairs with the seed committed; `fits_add` against `panic_on_overflow`; one mutant per rung
behind a committed feature, killed; `from_raw(v.raw()) == v`, and carrier equality iff slot equality;
the two associativity laws reproduced through the value type with the registry's own counts. One test
binary per width band. A consumer with no feature line is a trybuild pass case. Compile-fail pairs,
each with a twin differing in the offending token: width 0 and 65, `constant` of a non-member, the
identity on a format without one, `add` across two signatures, a host integer where a value goes and
the reverse, the field named from outside, `Clamp`, `Stochastic`, and a phased format.

The disassembly of `add` at 13 bits in 16 and at 64 bits beside the host's `wrapping_add` is owed as a
harness artifact, and the harness cannot commit a disassembly of a named function (AR10a's finding,
ticket `913abcb7fd46`). What this round can commit is an ad hoc reading, and it is called that.

## The lint the crate meets

The crate names the host's machine integers: they are the carriers the placement's widths become, the
type `raw()` returns and `from_host` takes. `no-bare-numeric` refuses every such token outside a crate
listed under `[primitive-introductions]`, which `mockspace.toml` describes as "an auditable
architectural claim" that a crate brings the numeric substrate. This crate is that claim's third
instance beside arvo-format and arvo-bits. The entry is a `mockspace.toml` edit and is the
coordinator's to make; it is asked for, not made here.
