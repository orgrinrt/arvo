## CHANGE: the member door's grid is one quantum

`## Division`, the `Divisor::<F>::member::<G>(m)` paragraph: on the grid means one quantum, both constant,
and the same reduced phase. The quanta are compared as `U_F V_G = U_G V_F` in the four limbs, so radix ten
and radix two share a grid at exponent zero and do not at `-1`; a pair whose powers leave the limbs is
refused as not formed, with a message that says so (finding 2).

## CHANGE: the gap the member door leaves is stated

A new paragraph after the one closing on `Divisor::slot`: a zero-free range at a whole phase lies on one
side of its zero slot, so a divisor read at run time of either sign has no door, and whether signed
division is the known use the runtime membership door waits on is an open question this crate does not
answer (finding 3). The question is ticket `may_arvo_divide_by_a_signed_divisor_read_at_run_time_of_eith`;
the design does not name it, being public prose.

## CHANGE: the member door's tests

The paragraph opening "The member door owes its own": nine swept pairs, adding the bytes by the same range
at radix ten and exponent zero, and a signed byte by the members of `1` to `127` and of `-128` to `-1`,
which reach the step where `n + b d` is below zero (finding 4). Four conditions in the table, each stated
false alone and read from the stated answers, and the zero column checked at every format the table
names. Four planted verdicts: the zero check reading `F`, the radix and exponent read apart, the exponent
without the radix, the verdict without the range inside. The compile-fail list: radix ten at exponent zero
admitted beside the zero-free twin, and a range in tenths beside a divisor in halves refused beside the
same range in halves admitted.
