# The room at its edge

**Date:** 2026-11-20
**Phase:** topic
**Scope:** `arvo-numeral` (its design's account of where the value forms a position in the host's integer, its remainder paragraph, and the suites that hold the arm at its edge)
**Job:** `arvo-numeral-doors`, the first review of round `202611192051`

Round `202611192051` closed with the value forming the exact position of its product, quotient and
conversion in the host's 128-bit integer where a constant bound over the range holds
(`arvo-numeral/src/room.rs`). A fresh review of the branch read the arithmetic as sound and the suites as
green, and found the design text claiming more than the bound gives, the suites never reaching the bound's
edge, and a claim in the round's topic resting on a run that is not on disk. This round corrects those, and
nothing in the arm's arithmetic changes.

## The product's bound is the radix power, not the width

The design says "every product at a negative exponent, at every width a value has and the 64-bit ones among
them, is one 128-bit division" (`mock/crates/arvo-numeral/DESIGN.md.tmpl`, the product's section), and round
`202611192051`'s doc changelist and topic say the same. The arm holds where `r^-e` is a 128-bit integer,
since `Sides::POWERS` is that power and a power past 128 bits is `Isnt`, which makes `PRODUCT` false. At radix
two that is down to -127, at radix ten -38, at radix three -80, while the format admits the product down to
-255 at radix two (`tests/common/exponents.rs`). Below the edge the four limbs take it, which is correct, so
the code is right and the sentence is wrong. The sentence becomes the bound as it is: one 128-bit division
wherever the radix to minus the exponent is a 128-bit integer, the four limbs past that. The `bound` module's
test named for "every negative exponent" asserts only -1 and -64; it takes the true edge, -127 inside and
-128 past, at 64 and 8 bits.

## The suites reach the edge of the room

The shape suites take, per width, the highest exponent, zero, minus one, minus half the width and the
format's deepest. None of those is the last exponent inside the room at the widths where the room ends
before the format does, so the 128-bit arm at its edge, with the range's ends as operands, was never held
against the oracle. A sixth exponent joins both the product's and the quotient's family: the deepest at
which the value forms the position in the host's integer, or the format's deepest where that is nearer
zero, worked out in the tests' own arithmetic (`common::edges::product_room`, `quotient_room`). The members
a shape takes include both ends of the range and the divisors include one, minus one and both ends, so the
extreme operands are there. The conversion's matrix is five by five exponents already and a sixth would
weigh on the container's memory, so the conversion's edge is a file of its own,
`the_conversion_at_the_room_edge`: from exponent zero at 32 and 64 bits onto the room's last exponent and
the first past it, both ranges each side, every radix, every adaptation of the target.

## The quotient by a value takes every divisor and every landing

`the_quotient_by_a_value` divided the unsigned 8-bit dividends only by 1 to 127 and skipped every value
outside the divisor's range before landing it, so the module doc's claim that `Member::adapted` lands any
value in range by its totality was never exercised. The unsigned sweep now divides by 1 to 255 as well as by
1 to 127, and no value is skipped: each is landed, the landing is checked to be the end of the range it
passed or the value itself, and the quotient is checked against the oracle's by that slot. The sweep counts
the values landed from past the range, so a sweep that stopped taking them would fail.

## The remainder paragraph names the rows that now exist

The design's quotient section says no remainder ships because "the canon has no row on one" and names a
workspace ticket. Since round `202611192051` the registry carries the panel's two proposals and the two
open questions they leave. The paragraph cites the two questions with registry references, so the reference
check holds them, and names no ticket.

## The first run's factors are void

Round `202611192051`'s topic, section "The residue is not reduced, and the division is the narrowest that
holds", quotes a first bench run, "four to ten times ahead of the four limbs and still seven to twenty times
behind the hand version", and says "The first run stays committed beside the second." No such run is
committed: the bench's results hold only `20261119T224053Z-1dd38711`, and the bench note says the earlier
version was timed only from a dirty tree and nothing from it is quoted. Those factors are void, and so is
the comparison they stood for: dropping the gcd and the 128-bit division in the 64-bit case was decided by
reading what a value needs of the position, not by a measured difference. A closed round's topic is not
rewritten, so this section is where that is recorded.

## Left for later, as tickets

The bench times the 16-bit point only, so the 128-bit arm, its `__udivti3` and the modulo inside
`Remainder::of`, is unpriced, and the quotient's 1.22 us behind the hand version is attributed by reading
rather than measured. A 64-bit sweep point with a hand arm on `i128`, an arm on a pre-landed divisor and a
disassembly are a bench of their own. The value carrying an exact position step beside the format's holds
only because the format has no position step over `Member<F>` operands; whether the format should have one
is a design question for `arvo-format`. Both are filed as tickets.
