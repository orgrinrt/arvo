# eighth-review-claim-audit

The eighth review of the platform-width round found no defect in the code. Every blocking finding is a
sentence claiming more than the tests or the probe show, and two of them were written in the round
that was meant to fix that class. So this round is an audit of the claims the branch adds rather than
a repair of three sentences: every sentence the branch adds or changes that asserts a guarantee, a
coverage, an independence or a totality is named beside the test or probe arm that makes it true, or
narrowed until one does.

## The lint is described as a total guard

The design says the lint "additionally refuses declaring, importing or aliasing anything to the name
`core` anywhere in this crate", the lint's own header says "nothing in `arvo-format` binds the name
`core` to anything else", and the platform-width test module's closing comment lists the forms as
`mod core`, `use ... as core` and `extern crate ... as core`. The lint reads three forms and the
design's own unguarded list, a few paragraphs down, names what it does not read: `fn core() {}` and
`use fake::*;` both give no finding. The same paragraph lists "an `extern crate`" among the shadows the
leading `::` closes, and the next sentence says it does not close that one. Each sentence is narrowed
to the three forms, and the leading `::` is said to resist two of them.

## What a manifest rename needs

The design, the lint header and the probe's note say a rename to the key `core` reaches `::core` when
the renamed crate re-exports the real `core`'s contents. A crate carrying only `pub use core::prelude;`,
and one carrying nothing of the real `core` but an empty `prelude::rust_2024` module, both let the
dependent build with `::core::primitive::usize::BITS` reading the fake 8. What the rename needs is that
the path the edition's prelude import names resolves in the renamed crate. The probe gains both arms
under `run.sh`, and the three sentences say what the arms show.

## What the oracle takes from the implementation

The oracle is described as applying "each mode's and each policy's stated meaning". For seven of the
nine readings that is so. For two it is not:

- It reads `Clamp` as `Saturate`, which is the collapse the shipped map makes because the declared
  signature has nowhere to put a bound. The policy's own doc says a declared bound that need not be
  the range's end. So the `Clamp` cells of the sweep check that the map collapses `Clamp` onto
  `Saturate`, and nothing about a clamp to a declared bound.
- It reads `HalfUp` ties away from zero, which is the reading `rounding.rs` documents. What `half_up`
  means is `question::which_operation_half_up_denotes`, open.

Both are named as readings taken from the implementation, in the oracle's own doc and in the design.
Neither meaning is decided here.

## The sweep's region, widened

The sweep feeds distances of at most eight whole slots past an end and quarter remainders only, and
none of its formats is a platform-width point. The reviewer's widening, distances up to `2^63` past
the index, denominators 1, 2, 3, 4, 7 and `i64::MAX`, one-slot ranges, `[0, 2^64 - 1]` and
`[-2^63, 2^63 - 1]`, and `adapt` at `USize` and `ISize`, is committed as its own test file, sized to
keep the run short, and the broken maps including the step-onto-the-bottom one are run through it.

## Catalogue

`panic_on_overflow`'s doc and a test constructor's doc narrate what code did before. The design says
the `Saturate` policy is the only place the map pins anything, which is false for `Clamp`. What
`Dither::at` does with a ratio outside `[0, 1)` is not written anywhere: the stochastic mode compares
the dither with the remainder, so a negative dither steps every off-grid position up and one at or
above one steps none. The open items section says "some other round".
