# The page says the constructors refuse what they store

`docs/FORMAT.md` tells a reader that `Phase` "carries the numerator and the
denominator as a pair, its constructors cannot produce a zero denominator, and
the condition is a property of the type rather than a sentence in a doc comment
asking for one."

`Phase` has two constructors. `format.rs:73` is `of(num, den)` and stores both
unchanged, so `Phase::of(1, 0)` is a zero denominator and compiles. `format.rs:79`
is `halves(num)`, which fixes the denominator at two. The doc comment above `of`
says a zero denominator "is the one thing that cannot be right", and then stores
it.

So the sentence is the shape `a-claim-of-totality-names-what-enforces-it.md` is
about: a claim quantified over every constructor, with nothing behind it, sitting
one paragraph above the design's own honest treatment of the same case. The claim
predates the round that rewrote this file's provenance framing and was carried
through it unread.

## What the design already says correctly, further down

Two paragraphs later, on the difference between a zero denominator and a negative
one: "A zero one names no value, so reading it as one loses nothing that existed."
And under the totality of the predicates: "a zero denominator names no position
and so carries none either. The obligation removes the case from binaries; the
total branch is what makes the answer defined everywhere else."

That is the real design and it is coherent. The predicates are total, a zero
denominator is defined to name no position, and an obligation is what keeps the
case out of shipped binaries. Nothing there needs the constructors to refuse
anything, which is why the false sentence went unnoticed: the design works
without it.

## What changes

The one sentence. It states what the type carries and what the predicates do with
a zero denominator, and it stops claiming a refusal that does not exist.

## What does not change, and is parked rather than answered

**Whether `Phase::of` should refuse a zero denominator is a design question and
this round does not answer it.** There is a real argument for it under
`ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path`: a const-evaluated refusal
is not a runtime check, and `Ambient::ADMITTED` already refuses a radix below two
by exactly that route. There is a real argument against, which is that the
predicates are already total over the case and an obligation already removes it
from binaries, so a second mechanism buys nothing.

That is a question for the canon, not for a docs round, and it goes to the
agenda. Writing the honest sentence now costs nothing if the answer later turns
out to be that the constructor should refuse, because the sentence would change
again either way.
