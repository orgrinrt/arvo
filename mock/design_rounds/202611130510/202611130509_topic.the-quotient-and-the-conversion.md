# Topic: the quotient and the conversion

**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Round:** step AR6 of the roadmap, its second round, on `work/oracle-fraction-wide`.
**Canon:** `ruling::the_derivation_is_a_placement_and_the_operation_set_is_an_admission_rule`,
`ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon`, `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path`.

## What is short

The previous round carried the remainder of an exact position over an unsigned 128-bit denominator,
so a position whose remainder is over a 64-bit member now has somewhere to land. Nothing forms one
yet. The crate has a sum, a negation, a difference and a product, and no quotient and no way to move a
value from one declared signature's grid onto another's. The canon is silent on both operations by
name: a search of the registry for division, quotient, divisor and conversion finds the probes that
used division as an instrument and no row about the operation itself. So both are derived from the
admission rule, and each stance this round takes is recorded as a proposal at one expert.

## The quotient, and why its divisor is non-zero by type

Two members at a constant quantum `q` are `(phase + a) q` and `(phase + b) q`. Their quotient is the
dimensionless `(phase + a) / (phase + b)`, which is the member at position
`(phase + a) / ((phase + b) q) - phase`. With the phase `n / d` in lowest terms and `u = r^max(e, 0)`,
`v = r^max(-e, 0)`, that position is the numerator `d (n + a d) v - n (n + b d) u` over the
denominator `d (n + b d) u`, both negated when `n + b d` is below zero so the denominator is positive.
It is a function of the declared signature at every phase and radix wherever `n + b d` is not zero, so
by the admission rule it is admitted there and the zero divisor is the one case it is not a function
at all.

How the zero is kept out is where the canon binds hardest. `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path`
rules out a test on the divisor's value, and so does any constructor that classifies a slot at run
time and hands back a maybe. What is left is a type whose values cannot be zero. That type is a
divisor of one format, `Divisor<F>`, and it has two doors, both decided before the program runs.

A constant divisor: `Divisor::<F>::constant::<B>()` takes the slot as a const parameter, and an
obligation refuses at build time a `B` outside the range and a `B` that is the format's zero member.
Its compile-fail fixture pairs a zero with a one.

A runtime slot of a format with no zero member: `Divisor::<F>::of(slot)` is admitted exactly when no
member of `F` is zero. The phase being in lowest terms, `n + b d` is zero only at `b = -n / d`, an
integer only when `d` is one, so the format has no zero member when its phase denominator is past one
or when `-n` is outside its slot range. A half-offset grid, and a consumer's slot range that starts at
one, both qualify; a plain integer range does not, and divides by a runtime value only once the
consumer has declared the range that excludes the zero. That is the compromise this stance carries:
the common case of dividing two integers read at run time needs the consumer to state a range without
the zero, and a format whose range holds it divides only by constants.

A slot that is not a member is still a slot, and `of` takes it. The step answers for it the way the
product answers for one, saturating at the end of the index on the position's sign, and for the one
non-member slot that names the zero, which exists only when the phase denominator is one and `-n` lies
outside the range, the position is saturated toward positive. This is a branch in the step over a
non-member input, the same kind the product already takes, and no validation of a member.

What the obligation asks besides. A phase that denotes, as every operation asks, refused by design.
A constant quantum, not yet admitted for the reason the sum and the product give. A denominator
`d |n + b d| u` up to `2^128 - 1` at every divisor in range, which reads the larger of the two range
ends since `|n + b d|` is affine in `b`. And every quotient of two members a position the slot index
holds. For a fixed divisor the position is affine and monotone in `a`, and for a fixed dividend it is
monotone in `b` on each side of the pole at `b = -n / d`, so its extremes over the members are at the
dividend's two range ends against the divisor's two range ends and the two members nearest the pole on
either side. Those eight corners, each counted one slot past where it sits when it is off the grid,
decide it.

## The conversion, one step onto another grid

A member of `F` at slot `a` is `(n_F / d_F + a) q_F`. On the grid of `T` it is at position
`(n_F / d_F + a) q_F / q_T - n_T / d_T`, and with `U = r^max(e, 0)` and `V = r^max(-e, 0)` per format
that is the numerator `(n_F + a d_F) U_F V_T d_T - n_T d_F V_F U_T` over `d_F V_F U_T d_T`. The radixes
may differ: a radix ten format moved onto a radix two grid is the same formula with each `r` its own.
The position is a function of the source format and the target signature, so `convert` is admitted by
the same rule, as one exact step and one adaptation under the target's signature. The denominator is
a constant of the pair, the position is affine and increasing in `a`, and the two range ends decide
whether the slot index holds every converted member.

Single step is the stance. A conversion is one placement onto the target's grid, never a chain through
an intermediate format, because two adaptations round twice and the second rounding reads a value the
first already moved. A chain is a composition a consumer can write from two calls, and its double
rounding is then visible at the call site rather than hidden inside one name.

What the obligation asks: both phases denote, both quanta constant, the pair's denominator up to
`2^128 - 1`, and both range ends held. As with the product, only the first is a refusal by design.

## The proof the step owes

The brief asks for three things: every existing fraction test unchanged, quotient positions at the
divisor's extremes, and `law::the_multiplicative_inverse_round_trip`. The first holds by construction,
since this round adds and changes no existing test. The second is the eight-corner sweep at widths
nine to 64 against the shared wide oracle. The third is a sweep through `mul` under wrapping at
unsigned widths one to 63 at exponent zero, with the inverse of three reduced to the width. The row
states truth through width 65 and failure at 128. Width 64 at exponent zero is held out by the
product's slot-coordinate placeholder, and 65 and 128 have no slot range in this crate, so those
three cells are not reached here, and a ticket carries them.

Besides those, every member pair at widths one to eight for both operations, every mode and shipped
policy, against the applied map's oracle fed the exact rational position from `Ratio`. A planted
mutant through the sweep machinery, a quotient step that forgets the phase's term, has to be caught by
the same oracle. And the compile-fail pairs: a zero constant divisor beside a one, a magnitude-indexed
quantum on each operation beside a constant one, and a conversion whose denominator passes
`2^128 - 1` beside one that fits.

## Reuse

The product, the quotient and the conversion all end the same way: a numerator in four limbs, divided
once by a denominator under `2^128`, placed at a slot with a reduced remainder or saturated past the
index, and a corner counted held when the index holds it and one slot past. That ending moves out of
`multiplication.rs` into a crate-private module the three share, with the reduced phase it starts from.
