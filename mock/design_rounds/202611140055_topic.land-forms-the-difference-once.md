## The cancellation, and the difference formed once

`land` formed the position at each magnitude as `num q radix^(t + c) - p den radix^(b + c)` over
`den q radix^c`. On a falling law with a phase both terms grow with the magnitude's fineness while their
difference stays the point's distance from the phase, so at a fine magnitude both terms leave the four
limbs although the position is small, and the walk passed that magnitude and landed on a coarser one.
The review's point is `1/2 + 1/(2^128 + 2)` on a falling law of 300 magnitudes, `BASE` zero, phase a half,
slots 8 bits: it lands at magnitude 128 on slot 0, where magnitude 135 holds it at slot 127.

Every magnitude's numerator is one difference times a nonnegative power of the radix. With
`e = min(power, BASE)`, or `power` where the phase is zero, the difference
`K = num q radix^(power - e) - p den radix^(BASE - e)` is the point's distance from the phase's point over
`D0 = den q radix^-e`, and at the magnitude of step `radix^s`, with `m = e - s`, the position is
`K radix^max(m, 0)` over `D0 radix^max(-m, 0)`. So `K` is formed once per landing, and no magnitude's
numerator holds two terms that cancel. That is the predicate read as one quantity, `x - phase`, which is
what it is, and not a rearrangement made for the code.

Where `K` itself leaves the limbs, one term left while the other is under `2^255`, so `K` is at least
`2^255` in magnitude on that term's side; past the index at a magnitude then follows where the divisor
times the range's largest magnitude plus one is under `2^255`, which reads the range and not the whole
index. The same holds of a numerator `K radix^m` that leaves the limbs.

A magnitude whose position is neither formed nor provably past ends the walk with nothing. Passing it
was the defect: a magnitude the four limbs cannot read may hold the point, and a coarser one is then not
the finest in range. The limit is the four-limb remainder's, under
`arvo_s_exact_position_carries_no_remainder_denominator_past`, and a test now asserts the nothing.

## Where the walk starts, from the difference

The walk-start bound read the numerator's bits and kept a falling law with a phase from skipping,
because there the phase's term grew with the magnitude. With `K` formed once there is no phase term
apart from the difference: `|position| >= 2^(bits(K) - 1) radix^m / 2^bits(D0)` at `m >= 0`, and with
`radix^m >= 2^(m L)` for `L` the floor of `log2 radix` at `m >= 0` and its ceiling below, the same closed
form names the first magnitude below the shift at which the position passes twice the range. Every law
skips the same way, falling and phased included. A zero `K` is the phase's own point, slot zero at every
magnitude, and is walked from the finest.

## Wrapping past the index

Under wrapping on a constant quantum, a point past the 128-bit index landed at the index's end and
`adapt` wrapped that end, not the point: `2^130` on `Integer<4>` wraps to slot 0, and the end wraps to
-1. Found while reading the review's second finding, not by the review. The residue modulo the range's
count is what wrapping reads, and the four limbs hold the floor, so where the floor is past the index and
the policy wraps, the floor is reduced modulo the count onto the range, the remainder kept, before
`adapt`. Where the floor is not formed the residue is not known, and under wrapping that is nothing.

is read as the admitted ratio with no branch for a zero denominator, and a compile-fail pair holds it.
