## Research: what the documents currently say

Recorded before any question is put, because the answer to "what do the docs say" turned out to be
the first finding.

**The shipped documents describe the pre-restructure taxonomy and nothing else.** `mock/DESIGN.md.tmpl`
lists fifteen crates in a layer table: `arvo-transparent` and `arvo-strategy` at L0a, `arvo-storage` at
L0, three contracts crates at L0.5, four crates at L1 including `arvo-refit`, four at L2, one at L3.

None of the previous round's decisions appear. `arvo-capacity`, `arvo-shape`, `arvo-numeric`,
`arvo-geom`, `arvo-algebra-contracts`, `arvo-algebra`, `arvo-num-systems`, `arvo-platform`,
`arvo-container`, `arvo-bitfield`, `arvo-float` and `arvo-pseudorand` are absent. `arvo-storage` and
`arvo-refit`, both of which D27 and D28 dissolve, are present and documented as current.

So the documents do not answer this round's questions, and they do not contradict its decisions
either. They describe a state the previous round decided to leave behind and never wrote down. The
doc changelist for this round is therefore large by construction.

**`arvo-strategy`'s documented identity is narrower than what it does.** Its DESIGN says it is "the
strategy-marker and container-projection layer" and defines strategy as "a compile-time tag that picks
the underlying primitive container for a given bit width". Its README repeats the container framing.

The prior round's research established the markers actually bundle three axes: container width policy,
overflow policy, and intermediate-precision policy. D34 had to prise the third apart by necessity to
establish what `Warm` means above 64 bits. So the document describes one of the three things the
marker does, which is why `202607300400` listed the crate's identity as reopened.
