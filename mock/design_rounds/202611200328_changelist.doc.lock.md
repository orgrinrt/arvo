# Doc changelist: the bit tier after its first review

**Date:** 2026-11-20
**Round:** `202611200328`, job `arvo-numeral-doors`. Overhauls `arvo-bits`.
**Topic:** `202611200327_topic.the-bit-tier-after-review.md`, which takes every finding of
`mock/research/202611200325_review-arvo-bits-tier/PASS_1.md`.

## `mock/crates/arvo-bits/DESIGN.md.tmpl`

CHANGE: under the type, `ADMITTED` stays private because it is how `Bits<N>` meets its own bound and every
door onto the type forces it, the width the tier gives back included. The old reason, that only the type's
own constructors force it, goes (topic decisions 2 and 7).

CHANGE: under the bit tier, another container implementing the traits owes the refusal stated for every
implementor (decision 7).

CHANGE: `BitWidth::WIDTH` for `Bits<N>` is given only after `ADMITTED` is forced, so the width of `Bits<0>`
or `Bits<65>` is refused (decision 2).

CHANGE: `BitLogic` and `BitSequence` take `BitWidth` as their supertrait, as `BitAccess` does (decision 7).

ADD: after the refusal paragraph, the refusal as an obligation each implementor owes by an assertion over
its own width, held by its own refusal cases, with `Bits<N>`'s assertion staying private (decision 7).

CHANGE: the law on `N` outside one to sixty-four names its cases through `masked`, `cast` and `WIDTH`
(decision 2).

CHANGE: the tier's laws are tested at every width from one to sixty-four and every index and amount below
it, against the host's operation or a value built one bit at a time (decisions 4 and 6).

CHANGE: the law on `WIDTH` is that the full mask sets `WIDTH.count()` bits, in place of `WIDTH` is `N`
(decision 5).

CHANGE: the refusal cases are each of the six methods taking an index or an amount, in a constant, in a
call at run time through the inherent method and in one through a function generic over the trait, with
one case that must compile so `trybuild` builds (decision 3).

ADD: the five mutant flags and what each breaks (decision 8).
