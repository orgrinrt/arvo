# Topic: the door question closed, and three tests that cannot fail

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`,
`mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_coordinates.rs`,

## The design reports a question that is closed, and declines a choice nobody is asking it to make

`DESIGN.md.tmpl` says what the door may carry out is open, names
`question::what_the_numeric_introduction_door_may_carry_out`, says four options are recorded against
it with the panel as decider, and declines to choose among them because that choice belongs a tier up.

**All of that was right while the question was open. It is closed**, by
`ruling::the_numeric_door_carries_the_coordinate_set_and_the_two_type_bound_is_not_canon` at
`467b91fe`, ratified on two blind instances, and none of the four was the answer. The question's own
`answered` field carries the same:

> None of the four, and the bound they argue over is not canon. `Width` is a count of bits over `u32`,
> and three of the ten constants carry values it cannot hold, which makes the first option
> arithmetically impossible rather than expensive. The two-type bound is a sentence in the crate's
> design and appears nowhere in the canon. What the door carries out is the coordinate set of the
> ratified parameterisation, spelled in types the stack owns.

**So the paragraph is stale in the awkward direction**: it reads as careful restraint and is a report
that a settled thing is unsettled, which is the reading a later round would build on.

## The sentence above it is the one the ruling names by path

`DESIGN.md.tmpl` states the two-type bound as the crate's own design, and the ruling's note names that
line and says the design tier changes before the code under it does. **The bound is not canon and was
never canon**, so the design saying it is the design's own is what has to go, not a qualification
added around it.

## What the ruling does not settle, and this round does not either

**How many types the door carries stays open.** The two derivations disagree: one answers a type per
coordinate, the other more than two and fewer than ten while reserving whether the signed coordinates
share one. Two instances establish their intersection rather than their union, so the count is outside
what was ratified.

The crate ships one per coordinate, which is one of the two live readings. **The design says what the
crate holds and says the count is not settled**, which is a different act from choosing. A one-expert
proposal exists to replace the count with a rule about positions,
`proposal::the_introduction_doors_bound_is_a_position_rule_not_a_count_of_types`, and it is one
instance and is named as open rather than adopted.

## Three tests in this crate cannot fail, and one of them says so in its own doc

Found by reading every test body in the crate rather than the names.

**`the_overflow_inventory_admits_a_member_this_crate_does_not_know_about`**, at
`the_adaptation.rs:69`. Its whole body is `assert_eq!(<DeclaredBound as Overflow>::POLICY,
Policy::Clamp)`, and four lines above, `DeclaredBound`'s own impl sets `const POLICY: Policy =
Policy::Clamp`. **A constant asserted against the literal its own definition sets**, which is the
tautology in its purest form. Its doc comment states the position outright: "If this file compiles,
adding a policy needed no edit to any existing item. That is the assertion; the body is incidental."
The claim in the name, that the crate's own machinery works with a policy declared elsewhere, is
asserted nowhere: nothing runs `DeclaredBound` through `Adapt`, `overflow_of` or `adapt`.

**`the_declared_width_is_read_rather_than_recovered`**, at `the_inventory.rs:172`. Four assertions of
the form `<Unsigned<13> as Slots>::WIDTH == Width::bits(13)`, where the macro that writes the impls
sets `const WIDTH: Width = Width::bits($w)`. The same class, and the file condemns the class six lines
later: "the previous cut of this file asserted a constant against its own literal instead, which could
not." **The property in the name is that the width is read rather than recovered from the bounds**,
and a range whose bounds do not determine its declared width is the only construction that can say so.
One is already in the file: `WidthTooNarrow` runs from zero to a thousand and declares four bits.

**`a_phase_and_a_fraction_do_not_stand_in_for_one_another`**, at `the_coordinates.rs:51`. It builds
`Phase::of(1, 2)` and `Fraction::of(1, 2)` and asserts they agree about both numbers. Two computations
compared against the same computation, on two types that store what they are given, which is asserted
directly in two other arms. **It cannot fail for any implementation of either type that keeps a
positive denominator**, and the property in its name, that neither converts into the other, is pinned
already by `tests/ui/a_phase_is_not_a_fraction.rs`. Its own trailing comment admits the gap: "What is
asserted here is that they agree about the numbers."

**The first two are repaired and the third is deleted.** A tautological test is not a weak test, it
occupies the space where a real one would be noticed missing, and the repair for one whose real claim
is already pinned elsewhere is removal.

## What is not in this round

**The open-inventory clause still runs inside the crate the bare-primitive rule exempts.** The
ruling's note carries that finding and its repair is a format declared in a crate the lints check,
which is a different crate's design and a different round.

## Changes proposed

Correct the door paragraph to what the ruling settled, drop the design's own two-type bound, and say
the count is open without choosing. Repair the two constant-against-literal tests so each asserts the
property its name claims, and delete the third.
