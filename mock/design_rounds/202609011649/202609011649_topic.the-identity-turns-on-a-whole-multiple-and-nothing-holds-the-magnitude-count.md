# Topic: the identity turns on a whole multiple, and nothing holds the magnitude count

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `mock/crates/arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl`, `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/format.rs`,
`mock/crates/arvo-format/src/quantum.rs`, `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/lib.rs`,

## What moved in the canon while this branch was being written

Two rows this branch predates, both read at the commits that carry them because neither is on `dev`:

- `retirement::a_nonzero_phase_removes_the_additive_identity`, at `cef092bb`. It retires the claim
  that a nonzero phase takes the additive identity off the grid, on two instruments that had not read
  each other: a sweep that found the shipped predicate disagreeing with an enumeration on 7375 of
  23800 coordinate tuples, every one a whole-multiple phase, and a run against the shipped crate.
- `proposal::the_additive_identity_is_decided_by_the_phase_being_a_whole_multiple_of_the_quantum`, the
  replacement, at the same commit, with `dimension::phase` and two `probe` rows beside it.

The retirement names where the claim still stands and says who corrects it: "The claim also stands in
the crate's design, its rustdoc and one of its tests, which the design and code tiers correct in their
own order rather than here." This round is that correction on this branch.

## The licence is the first sentence of the ratified row, not the second

`ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon` stamps
`proposal::membership_of_the_representable_set_is_one_affine_predicate`, and that row's `says` carries
both the definition and the claim being retired:

> Membership is one predicate over one parameterisation: an affine slot function, a quantum per
> magnitude and a phase, of which integers, fixed point, scaled integers and floats are points. The
> phase is stated explicitly: a nonzero phase decides whether the identity adaptation ever occurs and
> whether the set carries an additive identity at all.

The second sentence is offered as a consequence of the first and does not follow from it. Derived from
the first alone: membership is `phase + slot * quantum(magnitude)`, the phase is a rational in units of
the quantum at magnitude zero, so at magnitude zero zero is a member exactly when some integer slot
satisfies `slot * quantum = -phase`, which is solvable exactly when the denominator divides the
numerator. A phase of one whole quantum leaves zero on the grid one slot down. Only a fractional part
takes it off.

**So the two sentences of one ratified row disagree with each other, the first is the definition and
the second is a remark derived wrongly from it, and the code follows the remark.** The retirement says
correcting the stamped row itself wants a `ruling` carrying `corrects` on two agreements, which is not
this round's act and is not attempted here. Correcting the code and the design under it is, and it
rests on the definition rather than on the retired remark.

## What the code does

`format.rs:154`:

```rust
pub const fn has_additive_identity<F: Format>() -> Bool {
    F::PHASE
        .is_zero()
        .and(slot_in_range::<F::Slots>(Slot::ZERO))
}
```

`Phase::is_zero` at `format.rs:109` is correctly named and answers a different question from the one
asked of it. Whether the grid sits at zero and whether zero sits on the grid are two questions, and the
phase being a rational over the quantum is exactly why: `Phase::halves(2)` is one whole quantum, is not
zero, and puts zero on the grid at slot `-1`.

The correction on `dev`'s line, `12f91c58`, is one call site because the coordinate is two `i64`
consts there. Here the same defect sits one layer up, behind a type that also makes half of that fix
unnecessary: `Phase::of` normalises the denominator positive, so the `PHASE_DEN == 0` guard that fix
needs has nothing to guard against on this branch.

## The suite could not have seen it, and the reason is the type

`the_predicate.rs:202 a_nonzero_phase_takes_the_additive_identity_off_the_grid` runs
`Biased<7, -2, 1>`, `Biased<13, 0, 1>` and `Biased<31, -8, 3>`. `Biased` spells its phase with
`Phase::halves`, so the denominator is two and every numerator tried is odd. **Every phase the suite
has ever passed to this function is fractional**, so the region where a nonzero phase keeps the
identity is not one the suite narrowly missed, it is one no arm can reach. Fifty-eight tests pass and
the function is wrong.

## Nothing holds the magnitude count, and the same function reads past it

Found while deriving the above. `quantum.rs:171` states an obligation in prose:

> How many distinct magnitudes the law ranges over. One for the constant family, and never zero: a law
> over no magnitudes describes no values.

Nothing holds it. `Slots` carries `ADMITTED`, a const the reading functions force, and its doc names
the reason: an absent check is a thing a later edit can delete while every test at admitted widths
stays green. `Quantum` has the same shape of obligation stated in the same shape of comment and no
`ADMITTED` beside it, so `Indexed<-14, 0>` is writable and admitted.

The consequence reaches this round's function. `contains` conjoins `magnitude_in_range`, so under a law
over no magnitudes nothing is a member; `has_additive_identity` consults the slot range and never the
magnitude range, so it answers that the identity is present. **Two functions in one file disagree about
whether zero is in the set**, and the one that disagrees with `contains` is the one that is not derived
from it.

`proposal::the_concept_is_closed_and_the_inventory_is_open`, ratified through
`ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon`, is what `Slots::ADMITTED` already cites for itself: a new instance
earns admission by supplying the concept's obligations, and closing the concept while opening the
inventory is what makes admission a check rather than a negotiation. An obligation stated in a doc
comment is a negotiation. One of the two contracts that states one has the check.

## The bound the correction has, said rather than left for a reader

The phase is in units of the quantum at magnitude zero, so the divisibility test decides membership at
magnitude zero. Where the quantum shrinks as the magnitude rises, a fractional phase can still be
cancelled higher up: at radix two with `BASE = 0` and `SLOPE = -1` the quantum at magnitude one is a
half, so a phase of a half is cancelled by slot `-1` there. `SLOPE` is an `Exponent` and nothing bounds
its sign, and the trait is open, so the construction is writable.

Neither the retirement nor its replacement reaches this. The replacement's predicate names
`fraction_width: F in -2..=8, swept, as the constant family's exponent`, so under the omission rule it
says nothing about a magnitude-indexed law at all. **This round implements what the canon establishes
and catalogues the rest as a red arm** rather than inventing a decision procedure the canon has not
asked for. The design says which magnitude the answer is about instead of claiming all of them.

## Changes proposed

Derive `has_additive_identity` from `contains` so the magnitude range is consulted rather than
restated, and decide it by the phase being a whole multiple rather than by its being zero. Give
`Quantum` the `ADMITTED` obligation and the verdict function beside it that `Slots` has, so the wrong
construction can live in a test rather than in a comment. Correct the design's two phase paragraphs and
the rustdoc on `PHASE`, `has_additive_identity` and `points::Biased`. Sweep the region the suite could
not reach, keep the shrinking-quantum case as a catalogued red arm, and pin the magnitude-count case
that made the two functions disagree.

## Appended: the quantum law owes a second condition, and it is stated nowhere

Found while writing the first one, which is why it arrives after the section proposing it.

`Exponent::advanced` is the step law's whole arithmetic and computes `base + rate * magnitude` in the
exponent's own width, over a magnitude index cast down from the count's. Both narrow. A law declaring a
rate and a count that between them run the exponent past what it carries wraps twice over: the index
cast wraps first, and the sum wraps after it, and the answers come back inside the range with nothing
saying they are wrong. Under `overflow-checks` the second one panics instead, which is a runtime
failure on a path `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path` says nothing may reach.

**This is the slot range's own history one contract over.** That obligation once checked only that the
range was not inverted and that the width was in range, admitted a span of two to the sixty-third, and
`slot_count` then panicked under `overflow-checks` and wrapped without it. The repair there was a
condition computed in a domain wide enough that the obligation cannot overflow while checking that the
thing it is about does not, and this is the same condition on the same shape of quantity.

So the quantum law owes two conditions rather than one: at least one magnitude, and an exponent at the
largest admitted magnitude that an `Exponent` carries. Shipping the first alone would be a half
obligation, which is the shape the slot range already had to back out of once, and the round is
cheaper done whole. The doc changelist was unlocked to say so before any source was written under it.
