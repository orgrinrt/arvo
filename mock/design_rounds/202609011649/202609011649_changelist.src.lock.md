**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Round:** the identity turns on a whole multiple, and nothing holds the magnitude count
**Scope:** `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/format.rs`, `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/quantum.rs`,
`mock/crates/arvo-format/src/lib.rs`, `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests.rs`,

## CHANGE: `has_additive_identity` FROM its own conjunction TO `contains` at the cancelling slot
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/format.rs`
Verification: `has_additive_identity::<Biased<7, -2, 2>>()` is true and was false;
`has_additive_identity::<Biased<7, -2, 1>>()` is false in both. Asserted in
`a_whole_multiple_phase_keeps_the_identity_at_a_shifted_slot`, whose control runs the superseded
predicate against all four arms and gets no on every one.

Zero is a member exactly when some admitted slot cancels the phase, so the answer is the membership
predicate at the position zero would occupy. The phase is a rational in units of the quantum at
magnitude zero, so cancelling it needs a whole number of quanta, and the slot it lands on is the
negated multiple rather than slot zero.

**The quotient is taken in a domain wide enough to hold its own negation.** `Phase::of(i64::MIN, 1)`
is writable and the slot cancelling it is one past what an index carries, so taking the negation in
the index's own width would wrap it into a slot some range admits. Only the top can be out of reach,
because the negated quotient runs from `-i64::MAX` to `2^63`, so the guard names one direction rather
than two and there is no unreachable arm beside it.

## CHANGE: `Phase` FROM `is_zero` alone TO `is_zero` and `is_whole_multiple`
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/format.rs`
Verification: `grep -c "pub const fn is_whole_multiple" mock/crates/arvo-format/src/format.rs`
returns one. `Phase::halves(2).is_whole_multiple()` is true and `Phase::halves(2).is_zero()` is
false, asserted in `the_two_questions_about_a_phase_are_two_questions`.

`is_zero` was correctly named and asked in the wrong place. Whether the grid sits at zero and whether
zero sits on the grid are two questions, and the coordinate being a ratio is exactly why they come
apart: one whole step is not zero and shifts the grid onto itself. Both stay, because both are real
questions to ask a phase, and the one that decides the identity now has its own name.

## CHANGE: `Quantum` FROM three consts TO three consts and `ADMITTED`
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/quantum.rs`
Verification: `grep -c "const ADMITTED" mock/crates/arvo-format/src/quantum.rs` returns one, and the
two `tests/ui/` cases record the build failures, each with the message of the condition it trips.

Two conditions, and neither had anything holding it. A law ranges over at least one magnitude,
because a law over none describes no values, which was a doc comment. And the exponent it reaches at
its largest admitted magnitude is one an `Exponent` can carry, which was stated nowhere: `advanced`
computes `base + rate * magnitude` in the exponent's own width over an index cast down from the
count's, so a law running past that wraps in the cast and again in the sum, or panics under
`overflow-checks`.

**The reach is computed one domain wider than the coordinates currently need**, and the reason is not
that the narrower one overflows. It does not: at the present widths a signed 64-bit integer holds
every product a caller can declare, with `2^31` to spare at the worst corner, which
`the_reach_check_is_wider_than_the_widths_currently_need` computes rather than asserts. **An earlier
draft of this changelist claimed the opposite**, that the corner runs three hundred or so past the
bottom of that width, which is wrong by two to the thirty-first and was caught by working the
arithmetic instead of restating it. What the margin actually is, is an argument over the exact widths
of three coordinate types rather than a property of the check, so widening any one of them would
break the narrower form silently while every test still passed. That, and matching the shape the slot
range's own count condition already has, is the reason.

## CHANGE: `quantum::is_admissible` FROM absent TO the verdict beside the obligation
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/quantum.rs`
Verification: `is_admissible::<Constant<0>>()` is true across fifteen exponents including both ends
of the type, `is_admissible::<Indexed<-14, 30>>()` is true across six shapes, and `NoMagnitudes`,
`ReachRunsOff` and `MagnitudesBeyondTheIndex` are all refused. Asserted in
`the_law_rejects_a_quantum_law_that_does_not_meet_the_contract`,
`the_law_admits_every_quantum_law_this_crate_ships` and
`the_quantum_law_separates_the_two_constructions_rather_than_answering_one_way`.

The shape `Slots` already has: the same question asked without forcing the const, so a construction
that compiles and is wrong is reported on rather than refusing the build, which is what lets it live
permanently in a test.

**The two conditions are written once and read twice.** `Slots` states its five conditions in the
obligation and states them again in the verdict, and nothing anywhere asserts the two agree, so a
construction tripping one and not the other would be refused by the build and admitted by the law
with no test able to see it. Here each condition is one private predicate over the coordinates rather
than over the type, which keeps the obligation free of a `Self: Sized` bound it would otherwise need,
and the obligation and the verdict are two readings of the same two predicates.

**The `Slots` duplication is not repaired here.** It is a different contract, it is not what this
round is about, and repairing it is a source change with no design sentence under it yet.

## CHANGE: the quantum reading functions FROM reading the law TO forcing its obligation first
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/quantum.rs`
Verification: `grep -c "let () = Q::ADMITTED;" mock/crates/arvo-format/src/quantum.rs` returns
three, one each in `exponent_at`, `magnitude_in_range` and `is_constant_family`, and zero in
`is_admissible`.

Reading an associated const does not force the contract's obligation on its own, which is the
sentence the slot range already carries. `contains` conjoins `magnitude_in_range`, so a format whose
law is inadmissible now stops the build at the use site rather than answering.

## CHANGE: the rustdoc on `PHASE`, `has_additive_identity`, `Biased` and `MAGNITUDES` FROM the retired claim TO the whole-multiple one
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/format.rs`, `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/lib.rs`,
`mock/crates/arvo-format/src/quantum.rs`
Verification: `grep -rc "nonzero phase" mock/crates/arvo-format/src/` returns zero on every file.

`retirement::a_nonzero_phase_removes_the_additive_identity` names the crate's rustdoc as one of the
places the claim stands. `Biased` gains what its own parameter means for this: the denominator is
fixed at two, so an odd count is the half-step grid with no identity and an even one is a whole step
that keeps it.

## CHANGE: the identity arms FROM a section of the predicate's file TO `tests/the_identity.rs`

The predicate's file had reached five hundred lines with the new arms in it, and the identity is its

## CHANGE: `a_nonzero_phase_takes_the_additive_identity_off_the_grid` FROM half the space TO both halves
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_identity.rs`
Verification: the renamed arm plus `a_whole_multiple_phase_keeps_the_identity_at_a_shifted_slot` and
`the_superseded_predicate_differs_exactly_at_a_nonzero_whole_multiple`.

Every phase the old arm passed was an odd numerator over a denominator `Biased` fixes at two, so
every phase the suite had ever tried was fractional and the region where the identity survives was
not one it narrowly missed. It is renamed for what it actually covers and the other half is written
beside it.

## CHANGE: the two mutants FROM described in prose TO run as functions
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_identity.rs`
Verification: `the_superseded_predicate` and `the_narrow_cancelling_slot` are `const fn`s in the
file, and the arms assert where each disagrees with the shipped predicate.

A control nobody runs is a claim about a mutant nobody built. The predicate this round replaced is
kept as a function, so "the old suite could not have seen this" is an assertion; and so is the
negation taken in the index's own width, so "the wide intermediate is load-bearing" is one too. The
second is asserted against a slot range declared at the bottom of the index on purpose, because a
wrap to `i64::MIN` is only observable in a range that admits it.

**The disagreement is stated once rather than as a list of arms.** The two predicates part company
exactly at a nonzero whole multiple the range can cancel, and agree everywhere else, which is what
`the_superseded_predicate_differs_exactly_at_a_nonzero_whole_multiple` asserts with both outcomes
counted so neither side of it can be empty.

## CHANGE: the identity's agreement with `contains` FROM unasserted TO a law over a phase sweep
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_identity.rs`
Verification: `the_identity_agrees_with_membership_at_the_slot_it_names` computes the cancelling slot
independently of the crate and asserts `contains` there matches the predicate, over eighteen phases
either side of the edge where `Signed<9>` stops holding the slot, with both outcomes counted.

The two disagreed, which is the whole finding, so the agreement is a law now rather than a property
of how the function happens to be written.

## CHANGE: the shrinking-quantum case FROM absent TO a catalogued red arm
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_identity.rs`
Verification: `the_identity_survives_a_shrinking_quantum` is marked `#[ignore = "catalogue: ..."]`
with the gap and its reason, and `cargo test -- --ignored` fails it on
`has_additive_identity::<HalfOnShrinking>()` with the two geometry assertions above it passing.

A law whose step shrinks as the magnitude rises cancels a fractional phase higher up: at radix two
with intercept zero and rate minus one, the quantum at magnitude one is a half, so a phase of a half
is cancelled by the slot below it. The predicate answers at magnitude zero and does not look there.
**The canon does not reach this case**, since the replacement finding's predicate names its fraction
width as the constant family's exponent, so implementing a decision procedure for it here would be
inventing one, and the honest state is the assertion standing and failing.

## CHANGE: the quantum law's wrong constructions FROM nothing TO permanent tests
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_inventory.rs`
Verification: `NoMagnitudes`, `ReachRunsOff` and `MagnitudesBeyondTheIndex` are declared in the file,
all three compile, all three are refused, and the separation arm asserts each trips one condition
while passing the other.

The three constructions that meet the concept's shape and not its obligations, kept where they can be
read rather than in a scratch file. **The case that made the two functions disagree is now
unrepresentable rather than merely wrong**: a format over a law with no magnitudes does not build,
which is a stronger statement than a test asserting the two agree about it.

## CHANGE: the refusals FROM a runtime claim TO two `trybuild` cases
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/tests/compile_fail.rs`, `mock/crates/arvo-format/tests/ui/`
Verification: both `.stderr` files record `error[E0080]: evaluation panicked` naming the condition's
own message, and each names a different one.

The obligation fires where it is forced, so a runtime test cannot catch it and a claim that it
refuses would be a sentence about something nothing checks. Forcing it in a const context is what
makes the refusal reachable at check time rather than only at codegen, which is the qualification the
obligation states about itself, and `trybuild` holds both files.

## CHANGE: the inventory test's phase comment FROM nonzero TO fractional, with the assertion beside it
File: `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_inventory.rs`
Verification: `grep -c "The phase is nonzero" mock/crates/arvo-format/src/tests/the_inventory.rs`
returns zero, and the arm asserts `!PHASE.is_whole_multiple()` rather than leaving the reason in a
comment.

`WhollyForeign` carries `Phase::of(1, 3)`, which has no identity because it is fractional and not
because it is nonzero. The assertion was right and the reason beside it was the retired one, so the
reason is an assertion now.
