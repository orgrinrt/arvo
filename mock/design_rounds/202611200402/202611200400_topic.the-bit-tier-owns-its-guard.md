## The decisions

1. The refusal cases at the edges. Beside the cases at `Bits<8>` and index eight go: `Bits<1>` at index one for `bit` and `with_bit`; `Bits<64>` at sixty-four for all six methods taking an index or an amount, where the host's shift by the carrier's width lives; `bit::<{ u32::MAX }>`; and `shift_down::<9>` at `Bits<8>`, past the width rather than at it. A refusal moved to `I != N` or to any one width then turns a case red.

2. The tier owns the guard. The refusal of an index or an amount at or past the width stops being an obligation each implementor owes and becomes the tier's, once, sealed so no implementor can go round it. The sketch holds the shape building and refusing on the pinned nightly.

   `Within<B, const I: u32>` is a proof that `I` is below the width of the container `B`, with a private field, made only by its constant `NEW`, which asserts `I < B::WIDTH.count()`. It replaces the private `Within<N, I>` of `src/ops.rs`, so the guard of `Bits<N>` and of every other container is the one constant.

   Each of the two traits taking an index or an amount splits into the side an implementor writes and the side a caller names. `BitAccessAt` has `bit_at` and `with_bit_at`, each taking `Within<Self, I>`; `BitSequenceBy` has `shift_up_by`, `shift_down_by`, `rotate_up_by` and `rotate_down_by`, each taking `Within<Self, K>`. `BitAccess` and `BitSequence` keep the caller's shape arvo-contracts gave, the index or the amount a const generic alone, and each has its implementor's trait as its supertrait and one impl, over every implementor, that makes the proof and calls through. An impl of `BitAccess` or `BitSequence` by hand overlaps that impl or lacks the supertrait, so it does not build. `BitWidth` and `BitLogic` are unchanged.

   The inherent methods of `Bits<N>` keep their const-generic shape and force `Within::<Self, I>::NEW`, which reads `WIDTH`, which forces `ADMITTED`, so every door onto `Bits<N>` still refuses a bad `N`. `Bits<N>`'s impls of `BitAccessAt` and `BitSequenceBy` call the inherent method of the same operation and do nothing else.

   Pinned by refusal cases of a container written in the case with no assertion of its own, refused past its width in a call at run time and through a `[const]` bound in a constant, by an impl of `BitAccess` by hand, and by a proof built by hand; and by a second case that must build, that container at its last index.

3. The index mutant. `arvo_mutant_bits_index` moves into `Within::NEW`. The comment and the design stop saying the `compile_fail` cases catch it: trybuild removes `RUSTFLAGS` from its own build (`trybuild_drops_rustflags_so_a_cfg_mutant_never_reaches_a_com`), so the red run builds the index cases as examples of a sketch crate, as before, now every index case.

4. A mutant for every operation. Seven flags join the five: `arvo_mutant_bits_bit` reads whether any bit at or above `I` is set, `arvo_mutant_bits_and` gives the and of the left with the complement of the right, `arvo_mutant_bits_or` gives the exclusive or, `arvo_mutant_bits_xor` gives the or, `arvo_mutant_bits_is_empty` reads the low thirty-two bits only, `arvo_mutant_bits_shift_down` rotates rather than shifts, and `arvo_mutant_bits_rotate_down` rotates within the carrier and masks. The red run takes all twelve.

5. Redundant assertions. Over the values each sweep takes, every algebraic law the tests assert apart, the involution of `not`, `xor` with itself, commuting, associating, De Morgan, the intersection test, reading back a written bit, writing a bit to what it is, a rotation keeping its count, the round trips and the rotation down by `N - K`, follows from the equality with the host's operation or the bit-by-bit oracle, and none can fail first. They are deleted. The design states the laws as the oracle equalities and says the algebra is the host's and follows from them.

6. The lowering. The design's sentence claiming one instruction on the 64-bit carrier takes the note's predicate: at `Bits<64>`, on `x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu` with its default target features, under `nightly-2026-05-28` and the bench harness's release profile, across a C boundary. The note's predicate gains the toolchain and the target features. Nothing is said below sixty-four bits.
