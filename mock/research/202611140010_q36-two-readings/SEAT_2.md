# Q36, seat 2: whose reduction governs a lossy crossing

## Pick

option 2

Standing of this pick: one seat, so one expert. A second read that derives before reading this one is owed before it is anything more. The pick has a forced part and a derived part and they are kept apart below. The forced part rests on quoted ratified rows. The derived part is my reasoning from the mathematics where the ratified rows are silent, and it is a reading, not canon.

The pick is option 2 in one precise sense, and the sense matters. The reduction that governs a lossy crossing is the adaptation of the signature the crossing names as its target. That signature is written at the crossing site. So option 3, a reduction named at the crossing site, is not a rival to option 2: it is option 2 at a sibling signature of the destination, followed by a crossing between siblings that loses nothing. Option 1 is refused as the rule and survives as something a consumer can spell.

Gates run before the work. The canon gate: the question is an open `question` row with `decider = "panel"` and no `answered` field (`mock/registry/question.toml:782-794`), so reading it is licensed and no ratified row is reopened by doing so. The test gate: the whole suite was run, crate by crate because the workspace run was refused for disk room. It is red, with one failure in `arvo-format`, and the conversion tests sample the one coordinate this question is about. Both are reported under the last heading. I proceeded because the expected value those tests would have to assert is the answer to this question, so they cannot be repaired ahead of it.

## Region

Where the pick holds, and on what evidence.

Kinds of crossing examined, by a probe and by argument: a change of width (signed 8 bits onto signed 4 bits, and the chains 8, 6, 4 signed and unsigned); a change of signedness at one width (signed 8 onto unsigned 8); a change of exponent on a radix 2 grid (quantum 1/32 onto 1/8 onto 1/2, and eighths onto integers). Overflow kinds wrap and saturate. Rounding modes toward_zero, floor, ceil, half_up and half_even. All at widths of 8 bits and under, integers and fixed point, constant quantum, zero phase, one value at sole occupancy.

Kinds of crossing where the question does not arise: a change of overflow policy alone, and a change of rounding mode alone. Two signatures over one format hold the same members, so such a crossing loses nothing, and the question row scopes itself to lossy crossings (`mock/registry/question.toml:792`).

Kinds reasoned about and not probed: a change of phase and a change of radix. The forced part of the argument does not read either, so I expect it to carry. The composition counts in the probe were not taken there, and wrap composed through windows whose sizes do not divide is known to fail (the probe's own control, a ten member window, breaks on 216 of 256). Treat phase and radix as unexamined for anything that depends on a count.

Not examined at all: a magnitude indexed quantum, which is the float shape; redundant encodings and crossings of the pattern rather than the value; a change of ambient operation family; the stochastic mode, beyond noting what a conversion with no argument does to it; mixed operand resolution, the n-ary case; values at shared placements; compositions such as intervals and stored pair rationals, beyond one witness; ingest of data that carries no arvo signature; widths above 8 bits in the model. I also did not verify what MATLAB `fi` or IEEE 754 document for a conversion between two differently configured types. I had no network and wrote no claim that rests on either.

## Reasoning by option

### What the ratified rows force before any option is weighed

Four ratified sentences bound every option.

First, the format spine. "Arithmetic on a format is an exact operation in the ambient domain composed with a named total adaptation onto that set, and the adaptation is a first-class object with its own laws" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1491`). Whatever governs the loss is therefore an adaptation onto the target's representable set, and it is named.

Second, the datum row. "A datum of a format is one point of the format's ambient domain" and "the adaptation is not part of the format's identity" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1898`). A value in flight is a point. It does not carry a policy. A policy belongs to a signature, and two signatures that differ only in policy are over one format.

Third, the admission rule. "an operation is admitted exactly when it is a function of the declared signature, and where two realisations of one name disagree, the signature is missing a coordinate" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1583`). The panel measured two realisations of "the crossing from A to B" that disagree on 226 of 256 source values (`mock/research/202608072330_the-numeral-canon-panel/71_orchard_what_crosses_between_two_systems.md:314-337`, reproduced in `SEAT_2_probes/out/p1_model.txt` section A). By the ratified rule that is a missing coordinate, and the coordinate is the adaptation. The rule does not say which signature supplies it.

Fourth, the binding time intent. "Never any runtime checks, ever: invalids are caught at compile time and unused paths are cleared when lowered" with branching by "const-time ifs erased by monomorphisation" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:986`). A reduction named at a crossing site is therefore named in a type. A type that names an adaptation onto the target's set is, by the second point, a signature over the target's format. That is a sibling of the target signature.

Put together, this is the collapse. Option 3's "third named at the crossing site" is a signature over the target's format, written at the site. Option 2's "the target's" is the adaptation of the signature written at the site. They are the same mechanism, and they differ only in whether the value is then re-bound, at no loss, to another sibling. The ratified rows force that much. They do not force the choice between that mechanism and option 1.

One more ratified sentence bears, loosely by its own account. On a release build what stands in place of the panic is "an explicit declaration of the mode, saturate or wrap or whatever applies to the overflow or underflow at hand" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1095`). The row says of itself that the intent is deliberately loose, so I lean on it only as direction: the mode is declared, and it is declared for the overflow at hand.

### Option 1, the source's

Ratified text for it: none that I could find. The nearest is the spine's phrase that an adaptation has "its own laws" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1491`), which a defender could read as the source system's laws travelling with the value. The datum row closes that reading: the value is a point and the adaptation is not part of its format's identity (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1898`).

Ratified text against it, in three steps.

The option's own sentence is "a value finishes being a value of its system before it becomes one of another" (`mock/registry/question.toml:786`). A member of the source's set is already finished. The source's adaptation is a total map onto the source's set, so on a member it is the identity. Read literally the option does nothing, and the loss is still there to be handled. The only reading with content is that the kind of the source's reduction, wrap or saturate, a rounding mode, is carried over and instantiated at the target's set. That is a new object: an adaptation onto the target's set that the target's signature did not declare and the source's signature never stated a law for. The ratified row on constructions says a predicate "must be derived per construction" and is never transported for free (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1856`). That row is about constructions and not about crossings, so I cite it as kin and not as a verdict. The kinship is exact though: admission of a signature says nothing about its sibling.

Second, option 1 is not total over the crossings arvo has. The spine's own adaptation is a lossy crossing whose source is an exact result in the ambient domain, a point with no signature and no selection. A constant, a host integer at the door, and an exact intermediate are the same. Where the source has selected nothing, option 1 has no answer. Option 2 always has one, because a target that is a signature has an adaptation by definition.

Third, option 1 breaks the spine's factoring when a consumer widens. Take two signed 4 bit members, multiply them exactly in a signed 8 bit system, and narrow back. The exact product always fits in 8 bits, so the wide system's reduction never fires: 0 of 512 cases in `SEAT_2_probes/out/p1_model.txt` section E. Under option 1 the narrowing is governed by the wide system's kind anyway, and it misses the result the spine defines for the 4 bit product on 145 of 256 operand pairs whenever the wide kind differs from the result's kind. A label that never acted decides the answer. Under option 2 the route is the spine's sentence restated, which is analytic and is marked so in the probe; I do not count it as a measurement. The widening intermediate is the common case in the panel's own material, as the reply to the source file says (`mock/research/202608072330_the-numeral-canon-panel/72_kiselyov_reply.md:190-196`).

What it does to a consumer. The reduction applied at a crossing is decided wherever the value was produced, which can be far from the site. A reader of the crossing sees a target signature that says saturate and gets a wrap. The stated cost in the row, that it "reads badly at an ingest boundary" (`mock/registry/question.toml:786`), understates it: at an ingest boundary the source has no arvo selection at all, so the option is undefined there, not awkward.

What survives of option 1. Its motivating case is real. A wide wrapping value, a hash or a sequence number, narrowed into a saturating type should usually keep its low bits. A consumer who means that can say it, and the shipped doors already spell it (claim about code, `SEAT_2_probes/src/bin/p2_shipped.rs` section 2, output in `SEAT_2_probes/out/p2_shipped.txt`): convert onto the destination's wrapping sibling, then cross to the destination. Both routes written from the one door differ on 226 of 256 members, which is the question row's own figure. So option 1 is a spelling, not a rule.

### Option 2, the target's

Ratified text for it. The spine names the adaptation as onto "that set", the set of the format the result is in (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1491`), and behaviour "is stated per declared signature" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1514`). In arithmetic the result's signature supplies the adaptation. A conversion is the unary case of the same shape with the identity as its exact operation, which is how the source panel file itself frames it, "a conversion is the unary case with the target declared" (`mock/research/202608072330_the-numeral-canon-panel/71_orchard_what_crosses_between_two_systems.md:393-397`; unratified, cited for the framing only). No ratified row says in terms that a conversion is an instance of the spine's factoring. That step is mine, and it is the derived part of the pick.

Ratified text against it: none found. The row's stated cost is that "a value can be adapted by a policy its own system never selected" (`mock/registry/question.toml:787`). Against the datum row that cost is weaker than it reads. A point has no system that travels with it (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1898`). What the consumer selected for the source governs operations that produce source values. It was never a statement about what happens when the value leaves.

Mathematics.

Totality: argued above. It is the one rule of the two that answers at every crossing, including the ones whose source is a bare point.

Composition does not separate the two endpoint rules for overflow. Through nested windows 8, 6, 4, each rule composes, meaning two steps equal one, at exactly 4 of the 8 kind assignments, signed and unsigned alike (`SEAT_2_probes/out/p1_model.txt` section C). I state that plainly because it is a negative result: anyone hoping composition picks a winner between options 1 and 2 on overflow will not get it.

Composition does separate them, slightly, for rounding. I predicted equal counts and the probe refuted me: over the 125 mode assignments on the chain 1/32, 1/8, 1/2, the source's rule composes at 15 and the target's at 20 (section D). The extra case is floor followed by half_up, which equals half_up in one step, because half_up as the ratified row defines it is `floor(x + q/2)` (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1744`) and a floor taken first is absorbed by it. Nothing is absorbed by an earlier reduction. The general statement is the source file's absorption condition (`mock/research/202608072330_the-numeral-canon-panel/71_orchard_what_crosses_between_two_systems.md:376-383`): a later reduction can absorb an earlier one, never the reverse, and only the target's rule has the later reduction as its one step answer. It is a small asymmetry and I do not rest the pick on it.

What it does to a consumer. The crossing site shows the reduction, because the site shows the target signature. The failure the row names, a wide wrapping value that saturates, is real under option 2 for a consumer who writes the destination signature without thinking. Two things bound it. The ratified overflow row makes a debug panic the go-to for what the typestate cannot catch (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1095`), and the shipped conversion does abort in a debug build on a member that leaves the target's range unless the consumer names the quiet check (claim about code, `mock/crates/arvo-numeral/src/ratio.rs:84-91`). And the other intent is one sibling away.

### Option 3, a third named at the crossing site

Ratified text for it. The adaptation is "named" and "a first-class object" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1491`); a disagreement between realisations means "the signature is missing a coordinate" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1583`); the mode is declared for the overflow "at hand" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1095`). All three point at a reduction that is visible where the crossing is written. One stated row, not ratified, points the same way: the overflow behaviour "is not called one way. A default ships and is easy to override per case" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1932`). And the ratified row on exchange rates rejects a single generalisation over a category in favour of a recorded reason per arm (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1709`).

Ratified text against it: none against the capability. Against the form the row gives it, a separate third thing with "no default for a consumer who writes nothing" (`mock/registry/question.toml:788`), the binding time intent applies: the name must be a type (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:986`), and a type naming an adaptation onto the target's set is a signature over the target's format. So there is no third thing to add. The site already carries a signature, and that signature is the name.

The distinguisher the row asks for, a crossing whose correct answer differs from both endpoints' own selections, exists, and I give two. Requantising a fixed point value computed under floor into a coarser type that also computes under floor is commonly done under a nearest mode at that one site, to keep bias out of the output; the shipped doors spell it and it differs from both endpoints on 112 of 256 members (`SEAT_2_probes/out/p2_shipped.txt`). And an interval crossing onto a coarser grid must take its lower bound by floor and its upper by ceil. The two components have the same source format and the same target format, so no rule that reads only the endpoints' formats can serve both; every single mode fails to enclose on at least 2210 of 3321 intervals, and floor below with ceil above fails on none (`SEAT_2_probes/out/p1_model.txt` section F). The ratified row on compositions says such a thing owes its own laws (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1876`), which is the same point from above: the composition names the reduction per component, and it can only do that if the crossing takes a name.

Composition, the one clean result. A single kind named for a whole path composes at 2 of 2 on overflow, against 4 of 8 for either endpoint rule (section C). For rounding a single named mode composes for toward_zero, floor and ceil and not for half_up or half_even (section D), which is the double rounding everyone knows, and it is why the unratified conversion proposal insists on one placement and no chain (`mock/registry/proposal-the-exact-operations.toml:34`). I agree with that sentence and note that it is at one expert.

What it does to a consumer. Read as the row writes it, one more thing at every site. Read as the ratified rows force it, nothing more than the target signature the consumer was already writing, with a sibling when the intent differs from the destination's.

### The composition, and why the pick line says option 2

Options 2 and 3 are one mechanism: the signature named at the crossing governs. Option 2 is the case where that signature is the destination. Option 3 is the case where it is a sibling of the destination. Option 1 is the case where the consumer picks the sibling by copying the source's kind. The question's three options are therefore not three rules. They are one rule and two uses of it. Asked whose reduction governs, the answer that is true at every crossing is the target signature's, so the pick line reads option 2, and I would not object to a reader who filed the same content as a malformed question with this as the well formed one: does the reduction at a lossy crossing belong to the signature the crossing names, and what does a consumer get who names only a format.

The second half of that is the one thing I leave open. The row's cost on option 3, no default for a consumer who writes nothing, is real and is not settled by anything ratified. A stated row gives direction, a generic with a default (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1932`), and which of wrap and clamp the compromise preset names is held open in the lead designer's name (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1078`). I derive nothing from the open row and I do not pick a default.

The route by which this would flip. If a second reader shows a ratified sentence under which a value's signature, and not only its format, is part of what crosses, the datum argument against option 1 weakens and the tie returns. If the canon admits a target set on which one of the overflow kinds has no instance, as the unratified reading in `mock/research/202611132330_wrapping-over-a-union-of-magnitude-ranges/READ-1.md` proposes for wrapping, option 1 loses totality a second way and the pick hardens.

## What a target-only conversion commits to

A conversion method on a value that takes only a target signature and no further argument commits the stack to four things with respect to Q36. They are stated as commitments of that shape, whatever its spelling.

It commits to the reduction being a function of the source's format and the target's signature. The source's own selection is not an input. That excludes option 1 as the rule. As a claim about code: the shipped format tier conversion is typed over a source format and a target signature, with no place for a source signature (`mock/crates/arvo-format/src/conversion.rs:52`, `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/conversion.rs:233-235`), the value tier calls it with the source's format alone (`mock/crates/arvo-numeral/src/ratio.rs:100`), and through the public doors a wrapping source and a saturating source give the same answer on 256 of 256 members (`SEAT_2_probes/out/p2_shipped.txt`).

It does not by itself commit against option 3. Whether option 3 remains reachable depends on three neighbours, none of which is the conversion: that a signature carries its adaptation, that a sibling signature over the destination's format is admitted, and that a crossing between siblings exists that computes nothing. With all three, option 3 is two calls. Without any one of them, a target-only conversion is pure option 2 and forecloses the third. As a claim about code, all three are present today (`mock/crates/arvo-format/src/adapt.rs:84-95`, `mock/crates/arvo-numeral/src/num.rs:190`), and the generic caller pays for the second with a bound it must write out, because one signature's admission says nothing of its sibling's (`mock/crates/arvo-numeral/DESIGN.md.tmpl:433-435`). So the method's commitment on Q36 is carried jointly with the sibling crossing. Remove the sibling crossing and the stack has silently answered Q36 as option 2 with no escape.

It commits to every reachable reduction being nameable in a type with no datum beside it. That shuts out the sixth mode of the ratified vocabulary, `stochastic` (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:1730`), at a value's crossing, since that mode reads a dither and there is no argument to pass one in. As a claim about code, the value tier refuses a stochastic signature outright (`mock/crates/arvo-numeral/src/admit.rs:96-100`) and passes an unused dither (`mock/crates/arvo-numeral/src/ratio.rs:100`), while the format tier's conversion does take one (`mock/crates/arvo-format/src/conversion.rs:233`). Dither at a requantisation is one of the textbook uses of a site named reduction, so this is a real hole in option 3's reach, and it is a consequence of the no further argument shape and of nothing else.

It commits to nothing about the default. A consumer must write a full target signature, adaptation included. What a consumer gets who names only a format is exactly the open half noted above.

## Outside the question

Each of these is something the ratified canon does not license, met on the way. None was fixed, since I was told not to commit and no round is open.

The design and the source answer Q36 while the registry holds it open. `mock/crates/arvo-numeral/DESIGN.md.tmpl:332-334` states that the member "is placed on `T`'s grid by `T`'s rounding and completed by `T`'s policy", and `mock/crates/arvo-numeral/src/ratio.rs:69-101` ships it. The only registry row behind that is `proposal::a_conversion_is_one_placement_onto_the_target_grid`, at `standing = "one_expert"`, whose provenance is a design round and not a panel reading (`mock/registry/proposal-the-exact-operations.toml:29-36`), and the row that carries the question says of it that whose order it is "is unresolved" (`mock/registry/proposal.toml:402`). I happen to think the shipped answer is the right one. That does not make shipping it licensed. An open question was filled inside a design on one agent's word, with no marker at the site saying so: the two `FIXME` lines in that method are about the 128 bit step (`mock/crates/arvo-numeral/src/ratio.rs:95-99`) and neither names Q36. Had this reading gone the other way, the design, the format tier's `Convert` type and both test files would all have been built on nothing.

The conversion tests sample the one coordinate this question turns on. Every source signature in `mock/crates/arvo-numeral/tests/the_conversion.rs` is `Adapt<TowardZero, Wrap>`, fixed inside the macro at `mock/crates/arvo-numeral/tests/the_conversion.rs:107`, across ten target adaptations. The file then asserts in a comment, not in a test, that "The source's adaptation takes no part in a conversion" (`mock/crates/arvo-numeral/tests/the_conversion.rs:85`). That sentence is Q36's option 2 written as settled fact, and the suite never varies the source to find out. A law tested at one of its ten source shapes with the other nine waved through in a comment is a sampled law. My probe ran the missing arm at one cell and it holds there (0 of 256), which is one cell and not the matrix. The test is owed the full source sweep the day Q36 is answered, and until then its comment should say the question is open.

The suite is red. `multiplication::tests::the_inverse_round_trip::the_inverse_of_three_round_trips_at_every_unsigned_width_from_one_to_63` panics with "attempt to subtract with overflow" at `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/multiplication/tests/the_inverse_round_trip.rs:37`, where the helper forms `top - 2` with `top` equal to 1 at width one. The test's name claims widths one to 63 and its own scaffolding cannot survive the first of them, so it asserts nothing at any width. Result of the run: `arvo-format` 301 passed and 1 failed; the other four crates green. Three test binaries in `arvo-numeral` report zero tests in a default debug run because they are gated on a feature or on a release build (`mock/crates/arvo-numeral/tests/the_count.rs:12`, `mock/crates/arvo-numeral/tests/the_debug_check_in_release.rs:12`); a count that cites them as coverage from a plain run is citing nothing.

The question row's distinguisher has a shape a stated row rejects. It asks "whether any consumer writes a crossing whose correct answer differs from both endpoints' own selections" (`mock/registry/question.toml:792`). The stated row on universal answers records that whether some consumer somewhere writes a declaration "is not a precondition for building the arm" (`mock/registry/ruling.toml:944` and its note). That row is at `stated`, not ratified, so this is direction and not a verdict. I answered the distinguisher anyway, with two witnesses, and the answer did not depend on it.

The source panel file routes the call to the lead designer: "this is a choice about what a consumer's written crossing means, and `RULES.md` puts naming calls of that kind with op" (`mock/research/202608072330_the-numeral-canon-panel/71_orchard_what_crosses_between_two_systems.md:334-337`), and the proposal's gap repeats that it "is op's" (`mock/registry/proposal.toml:402`). The question row says `decider = "panel"` (`mock/registry/question.toml:790`). The two disagree about who decides. The ratified row by which the panel finishes the canon without him is what the row's field follows; the proposal's gap text was not brought in line.

## Evidence trail

The probes are spikes and are cited for what they checked, never for how they are written. They sit beside this file in `mock/research/202611140010_q36-two-readings/SEAT_2_probes/`: `src/bin/p1_model.rs`, a model in plain integers with no arvo crate in it, output in `out/p1_model.txt`; `src/bin/p2_shipped.rs`, against the shipped crates through public doors, output in `out/p2_shipped.txt`; toolchain in `out/toolchain.txt`. Run either with `cargo run --bin p1_model` or `cargo run --bin p2_shipped` from that directory. They are written and run and not committed, because this seat was told not to commit; until someone commits them, every sentence above that cites them is uncommitted evidence and should be treated as such. They are one author's instruments on one model, so they are one instance, and the counts are existence results and refutations at small widths. Nothing here prices anything.

Routes tried that did not decide the question, so the next reader need not repeat them. Composition of overflow crossings: symmetric between options 1 and 2, 4 of 8 each. The admission rule: admits both endpoint rules as functions of declared signatures, so it forces a coordinate and not an owner. The typestate: both orders are well typed, as the source file found. The standards bound: not consulted, no network, and nothing here leans on it. What did decide it, for me: the datum row making the source's reduction the identity on its own members, totality at sources with no selection, the widening product, and the binding time intent turning a site named reduction into a sibling signature.
