# Whose reduction governs a lossy crossing, two readings

`Num::convert` in `arvo-numeral` rounds by the target's mode and completes by the target's policy. That is the second option of `question::whose_reduction_governs_a_lossy_crossing`, which is open with a panel as its decider, and the third reading of the branch found the conversion built on it without the question named. No ratified row answers the question and nothing in the maintainer's captured words does. So it was put to two experts, one after the other, the second without the first's file. Their notes are `SEAT_1.md` and `SEAT_2.md` beside this one, kept whole.

Both picked the second option. That agreement is the provisional stamp `proposal::a_conversion_is_one_placement_onto_the_target_grid` carries, and question ticket `does_the_target_s_reduction_govern_a_lossy_conversion_in_arv` asks for it to be made formal.

## Where the two readings agree

The reduction that governs a lossy crossing is the adaptation of the signature the crossing lands on. A source's adaptation is the identity on the source's own members, so it has nothing left to say about a member that is leaving, and a source with no selection of its own, a constant or a host integer, still has to cross. A site that wants another mode or policy names a sibling of the target carrying it and crosses there, which is the third option reached through the second and not a separate mechanism.

Both say their structural arguments read neither the radix nor the phase and hold at any of either. Both say their probes did not go there.

## The region

What both examined with an instrument is a change of width, of range and of exponent on a radix two grid at zero phase and a constant quantum, under wrap and saturate and the five modes other than the stochastic one, at widths of 8 bits and under. Above 8 bits, at another radix and at a nonzero phase the pick rests on argument alone in both readings. Neither examined a magnitude-indexed quantum, the stochastic mode, a mixed-operand operation or a shared placement, and the stamp says nothing there.

## The instruments

The second reading's probes are a Rust crate under `SEAT_2_probes/`, with their outputs and the toolchain under `out/`. The first reading's model was a Python script. This workspace takes no Python, so the script is not kept and only its output, `SEAT_1_probes/q36_model.out`, is. Every count the first reading cites from it is an ad hoc spike with no instrument behind it and is to be read as unproven. Where the second reading's model reproduces a count, 226 of 256 between the first two options in the cell both name, it stands on that probe.

Both are spikes: one author each, one model each, existence results and refutations at small widths.

## Where they differ

The first reading found, against its own expectation, that the second option does not make the crossing a homomorphism for addition more often than the first, and says so. The second reading found the composition of overflow crossings symmetric between the two, 4 of 8 each. Neither takes that as a reason for the first option, since both decide on the datum, totality and the widening product.

## What the stamp leaves open

Whether the maintainer agrees, and whether he wants the crossing between two radixes or two phases held apart from the rest. The question row stays `open`.
