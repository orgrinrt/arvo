# Q31, one word or two for "is a number system" and "can arvo carry it"

**Verdict, staked before I read anything but the registry and the shipped crate: two words.
Not because the split is tidy, but because the ratified format spine is unstateable without
it, and the shipped tree already welds the two into one const and is wrong to.**

This first commit is the route, staked before I opened the two research files that landed on
`dev` on the second, before I built any probe, and before I read anything outside
`mock/registry/` and `mock/crates/arvo-format/src/`. The rest of the file arrives in later
commits on this branch. The ordering is checkable from `git log` on
`research/q31-q31b` rather than asserted.

## The route, in the order I walked it

1. `ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon`, ratified `both`, ratifies four propositions. Two of
   them do the work here: `a_format_is_identified_by_its_ambient_domain_and_its_representable_set`
   and `arithmetic_on_a_format_factors_as_an_adaptation_of_an_exact_operation`.
2. The second of those says arithmetic on a format is an exact operation **in an ambient
   domain** composed with a total adaptation onto the representable set. So the ambient domain
   has to be closed under the exact operation, or the sentence names nothing.
3. `arvo-format/src/ambient.rs` ships that domain as `BinaryRationals`, "the rationals at
   radix two". The rationals are unbounded. `ruling::the_operating_constraints_are_intents_and_rules`
   (I14, in force) forbids alloc and demands const sizes with no runtime growth.
4. So every format arvo hosts is identified, by ratified canon, half by a structure arvo
   cannot host, and its arithmetic is defined by an operation performed there.
5. Under one word, "is a number system" means "is a number system arvo can carry", which makes
   `BinaryRationals` not a number system, which leaves the ratified identity clause identifying
   a format by a thing the canon has no noun for. One word is not a cost. It is a contradiction
   with the spine.

## What I expect to have to prove, and how

- That the ambient domain of a format arvo hosts is not itself hostable, mechanically rather
  than by the argument above. Instrument: arvo's own `Slots::ADMITTED` refusing the slot range
  the exact product of two admitted operands lands in.
- That `Slots::ADMITTED` in the shipped tree contains both kinds of assertion in one const.
- That hosting does not vary with the compilation target, which is what decides between the
  row's second and third options.

## 1. The verdict

**Two words. Option two of the row, kept as written, with a rider that is mine and
needs a second reader before it counts for anything.**

Option one is not a costly choice, it is a contradiction with the ratified format
spine, and I can compile the contradiction rather than argue it. Option three buys a
quantifier over compilations that ranges over a constant, and buying it would be the
platform dependency `ruling::the_operating_constraints_are_intents_and_rules` puts out
of bounds.

**The rider, mine, unratified:** the two words do not both carry canon content. The
canon says what a number system is and says that hostability is a second and separate
predicate. **What that predicate contains is the design's**, because its content is a
function of carrier types the canon has no business naming, and section 3.2 measures
that it moves when a carrier moves. That is what the row's second option costs in
practice and it is smaller than the row prices it: not two admission passages in the
canon, but one, plus a sentence saying the other question exists and lives one tier
down.

## 2. Why one word is not available, derived from ratified rows

`ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon` is `rung = "ratified"`, `ratified_by = "both"`, and
it ratifies four propositions as one shape. Two of them carry this.

### 2.1 The first leg: the ratified factoring names a set arvo refuses

`proposal::arithmetic_on_a_format_factors_as_an_adaptation_of_an_exact_operation`,
ratified through that spine:

> Arithmetic on a format is an exact operation in an ambient domain composed with a
> named, total adaptation onto the representable set.

For that sentence to name anything, the ambient domain has to be closed under the
exact operation. If it is not, "the exact operation in the ambient domain" picks out
nothing and the factoring the whole error-analysis argument rests on has no first
half.

So take the two operands to be `Signed<32>`, which arvo admits: it is in the shipped
impl set at `arvo-format/src/slots.rs:297`. The exact product of two such values is an
element of the ambient domain, by the ratified sentence. The slot range it occupies is
63 bits wide. **`arvo-format/src/slots.rs:219` refuses it**, and the message names the
reason:

> declared width is wider than a slot index carries; the count of slots is 2^width and
> 2^63 does not fit a signed 64-bit integer

Probe arm `a1`, `out/a1_exact_product_of_admitted_operands_is_refused.txt`. Its
endpoints both fit that integer and its span fits a count, so `slots.rs:228` and
`slots.rs:232` cannot be the cause and only `slots.rs:219` can be. Control `a2` is the
same construction at sixteen bits, where the exact product needs 31 bits, and it
builds and runs.

**Under one word, the set in which arvo's own arithmetic is defined to happen is not a
number system.** That is not a cost the canon can absorb by telling readers which word
is which. It empties the ratified factoring's first half, for every format arvo ships,
at every width where the exact result needs more than 62 bits, which is every width
above 31 for multiplication. `arvo-format/src/format.rs:158` makes it unavoidable:
`type Slots: Slots` is a bound on the trait, so a format whose slot range is
inadmissible is not an arvo `Format` at all.

The two-by-two the row's note calls inhabited is inhabited by construction and I have
the witness compiled. Probe arm `a3`: a 63-bit two's complement grid over
`BinaryRationals`. It is a format by
`proposal::a_format_is_identified_by_its_ambient_domain_and_its_representable_set`,
which identifies a format by its ambient domain and its representable set and says
nothing about carriers. Arvo refuses it at `slots.rs:219`. Control `a4` is the same
shape one bit narrower and it builds.

**Nobody has amended the ratified identity clause to add a residue rider, and one word
would require that amendment.** That is the whole of the refutation and it does not
need the rationals, or mathematics, or anything the row's option-one text argues. It
needs one multiplication.

### 2.2 The second leg: one word makes "closed" false

`proposal::the_concept_is_closed_and_the_inventory_is_open`, ratified through the same
spine, `topic = "the_number_system"`:

> The canon defines once what a number system is and what admission requires; the set
> of admitted instances is open, and a new one earns admission by supplying the
> concept's obligations rather than by amending the canon.

Under one word, "the concept's obligations" include the residue clauses. Then the
concept contains a bound derived from `Slot` being `i64` at
`arvo-format/src/slots.rs:41`. **Change that type and the concept changes, with no
canon amendment anywhere.** A concept that moves when an implementation moves is not
closed, and "closed" is the word the ratified row uses.

That is measured rather than argued. `carrier/` holds two copies of the shipped
`slots.rs` side by side in one crate, differing in exactly one thing that is not a
design choice: whether a slot index is `i64` or `i128`, with the two literals written
from that type moved with it. The same 63-bit declaration is **refused by one and
admitted by the other** (`carrier/src/lib.rs`, module `verdict`), and both controls
hold: an inverted range is refused by both, and an eight-bit grid is admitted by both,
so neither copy is stuck at one answer. Two further arms assert the opposite of each
half and both fail (`c1`, `c2`), so the assertions are not vacuous in either direction.

Note what that second control establishes, because it is the useful half. **The
mathematical refusal is invariant under the carrier change and the width refusal is
not.** The two kinds are not merely describable apart; they are separable by a
measurement that reads no message text.

## 3. Which of the two remaining options, and why not the third

### 3.1 What option three would have to be true for

Option three scopes the hosting word to a target and pays a quantifier over
compilations. That is worth paying only if what arvo can carry varies with the target.

### 3.2 It does not, measured, and it may not, by canon

**Measured.** `cross/invariance` asserts, at check time, the admitted set's size, both
sides of its boundary, the exact-product range from arm `a1`, and the exact endpoints
of the widest admitted grid. It builds on **aarch64-apple-darwin,
x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu, i686-unknown-linux-gnu and thumbv6m-none-eabi**: pointer
widths 32 and 64, three operating-system values including bare metal, two architecture
families. `cross/mutant` asserts the 63-bit grid is admitted and **fails on all four**,
so the instrument could have reported a difference had there been one.

**Structural.** `target_pointer_width` and `cfg(target...)` in every spelling appear
zero times across `arvo-format`, `arvo-placement` and `arvo-strategy`, tests included,
and `usize` appears once, at `arvo-format/src/tests/the_inventory.rs:295`, as a cast for
array indexing inside a test that no admission bound reads. `isize` appears nowhere. The
carriers the bounds are written from, `Slot(i64)` at `arvo-format/src/slots.rs:41`,
`SlotCount(i64)` at `:83` and `Exponent(i32)` at `arvo-format/src/quantum.rs:40`, are
fixed-width on every target rustc has.

**Canon.** `ruling::the_operating_constraints_are_intents_and_rules`, key `I14`,
`rung = "in_force"`, `topic = "operating_constraints"`, whose `says` enumerates **"no
platform dependency"** and whose `quote` is "not to be questioned". A hosting predicate
indexed by compilation target is a statement that what arvo carries is a function of
the platform. That is the dependency the row forbids, and the measurement says the tree
is currently on the right side of it.

So option three's quantifier ranges over a constant today and is forbidden from ranging
over anything else. **It is not a more careful version of option two, it is option two
plus a variable the canon does not permit.**

## 4. Attacking the row itself, since existence and locus are in scope

**4.1 The row's first option contains its own refutation and cannot be cited as
evidence the option was fairly put.** Verbatim from
`question::one_word_or_two_for_is_a_number_system`:

> One word, folding the residue clauses into the concept, which makes the concept
> exclude unbounded exact rationals as a matter of mathematics, **which is false**, and
> makes every hosting clause conditional on unratified constraints inside a sentence
> about arithmetic.

An option carrying "which is false" in its own text is not an option, it is a
conclusion with a letter in front of it. I reach the same verdict, so nothing turns on
it here, and it matters anyway: **a later reader counting "the panel considered three
options and chose the second" would be counting one option and two labels.** Two of the
three options in this row also share a stem, so the real fork is binary and the row
presents it as ternary. The fix is a rewritten option-one text stating the position
without arguing against it, and it is a registry act, not a design one.

**4.2 The topic row folds the two words in its own `what`.** `topic::the_number_system`
reads "What counts as a number system **arvo can carry**, and what a candidate has to
expose to be admitted as one." That sentence uses one word. It is descriptive metadata
rather than a ratified claim, so it settles nothing, and it is the first thing anybody
navigating to this question reads. If the answer is two words the topic's own `what`
should stop saying one.

**4.3 The row is in the right namespace and under the right topic, and its
`decider = "panel"` is right.** I looked for a reason to say otherwise and did not find
one. This is a vocabulary question about what the canon says, which is the panel's, and
it belongs with the number-system topic rather than with `naming`, because it is not a
question about what to call a thing: it is a question about how many things there are.

**4.4 A fourth option, named and then folded into the second rather than proposed
against it.** "The canon carries only the concept and never mentions hosting at all."
It fails, and it fails for a reason worth writing down: the canon has to say the concept
excludes the residue, or a reader has no way to know that a clause about `i64` is not
part of it. **Silence about the second word is one word by default.** So the canon needs
the word in order to say the word is not its business, which is my rider in section 1
and is why I present it as a rider rather than as a rival.

## 6. The standing instruction: what the canon does not license

**6.1 `arvo-format/src/slots.rs:210` to `:238` folds two kinds of refusal into one
const, on an open row whose `decider` is `panel`.** Five assertions: `:211` a range
that is inverted, `:215` a width of zero, `:219` a width past what an `i64` slot index
carries, `:228` a span past what a count carries, `:232` a width that cannot address
its own range. Three of those are about grids and two are about machine types, and the
surface offers a reader nothing to tell them apart. **The canon has no sentence behind
`:219` and `:228`.** The doc comment three paragraphs above, at
`arvo-format/src/slots.rs:141`, cites
`proposal::the_concept_is_closed_and_the_inventory_is_open` and says "This is that
check", so a ratified open-inventory clause is named as the licence for the const that
closes the inventory at 62. That is the drift and it is exactly what section 2.2 says
cannot be true at once.

**It is not a mechanism to delete.** The refusals are correct, the diagnostics are good,
and `ruling::validate_means_all_three_readings` licenses the mechanism outright. What is
wrong is that it is one door.

**6.2 `arvo-format/src/slots.rs:228` cannot be the sole cause of any refusal, and it is
the one the crate's own test believes it is pinning.** `:219` bounds the declared width
at 62 and `:232` bounds the span by `2^width`, so the largest span reaching `:228` is
`2^62 - 1`, and `:228` asks whether the span is below `2^63 - 1`. Proved exhaustively
over all 62 admitted widths rather than sampled, in `carrier/src/lib.rs`,
`classify::ASSERTION_FOUR_IS_IMPLIED`, with `AT_SIXTY_THREE_IT_FAILS` beside it as the
control saying the sweep is not vacuous and that the bound at 62 is what carries it.

The assertion's own source comment says it was added because an earlier version
"admitted a span of 2^63, which made `slot_count` panic under `overflow-checks` and wrap
without it". That is true of the earlier version and `:219` and `:232` now exclude the
case between them. **It is a redundant guard rather than a wrong one**, which is why the
finding is that a test names it as a cause and cannot see that it was not one.

**6.3 `arvo-format/src/width.rs:14` states a bound a ratified ruling says is not canon
and is wrong.** Verbatim:

> //! Two types and no more. A count of bits, and a truth value.

`ruling::the_numeric_door_carries_the_coordinate_set_and_the_two_type_bound_is_not_canon`
is `rung = "ratified"`, `ratified_by = "experts"`, and says "The bound the question
inherits, that the door is two types and no more, is a sentence in the crate's design
and appears nowhere in the canon."

**The design tier has already been corrected and the code tier has not.**
`arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl:346` now says "They are not the bound on what the door
carries" and cites that ruling at `:348`, and `:362` says the count is not settled. So
the source contradicts its own design document, which cites a ratified ruling against
it. **The ruling named one site**, `arvo-format/DESIGN.md.tmpl:235`, and there were at
least two; the design was fixed and the source comment was not, because nobody grepped
for the class. One line to delete.

**6.4 A recorded gap rather than a defect.** `arvo-format/src/tests/the_inventory.rs`
holds the only tests asserting the open-inventory clause, and the ruling above already
carries the finding that they live inside the crate-wide bare-primitive exemption. I
confirmed it stands and add nothing to it.
