# Section twelve: reconciliation against `195_mcsherry_the_obligation_coverage`

This is the twelfth section of
`panel::202609030724_the_composition_cluster_is_four_obligations_and_two_rows_are_gateable`
and it is a separate file for one reason: it was written after that file was committed, and
keeping it separate is what makes the ordering git's to attest rather than mine to claim. The
commit that lands the first file precedes the commit that lands this one, and the first file
says in its own text that `195` was left closed until then.

`195` is the one file in the tree whose name says it is about this obligation. It is the seat
that wired three of the ten edges, and it is the same persona as seat 35, which is where three
more of the ten come from. So it is not an independent instance on most of this cluster, and

## Where we agree, and one of the two agreements is worth something

We agree that `the_format_concept_carries_three_things_upward_and_compositions_owe_their_own_laws`
is the strongest of the rows on the concept side and that its `because` turns on the same word
as the obligation's `need`, which is `expressible at all` against `cannot state its own error
behaviour at all`. I reached that from the row and it reached it from the row, so it is one
reading twice rather than two arrivals, and it changes nothing about the row's standing.

We agree, arriving from opposite directions, that this obligation has become an attractor. `195`
declined twice to wire a ruling about trait-contract structure to it, on the ground that saying
how arvo expresses things is not saying that composition contracts exist, and it says in terms
that what that ruling wants is an obligation of its own. I say the ten edges the obligation does
carry are four demands rather than one. Those are different observations and neither entails the
other, so I record them as adjacent rather than as one thing established twice.

The agreement that is actually worth something is about the schema. `195` says of a different
obligation entirely that the obligation itself splits into two, that one half is answered and
one is reached by nothing, that it would not defend reading the edge as the whole obligation
discharged, and that the schema gives it no way to say half. That is the same structural finding
I reach on this obligation by a different route: an obligation that splits, edges that report
arrival against the whole of it, and a coverage tool with no vocabulary for a fraction. Two
obligations, two seats, two routes, one defect in the demand side's shape. That one I would
carry forward as a real convergence, and it is a finding about `obligation` as a namespace
rather than about either obligation.

## Where I disagree, and it matters because acting on it would inflate a standing

`195` records `the_format_concept_carries_three_things_upward_and_compositions_owe_their_own_laws`
and `a_composed_expressions_region_is_never_inherited_from_its_parts` as saying the same thing
from two sides, neither citing the other, and calls it a convergence the corpus has not noticed
it has. I do not think they are the same claim, and I think recording them as one convergence
would raise a one-expert row to two by pairing it with an arrival that is not an arrival at it.

They are adjacent and they are not one. The first says a composition owes laws its base does not
have: an interval owes containment, a stored-pair rational owes whatever its normalisation
demands, and the base format has no analogue of either. The second says that for a law the
composite and its parts both have, the parts' verdict does not carry: unsigned saturating
addition associates on every triple at eight bits and the composition of that addition with the
matching subtraction fails on 82.7484 per cent of them. One is about laws that only the
composition has, the other is about a law the parts and the composition share. Neither entails
the other, and a design could satisfy either while getting the other wrong.

The sharpest way to see it is the evidence. The second row is decided by an instrument and has
one, committed, controlled and reproduced three times. The first is `normative`, enumerates what
a concept hands upward, and no instrument can bear on it at all. If the two were one claim, the
instrument would bear on both, and it does not touch the first.

The registry is on my side of this and `195` is not, which is the pleasing part. The format row's
own `note` says one expert, uncontested rather than corroborated, absence of contest is not a
second instance, and the consolidation does not claim it is. That note is careful in exactly the
way the proposed convergence would undo, and it is the note that should govern.

## What I would carry out of `195` that I did not have

Two things.

The obligation carried five proposals when `195` was written and carries ten now, so half of the
present cluster arrived after the file that surveys it. That is a fact about the shape of the
growth rather than about any row, and it is the reason the coverage tool's ten-against-one
reading is more misleading now than it was then.

And `195`'s section six names a limitation I did not reach: a `retirement` reaching an obligation
cannot say so, because the edge is on `ruling` and `proposal` only, so `refsto` cannot
distinguish an obligation nobody has looked at from one where a route was tried and closed. That
is a real gap in the demand side and it bears on this cluster, since
`dl_interior_wrapping_with_a_reserved_absorbing_top` is named in the min-plus row's `gap` as a
retired route into exactly this territory. I have nothing to add to its reasoning and I record
that I agree with it, as one reader rather than as an instance.

## What this changes about the first file

Nothing in its verdicts. The two gateable rows are the two gateable rows, the eight at one
expert are still eight, the four-way split still reads to me as the honest locus, and the
measured decomposition in section seven stands on its own instrument and did not come from here.

What it adds is a caution I would not otherwise have written down: the one prior survey of this
obligation proposes a convergence between two of its rows that I think is not one, and if that
proposal is ever acted on it will lift a row from one expert to two on a pairing rather than on
an arrival. Nothing marks it as a proposal rather than an observation, and it sits in a file
whose whole subject is which obligations are covered, which is where a reader would go to find
out.
