# The remainder in arvo: the fabian-giesen seat

Seat one of two on ticket `what_a_remainder_over_a_fractional_arvo_format_is_beside_the`, written blind to the other seat. The evidence this file stands on is one committed sketch, `mock/research/sketches/202611192130_the-remainder-region/` (its `FINDINGS.md` and `outputs/run.txt`), cited below by section number as "sketch §n", plus three external references named where used. Everything else is argument from registry rows, quoted by slug.

The short form of the answer, so the reader knows where this goes: a remainder is the exact operation `R = x - n y` with `n` the integer nearest `x / y` under a count convention drawn from the six modes, and that convention is a coordinate of the operation, not the signature's mode. It is exact on the dividend's grid exactly where the divisor's values lie on the dividend's quantum lattice (within one format: exactly where the phase is whole), at every radix and every exponent, because its exact step reads no quantum. None of the three filed options is that shape: option a has the lattice right and the keying wrong, option b draws its region on the wrong axis and is wrong inside it, and option c is not expressible from the shipped operations outside one cell.

## 3. The answer

### The definition

Let `F` be the dividend's format and `G` the divisor's, both with a constant quantum: `F` at quantum `q_F` and phase `p_F`, `G` at `q_G` and `p_G`, each phase a reduced rational in units of its quantum. Let `S` be a declared signature over `F` (its mode `m_S` and policy `o_S`). Let `c` be a count convention, one of the six modes `toward_zero`, `floor`, `ceil`, `half_up`, `half_even`, `stochastic`, with a dither `delta` when `c` is the stochastic one. For a member `x = (p_F + a) q_F` of `F` and a non-zero member `y = (p_G + b) q_G` of `G`:

```
n            = round_c(x / y)                       an integer, exact
R            = x - n * y                            exact, in the ambient domain
position     = R / q_F - p_F                        in F's slot units
rem_c<S>(a, b)   = adapt_S(position)                a slot of F
count_c(a, b)    = n                                exact, never adapted
```

`round_c` is the six modes' own rules applied with the integers as the grid: `half_up` is `floor(t + 1/2)`, `half_even` sends a tie to the even integer, and `stochastic` is `ceil(t - delta)`, the same rule the format's oracle states for the mode.

The pair law, which is what a test is written from first: `x = count_c(a, b) * y + R` holds exactly in the ambient domain for every pair, every `c` and every dither, before any adaptation. After adaptation, `rem_c<S>(a, b)` is the slot of `R` exactly (the adaptation is the identity) on the region E1 and E2 below, and only there.

The bounds, also exact in the ambient domain for every pair (sketch §2, asserted at every pair it sweeps): `|R| < |y|` under all six; under `toward_zero` `R` is zero or has the sign of `x`; under `floor` it is zero or has the sign of `y`; under `ceil` it is zero or has the sign opposite `y`; under `half_up` and `half_even`, `|R| <= |y| / 2`; under `stochastic` it is the floor or the ceil remainder as the dither decides.

### The conventions in the standards' own words

C's `fmod` and Rust's integer `%` are `rem_toward_zero` (sketch §5, at every signed pair of widths 1 to 8 against the host's `%`). MATLAB's `mod`, documented as `b = a - m.*floor(a./m)`, is `rem_floor` ([MathWorks, `mod`](https://www.mathworks.com/help/matlab/ref/double.mod.html)), and for `fi` objects it "ignores and discards any fimath attached to the inputs" and lands on "the aggregate type of both input signedness, word lengths, and fraction lengths" ([MathWorks, `mod` for `fi`](https://www.mathworks.com/help/fixedpoint/ref/mod.html)). IEEE 754's `remainder` is `rem_half_even`, defined "regardless of the rounding mode" with `n` the integer nearest `x / y`, ties to even, "always exact" (IEEE 754-1985 section 5.1 as quoted in [golang/go#30814](https://golang.org/issue/30814); the same independence from the rounding mode in [Oracle's `remainder(3M)`](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/E19455-01/817-5434/6mkt1va6l/index.html)). Rust's `rem_euclid` is not one of the six: it is `rem_floor` by `|y|`, and equally `rem_floor` where `y > 0` and `rem_ceil` where `y < 0` (sketch §5, both identities at every pair, with the planted Euclid-as-floor-by-`y` reported at every width from 2).

### Why the convention is the operation's coordinate and not the signature's mode

This is the correction to option a, and it is the one place in this file I make a call no row makes for me; it is marked as mine and needs the second seat.

The spine row says an operation is an exact operation composed with a named adaptation. `round_c` is inside the exact step: it decides which `R` exists before anything is adapted. Reading `c` off `m_S` makes the exact step a function of the adaptation, so the remainder would be the only operation in arvo whose exact half reads the adaptation's coordinate. Both standards in scope agree with the separation: IEEE's `remainder` is fixed at nearest-even whatever the rounding attribute, and MATLAB's `fi` `mod` is fixed at floor whatever the `fimath`. In the region where `R` is exact (E1) the adaptation is the identity and `m_S` is never read by the remainder at all, which is the honest statement of what a signature's mode means: what happens between grid points, and here nothing lands between grid points. Where `R` is not exact (E5, E7) the two coordinates both act and separating them is the only way to say, for example, "count by floor, round the residue to nearest", which is what every floating `%` that floors its quotient computes.

Under the admission rule this is not a new coordinate on the signature. `rem_toward_zero`, `rem_floor`, `rem_half_even` and the rest are six operations, each a function of the declared signature, the way the sum and the difference are two. The name "remainder" with no convention is the name whose two realisations disagree (`fmod` and `remainder` differ on every pair where the count's rounding differs), and the admission rule's repair is to declare the coordinate that separates them; it is declared on the operation because it belongs to the exact step, and the six-mode enumeration is reused as its type rather than a new vocabulary invented.

### The regions, and which dimensions each is quantified over

E1, exact on the grid. For every pair and every convention, `position` is a whole slot exactly where every member of `G` is a whole multiple of `q_F`. With `p_G = h / d` reduced that is: `q_G / q_F` is an integer and a multiple of `d`. Proof: if `y` is in `q_F Z` then `n y` is too for every integer `n`, so `R = x - n y` lies on `x`'s coset `p_F q_F + q_F Z`, which is `F`'s grid; conversely the floor or ceil convention reaches `n = 1` or `n = -1` at some pair with `|x| < |y|`, so `R = x - y` or `x + y` is off the grid unless `y` is in `q_F Z`. With `G = F` the condition is `d_F = 1`: the phase is whole. Quantified over every radix, mixed radixes included (radix ten at `10^-1` divides exactly by radix two at `2^-1`, since one half is five tenths), every exponent, both signednesses, every convention, every policy; `F`'s own phase never enters. Evidence: the proof, sketch §1a (720 formats, exact exactly where the phase is whole) and §1b (960 format pairs, the lattice condition right at all 960; forgetting `G`'s phase denominator is wrong at 202, the member door's one-quantum-one-phase rule at 440). This is the region option a asserted and did not state; option b's region (the zero exponent) is not it.

E2, inside the range. On E1, `R` is the identity of the adaptation, and the policy acts only where the slot of `R` leaves `[MIN, MAX]`. At a zero phase: on a signed range never, under all six conventions; on an unsigned range never under `toward_zero` and `floor`, and at some pair from width 2 under `ceil`, `half_up`, `half_even` and `stochastic`, whose remainders go negative (sketch §2, 576 cells over radix 2 and 10, exponents -3, 0, 2, widths 1 to 8). There the policy completes it: under wrapping to `R + 2^W q_F`, so the pair law holds in the wrapping ring modulo `2^W q_F`; under saturation and clamping to zero, where it does not hold. At a whole nonzero phase the same statement holds with the condition stated rather than tabled: `R` lies between 0 and `x` (`toward_zero`), between 0 and `y` exclusive (`floor`), between 0 and `-y` exclusive (`ceil`), within `|y| / 2` (half modes), and the policy acts exactly where that value is outside the range's values. Quantified over radix, exponent, signedness, convention, policy; the phase only through the range's position.

E3, the exact step reads no quantum. With `G = F` at phase `h / d`, `n = round_c((h + a d) / (h + b d))` and `position = a - n b - n h / d`: neither reads the radix or the exponent. So at one phase and one slot range the count and the slot are the same at every radix and exponent (sketch §3, 11,709,630 points; option b's remainder moves with the quantum at 8,153,320 of them). This is the property addition has and multiplication and division lack, and its consequence is that the remainder has no "step not formed" condition at any exponent: the product and quotient stop at `2^256`, the remainder does not stop. The step is formed in the slot domain: the count needs one bit past the slot width at a signed range (`[MIN, -MIN]`, one past the top at `MIN / -1` only) and none at an unsigned one (`[0, MAX]`), sketch §4 at widths 1 to 8 except signed width 1, whose range `{-1, 0}` has no positive divisor and whose count lies in `[0, 1]` (the sketch's first prediction missed that case and says so). At a fractional phase the count passes the slot range (63 at phase one half over a signed 6-bit range whose top is 31), bounded by `|h + a d|`, which a 64-bit slot range and a 63-bit phase denominator keep under `2^128`.

E4, the divisor's zero. The remainder is admitted over a divisor that cannot be zero by type, through the quotient's own `Divisor<G>` doors, unchanged. Two readings of `rem(x, 0)` disagree, and neither is forced: the residue class of `x` modulo `0 Z` is `{x}`, so a class reading gives `x` (MATLAB's `mod`, "`mod(a,0)` returns `a`"), while the limit of every bounded convention as `y` goes to zero is 0, and IEEE and MATLAB's `rem` give an invalid result. Two realisations disagree and no row declares the coordinate between them, so by the admission rule the zero is not a point at which the remainder is a function of the declared signature, and by `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path` it cannot be screened at run time. The consequence travels with it: a divisor read at run time of either sign over a whole-phase range holding the zero has no door, exactly as for the quotient, which `proposal::a_quotient_s_divisor_is_non_zero_by_type_and_a_member_divides_on_one_quantum` records and `ruling::the_ingest_door_waits_on_a_known_use` holds. The remainder is the strongest known use for that door, since modular reduction by a runtime period is its ordinary case; that is a fact for whoever decides the door, not a door.

E5, a fractional phase with `G = F`. The remainder is a function of the declared signature here (`adapt_S` is total), so by the admission rule it is admitted and is not refused by design. `R` is off the grid wherever `d_F` does not divide `n` (sketch §7: over the half-offset 6-bit range, 2,500 of 4,096 pairs on the grid under `toward_zero`), and `adapt_S` places it by `m_S` and completes it by `o_S`. The pair law holds before adaptation only, and `|rem| < |y|` can fail after it, by less than one quantum, since the adaptation moves a position by less than one. The exact remainder of a fractional-phase dividend exists, and it is the cross-format one: by any divisor format satisfying E1, a zero-phase format at the same quantum among them.

E6, the cross-format remainder. The divisor's format `G` is a parameter, the dividend's `F` another, and E1 is the obligation that makes the result exact. With `G = F` it is the one-format operation. MATLAB's `fi` `mod`, landing on the aggregate type of its two operands, is this operation with `F` the aggregate (the finer fraction length and the union of the ranges), into which the dividend converts without loss and on whose quantum lattice the divisor's values lie, so the parity alias is a lossless conversion followed by `rem_floor` and nothing else.

E7, the magnitude-indexed family. Not admitted yet, for the reason the quotient gives: the applied map has no magnitude coordinate to land a result at (the quotient's placeholder ticket `arvo_quotient_and_conversion_over_a_magnitude_indexed_quantu`). The intent stated for when it is: on a binary float with gradual underflow, `R` is a member at every pair under `toward_zero`, `half_up` and `half_even`, and is not a member at some pairs under `floor`, `ceil` and `stochastic` (sketch §6, precisions 2, 3, 4 over exponents -3 to 3: 132 of 930, 552 of 3906 and 2256 of 16002 pairs off under floor). Off the grid, `m_S` places `R`, and the bound can fail after placement; Python documents the case, `-1e-100 % 1e100` "is numerically exactly equal to `1e100`" ([Python reference, binary arithmetic operations, footnote](https://docs.python.org/3/reference/expressions.html)). The count is not representable in the format (192, 448, 960 at significands of 2, 3, 4 bits in the sweep; past `2^2000` for binary64), so the float remainder is not composable from the quotient and product at all, and the exact step cannot be "form the count, then multiply": it is a reduction that keeps the residue, the way every libm implements `fmod`.

### What composes and what has to be an arm

Option c's composition, run through arvo's shipped `div`, `mul`, `sub` and `convert` (sketch §7, ten 6-bit formats, every convention and policy): from `S`'s own operations it reproduces the definition at the zero exponent and zero phase under wrapping, and nowhere else. Under saturation the intermediate product is completed (`-32 / -31` under `ceil`: product `-32` where it is `-62`, answer 0 where the remainder is 30). At `e = -3` the count is rounded twice (onto eighths, then onto the integers: `-32/-22` gives 2 where the count is 1) or completed by `S`'s range (`-4 / -1` lands past the top and wraps the count to -4). That is the fused multiply-add's shape exactly, by the same mechanism the stated ruling records: wrap is a ring homomorphism and saturation is not. A consumer who reads the slots as members of an integer format two bits wider and computes there (arm c3) reproduces the definition at every zero-phase format and every policy (4,032 of 4,032 at each cell), which is a reimplementation in integers, not a composition of `S`'s operations; it does not exist at a nonzero phase, across formats, or for floats. So the remainder is a required arm over everything but one cell, and the framework carries it as an operation rather than leaving it to consumers.

## 4. The strongest arguments against this, and how they fall

1. Keying the count on `m_S` (option a as filed) is simpler: one signature, no extra parameter, and the value already lets one site override its mode through a sibling signature. It falls on the spine row: the count is inside the exact step, and an exact step reading the adaptation is the operation-and-adaptation-point-under-one-name that the factoring exists to separate. Both in-scope standards fix the count per operation and independently of the arithmetic rounding. The sibling crossing changes the adaptation of every operation in the chain the site wanted unchanged (kaski's half-to-even product lane wrapping an angle by floor is the concrete case). And where `R` is inexact the coupling is observable and makes a real computation inexpressible. What survives of the objection: one more parameter at the call. That is the cost and I accept it.
2. Option c is enough, since c3 shows the consumer can reproduce the remainder exactly, and the admission rule admits without obliging. It falls because c3 is a reimplementation in integers that exists only at a zero phase, and the composition of `S`'s operations fails everywhere outside one cell (sketch §7). At the float family the count does not fit any carrier, so there is no composition to fall back on. By the stated FMA ruling an operation not reachable by composition is a required arm in the region where it is not, and by `ruling::the_standard_is_parity_in_output_not_in_the_internals` a hole in the framework is a defect.
3. Option b is the conservative first step: ship the zero exponent, where every option agrees, refuse the rest until a use is known. It falls three ways. They do not agree there (section 1). b's own claim that `q` and `r` are both members of `S` is false at the zero exponent on unsigned ranges under `ceil` and the half modes, where `r` is negative. And refusing the rest by design refuses an operation that is a function of the declared signature, which the ratified admission rule forbids as a refusal; at most it could be a placeholder, and a placeholder drawn on the exponent would be drawn on the wrong axis (sketch §1a). The "until a use is known" bar is `ruling::the_ingest_door_waits_on_a_known_use`, which is about a runtime door, not about an operation.
4. The zero divisor is defined under the class reading, so the `Divisor` type over-restricts and keeps the signed runtime-divisor hole open for nothing. It falls because the class reading and the limit reading disagree and the standards in scope split across them; declaring one would be inventing a coordinate no row states. It is the strongest objection after the first, because the class reading is mathematically clean, and it is recorded in section 6 as open rather than dismissed.
5. The cross-format divisor is scope beyond the quotient, which divides only within one format. It falls because without it a fractional-phase dividend has no exact remainder at all, and the MATLAB `fi` parity alias is this operation by the standard's own output type. The quotient's member door is a different condition (one quantum and one phase, range inside), and is wrong for the remainder at 440 of 960 format pairs (sketch §1b), so the remainder cannot borrow it.
6. A count one bit past the slot width at a signed range runs off the shipped width ladder at 64 bits. True at the value layer and not at the format layer, whose `Slot` index is 128 bits; section 6 records it.

## 5. What it requires, by crate

### arvo-format

The operation, as a design statement to be written in `DESIGN.md.tmpl` before any code, under the admission rule section's pattern: an exact step `remainder_position` over `F`, `G` and the convention `C` (one of the six `Rounding` types), taking a slot of `F` and a `Divisor<G>` and returning an `Exact` on `F`'s grid; the composition `rem` with `adapt::<S>` under one declared signature over `F`, the dither passed through for whichever of `C` and `S`'s mode is stochastic; `remainder_count`, returning the exact count as an index, never adapted; and a verdict `is_remainder_exact::<F, G>()` stating E1. Its obligation carries the same first two conditions the quotient's does (a phase that denotes, at both formats; a constant quantum, at both, the second a placeholder with the quotient's ticket), the lattice condition is a verdict and not a refusal, and the third condition is a step formed in the four limbs for `G` not `F`, which needs its bound derived (section 6). At `G = F` there is no exponent bound.

The tests it owes, each with a planted twin: the denotation at every member pair at widths 1 to 8 against an oracle in `Ratio`, all six conventions crossed with every mode and shipped policy, at exponents on both sides of zero, at a whole and a fractional phase, at radix two and ten; the pair law exactly before adaptation; E1's verdict against an exhaustive search, with the quantum-ratio-only rule and the member door's grid rule planted; E3's quantum independence across radix and exponent; the bounds of section 3 at every pair; the host's `%` and `rem_euclid` at every signed pair to width 16; `MIN / -1` at every width with count `-MIN` and remainder zero under every policy; an unsigned range under `ceil` showing the completion under each policy; planted conventions read from `m_S` and from `div::<S>`; compile-fail pairs for a constant zero divisor and a magnitude-indexed quantum, each beside a twin that builds.

The standards: `standards::ieee754` gains `remainder` as `rem` under `half_even`, a declared placeholder until the float family divides; the MATLAB half gains `mod` as a lossless conversion onto the aggregate format followed by `rem` under `floor`, with a parity test against the documented formula at every word length the existing `fimath` suite sweeps.

### arvo-numeral

`rem::<C>(self, by)` beside `div`, `C` one of the five conventions the value admits (its test oracle states that a value admits no stochastic mode, so neither the value's mode nor its count convention is stochastic), taking the format's `Divisor` re-exported as the quotient's is, total, equal to the format's `rem` on every member and divisor, with `fits_rem::<C>` its verdict; `rem_count::<C>` where the derived integer value type of the count exists; and the pair asserted by equality, `self` against `count * by + rem` in the test's exact rationals, over every member pair at widths 1 to 8. At a zero phase the remainder is carrier integer arithmetic on slots one bit wider (the host's `%` panics at `MIN % -1`, so the wrapping form or the wider carrier is what the value uses). The debug check fires only where the policy completes the remainder, which by E2 is the unsigned ranges under the four conventions that round the count up. The sentence in `DESIGN.md.tmpl`, "No remainder is here: the canon has no row on one", stays true until this answer is promoted, and is then replaced in a design round.

## 6. What is left open

1. The zero divisor's convention (E4). Class representative or excluded; this file excludes it by the admission rule, and the class reading is the live alternative. Moving it needs a row declaring the convention, which is the panel's to write and which two seats must agree on.
2. The divisor read at run time of either sign, shared with the quotient and held by `ruling::the_ingest_door_waits_on_a_known_use`. The remainder is the known use that ruling waits for; whether that suffices is not this seat's to rule.
3. The cross-format step's formation bound in four limbs (the count reads the quantum ratio there, unlike E3). Not derived.
4. The float family's exact step: a residue-keeping reduction rather than count-then-product, and the sign of a zero remainder, which IEEE gives as the sign of `x` and arvo has no signed zero to carry.
5. The count's value type at signed width 64 (65 bits), past the shipped width ladder.
6. The Euclidean convention, which is not one of the six. Through the member door a runtime divisor's sign is known at build time (its range lies on one side of zero), so Euclid is a const selection of `floor` or `ceil` there; through `Divisor::of` at a fractional phase its sign is read at run time, and whether that selection is a check is not settled here.
7. Whether MATLAB's `fi` supports `rem` was not confirmed from MathWorks' pages; the `mod` alias is.
8. A second, independent read of this answer, owed by the two-seat rule before anything here is promoted. Agreement with the other seat is evidence only if that seat reached it before reading this.
