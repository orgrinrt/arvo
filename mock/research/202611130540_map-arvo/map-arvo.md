# arvo: the night's work against arvo's own record

Read on 13 November 2026, read only, nothing built and no suite run. arvo at `origin/dev` (head `989e02f9`) and `origin/work/numeral-integers` at `854b0c5c`. The old tree at `1b2796e9`, the rev hilavitkutin pins (`hilavitkutin/mock/Cargo.toml:39-48`).

Three kinds of sentence are kept apart. CANON is a registry row with its file and line and a quote, with the rung named (a `ratified` or `in_force` ruling binds; a `stated` ruling is his direction; a proposal at `one_expert` is not canon). DESIGN is a passage of a design document at head, which is the tier under the canon and is derived, not ratified. CODE is what a file does. MINE is judgement. Line numbers into the registry point at the row's `id` line or its `says` line.

## The short answer

The night built the right kind of thing in a place the designs can carry, and built it ahead of the gate the record puts in front of it, with four decisions inside it that the record either leaves to the panel or states differently.

A value type held alone in the carrier the placement derives is what the ratified container ruling presupposes and what a proposal has said since August. Putting it in a crate above `arvo-format` and `arvo-placement` follows the dependency reasoning the top design already gives. It is no parallel home in the sense of the hilavitkutin case: there is no existing crate at head that holds a numeral value, and the old crate named `arvo` was deleted on purpose, so nothing was built beside a live thing.

What is beside the record is narrower and is inside the crate. The carrier is chosen by a transcribed table in the value crate where the placement crate owns the derivation. The debug panic became a pair of overflow markers where the format design says the panic is no policy and the ratified row asks that it not be sharpened. The value closes two sets the ratified spine declares open. The aliases take a written adaptation where the design says every alias carries a strategy parameter defaulting to `Warm`. And the whole crate rests on an answer to an open question whose decider is the panel, at one expert, with the design document that says "no numeric value type exists yet" left unchanged on the branch.

The plan after it (AR5 to AR11) has one step that works around the record (AR8), one that picks an option of an open panel question without saying so (the accumulator in AR7a), and one ordering that puts the thing the record calls load-bearing, the strategy binding, at the far end behind division (AR9).

## 3. `arvo-numeral` as a separate crate

CANON says nothing about crates. No ratified row names one.

DESIGN, by its own reasoning, has room for exactly this crate and for nothing else to hold a numeral value. `arvo-format` cannot ("cannot name a carrier because it does not depend on the crate that has one", `mock/DESIGN.md.tmpl:29-30`). `arvo-placement` derives widths and "is checked against ever holding a host primitive itself" (`arvo-bits/DESIGN.md.tmpl:17-19`). `arvo-bits` is "not a numeral" by its first paragraph. So a crate above format and placement is where the design's dependency argument puts a value, and the branch's edit to `mock/DESIGN.md.tmpl` says so in the design's own terms.

MINE, on whether it is a parallel home. It is not parallel to `arvo-format` (which keeps the operation on slots and stays the statement of meaning) and not to `arvo-bits` (no arithmetic, a fixed `u64`, a different question). Against the old crate named `arvo` it is a successor, since that crate held `UFixed`, `IFixed`, `Uint`, `Int`, `USize`, `Bool` and the strategy markers and was deleted so the canon could be rebuilt (`929c46ed`). Three things are still unsettled around it and none is a shortcut by itself:

- Whether it sits above `arvo-strategy` or beside it. The record's alias needs the strategy (section 2 item 2), so the value's consumer facing spelling depends on `arvo-strategy`; either the value crate takes that edge or the aliases live one crate up. The night's plan has `arvo-strategy` depend on the value crate (AR9), which puts the default spelling in the strategy crate. That works and should be decided before 419 sites adopt `arvo_numeral::USize`.
- The name. Consumers write `arvo::USize`. The design says there is no facade "today" (`mock/DESIGN.md.tmpl:85`). Whether the value crate is the one named `arvo`, or a facade returns, or neither, is a call about arvo's public identity, which `ruling.toml:1417` leaves with him ("intent, fundamental design direction and broad strokes"). The night settled it at one expert (`proposal-the-value-stance.toml:84`).
- The carrier table, which is the one part that is a second home for something an existing crate owns (section 1).
