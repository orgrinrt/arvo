# The composition cluster is four obligations wearing one slug, and two of its ten rows are gateable

I was sent to say whether any of the ten proposals reaching
`obligation::composition_contracts_above_the_numeral` is ready to be promoted to a ruling, and
on what grounds. The answer is that two of them are within one mechanical repair of being
promotable and eight of them are not promotable at any level of argument, and that the more
useful finding is the one underneath: the obligation is not one thing, the ten rows do not
answer one demand, and the coverage tool reporting ten arrivals against one obligation reads as
a demand nearly met when in fact none of its four parts is met.

Before any of that, one correction to the shape of the dispatch, because it changes what my
answer can be. The brief says I am one instance. I am not, and I cannot be, for any of these
ten. The registry's own vocabulary for `standing` is declared in `mockspace.toml` and says
`two_experts` means each deriving before reading the other, and the repo's generated
instructions gloss it in terms that leave no room: a seat handed another's conclusion before
writing its own has not reached anything, and no quality of argument makes it count. I was
handed ten conclusions by slug, and the slugs are whole sentences, so my agreeing with one of
them is confirmation rather than corroboration. What I can be, and what the rest of this file
is, is a second reading of the gate: whether the reasoning is spelled out, the evidence real
and the region stated well enough to promote, which the `promotion` field on a
`ratified_by = "experts"` ruling exists to carry and which the canon says is a separate
question from the count. Where I say a row is gateable I mean the gate, not the count, and I
say which of the two is missing every time.

Two of the ten also gained something better than an opinion while I was reading them. I rebuilt
one of their instruments from its specification rather than rerunning it, and the rebuild
reproduced the headline, refuted the control the row leans on, and priced the mechanism the row
names against the one its own `gap` field says it never filed. That is in section seven, and
both probes are committed at `panel::202609030724_probes` with their sources, their drivers and
their whole stdout beside them.

## The shape of the cluster, which is the finding I would lead with

Ten rows reach this obligation and they carry five topics between them: `the_chain` has five,
`algebraic_laws` two, and `the_primitive`, `the_format` and `execution_environment` one each.
They share no axis, no region and no instrument. Nothing in the registry relates any of them to
any other except the obligation edge itself, and the obligation edge is the only thing they have
in common.

That would be tolerable if the obligation were a single demand met from several directions. It
is not. Reading the ten against the `need` field, which asks for contracts over units bigger
than a single numeral so that what a chain guarantees is expressible at all, they fall into four
groups that do not overlap and that a design would satisfy separately.

The concept group asks what a composition is and whether the contract written for a primitive
survives composition. `configuration_is_not_composition_and_a_composite_is_a_primitive` says it
does, so one concept serves; `the_format_concept_carries_three_things_upward_and_compositions_owe_their_own_laws`
says a composition is not a format instance and owes its own laws. Those two read as opposites
and are not, and the reason is worth writing down because nothing in the registry says it: the
`topic` rows define a primitive as a value set with a realisation map over a declared operation
set, and a format as the widths a numeral declares with its signedness and its relation to the
container, so format is the narrower concept and an interval of fixed-point values is a
primitive that is not a format. Both rows hold under that reading and the composition of them is
the useful sentence, that a design composes at the primitive tier and consumes at the format
tier. Neither row says it, and a reader meeting them separately has two sentences that appear to
contradict.

The region group asks how a composition's region is derived.
`a_composed_expressions_region_is_never_inherited_from_its_parts` is the rule,
`a_min_plus_fold_needs_an_absorbing_top_and_wrapping_supplies_none` and
`splitting_a_reduction_is_sound_in_three_of_the_four_sign_and_policy_cells` are two instances of
it at different coordinates.

The schedule group asks what a chain is and where adaptation sits inside it.
`a_chain_is_exact_operations_together_with_a_schedule_of_adaptation_points`,
`a_multiplicative_chain_is_writable_without_an_ever_growing_intermediate_by_windowing` and
`within_an_unbound_stretch_the_design_may_select_any_realisation`.

The binding-time group asks what a composition may decide at run time and what it must fix
statically. `no_derivation_reads_the_grid_so_a_composition_may_hold_it_at_run_time` and
`a_fold_needs_a_closed_operation_and_a_separately_determined_accumulator`.

So the honest locus is four obligations rather than one, and the cost of the present filing is
visible in the tool: `mock coverage obligation composition_contracts_above_the_numeral` reports
`tier: proposed` with ten rows named by it, which is what an obligation looks like when it is
nearly answered. Split four ways, each of the four is at one or two proposals, three of the four
have no row above `one_expert` at all, and the concept group's two rows are the only pair that
compose into anything. That is a much more accurate picture of how much of this demand is met,
and it is a picture the registry can carry today, since `obligation` rows are additive and
adding one nukes nothing.

I am not filing the split. Where an obligation's boundaries sit is a claim about what arvo owes
a consumer, and under the two-expert rule I am one reading of it. What I am saying is that the
question the brief asked, whether one of these ten is ready, presumes a single decision that
does not exist, and that the reading of it I would defend is that four decisions exist and the
ten rows are unevenly spread across them.

## The two rows at two experts, and what each still owes

Both are gateable in the sense that matters: the claim is real, the evidence exists, and the
region is stated. Both fail the gate today on something mechanical, and in each case the repair
is an edit to the registry rather than new work.

### `a_composed_expressions_region_is_never_inherited_from_its_parts`

This is the strongest row in the cluster and I would promote it once one edge is filled.

The count is genuine and checkable. Its `provenance` names two distinct files by two distinct
authors, `79_fallin_attacking_the_two_cold_derivations_on_derived_laws` and
`90_giesen_consolidation_derived_algebraic_laws`, and a third arrival at seat 215 recomputed
the figure rather than taking it. I reran seat 215's instrument on this machine and it
reproduces exactly: 13,882,880 of 16,777,216 triples fail, 82.7484 per cent, with the negative
control under wrapping at zero as a group requires. That is a fourth run rather than a fourth
instance, and I record it as a run.

The sentence is `normative` and carries no predicate, and that is right rather than a gap. The
claim quantifies over derivation rules, not over values, and a derivation rule is refuted by one
counterexample, so the universal "never" is doing exactly the work a universal should. It is
worth being explicit about why, since the notation invites the opposite reading: under wrapping
the composition of saturating addition and subtraction does inherit its parts' region, in the
plain sense that both parts hold everywhere and so does the composite, and someone could read
that as a counterexample to "never". It is not, because the rule forbids deriving the
composition's region by inheritance rather than asserting that the two regions always differ.
Under wrapping the separate derivation happens to give the same answer. Under saturation at
eight bits it does not, on 82.7 per cent of triples, and one such case is enough to kill a
derivation rule.

What it owes is one edge. The law row it points at,
`law::associativity_of_a_composed_saturating_add_and_subtract`, carries an exact measured
witness and names no `evidence`. The instrument exists and is committed, at
`panel::202608072330_the-numeral-canon-panel::79_probes::p1_compositional_predicate_search.rs`
with its output beside it, and there are two independent reruns at `82_probes` and `215_probes`.
So nothing is void and the claim is not in doubt; what is missing is a `probe` row and the edge
from the law to it, which is what makes "which claims rest on an instrument nobody controlled" a
query rather than a reading exercise.

This is not one row's oversight. Of twenty-four `law` rows across the two law files, twelve
carry a `witness` and exactly one carries an `evidence` edge, and the lint that exists for this
class cannot see any of them, because `mock/lints/measured_claim_cites_no_probe.rs` declares
`const NAMESPACE: &str = "proposal"` and reads that namespace only. Section eleven carries that
as a finding of its own.

### `configuration_is_not_composition_and_a_composite_is_a_primitive`

Also real, and further from the gate than the first, on two separate grounds.

The count is not checkable from the registry. Both `provenance` entries name
`161_leroy_the_canon_candidate_for_the_primitive`, differing only in line number, and one file
has one author. The repo's own lint agrees and is explicit about it: a standing asserting
several arrivals must cite more than one file, and this row is one of the twenty-nine
grandfathered under its ceiling. The second instance does exist. Opening the establishing lines
shows clause L28 recording `110` section 4 and F10 as the first arrival and `154` section 7 as a
blind arrival in a later sitting from a different instrument set, classified by `157` 1.3 as a
genuine convergence. None of those three files is in the row's `provenance`, so a reader cannot
get from the row to the convergence, and the repair is to add them.

I went looking for a second defect and found the opposite of one, which is worth recording
because I was wrong in the direction that would have blocked the row. The row's `says` fuses two
clauses, the configuration-against-composition distinction with the closure, and the
construction carrying a predicate on its base and a transformer for its base's refinements. The
row's `note` attests two instances for "the distinction and the closure" only, so the prose
appeared to reach past what the standing covers, which is precisely the failure the promotion of
`the_additive_and_absorption_verdicts_are_canon` names as what its gate is for. It does not.
The establishing file carries the second clause as its own numbered L29 with its own evidence
and its own rung of two or more instances. So the row's `note` undersells its second half rather
than overselling it, which is a wording defect in the safe direction.

What it does owe, and this one is substantive rather than clerical, is a disambiguation of the
equality clause, and I built an instrument for it rather than arguing about it. The clause reads
"equality transports through a construction for free where a predicate never does", and it has
two readings that license different code.

On the congruence reading, two values related by the base's equality lifted componentwise denote
the same thing. That is what `110` R6's p11 measured, congruence transporting four of four with
the sabotage control catching zero of four, and it is true.

On the adequacy reading, the lifted equality is the construction's equality, so two values
denote the same thing exactly when the lifted relation holds. That is what a reader takes from
the words "for free", because free means nothing further is owed, and it is what somebody
writing a derived `PartialEq` on a composite and moving on has assumed.

The two come apart on any construction that normalises, and the sibling row names one in its own
sentence: stored pairs. My probe 2 walks the whole of both constructions at base width four. On
the stored-pair rational the lifted equality is a congruence with zero violations of 57,600
ordered pairs, and it fails adequacy on 688 of the same 57,600, first witness `(0/1)` against
`(0/2)`. On a pair whose denotation is the pair, the control construction, both directions
report zero. The sabotage arm, which replaces the rational's denotation with the lifted relation
itself so that the two are one relation, reports zero as it must. Every control passed.

So the clause is true on the reading its evidence measured and false on the reading its wording
invites, on a construction the canon already has in view. That is not a refutation of the row and
I am not proposing one. It is a wording repair that has to land before promotion, because the
sentence as written is a licence a design would take.

The same probe also confirms the clause's other half on both constructions, which is worth
saying since I went in expecting to dent it. The rational's borrowed admission rule admits
sixteen of 256 candidate pairs the construction must refuse, every one of them a zero
denominator. And on the pair construction, read as a complex number, the componentwise lift of
the base's nonzero refinement disagrees with the construction's own on thirty of 256 values,
namely every value with exactly one zero component. That second arm is the more interesting of
the two, because it exhibits the asymmetry the row asserts inside a single construction: on the
pair, equality transports perfectly in both directions and the refinement does not transport at
all. Nothing in the record does that, and it is the cleanest statement of why the two halves are
different obligations rather than one.

## What I measured, and what it changed

The min-plus row's `gap` says its end-to-end half stands at one expert and that nobody has
re-run it. Rerunning `35_probes/p5_algorithm_end_to_end.rs` would have been a rerun of one
instrument, and the `probe` schema says in its own words that a reproduction in a different
language is worth more than a rerun of the same code, so I wrote my own from the specification
in `probe::a_dag_dynamic_programme_returns_wrong_answers_on_in_range_instances` and from the
mechanism the proposal states, without opening the earlier source. It reports its own
enumeration and its own denominator, and it cannot corroborate the figure 5,414,255 of
11,932,446, because matching a count means matching an enumeration and matching an enumeration
means reading the code. What it can do is corroborate or refute the claim, which is what a
promotion turns on.

It did three things and only the first is what I went looking for.

The headline reproduces. In band, with unreachable standing on the numeral's top, min-plus is
wrong under wrapping at both widths and right under saturation at both, and the sabotage arm,
which moves the sentinel off the top to zero under saturation, turns that zero into 24,640
wrong of 495,108 at width three and 653,600 of 23,188,680 at width four, so the clean saturating
column is caused by absorption rather than free.

The row's control does not reproduce, and the reason is an instrument defect. The proposal's
`because` says the max-plus longest path over the same graphs and the same policy agreeing
everywhere is what says the failure belongs to the operation rather than to the harness. Run in
the same encoding as the failing routine, max-plus fails under both policies: 437,868 wrong of
4,112,248 at width four under wrapping and 458,880 under saturation. The earlier instrument's
clean max-plus column is an artefact of how that file encodes unreachability. Its `longest`
carries `[Option<u128>; N]` and skips an unreachable predecessor with `if let Some(bu)`, so no
sentinel is ever added to anything, while its `shortest` stands the numeral's top in for
infinity in band and adds to it. The comparison between them measures the encoding, not the
operation, so the clause supports nothing. The control that does the job is the one my probe
carries at C1, which moves the policy inside one routine and one encoding and leaves everything
else alone.

That matters beyond one clause, because `probe::a_dag_dynamic_programme_returns_wrong_answers_on_in_range_instances`
carries `standing = "sound"` on the strength of that same cross-routine comparison, quoted in
its `control` field. The probe's numbers are not in doubt and I am not proposing they are. What
is in doubt is its warrant for calling itself controlled, and under the `probe` schema's own
vocabulary the honest value is closer to `uncontrolled`, which the schema is careful to say is
not a lesser `defective`: the numbers may well be right and what is missing is any way for them
to have been wrong.

The third thing is the one I would send anybody who works on this next. My own first control was
also wrong, and its failure is the most useful number in the file. I predicted that taking the
sentinel out of band would make every cell clean, on the reasoning that nothing is then added to
a sentinel. Out of band under wrapping still reports 177,408 wrong of 495,108 at width three and
9,947,584 of 23,188,680 at width four. The reason is that an ordinary candidate `dist[a] + w`
can overflow and wrap small even when every exact intermediate is in range, because the exact
intermediate is the minimum and the overflowing candidate is one that loses the minimum, so the
in-range control never sees it. Under saturation the same candidate clamps to the top and the
minimum discards it, which is why saturation is clean in both encodings.

So the mechanism is mostly not absorption, and the row's own `gap` already names the other half
without pricing it: absorption is one of the two properties a min-plus computation needs and
monotonicity is the other, measured in the same file and not filed here. Taking the sentinel out
of band removes every absorption failure and leaves every monotonicity failure, so the
difference between the two min-plus wrapping cells is exactly what absorption was worth.
Absorption accounts for 17,360 of 194,768 failures at width three, 8.9 per cent, and 412,096 of
10,359,680 at width four, 4.0 per cent. The unfiled half is the other 91 and 96 per cent.

That is the finding, and it changes what the row should be promoted as. What holds is not that a
min-plus fold needs an absorbing top. What holds is that a min-plus reduction over a bounded
numeral is correct when overflow maps in the direction the reduction discards, of which the top
absorbing the sentinel is the special case applied to one value. Saturation gives both because
it maps every overflow upward and minimum throws upward things away. Wrapping gives neither.

My predicate, written as narrowly as the run supports:

```
holds for: total_width: W in {3, 4}: exhaustive, over every weight assignment the
           enumeration admits at that width,
           fraction_width: F = 0,
           signedness: signedness = unsigned,
```
