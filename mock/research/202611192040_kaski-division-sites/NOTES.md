# Kaski division sites read against arvo's typed divisor forms

Op ruled on 15 November (`.shared/research/202611150830_stack-path/42-op-settles-the-open-calls.md`, section "A door in arvo for a signed run-time divisor") that what kaski's code does is no requirement, and that the division sites of the new kaski design are read against the typed forms, with a door proposed only if a site remains that the types cannot settle. This note is that reading. It covers the integer and fixed point side of the deterministic simulation in `/home/user/kaski`, branch `dev`, and takes the design as the source: every `mock/crates/*/DESIGN.md.tmpl` and the root `mock/*.md.tmpl` were scanned for division, quotient, ratio, mean, average, normalisation, modulo, remainder, reciprocal, share-of, rate-over and halving language, and about forty crates were then read at the paragraph. Source under `mock/crates/*/src` was opened only to locate the divisor a design sentence refers to, and each row says whether the design (D) or the code (C) was the evidence.

The result is 55 sites: 16 settled by a constant divisor (a), 14 by a zero-free range (b), 6 by a power of the radix or a shift (c), 2 by a compile-time count (d), and 17 that the typed forms do not settle (e). The (e) sites fall into two kinds. Eleven are divisors that are a run-time state in which zero is a legitimate value with its own outcome in the text (an average over the neighbours that pass, a normalisation by a length that vanishes at the core and at the poles, a mean over genes carried, a sea rate over a fetch that is nothing where the water has left). Six are divisors read from a content row, whose sign and non-zero-ness the loader refuses but no type carries. No site, on the design's text, takes a divisor of either sign as an ordinary input. The one sentence that says "a divisor of either sign" (`kaski-engine-carrier/DESIGN.md.tmpl:11`) describes what the carrier's own `div`, `from_ratio` and `scale` do for any input, and is not a site. The (e) sites where a negative divisor is possible at all are those whose divisor is a difference or a sum of content figures, which the pack reader refuses when it is not positive (E8, E9 most plainly).

The canon was not asked. `cargo mock canon find divide` from `/home/user/kaski` was tried once and sat waiting on the build directory lock until the call timed out, so `cargo mock` was unavailable and no further cargo was run. A plain grep of `mock/registry` for quotient, divisor and division found no row that requires a run-time or a signed divisor; the one hit that bears on the question is the op answer recorded in `.data/op-responses/202611130559_network-kept-copies-channels-and-the-zero-divisor.md`, which chose "Explicit per call" for the zero divisor (each site picks a non-zero divisor type or a named `div_or_zero` form). The (e) sites below are the ones that answer would put on the named form.

## How a site was classed

The arvo forms read are `Divisor::constant` (`arvo-format/src/division.rs:148`), `Divisor::of` (`:164`, refused wherever a slot names the zero, which is every whole-phase format today, so it settles nothing run-time in a whole-phase format), `Divisor::member` (`:182`, over a range on the same grid that leaves the zero out, obligation in `division/member_door.rs:32-58`), and `Member::adapted` (`member.rs:108`), which lands any exact value in the member's range by the totality of the applied map. The last matters for the reading: a run-time value clamped onto a zero-free range is a typed non-zero member with no check, which is what the kaski code's `.max(1)` and `.max(1/16)` clamps do by hand. The signed case has no door (`division.rs:121-125`, the FIXME naming the ticket), and `arvo-numeral/DESIGN.md.tmpl:330-334` and `:540-541` confirm that a value cannot yet be divided by a value.

A site is (b) when the design or an evident construction keeps the divisor in a zero-free range that no operation can drive to zero, so that a member over the range, or a clamp onto it that changes nothing the text says, settles it. A site is (e) when the text gives a divisor value of zero its own outcome (a zero arm that is not what a clamp would give), when the divisor is read from content at load, or when an operation can drive the divisor to zero and the text handles that by a case. Where the zero arm is only in code, and the code arm equals what a clamp gives, the site is (b) and says so. The carrier itself returns ZERO silently for any zero divisor (`kaski-engine-carrier/src/fixed.rs:77-82`, `:180-183`, `:245-248`), so a code-only zero arm carries little weight and is marked as such.

Paths below are relative to `/home/user/kaski/mock/crates` and name the `DESIGN.md.tmpl` unless a source file is given.

## The sites the typed forms do not settle

The eleven run-time state sites, in the order of the table, and what in the text keeps each from being a clamp or a constant.

E1 divides by the number of neighbours that pass, which lies in 0 to 4 on a surface and 0 to 6 in a volume and changes as a player builds. The text gives zero passing neighbours the outcome "keeps what it holds", which is not what a mean over an empty set clamped to one gives (that is nought). The same paragraph also reads a wall as "a side a value is turned back from" and gives each face the "plain quarter" with "the part of the weight a lesser share withholds ... kept by the cell" (:57), a constant-divisor formulation of nearly the same step. The two readings are different quantities (weights of one over the passing count against weights of a quarter), so the text states both and settles on neither. If the average over the passing cells is the intended one, the type cannot carry the count; a branch into six constant-divisor arms would, because the count is a closed small set.

E2 divides by the figure that a roll of one in `mean` gives per frame, a logarithm of `mean` over `mean - 1`. A mean at or under one frame has its own outcome (the next frame), so the divisor is zero or undefined on a defined input. The mean is the scaled birth interval (life:88-89) or a content figure, run-time, and arms exist only in code. The text says nothing of the case, which makes this the weakest (e) row; a mean typed to start at two frames would be (b).

E3 divides a position by its length. The length is zero at the core, and contact:27 gives that point the outcome "nothing". The text does not say whether a cell can lie at the core; contact:27 gives the case an outcome, so it is treated as reachable. The divisor is never negative and has one zero.

E4 divides the cross product of the axis and up by its length, which is zero at the poles, and the text gives the poles two outcomes (no north; the tangent of the least-aligned lattice axis for a grid within a degree). The divisor is non-negative with a zero on a set of measure nought and a near-zero band around it that the text treats by the "within a degree" rule.

E5 divides the sum of the reaches carried by their count. With none carried the text's outcome is the axis `default`, which is not the sum over one. The count is a run-time property of a genome.

E6 and E7 are the same shape: a count of things present, zero giving "none" or zero where a clamp would give a member. E7 is a remainder rather than a quotient.

E11 forms no quotient when written as a draw below the sum of the weights (a cumulative walk, as `engine-hash` `below` is "without a remainder"), and the text calls it a share. The all-zero pool still has its own outcome, so the sum is the divisor-like quantity either way.

E12 divides by a pace that the heat curve can set to nought, and the answer then is that the edge is never reached, an `Option`, not a frame. The text does not say pace may be negative; level reads a negative pace as nought in code (kaski-items/src/gauge.rs:70).

E14 divides by a depth that melting, settling and failing drive toward zero; the text answers with "let go when it is gone", a state split. A clamp of the depth to one unit in the last place would keep every bed alive for ever, so it is not the text's behaviour. The nonzero invariant holds because a data structure drops the bed, which no type expresses.

E15 divides a group velocity by a fetch (the code writes it through logarithms, `exp(ln speed - ln fetch)`, with an arm for a non-positive speed, kaski-weather/src/sea/grid.rs:93-100, :132-137). The fetch and the depth are "worked from the lattice's water by `open_water`", and where the water has left a cell the text gives the cell "a target of nothing and a keep of nothing". So the divisor is nought on a defined input and the outcome is a state, not a clamp.

The six content sites (E8, E9, E10, E13, E16, E17) read their divisor from a pack row. The loader refuses a row whose figure is out of its band in four of them (E8 through `Span::new`, E9 through the non-positive arm in code, E10 through "wherever a pack could be read", E16 for `radius` only), and none of the texts states what a zero figure does. A type carries the proof only for a constant or a range, and a figure read from a file is neither, so the proof reaches the division only through a door that classifies a value read at run time (the member door's docs, `member.rs:14-17`, say none ships) or through a clamp that turns a refused row into a one-unit divisor without saying so. A pack whose rows were built into the program as typed constants would be (a). Two of them (E8 and E9) are the clearest places where the sign of the divisor is open in principle: `hi - lo` and a sum of weights written as `Quantity` can be negative in a file, and the reader refuses it.

What the (e) sites need, stated as what the existing forms lack and not as a proposal: for the eleven run-time state sites, a way to turn a count or a length read at run time into a divisor on the arm where it is not zero, with the zero arm written beside it as the text's own outcome (the "named `div_or_zero` form" of op's 13 November answer is that shape); for E1 alone the case split into constant arms is available today. For the six content sites, a decision about the load boundary. None of them needs a divisor of either sign. The door a signed divisor would need (`division.rs:121-125`) has no site in this reading.

## Ratios the design states that the code forms without a quotient

Three sites state a ratio and the code compares by cross multiplication, so no divisor arises: the child's share of a gene carried by the parents (engine-genome:53-56; engine-genome/src/genome.rs:411-417 compares `draw * n < held * 65536`), a garment given to "a count out of a number drawn" (loot:25; kaski-loot/src/draw.rs:34 draws `below(of)`), and the draw under a bound (engine-hash:28). They are noted because a reader of the design alone would count them as division sites.

## What was not read

About forty crates were read at the paragraph where a scan hit (engine-carrier whole; engine-contracts, engine-lattice, engine-genome, engine-nature, engine-agency, engine-advancement, engine-space, engine-volume, engine-hash, engine-ledger, weather, fire, ground, items, work, tread, knowledge, life, starsystem, planet, physics, contact, minds, realm, culture, beings, building, hunting, loot, vocabulary, plants and clock at the hits only), and the remaining sim crates were only scanned by keyword. A division written without one of the scanned words, or in a paragraph the scan did not hit, is not in the table; the table is therefore a floor, not a census. The 55 rows are sites, some of which gather several sentences of one shape.

One float site on the simulation side is worth naming, and is not classed. The star system's draw is worked in deterministic `f64` (starsystem:102-108), and its mean solar day is "the reciprocal of the difference of their reciprocals" of the sidereal day and the year (starsystem:37-39), a divisor of either sign that is zero at a synchronous rotation. The text keeps it away from both by the ranges the draw is held to ("a year of at least a few solar days") and by "prograde". It is float, so it is outside this note, and arvo has no float value to read it against.

Also not done: the kaski canon rows were not queried with `cargo mock canon`, the code under `mock/crates/*/src` was read only where a row says C, and nothing was benched or built.

## Read against the doors that ship, by the job that asked for this note

Added by `arvo-numeral-doors` after opening the cited lines: `division.rs:121-125` (the marker on the signed divisor), `member.rs:5-15`, op's 13 November answer at `.data/op-responses/202611130559_network-kept-copies-channels-and-the-zero-divisor.md:130-136` ("Explicit per call", "a non-zero divisor type, or a named `div_or_zero` form"), and `kaski-engine-lattice/DESIGN.md.tmpl:55`.

The member door's docs say no door classifies a slot read at run time, which is so, and the note reads that as no door at all. `Member::adapted` is the second door, and it lands any slot in the member's format by the totality of the applied map. So a site whose divisor is a count or a length read at run time divides in the shipped types: the site branches on the zero, which is its own outcome in the text and the "explicit per call" of op's answer, and on the other arm lands the count in a format on the dividend's grid whose range leaves the zero out, then takes `Divisor::member`. Nothing in arvo reads the value to decide anything, and the branch on the zero is the consumer's state split rather than a check arvo makes. The six content sites are the same once the loader has refused a figure out of its band.

So every site of the reading is settled by the typed forms, none needs a divisor of either sign, and no door is proposed. The numeral's test `the_quotient_by_a_value` is the shape a site writes.
