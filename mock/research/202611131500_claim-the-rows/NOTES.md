# Which unratified rows the written history shows to be his

The registry held sixty rulings at `stated` and one at `in_force`, and a
hundred and thirty-five live proposals no ruling had stamped, and every one of
them was a question somebody would at some point have put to him. He declined
to be asked them one at a time and said how the bulk of them is settled
instead, which is from what is already written down. This note records that
direction in his words, what was read under it and by whom, which rows were
raised on the strength of it, which were left as they stood and why, and which
go to him.

The reading was done by seven readers, each given a lot of rows and the same
question with no class preferred, and each wrote one block per row with the
file, the lines and the passage. Their blocks are kept whole beside this note,
in `CLASSES-rulings.md` and `CLASSES-proposals.md`, and what is said here about
a row can be checked against its block there.

A row was raised only where the whole of what it says is his. A row with one
sentence of his and one of the panel's is left as it stands, since a tier is
held by a row and not by a sentence of it, and the part that is his is in most
cases a ruling of its own already.

The intent in it is three things. What the written history shows to be his is
filled as ratified by default, with nothing more asked of him. What is clearly
not backed by him, or is against the canon, is brought to him. Everything else
stands provisionally. The choice of sonnet agents and of how many is the
vehicle he named for the reading and is kept for provenance.

## What was read, and how it was checked

The sixty-one rulings went out in two lots and the hundred and thirty-five
proposals in five. Two rulings at `open` were left out of the lots, since an
open row is a call he has reserved and is not unratified by oversight. Every
reader worked in a worktree cut at the commit before any row was raised, on the
same brief with only the lot changed. The brief named the registry as the
governing canon, gave the three classes in the same number of words each, and
told the reader to search the registry, the research tree, the design rounds
and the captures of his words before classing a row as neither.

Each ruling passage a reader cited was checked by script against the cited
file near the cited lines, sixty-one in all. Sixteen passages were then opened
by eye, five from the rulings and eleven from the proposal lots, and each read
as the reader had quoted it and said what the reader took it to say. One lot of
proposals came back with every row in the third class and no passage of his to
open.

Of the fifty-two rulings that are his in whole, thirty-six were raised to
`ratified` with `ratified_by = "op"`, and each carries a `ratification` that
says it was filled by default from the written history, cites the words above,
and names the passage that shows the row to be his. Sixteen were not raised,
for reasons that are a judgement made after the reading and not the readers':
his words there are hedged, or were made general by the row, or steer one
stretch of the run, or he sent the ratification elsewhere. They are named with
their reasons in `PROVISIONAL.md`, and one of them, which he was asked to bless
once and could not for want of an explanation, is in `FOR-HIM.md`.

No proposal is his in whole, so no proposal was stamped. Where a proposal
carries a sentence of his, the sentence is a ruling of its own already, most
often `ruling::every_family_of_the_old_arvo_returns_and_the_shape_is_open`, and
what the proposal adds to it is the panel's. The six rows the value crate was
built on are among these: four have no words of his behind them and two are
his in one sentence, so all six stand provisionally and still want the two
independent reads the plan gave them.

`FOR-HIM.md` holds six rulings and fifteen proposals, the proposals under
twelve headings since four of them turn on one question. `PROVISIONAL.md`
