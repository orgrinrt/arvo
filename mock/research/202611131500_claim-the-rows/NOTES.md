# Which unratified rows the written history shows to be his

The registry held sixty-one rulings at `stated` and one at `in_force`, and a
hundred and thirty-five live proposals no ruling had stamped, and every one of
them was a question somebody would at some point have put to him. He declined
to be asked them one at a time and said how the bulk of them is settled
instead, which is from what is already written down. This note records that
direction in his words, what was read under it and by whom, which rows were
raised on the strength of it, which were left as they stood and why, and which
go to him.

The reading was done by seven readers, each given a lot of rows and the same
question with no class preferred, and each wrote one block per row with the
file, the lines and the passage. Their blocks are kept whole beside this note,
in `CLASSES-rulings.md` and `CLASSES-proposals.md`, and what is said here about
a row can be checked against its block there. An eighth reader then read the
rows that were to be raised a second time, without the first reading, and its
blocks are in `SECOND-READ.md`.

A row was raised only where the whole of what it says is his, and where his
words keep holding, carry no hedge of his own and are not corrected by a later
row, on both readings. A row with one sentence of his and one of the panel's
is left as it stands, since a tier is held by a row and not by a sentence of
it, and the part that is his is in most cases a ruling of its own already.

The intent in it is three things. What the written history shows to be his is
filled as ratified by default, with nothing more asked of him. What is clearly
not backed by him, or is against the canon, is brought to him. Everything else
stands provisionally. The choice of sonnet agents and of how many is the
vehicle he named for the reading and is kept for provenance.

The words are carried into the registry as
`ruling::what_the_written_history_shows_to_be_his_is_ratified_by_default`, a
process ruling of his with the quotation whole, and every row raised under
them names that ruling in its `ratification`. The row corrects one reading of
`ruling::the_panel_finishes_the_canon_without_him`, that nothing may reach him
again, and leaves the rest of that row standing.

## What was read, and how it was checked

Sixty of the sixty-one rulings at `stated` and the one at `in_force` went out
in two lots, and the hundred and thirty-five proposals in five. The stated
ruling left out is `ruling::warm_behaves_as_a_native_rust_primitive_would`,
which a ratified row replaces. The five rulings at `open` were left out as
well, since an open row is a call he has reserved and is not unratified by
oversight. Every reader worked in a worktree cut at the commit before any row
was raised, on the same brief with only the lot changed. The brief named the
registry as the governing canon, gave the three classes in the same number of
words each, and told the reader to search the registry, the research tree, the
design rounds and the captures of his words before classing a row as neither.

Each ruling passage a first reader cited was checked by script against the
cited file near the cited lines, sixty-one in all. Sixteen passages were then
opened by eye, five from the rulings and eleven from the proposal lots, and
each read as the reader had quoted it. One lot of proposals came back with
every row in the third class and no passage of his to open. Six passages the
second reader cited were opened the same way, the ones its hedges and its two
divided rows rest on, and each read as quoted.

A review of the first fill read all thirty-six rows it had raised against
their passages and found that the reasons given for leaving sixteen other rows
applied to some of the thirty-six as well. So one test of five parts was
written down and applied to all fifty-two rows the readers had classed as his,
and it is given in `PROVISIONAL.md` with the rows that fail each part.
Nineteen rows passed it, and those nineteen went to the second reader.

The second reader classed seventeen of the nineteen as his in whole and two as
his in part, and on six of the seventeen it marked a hedge of his own, or his
saying that the statement is intent and not an absolute. Eleven rows stood on
both readings, and those eleven were raised to `ratified` with
`ratified_by = "op"`. Each carries a `ratification` that says it was filled by
default from the written history under the ruling named above, with nothing
put to him on the row itself, and names the passage that shows the row to be
his.

Forty-one of the fifty-two were not raised. Thirty-three fail one part of the
test on the first reading, and eight are rows the two readings did not agree
on. All of them are named with their reasons in `PROVISIONAL.md`, and one,
which he was asked to bless once and could not for want of an explanation, is
in `FOR-HIM.md`.

No proposal is his in whole, so no proposal was stamped. Where a proposal
carries a sentence of his, the sentence is a ruling of its own already, most
often `ruling::every_family_of_the_old_arvo_returns_and_the_shape_is_open`, and
what the proposal adds to it is the panel's. The six rows the value crate was
built on are among these: four have no words of his behind them and two are
his in one sentence, so all six stand provisionally and still want the two
independent reads the plan gave them.

`FOR-HIM.md` holds six rulings and fifteen proposals, the proposals under
twelve headings since four of them turn on one question. `PROVISIONAL.md`
holds the rest.
