# The readers' classes for the proposals

Five lots cover the hundred and thirty-five live proposals no ruling has
stamped, and each reader wrote a block per row. A block names the row, gives
its class, the file and lines the class rests on, the passage quoted from
there, and a note. Class `a` is a row the written history shows to be his, `b`
is a row he does not back or that stands against a ratified row or the record,
and `c` is neither. Where a row is his in one part and not in another the class
names both and the note says which sentence is which.

The blocks are the readers' own and are kept as they wrote them. A path
beginning `op-responses/` is a capture in the workspace's store of his
responses, which is outside this repository. `NOTES.md` beside this file says
what was done with each class.

## proposal::a_law_is_read_off_the_algebra_and_never_declared
class: b
evidence: mock/research/202608072330_the-numeral-canon-panel/85_op_no_runtime_checks_ever_and_stop_policing_law_shapes.md:51-55 (also mock/registry/ruling.toml, INTENTS.md I16)

The row's own words: "A law is read off the algebra and never declared ... a surface a consumer may query, and never a field a consumer may set." The only source of the claim is a panel member's candidate (161_leroy_...md:531, marked "Offered, not settled"), restated by 110, 112, 155, 160; no op passage says it. The row's reasoning (a declared law compiles while false) matches his functional test, so the clash is the word "never", a structural ban on one shape.
options: 1. Reword to the functional form his quote allows: a law a consumer relies on must be one the design can check or derive, so it works; declared laws are not banned as a shape. 2. Keep "never declared" but move it out of the canon namespace to the design tier as a design choice that has to be derived from his functional test, not stated as a canon prohibition. 3. Withdraw the row and let the existing ruling stand alone. 4. Put it to two independent experts to say whether "never declared" is implied by "must actually work" (hence not a policing of shape) before any promotion.
note: class b rests on his stated quote; the row is also `normative`, so no measurement stands behind it.
