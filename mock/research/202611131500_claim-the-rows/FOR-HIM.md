# The rows that go to him

These are the rows the reading could not fill from the written history and
could not leave standing provisionally either, because each one says
something he is not on record as saying, or stands against a row that is
already ratified, or is one he was asked to ratify and declined as it was put.
They are left in the registry exactly as they were. Each is
given here with what it says, what it runs into, and the ways it could be
settled, so that the answer to each is a choice among real options.

A row is named in full where the whole of it is in question and by its sentence
where only a part is. The readers' blocks in `CLASSES-rulings.md` and
`CLASSES-proposals.md` carry the file and lines behind every statement made
here about the record.

The rulings come first, since they are in the namespace that is meant to hold
his own words, and the proposals after them.

## Rulings

### `ruling::the_run_ends_at_ratification_or_when_he_clears_it`

The row says the standing goal is not met until the full canon is ratified by
him, or until he clears the goal himself. The words are his, from the addendum
he put on the overnight goal of the strategy-axis unit in August.

It runs into `ruling::the_panel_finishes_the_canon_without_him`, which is
ratified and has the remaining canon questions derived from what he has said
and gated by the coordinator, and into
`ruling::two_experts_converging_is_a_ratification_and_the_coordinator_holds_the_gate`,
under which promotion does not wait on him. What his words of 13 November
bring back to him is a row he does not back or that is against the canon, and
not the finished canon as a whole. This row makes his ratification the finish
line and those two take it away.

Two more rows carry the same expectation and are settled by the same answer.
`ruling::the_canon_is_settled_in_full_then_he_reviews_it` has him review the
canon once it is settled in full, and
`ruling::the_canon_must_support_a_full_design_and_impl` sets what the canon
has to be before it goes to him for review. Both are his words, and both are
left at `stated`.

1. The finished canon still gets his read and his stamp as a receipt, and a
   new row corrects the ratified one to say so.
2. The row is reworded so the finish line is the canon promoted by expert
   convergence and gated by the coordinator, and his stamp is not part of it.
3. The row is demoted to a record of that one overnight goal, which has
   expired.

### `ruling::the_fused_result_is_composable_except_at_signed_saturating`

The row says the fused multiply-add's result is reachable by composing multiply
and add everywhere except at signed saturating, so the fused operation is a
required arm there and not a required member of any operation inventory.

Nothing in the registry is against the claim. What it runs into is the
namespace, which is declared as holding what he has stated in his own words.
This row has no words of his under it. It is a seat's measured derivation,
reached a second time by another seat, and only the parity bound it leans on is
his.

1. The claim moves to a proposal with the standing its two derivations give it,
   and only the parity bound stays a ruling.
2. The claim becomes a law row carrying its region and its probes, since it is
   a measured claim about a region.
3. It stays where it is and he stamps it as his.

which a question filed as his is derived from what he has already said and is
not kept for him. His words of 13 November bring him the rows he does not back
or that are against the canon, which is narrower than every question.

1. The second sentence is narrowed to questions of intent, direction and broad
   strokes, which the ratified rows still leave his.
2. The second sentence is dropped as belonging to the August goal, and the
   first is kept.
3. Both are kept and a new row corrects the ratified one, on the strength of
   what he said on 12 November about bringing him the questions to settle at
   the end.

### `ruling::the_canon_is_written_once_at_the_end`, its last clause

The clause that he ratifies that act stands against the same two ratified rows

1. The last clause is cut and the rest is kept, as the choice he made that day.
2. The whole row is marked as overtaken by the two ratified rows.
3. The row is kept as the record of that one call, with a note that two later
   rows overtake it.

### `ruling::ingest_is_the_consumers_and_the_c_abi_is_where_it_ends_up`

This one is his statement, and he was asked once to ratify it and declined. He
answered that the label he was shown did not explain what it was, so he could
not bless it or comment on it, and
`ruling::he_declined_to_bless_the_ingest_row_as_written` records that. A
default from the written history does not override a refusal the same history
holds, so the row is not backed by him as it was put and is not raised. It has
not been put to him again with the explanation.

What the row says is that everything written in this stack ends up behind a C
ABI sooner or later and that this is no problem for arvo, since whoever writes
the interface defines it with arvo's shapes and generics. arvo may ship helpers
for casting and converting, and it does not use them in the consumer's place.
The question it answered was who owns data that enters from outside the
program, and his answer was that the consumer does, so the intent that there is
never a runtime check needs no exception carved into it for ingest.

1. It is ratified as it reads.
2. It stays his direction and is not canon.

## Proposals

A proposal binds nothing while it is a proposal, so none of these is doing
harm where it stands. They are here because each one says something the record
is against, and a row of that kind should not sit beside the provisional ones
as though the only thing it lacked was a stamp. Where the row it runs into was
ratified by the experts and not by him, the panel can make the correction
itself under the standing rule, and that is given as an option.

### `proposal::a_law_is_read_off_the_algebra_and_never_declared`

The row says a law is read off the algebra and never declared, since a declared
law compiles while false. It began as one seat's candidate, offered and not
settled, and four later files restate it.

It runs into `ruling::the_canon_does_not_police_what_shape_a_law_takes`, his
direction, which makes the requirement on a law functional and not structural:
it has to work, meaning it reaches one lowered path. The row's reasoning agrees
with that test. The word "never" does not, since it forbids a shape.

1. The row is reworded to the functional form: a law a consumer relies on is
   one the design can check or derive, and a declared law is not banned as a
   shape.
2. The row leaves the canon and becomes a design choice that has to be derived
   from his functional test.
3. The row is withdrawn and the ruling stands alone.

### `proposal::no_multiplicative_structure_survives_a_nonzero_fraction_width`

The row says no multiplicative structure survives a nonzero fraction width for
any policy, sign domain, range or rescale spelling. It was measured over total
widths three to seven, and its own gate already refused it promotion for
saying "any range" over a band of five widths.

It runs into `ruling::the_work_is_predicated_arms_composed`, which is ratified:
a finding holds in the region it was shown in. The mechanism the row gives, that
the rescale destroys ambient associativity before any reduction acts, is not
what is refused.

1. The sentence is narrowed to the band it was measured over, with the
   mechanism kept as the reason.
2. It is split into the measured claim over its band and the structural
   argument, the argument carrying its own region and no measured weight.
3. The wider range is measured and filed as a new claim, and this one is
   narrowed.

### `proposal::a_value_takes_no_ordering_trait_from_core`

The row says a value in a crate that does not introduce fallibility implements
neither `PartialOrd` nor `Ord`, because `partial_cmp` returns a bare `Option`.
It was written in the round that brought the numeral crate in, as a consequence
of a lint, and there are no words of his on ordering traits anywhere.

It runs into `ruling::warms_objective_is_the_intuitive_best_choice`, ratified,
under which Rust is the baseline for intuition, and into
`ruling::the_imitation_is_ergonomics_not_an_arithmetic_boundary`. A numeral
that cannot be compared with `<` is a departure from that baseline. The guard
that refuses a bare `Option` also names its own escape for parity with a
standard trait.

1. The traits are implemented under the guard's escape for parity with a
   standard trait, and the inherent comparison stays beside them.
2. The refusal is kept and recorded as a departure from the baseline that he
   has blessed.
3. The order is offered through a trait of the stack's own, and parity with
   the standard traits is a separate decision.

2. The clause is kept as the working assumption of the first slice, marked as
   one, with the question held open for the port.

### `proposal::the_old_storage_crate_is_gone`, its width-wrapper clause

The row says the old storage crate does not return as a crate, which his words
neither back nor refute, since they leave the taxonomy open. The part in
question is where it sends the width wrappers away in favour of bare integers
at const generic positions. His sentence is that every family returns in
function, and his bound on bare integers there is narrower than the row makes
it: only in const generics, and only where the typed way is truly painful.

1. The row says the width wrappers return in function with their home open, and
   no longer names bare integers as what stands in for them.
2. The row states his bound with its exception as he gave it.
3. He says whether the width wrappers are among the families he wants back.

### `proposal::const_availability_is_the_axis_and_a_trajectory_condition_is_not_an_arm`, its second and third sentences

The first sentence, that a condition available at const time may gate an arm
whatever it reads, is his. The other two say a condition not available at const
time may not, and that a trajectory condition known only at runtime stays a
finding. The file that carries his words says in so many words that they do not
settle this: he said what a const predicate may reach and not what happens
beyond it.

1. The row keeps its first sentence and drops the rest.
2. The second and third sentences become a proposal of their own, derived from
   `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path`, and go to two
   independent agreements.
3. He says whether a condition that is not const-available may gate an arm at
   all.

### `proposal::the_concepts_edge_is_not_an_order_and_wrapping_is_the_test`, its appeal to his intent

The row says wrapping sits in the kernel of the concept on his four times

The measured part of the row, that no order is compatible with wrapping, does
not depend on the appeal.

1. The clause is dropped and the measured claim is kept.
2. Wrapping's place in the kernel is derived again from an intent that does
   reach arithmetic boundaries, and cited.
3. Wrapping stays the first test of a proposed boundary as a matter of method,
   with no appeal to his intent.

### Four proposals that have a strategy state a rate, as one question

`proposal::generation_relocates_the_check_rather_than_removing_it`,
`proposal::what_ships_is_four_arms_with_disjoint_predicates_over_one_generated_artifact_set`,
`proposal::a_strategy_is_a_declared_semantics_together_with_a_weighting_over_the_arms_that_realise_it`
and
`proposal::a_strategy_is_a_preference_over_measurements_resolved_as_a_compile_time_argmin`
each have a strategy carry a weighting over cost coordinates from which the
winning arm follows, in one of them spelled as a rate, the exchange among the
coordinates, and in two of them as a winner table generated from the
weighting.

`ruling::there_is_no_exchange_rate_because_there_is_no_generalisation`, which
he ratified, says no exchange rate exists between objectives and each arm
carries its own justification in words. He also ratified that the strategies
weigh different measurements differently. Whether these rows are against the
first depends on whether a weighting a strategy states is an exchange rate, and
his two sentences pull in different directions on exactly that, so the reader
would not call it and neither is it called here.

1. A weighting that says which measurements a strategy cares about most is not
   an exchange rate, the rows stand as proposals, and the open question of
   which wording succeeds his strategy answer settles between them.
2. It is one. The rows keep what they say about which measurements matter and
   lose the rate and the generation of winners from it.
3. A weighting may exist inside one arm's region as that arm's recorded
   justification, and none spans arms.

### `proposal::a_strategy_is_a_consumer_supplied_weighting_over_measurements_never_over_implementations`

independent axes and the third is the pure weighting this row takes whole. One
statement over the whole table also sits against
`ruling::arms_over_regions_are_the_fundamental_heart`.

1. The row is cut down to the clause he backs, that strategies weigh
   measurements, and the rest is dropped.
2. The row is rewritten so a strategy is mostly a point in independent axes
   with the weighting mixed in, as he put it.
3. The row stays a proposal and the question of which wording succeeds his
   answer is settled against it by name.
