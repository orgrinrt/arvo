# 206. Op: the canon test, and the standards bound

Op's own file. His words verbatim, the options each was chosen among, and what
he left open. Required reading for every member after this point.

The round was put to him after `201` measured that of 103 proposals in the
registry **none is ratified**, and that 15 open questions name something they
block, 12 of them with `decider = op`. Four of those twelve went to him, chosen
as the highest-leverage. **He declined to pick an option in any of the four**,
and the declining is the result.

---

## What makes a thing canon

**Asked:** May the canon carry a proposition that has no predicate?

**Options he was given:**

1. The canon may carry imposed propositions alongside established ones, and it
   marks which kind each sentence is.
2. Everything in the canon carries a predicate, which means an imposed
   proposition is expressed as something else or not at all.
3. An imposed proposition is a design-tier statement rather than canon, so it
   lives one tier down.

> This is something you should decide based on the workflow and soundness and
> validity of the canon and work. If it's a real thing that constrains the work
> and is needed to know, it's canon

---

**Asked, separately:** Where does the line between canon and design fall inside
a candidate's statement section?

**Options he was given:**

1. The line as its author drew it: the impossibility, the decomposition, what
   the axis selects, the vacuity region and the coupling structure are canon,
   and the pairing recommendations are design, because a concrete pairing names
   a mode spelling and would need editing when the implementation moves.
2. All of the section is canon.
3. All of it is design, and the canon carries only the impossibility result.

> This is, again, something that bears on the two priors. I think, if it's a
> rule that can't be avoided or designed away, a law, or something that
> constraints the work of the design, it's canon.

**The two answers give one test and it is not any of the six options.** What
belongs in the canon is decided by whether a thing constrains the work, is
needed in order to do it, is a law, or cannot be designed away. The form of the
sentence does not decide it. Filed as
`ruling::a_thing_that_constrains_the_work_and_cannot_be_designed_away_is_canon`,
answering both questions.

## The container premise, and a bound nobody had filed

**Asked:** Is a declared numeral's footprint observable, so that behaviour is
stated over the container rather than over the declared width?

**Options he was given:**

1. Footprint is observable, under which the clause saying the realisation is not
   part of identity is false as written.
2. A strategy changes a computed value.
3. Two markers over one value set and one realisation map must not be two types.

> Hmm, this is not mine to call, it is bounded by soundness and also the rule
> that demands we provide first-class matlab and ieee754 compatible apis as
> aliases over arvo primitives and such

**He named a bound rather than a branch, and the bound was in no registry row.**
The corpus has held it since before this panel: the standards appear as a test
rather than an inspiration, an earlier pass recorded that the table-changing
files had never once been run against MATLAB, IEEE 754 or SystemC, and the first
gap that test caught was slope-bias, which is why a retirement about the bias
axis exists at all. None of it had reached the demand side. Filed as
`obligation::every_standard_convention_expressible_as_an_alias_over_the_primitives`.

**The question itself stays open**, and it is now open under a stated bound
rather than open in the abstract.

## The operation set

**Asked:** Which operation set does the design ship?

**Options he was given:** name the set now, under which the shape-to-count table
becomes writable; declare it open-ended, under which the table is not writable
by construction and the canon says so; or name a core set and declare the rest
open.

> This bears on the prior answer and isn't mine to call, sine I'm not an expert
> or a domain specialist on maths. This is why you and the panel exist.

**Downstream of the container premise, and his to ratify rather than to
derive.** The panel had recorded this as blocked on him. It is not. It is
blocked on the container premise, which is blocked on soundness and on the
standards bound above, both of which the panel can work.

## What this changes for the queue

**Two of the twelve close.** The canon-form question and the canon-or-design
line are answered by one test.

**Two do not, and their reason changes.** The container premise and the
operation set were filed as waiting on op. They are waiting on the panel, under
a bound he has now stated. **A question recorded as `decider = op` that he
returns is a question that was misfiled**, and the remaining ten want reading
against that before any of them goes to him again.

He also said, of the standards bound and of the maths: this is why the panel
exists. Read the four answers together and the instruction is that **the panel
converges and brings him a converged thing**, which is what `I12` already says
about acks and ratification.
