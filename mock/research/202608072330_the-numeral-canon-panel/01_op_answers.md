# 01. Op's answers, and the correction to what ratification means

**Date:** 2026-08-07. **Status:** the panel's founding input. Required reading for every member.

Four questions were put to op in one batch as the previous panel closed. His answers are quoted
verbatim below, each with the options he was choosing among, because an answer that picks among
choices is unusable once the choices live only in a tool call nobody kept.

One of the four answers is not about its question. It changes what every other answer in this panel
is worth, so it is stated first.

## 0. The correction: an ack is not a ratification

Op, answering the `Precise` question and going past it:

> we don't need to settle this with so loose base. We can explore more. And where there aren't any
> ratifications, it only makes sense to make the experts work it out until they agree and stop
> attacking each other and concede that this is actually good. Until that time, my word is only thing
> that ratifies shit, and the last panel process taught me that I shouldn't go and ratify anything
> before the experts actually agree and have a converged thing to bring to me, with all the angles
> considered and perhaps some options too, alternatives. Until that time, we are wasting time taking
> my opinions as anything other than "yeah checks out, direction is good" acks. Which is important we
> do, but we need to stop treating them as a ratification that stops work on it and closes the issue.
> That only happens once all experts agree that this is the best we could ever achieve, it is
> perfect and there's simply no open things to settle.

Three things follow, and each reverses how the previous panel operated.

**Op's word remains the only thing that ratifies.** That is unchanged and absolute.

**But an opinion given before the experts have converged is an ack, not a ratification.** It means
the direction checks out. It does not close the question, it does not stop work, and it must not be
quoted later as though it did. Acks are wanted and are worth asking for; they are simply not
terminal.

**Ratification is the last step, not an early one.** It happens when the experts agree that this is
the best achievable, every angle has been considered, alternatives have been laid out, and nothing
is open. Only a converged thing is brought to op to ratify.

The failure this corrects is specific and expensive: the previous panel treated early opinions as
closures, which stopped work on questions that were not actually settled, and then had to reopen them
when a later expert found the gap. Several of its checkpoint titles record op restating something the
panel had filed as closed.

**Consequence for the carried material.** Every row in `SETTLED.md` marked RATIFIED was classified
before this correction existed. Those rows are **not** re-labelled here, because whether each one
followed a convergence cannot be decided by relabelling; it has to be read. Until a row is checked,
read RATIFIED as **ack unless the panel had converged first**, and treat a row as terminal only where
the record shows the experts had stopped disagreeing before op spoke.

## 1. Is the numeral space one family, or several?

**Options offered:** one family with a closed shape space (zero-width numeral exists, negative
integer width allowed, meet and join both total); one family with the shape space not closed (meet
goes partial); several families (both operations fail across families, so a common target is always
a named rule); or a written comparison first.

**Op's answer: the comparison first.** He wants the consequences of the three laid out before ruling.

That comparison is this panel's first expert task. It is not a survey: it must state, for each of the
three, what becomes derivable, what has to be named, what the canon has to say that it would not
otherwise, and what it costs a consumer. Knuth's carried result is that every structural failure in
the record reduces to this one tie, so the comparison decides more than its size suggests.

## 3. Warm's headroom rule

**Options offered:** dispatch a second expert to audit the existing bench work; delete headroom now
on the evidence in hand; or keep the ratified rule until something better is proven.

**Op's answer is that this should not have been asked.**

> This is no conflict or case to bring up to me. I was very clear. At some point, somebody has to be
> confident enough about their take on it to write the benches, and once benches exist, it's hard to
> deny what they tell, and if it isn't, then the thing is still settling and there's no reason to
> rule on it

The general rule inside that, which reaches past headroom: **a bench that exists is hard to deny, and
where it is deniable the question is still settling and there is nothing to rule on.** So a
measurement dispute is not escalated. It is answered by someone being confident enough to build the
arm, and if the answer stays contested then the work is not finished.
