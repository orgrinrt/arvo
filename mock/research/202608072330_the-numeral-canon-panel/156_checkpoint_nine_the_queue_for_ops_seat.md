## 2. Whether the canon may carry a proposition that has no predicate

**The item.** The predicate notation is in force and it is exact: a predicate records the region where
a claim was **established**, and a dimension left absent means the claim holds nowhere that dimension
exists. Topic eight's firewall proposition is not of that kind. It is **imposed** rather than
established, so writing a predicate on it would say the design may violate it everywhere unmeasured,
which inverts it. Both signers who examined it agree the unpredicated form is correct rather than a gap
(recorded at Q62, and `146:657` names it "the question this candidate cannot answer from inside
itself").

`151:496` adds that this reaches further than `146` stated: it decides section 6.3 as well, so the
firewall and the count question are **one decision** and op should have that before answering. The
coupling is the reason this item sits next to item 1 rather than at the end.

**The options.** (1) The canon may carry imposed propositions alongside established ones, and it marks
which kind each sentence is. (2) Everything in the canon carries a predicate, which means an imposed
proposition is expressed as something else or not at all. (3) An imposed proposition is a design-tier
statement rather than canon, so it lives one tier down.

**What it unblocks.** The form of every canon sentence, in every topic, including the ones already
written. It is the largest of these in reach and the cheapest to answer.

## 4. Whether the default rounding position is chosen for familiarity or for what it licenses

**The item.** This is an intent question, not a measurement. Topic eight's central result makes it
sharp: the IEEE default is the one mode that is **not free under either signedness**, so a design that
defaults to it has every relocation unavailable at the default (`146:650`). Familiarity and licensing
point at different modes here, and I3 (behave like a native Rust primitive would, which op has stated
four times) pulls toward the first.

**The options.** (1) Familiarity governs the default, and the licensing loss is accepted and named.
(2) Licensing governs, and the default is not the IEEE one, with the surprise named. (3) There is no
single default; the choice is carried per concern, which is the shape I13 would predict and which
neither candidate wrote down.

**What it unblocks.** Whether the rounding topic's arms are reachable at the default, which is the
difference between an arm that exists and an arm anyone will ever hit.
