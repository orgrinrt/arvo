what this settles, in the author's own words
==============================================================================
  My section 6 varied UNITS on one target and measured 24.6%.
  My section 4.3 varied BASELINE PROVENANCE across two targets, and it did so while
  holding units at baseline-relative, without naming either as a choice.
  The two sections sit at two points of the table above and measure two different
  axes. There is no operation that is required in one and forbidden in the other.

  What IS wrong in my file is one clause. Section 6's repair reads 'a per-coordinate
  normalisation applied before the weighting is a change of weighting, and the arm a
  consumer asked for is not the arm they get.' The second half presumes the consumer
  asked in absolute units, which is exactly the undeclared choice. Under relative units
  the per-coordinate figure IS the arm they asked for, and AA1 shows the reporting
  normalisation is then division by one, so the two operations coincide rather than
  competing.

  So the design owes two declarations rather than one: which units a weighting is in,
  and whether the named baseline is fixed once or re-measured per target. Only the
  conjunction buys portability, and the second declaration is what decides whether a
  cost claim compares across targets, because a per-target baseline makes '0.8 of
  baseline' two different absolute costs on two machines.
