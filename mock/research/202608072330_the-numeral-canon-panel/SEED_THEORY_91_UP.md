### 1.1 Number theory and algebra

**Ostrowski's theorem** (`110:1382-1384`, restoring `68:301-309`'s refutation). The panel's claim: the
theorem is what refutes "the numeral tower is a chain, so its finest embedding is unique" when applied
to the full ten-member vocabulary D38 ratified (ℕ, ℤ, ℚ, ℝ, ℂ, ℍ, 𝕆, surreal, hyperreal, p-adic), because
the surreals and hyperreals are both ordered-field extensions of ℝ (hence comparable to each other) while
each p-adic completion is not an ordered field and is incomparable to the rest of that set. **This is a
correct characterization of Ostrowski's theorem's content** (the classification of absolute values on ℚ:
every nontrivial absolute value is equivalent to either the usual one or a p-adic one, which is exactly
what makes the p-adic completions structurally incomparable to the real/hyperreal/surreal line as ordered
structures) and is standard number theory, checkable in any text covering Ostrowski's theorem. What was
built on it: the correction that the design's "finest inhabited system" claim needed scoping to the
sub-ℚ chain (ℕ ⊂ ℤ ⊂ ℚ), independent of every branch above ℚ, which is the part re-derived and
two-read-confirmed at `91:183-195` (files 80, 82) and restored unchanged at `110:1375-1384`.

**Conway's surreal numbers as the universal ordered field** (`91:190-195`, `110:1379-1381`, `82:246-257`).
The panel's claim: surreal numbers `No` are the universal ordered field, so the hyperreals `*ℝ` embed
into `No` under ordered-field embedding, giving a chain (ℝ, `*ℝ`, `No`), while under plain field embedding
(not ordered-field) every characteristic-zero field of cardinality at most the continuum embeds into
every other, so a branch count "above ℚ" is meaningless without naming which embedding signature is
meant. This is Conway's own stated theorem about `No` (every ordered field embeds in it), and is standard
(cited, e.g., in Conway's *On Numbers and Games* and standard surreal-number literature). What was built
on it: the correction to the sixth consolidation's "three mutually incomparable branch families above ℚ"
(itself a correction of an earlier four-branch count), landing on "the branch count depends on which
embedding signature is being used and is not a fact to ship without naming one." **This is exactly the
question the current panel's Q8 ("one numeral family, or several?") sits adjacent to**, though Q8 as
posed in `OPTIONS.md` is about arvo's own numeral families rather than about ℕ/ℤ/ℚ/ℝ/... so the overlap
is conceptual (both are "how many incomparable structures does the design admit, and under what notion
of comparison") rather than a direct hit.

**Lindemann-Weierstrass theorem** (`99:212-220`, `99:256-260`, cited again at `135`'s E5). The panel's
claim: for nonzero rational `x`, `exp(x)` is transcendental, and so are `ln(x)` for positive rational `x`
other than one, and `sin`, `cos`, `atan` at nonzero rationals; combined with the design's own membership
theorem (every arvo value is `m · r^q`, hence rational), a transcendental function's exact result never
lands on a grid point or a midpoint off a finite, per-function, enumerable list of removable special
points (`exp(0)=1`, `ln(1)=0`, `sin(0)=0`). This is a correct, standard statement of the theorem's
consequence for transcendence of values of `exp`, `ln`, `sin`, `cos` at nonzero algebraic (a fortiori
rational) arguments. What was built on it: the "moved count equals site count at every operand off the
removable list" theorem for the elementary-function family (99's E5, `135`'s row E5), i.e. the design
gets the expensive per-value grade bookkeeping for free at exactly this one family, because ties and
exact hits are provably impossible off a finite enumerable set.

**Abel-Ruffini theorem** (`100:481-490`, restated in `100`'s D10 correction). The panel's claim: the
closed-form bivector exponential (what makes rotor interpolation and composition cheap) survives to
rank 4 via a closed-form split into two commuting simple bivectors, but at rank 5 and up becomes a
degree-`floor(n/2)` root-finding problem, and at rank 10 Abel-Ruffini forecloses a closed form entirely
(quintic and higher have no general radical solution). Correct and standard. Not built on further within
my slice; flagged as input to the rotation-representation bench D41 reserves to op.

**Spin(n) dimension `2^(n-1)` versus `SO(n)` degrees of freedom `n(n-1)/2`** (`100:439-490`, a compiled
correction to D10's own stated ground). This is not literature-cited by the panel as a named theorem, but
it is standard Clifford-algebra fact (the even subalgebra `Cl^+(n)` of the geometric algebra `Cl(n)` has
dimension `2^(n-1)`, and `Spin(n)` sits inside it as a curved submanifold rather than a linear subspace,
so storing a general element of the even subalgebra needs `2^(n-1)` coordinates even though `SO(n)`
itself has dimension `n(n-1)/2`). The panel exhibited a **compiled exact-rational counterexample**
(`R1 = 3/5 + 4/5 e01`, `R2 = 5/13 + 12/13 e23`, product has nonzero grade-4 part `48/65`, `RR~ = 1`)
proving the two counts diverge from rank 4 and that the storage comparison D10's own reasoning used
(bivector count `n(n-1)/2` against matrix `n^2`) is wrong from rank 4 on and reverses in D10's own
favor's direction at rank 7 (rotor storage `2^(n-1)` first exceeds matrix storage `n^2` at rank 7: 64
against 49). This is a genuine correction to a design decision's stated ground, kept and confirmed at
`91:939-941` and restored at `110`.

**Boolean algebras as a variety, closed under direct products (Birkhoff's theorem)** (`105:270-287`,
`108:88-95`). The panel's claim: Boolean algebras form an equationally axiomatized class (a "variety" in
the universal-algebra sense), and varieties are closed under arbitrary direct products with operations
defined pointwise, citing Birkhoff's theorem on varieties and Sikorski's *Boolean Algebras* as the
standard reference. This is correct standard universal algebra. What was built on it and then
**sharpened by a second, independent, exhaustive compiled result** (file 108): a Boolean-algebra
homomorphism from an n-lane truth algebra to the one-lane algebra is exactly one of the n coordinate
projections, checked exhaustively over all sixteen candidate functions at n=2 and all 256 at n=3 (`108`'s
`p9`), which means `all()` and `any()` are provably **not** homomorphisms above one lane (they are a
meet-semilattice map and a join-semilattice map respectively, each breaking the other operation), and the
unique homomorphism coincides with the identity exactly at n=1. This is a genuinely sharper result than
the citation alone gives: the citation says products of truth values are truth values (an existence
claim); the exhaustive check says which maps out of the product are structure-preserving (a
classification claim), and it is the classification that actually decided the design fork (whether the
truth contract's "exit" belongs on the algebra or on a separate partial trait).

**IEEE 754-2019, cited at many points, secondary throughout with "primary read owed" flagged
repeatedly.** Specific clauses invoked: clause 5.2 (preferred exponent rules for decimal, minimum of
operand quanta for addition, sum of quanta for multiplication, `138`'s DL7), clause 7 (the five
exceptions and their raising conditions, `93`'s x/0 derivation and `110`'s sticky-flag convergence),
clause 7.6 (`invalid`'s raising condition, still owed a primary quotation as of `91`'s section 4),

**Flocq (Coq formalization of floating point) and CompCert** (`110:876-878`, restoring a finding from
files 27/28). The panel's claim: op's `30b` overturn of D65/D69 (moving precision and exponent bounds to
be primitive rather than derived from field width) was reached by **two independent readings formed
before reading each other** (files 27 and 28), "one from the shipped facade's own declaration, one from
Flocq's two-sided float formalisation that CompCert ships on." This is a genuine external reference to a

**Ziv's staged refinement / the table-maker's dilemma** (`99:234-236`). The panel's claim: correct
rounding of a transcendental function terminates for any single value (via Ziv's staged-precision
technique, escalating working precision until the boundary is provably missed), but the working precision
sufficient for *every* operand of a numeral (the "hardness constant") is given by no closed formula and is
obtainable only by exhaustion over the finite value set or by citing a published worst-case search (e.g.

**Dependent-product / vector-versus-record correspondence in type theory** (`105:340-361`). The panel's
claim: a homogeneous product indexed by a natural number (a vector, `Fin n -> A`) and a heterogeneous
product indexed by a finite label set with a per-label type (a record) are both instances of the general
dependent product `Pi (i : I) . A i`, differing only in whether the fibre `A i` is constant or varies per
index; the bitfield-versus-bitpacked-column relationship is exactly this specialization at two points.
This is standard Martin-Löf-style type theory, correctly invoked as an independent confirmation (reached
"before reading section 2.1's own argument") of a design finding (`104`'s bitfield-as-heterogeneous-product
claim) that had already been reached from the design's own `place`/`materialise` machinery. Two
independent routes to one conclusion, which the file records explicitly as stronger corroboration than
either alone.

### 1.2 Compiler and instruction-set theory (external, checkable independent of the panel)

**LLVM `sdiv`/`udiv` are UB on a zero divisor** (`93:150-166`, compiled and silicon-read). Cited as LLVM
LangRef fact, confirmed by compiling and observing the optimizer delete a zero-divisor check placed after
a division on both aarch64 and x86-64 targets (`93_probes/probe_3`). This is a real, checkable fact about
LLVM's IR semantics.

**ARM `sdiv`/`udiv` return 0 for every dividend at a zero divisor, including at a zero dividend** (`93`,
compiled and executed, aarch64). **x86 `idiv`/`div` trap (#DE, SIGFPE) at a zero divisor**, compiled and
executed under Rosetta 2 on the panel's own host (`93_probes/probe_2`). **RISC-V `DIV`/`DIVU` by zero
return all-bits-set, `REM` by zero returns the dividend** (external, cited from the unprivileged ISA
spec, not executed). These three facts, taken together, are what refuted the "the target's own divide
instruction defines the answer" design instinct (`90b`'s division-by-zero placement): three targets, three
different behaviors, one of them not a value at all, which is a genuine and checkable ISA-level finding.

grid) and reach (wider range) stop moving together, producing antichains of incomparable minimal covers.

second radix, fixed-point-beside-float). This is a genuine, checkable, math-content result, not a design
opinion, and it is directly relevant to the current panel's **Q8 ("one numeral family, or several?")**
and **Q10 ("is the inclusion order's own predicate amended to identify shapes denoting the same value

**The elementary-functions carrier-kind classification** (`99`, full file read). Real, compiled, exact
math: the root family (`sqrt` and n-th roots) reduces correct rounding to a single comparison `r > m`
where `m^2 + r = k*2^F` (exhaustive at nine `(P,F)` shapes against an oracle that never computes a root),
proves ties are impossible by a parity argument (`4r = 4m+1` needs even=odd), and shows the overflow band
is inhabited exactly on identity-free numerals (the same absent-multiplicative-identity defect that opens
sqrt's overflow band also breaks `x*ONE=x`, independently discovered twice in the same stretch per the
file's own note). The radix-power exponential is decidable (exact integer comparison, no ties ever) but
the deciding integer doubles in width per fractional bit (13/25/57/113 bits at F=1..4), which is division's
own exponential-accumulator problem recurring in a second operation family. The transcendentals have no
finite exact carrier at all and their correctness depends on a per-width, per-function "hardness constant"
that is provably a well-defined function of the type's parameters (the value set is finite so a worst
case exists) but is only obtainable by exhaustion (capped at model widths by the const-eval wall,
measured elsewhere in the archive at 28.45 seconds at eight bits with rustc refusing at nine) or citation
of a published search. This taxonomy absorbed the entire family with zero edits to the existing
Kind-1/Kind-2/Kind-3 failure classification and zero new grade generators, which the file itself and
