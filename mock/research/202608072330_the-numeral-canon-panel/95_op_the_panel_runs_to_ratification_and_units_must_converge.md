**Op's own file.** Required reading for every member after it.

## The intent inside it, named separately

Quoting is provenance. Naming what binds is a second act, and these are the four things this file
carries. They are the coordinator's reading of op's words and are marked as that, not as further
quotation.

**One. A unit ends in agreement, or it has not ended.** This is the steer that changes conduct, and it
is aimed at something the panel has been doing. Attack stays vital and is not being demoted: op says so
in the same sentence. What is added is that attack is the middle of a unit and cannot be its end. A
unit that closes having refuted four things and settled none has produced a shorter option register and
nothing else.

So the second half of every unit from here has a different job from the first half. The first four go
wide and break things. The second four are pointed at **converging**: strengthening what survived
attack into a shape the consolidation can state, replacing what failed rather than only recording that
it failed, and reaching agreement with at least something. Where two members genuinely cannot converge,
the honest output is the located disagreement stated precisely, and that is a result. A settlement
manufactured to fit the budget is not.

This does not touch the explore-do-not-settle mode, which governs whether the **coordinator** closes
questions on its own authority. It never could and still cannot. What op is asking for is that the
**experts** stop leaving every question open for someone else.

**Two. The run does not end at the strategy axis.** After that unit's consolidation, the next topic
opens the same way, and the one after it, and so on. The coordinator picks the topic under the criterion
op gave at `87`: strictly bottom-up, and among what is available at the current tier, take what settles
the most downstream at once. That call is the coordinator's, is attackable, and is recorded as such
every time.

**Three. The goal is met only at ratification, or when op clears it.** Not at a consolidation, not at a
tidy stopping point, not when a unit closes well. This is consistent with `87`: nothing moves into
`mock/canon/` until every topic is done and the canon is written fresh from all the consolidations as
one act, and op ratifies that. Op holding the goal open to that same point says the two are the same
finish line.

**Four. Panel work only, with one named exception.** "Avoid any other extra work" is a scope limit on
the coordinator, and it forecloses the drift where a run wanders into unrelated repair because
something looked wrong in passing. The exception is work the panel's continuation actually needs, and
op named the shape of it himself: a missing feature in the mockspace bench harness, upstream. So a
harness gap that blocks pricing is inside the goal. Tidying that blocks nothing is not.

---

## What this changes, concretely, for whoever dispatches next

- Attacker briefs keep their mandate to break things and gain a second one: say what replaces what you
  broke, and carry forward what survived as shape rather than as a verdict.
- Second-half briefs are convergence briefs. Resume a member to answer an objection rather than
  dispatching a fresh opinion, because a reply needs the context that produced the claim.
- A consolidation that reports only refutations has failed its unit, and its independent check should
  say so.
- The known harness defect, thirteen bench variant crates defining a `validate_output` the harness never
  calls, is now inside the goal rather than beside it, because pricing is part of every unit and a
  validator that has never run is a pricing gap.

## What it does not change

Nothing about the provenance ladder, the predicate discipline, the evidence bar, or who ratifies. Op
ratifies. An ack before the experts converge is still an ack. The coordinator still settles nothing.
