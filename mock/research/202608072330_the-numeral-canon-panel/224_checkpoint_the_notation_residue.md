# 224. Checkpoint: the predicate notation's two open questions, derived

The layering derived at `223` puts the predicate notation ahead of the stack work, on the ground that
every finding in the stack states its region in it, predicates are append-only, and a notation change
after the fact would need a rewrite the append-only rule does not permit. Two questions were open
there. Both are derivable now, and one of them was closed earlier today by a ruling that did not
mention it.

**One expert, the coordinator's own, which is the tier most likely to be wrong.** Recorded for the
returning seats to attack, not as settled.

## Q53 transfers: closed by the warrant marker, without anybody noticing

The question asks whether anything but the finiteness theorem transfers from a model width to a real
one, and offers two options: per-claim transfer on one of three honest arguments (a symmetry making
the property invariant under the width, a saturation point past which nothing changes, or an
induction on the width), or nothing transfers, so a claim swept at width three is a claim about width
three.

**Both are true, in disjoint regions, and the notation already distinguishes them.**
`ruling::the_warrant_is_a_token_and_a_clause_on_the_values_side` landed today and says a region
earned by construction rather than by a sweep carries `construction` and a clause saying what makes
the axis unable to enter the argument. **A transfer argument is exactly such a clause.** A symmetry,
a saturation point and an induction are three shapes of one statement: that the width cannot affect
the property. Written in the ratified notation:

```
total_width: W any: construction, <the symmetry, saturation or induction that keeps the width out>
total_width: W in 3..=7: swept
```

The second is option (b) and needs no new rule, because it is what an entry has always meant. The
first is option (a) and needs no new rule either, because the marker that expresses it is ratified.

**So the question is answered by being dissolved.** It presented as a fork over a whole category, and
the answer is the one op has given repeatedly under other names: take the win where it applies, gate
it out from where it does not, and let the arms compose. There is no policy here to pick.

**What the ruling adds that the question did not have**, and this is the part worth keeping: a
`construction` entry obliges the row's `evidence` to name an instrument that varied that axis and
found no movement. So a transfer argument is not merely asserted in prose. That converts the
question's "the canon naming which one per claim" from a documentation convention into something a
check can reach, which is the difference between a rule and a hope.

**What stays open and is narrower than the question was.** A transfer argument of the induction shape
has no differential control available in general, since the whole point is that no finite sweep
establishes it. `219`'s own reply named this cost against its `evidence` obligation and said so
plainly. So the honest residue is: which transfer shapes can meet the `evidence` obligation and which
cannot, and what a row does when its argument is sound and uncontrollable. That is a real question and
it is not this one.

## Q39, an arm's predicate: the stated options are stale and the residue is elsewhere

The question offers three: typestate only, typestate or data with a value-gated arm, or typestate for
selection with data at a declared ingest boundary.

**Op answered the axis on 2026-08-13 and the entry was never amended.** Its own `note` records this:
the axis is const-availability, and the admissible category is whatever is available at const time,
which reaches const functions and const data from outside the typestate, and leaves the typestate
usable inside a const expression rather than being the only source. His later file counting the
rejections of this shape names this one as the third of three.

So option (a) is stale in its own self-description. It calls itself the intent read literally, and the
intent was sharpened past the literal reading before the entry was written. Options (b) and (c)
present a fork over a category that op has refused three times under three names.

**Derived, from the answered axis rather than from the options.** A condition available at const time
may gate an arm, whatever it reads and wherever it comes from. A condition not available at const time
may not gate an arm, because selection happens at monomorphisation and there is nothing there to read.
That is not a policy choice between the three options; it falls out of what const-availability means.

**What follows for a trajectory condition, which is the thing the entry is actually about.** A
condition known only at runtime does not become an arm. It remains a finding about where a law holds,
which is a real and useful thing: a consumer who knows their data satisfies it can select the arm
themselves, at their own call site, from their own const knowledge. **The substrate ships the choice
rather than making it**, which is the toolbox posture rather than a new rule.

The measurement in the entry supports this without deciding it: a value-gated selection materialises
both arms and picks with a conditional select, thirteen instructions against six and three for the two
const-gated arms. That is worse than either const-gated arm, so the runtime-gated form buys nothing at
the substrate level even where it is permitted.

**What stays open**: whether a declared ingest boundary is worth building, so a trajectory condition
is checked once where values enter and is a typestate fact afterwards. That is option (c) and it is a
genuine design question about a mechanism, not a question about what a predicate may read. It belongs
to `validation`, which the layering puts on the format and binding time, and it is filed there rather
than here.

## What would refute both

**Q53**: a transfer argument that is not a symmetry, a saturation or an induction, and that the
`construction` clause cannot express. The three shapes were enumerated by the question's own source
and nobody has shown the list is exhaustive.

**Q39**: a const-available condition that nonetheless should not gate an arm, or a runtime condition
that should. Either would mean const-availability is not the axis, which is op's answer rather than
the panel's, so refuting it is a finding about his statement rather than about this derivation.
