VERDICT
==============================================================================
PREDICTION HELD in both halves.

Reading. Every single-coordinate crossing is canonical, because the coordinate not
moving is shared and so nothing is chosen inside a step. The composite is not: a
crossing that both narrows the representable set and changes the selected reduction
computes a different function depending on which move happens first, and the two
answers are exactly 'the source's reduction governs the loss' and 'the target's does'.
So the endpoints do not determine the crossing, and 'the crossing from A to B' is not
a well-formed phrase unless the systems differ at one coordinate, or an order is named.
