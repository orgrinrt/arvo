# 212. Op: the alias scope, what the demotion actually did, and observability

Op's own file. His words verbatim, the options each answer chose among, and the
one he could not answer because the question was badly built. Required reading
for every member after this.

Four questions, put to him after a comb reduced the queue genuinely waiting on
him from twenty-nine to thirteen. These were the four ranked highest by what
they unblock. **He answered three and refused the fourth on its wording.**

---

## The alias scope

**Asked:** which industrial conventions must be expressible as first-class
aliases over arvo's primitives? The bound he gave for the container premise
names two, five appear in the corpus, and no row said whether the set is those,
more, or a core with the rest open.

**Options he was given:** the two named and nothing else owed; every convention
the corpus carries, being those two plus SystemC `sc_fixed`, Vitis `ap_fixed`
and Algorithmic C `ac_fixed`; or a named core with the rest declared open.

> Let's start with the two named, and reserve the rest named for future, so it
> can't close them out

**A fourth shape, and the reservation is the operative half.** The parity suite
is built against MATLAB `fi`/`fimath` and IEEE 754, and those two are what the
adequacy test runs on now. The other three are **reserved rather than excluded**,
named in the canon precisely so that answering this question cannot be read
later as having ruled them out.

So a candidate that cannot express `sc_fixed` is not refuted by this answer, and
it is not cleared by it either. The set starts at two and the door stays open by
construction.

## What the demotion of his own voice actually did

**Asked:** he demoted his own voice across the prior corpus with one stated
exception, where he explicitly frames something as absolute, in this panel's
transcripts and checkpoints "or other files". The closed formalization panel is
an "other file" and several of its statements read that way. Nine of his
statements there hang on which of them the carve-out reaches.

**Options he was given:** none, the closed panel demoted whole; only the two he
recalls, which he would name; every one framed as absolute, found by reading
rather than by recollection; or that the carve-out restores currency rather than
authority, so a reached statement must be re-derived and put back to him.

> It's a rule of thumb that absolutes will fit but not itself an absolute. There
> must be exceptions. But as a general rule. Otherwise yes, demoted a bit, but
> not entirely. My calls and remarks and statements are still valid for the time,
> and valuable as precedence

**He chose none of the four, and the question was built wrong.** All four
presented the demotion as total and the carve-out as its one exception, so all
four asked how wide the exception is. His answer says the premise is wrong: the
demotion is **partial rather than total**.

Three things follow, and they are different from each other:

- **The absolute-framing carve-out is a rule of thumb**, not a rule. Absolutes
  will generally fit through it and it is not itself absolute, so exceptions
  exist in both directions and no mechanical test decides membership.
- **His prior statements remain valid for their time.** They are not void and
  they were not wrong; what they are not is current authority over a canon being
  written now.
- **They carry precedent value**, which is a positive standing rather than a
  residue. A prior call is evidence of how he thinks about a class of question,
  and that is usable even where the call itself does not bind.

**So the nine statements are neither lost nor restored.** They are precedent,
readable and citable as precedent, and a member wanting one to bind has to put
it to him again rather than argue it through the carve-out.

## Observability becomes an intent

**Asked:** the tenth unit's licence clause says everything inside an unobserved
stretch is arvo's to choose, and its ground was a workspace rule rather than
anything of his. Three members failed to derive it. A workspace rule is not a
ratification.

**Options he was given:** it becomes his intent and is quoted into the catalogue;
it stays a workspace rule and the canon says so visibly; or the clause is not
canon and drops to the design tier.

> It becomes your intent

**Clean, and it lands the same day the panel established what observability
actually is.** Seat 210's derivation of the container premise turns on the same
notion: observability is induced by a signature rather than carried by a
representation. The licence clause now has ground of his, and the derivation
under it has a definition.

## The rounding vocabulary, refused on its wording

**Asked:** which mode vocabulary the canon uses, given that one word names two
distinct operations on a signed domain.

**Options he was given:** six mode names with the ambiguous word retired, or the
older spelling kept.

> Not really sure what this means, can't say anything

**A defect in the asking.** Neither option named the ambiguous word, said which
two operations it collapses, or listed what the six replacements would be, so
there was nothing in the question to judge. **This is the second consecutive
round in which an option restated a row's title instead of carrying what the row
claims**, the first being the ingest and C ABI row in `211`.

It returns carrying the word, the two operations, the six names and what each
costs a reader arriving from an existing fixed-point library.
