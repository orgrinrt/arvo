# Op: the strategy set is not closed, and the carried RATIFIED rung is not trustworthy

**Date:** 2026-08-08. **Position:** after `38`, correcting `INTENTS.md` on the day it opened.
**Required reading.**

## His words, verbatim

> Okay let me just say that the strategy set is not closed at exactly four. These are the ones the last
> panel settled with, and what my amateur ass had written for arvo that we are now redesigning (infer
> from that what you will..), so it's entirely open to discussion and exploration

## What this demotes

`INTENTS.md` opened with three entries marked **RATIFIED**, carried from the prior panel's record. The
first was "the strategy set is closed at exactly four". **It is not ratified and it is not closed.**
`Hot`, `Cold`, `Warm` and `Precise` are the set the previous panel settled on, and they came from op's
own earlier design of arvo, which is the design being redesigned.

So the four names are **a prior attempt at the intent, not the intent**. The number four is open, the
names are open, and the decomposition is open: a strategy set of three, or six, or a different cut
entirely, is admissible.

## The mistake underneath it, which is mine and is larger than this entry

`01` section 0 records op's correction that an opinion given before the experts converge is an ack
rather than a ratification. The panel's own continuation state draws the direct consequence: **the
carried `SETTLED.md` rows marked RATIFIED were classified under the old reading of ratification**, and
were deliberately left unrelabelled rather than being trusted.

`INTENTS.md` was built hours later and imported that rung anyway, marking three entries RATIFIED on
the strength of a classification the panel had already been told was unreliable. The catalogue meant to
hold what is most trustworthy took its top rung from the least examined source in the room.

**Every carried RATIFIED entry is now suspect for the same reason**, not only the one op happened to
read. That is the general form and it applies to anything else imported from the seed on that basis.

## Where that leaves the other two carried entries

**I2, the four intent statements.** The specific claim "each preset names a stated intent rather than a
derived rule" survives, because op has spent this evening doing exactly that: stating intents rather
than deriving them, at `34`, `36`, `37` and `38`. What does not survive is the enumeration attached to
it, since the set it enumerates is open. The shape of the claim holds; its extension does not.

**I3, `Warm` behaving as a native Rust primitive.** This one has independent standing that does not
rest on the seed at all: op restated it in his own words on 2026-08-08, and refined it at `38` into the
intuitive-best-choice objective with imitation downstream. It is his current word rather than a carried
row, so its authority is unaffected by this demotion. Whether a strategy named `Warm` exists in the
final decomposition is a separate question, now open.

## What is now open that was not

**The number of strategies.** Not four by warrant. Four by inheritance.

**The names.** `Hot`, `Cold`, `Warm`, `Precise` are the prior design's vocabulary, and the workspace's
own guidance is that a name surviving scrutiny should be kept and said to have held, while a name
inherited without scrutiny is not the same thing as a name that survived it.

**The decomposition itself.** `25` found the four names are an exact bijection with a two-by-two of
headroom against layout, with zero cells spare, and separately that the arithmetic column is two axes
with each preset answering only one. Under a closed set of four that reads as a tidy coincidence to be
explained. Under an open set it reads as evidence about **what the axes actually are**, and the four
presets as one sampling of a space rather than its partition.

That is a materially different question and it is now the live one.

## The counterweight, which op has stated repeatedly and applies here

Nothing above says the four are wrong. Op's standing framing is that a prior design can have named the
parts well and gone wrong in the execution, and that where a name or a boundary survives scrutiny it is
kept and the keeping is a result. An open set is a licence to question the decomposition, not an
instruction to replace it, and a panel told everything is open will churn vocabulary because renaming
reads as contribution.

The honest position is that the four have never been examined **as a decomposition** by this panel.
They were the ground everything else stood on.

## Standing

**I1 is demoted from RATIFIED to OPEN**, on op's direct word, and `INTENTS.md` records the demotion
rather than deleting the entry, so a later reader can see what changed and why.

The intents op has stated in his own words this evening are unaffected. What is affected is anything
whose authority came from the prior panel's classification, and the correct treatment of that rung is
the one the panel already wrote down and then failed to apply: presume it wrong where it has not been
independently re-established.
