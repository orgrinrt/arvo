--- P1: does the layout observation separate the two carriers? ---
  N13U16: size 2 align 2   N13U32: size 4 align 4
  separated by size: true (predicted true)

--- C1: does it separate two newtypes over the SAME carrier? ---
  N13U16: size 2   AlsoU16: size 2   separated: false
     (must be false, or the observation reads type identity rather than the carrier,
      and P1 would be measuring the wrong thing)

--- C3: does it read real layout? ---
  zero-sized marker: size 0 (must be 0), u8: size 1 (must be 1)

--- P2: does any declared-width operation separate the two carriers? ---
  roundtrip over all 8192 values: 0 differences (predicted 0)
  over all 67108864 ordered pairs: wrap-add 0, sat-add 0, wrap-mul 0, sat-mul 0, xor 0 (all predicted 0)

--- C2: the mutant, so the zeros above mean something ---
  saturating multiply with a carrier-held intermediate differs on 8346078 of 67108864 pairs
     nonzero: true (must be true; if this were 0 the five zeros above would establish
      nothing, because the comparison could not report a difference at all)
  dead route one, addition at the carrier width:      0 of 67108864
  dead route two, wrap-multiply at the carrier width: 0 of 67108864
     Both zero, and both for a stated reason rather than by luck: the sum cannot
     leave the narrower carrier, and a low-bit mask at the declared width absorbs a
     wider low-bit mask above it. Together they say when a carrier is
     arithmetically observable: the intermediate must be able to leave the narrower
     carrier, and the projection must read magnitude rather than low bits.

--- P3: is the footprint available as a const predicate? ---
  const ARM_U16 = "narrow arm", const ARM_U32 = "wide arm"
  Both are `const` items whose value came from `core::mem::size_of`, so the arm was
  selected at compile time and no runtime branch exists. That is the part the
  container-premise rows do not state: the footprint is not only observable, it is
  observable in exactly the place a predicated arm is allowed to read.

  `core::mem` rather than `std::mem` throughout, so the observation is available to
  a no_std crate and the answer does not depend on the standard library.
