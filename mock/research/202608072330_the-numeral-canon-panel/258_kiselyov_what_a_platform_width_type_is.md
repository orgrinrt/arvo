# 258. What a platform-width type is, decided by compilation rather than by reading

Seat 258. Kiselyov. Q26, `question::what_a_platform_width_type_is`, open, `decider = "panel"`.

`195` called this "the cheapest of the nine to close" and `187` called the reconciliation behind
it "worth a dispatch on its own". This is that dispatch. What it adds over both is that the step
they left interpretive is measurable, and I measured it: five committed instruments, seven
paired refusals and eight cross-target builds of one source, every refusal paired with a
control that differs from it in exactly one thing.

**The short version.** None of the four options in the row is the answer, and a fifth already
exists in the corpus and in the registry. The clause Q26's first option is built on does not
exclude a platform-width type, and I establish that by its own criterion rather than by reading
it more sympathetically. What the shipped design cannot do is express one at all on any
64-bit target, refused three ways, with the escape its own diagnostic names closed by its own
obligation. That refusal is an artifact rather than a wall: the same affine predicate with its
endpoints derived rather than declared admits 64 and agrees with the shipped family at every
width the shipped family has. And no canon sentence can currently say where any of this holds,
because the axis it would be predicated over is not one of the twenty-five that exist.

Every claim below is about `origin/dev` at `5644b8f0`, which is the base of this worktree.
Instruments are under `258_probes/` with their sources, their controls and their raw output
committed beside them.

## 1. The answer, as a composition

Q26 asks what kind of thing a platform-width type is and offers four. The honest answer is that
one option holds nowhere, two hold over regions that do not overlap, one is a true sentence
about the canon reached by a false route, and the reading that actually covers the case is a
fifth the row does not carry.

### 1.1 The fifth reading, which is the answer

**A platform-width numeral is a target-indexed family of formats.** Its representable set is
fixed by the compilation rather than by a value, so within any one compilation it is a format
in the full ratified sense and owes and satisfies everything a format owes. The exclusion in
`proposal::a_format_is_identified_by_its_ambient_domain_and_its_representable_set` reaches
dependence that survives to runtime and not dependence the build resolves.

That sentence is not mine. It is `70` L4's, offered "to the register beside its existing three"
at `70:240-250`, and it is carried in the registry as the platform-width half of
`proposal::each_choice_in_the_sequence_has_an_owner_and_a_resolution_time`, at
`standing = "one_expert"`. **I read that row before forming my own view**, when I grepped the
registry for `platform` at the start of this dispatch, so on the headline claim I am
confirmation and not a blind second instance. Section 5 says exactly what I do and do not add.

What I establish that neither carries is that the interpretive step is not interpretive.
`189` section 4.3 put it precisely: "Whether the two rows conflict turns on whether
target-indexing counts as depending on other data, which neither says." Section 3.2 answers
that by compiling both cases against the shipped contract, with controls, and the answer is no.

### 1.2 The four options, one at a time

**Option 1, storage rather than format. Holds nowhere.** Not because the clause it quotes is
weak, and the row's own note misdirects here: that clause is now inside a ratified ruling. It
holds nowhere because the clause's criterion, applied, puts a platform-width type on the format
side. Section 3.2 is the measurement and section 2.2 is the establishing source.

There is a second reason to refuse it that is independent of the first, and `235` section 4.1
found it before me: **the tier the option names does not exist.** "Storage" is already taken
in the canon, twice, in two senses that are neither this one nor each other: the objective the
storage-minimising strategy weighs (`ruling::cold_is_for_cold_paths_and_cold_storage`), and a
role a representation is derived for
(`proposal::roles_derive_representations_and_a_realisation_variant_computes_nothing_new`, and
`question::is_the_role_set_closed`). As a tier of the number concept it is named nowhere, so
option 1 asks a design to file a type into a category the canon has not got, and the design
would have to invent one.

**The clause is ratified and the tier it names is not**, and Q26's first option imports both
while only one has standing.

**Option 2, a degenerate instance of the shape family. Holds within one compilation, and it is
not degenerate.** At a fixed target `USize` is `UFixed<W, 0>` at one `W`, with the ambient
domain, the quantum, the slot range and the phase every other point of the parameterisation
has, and nothing about it is a special case. `258_probes/p02` compiles a target-derived
declaration beside a literal one and reads every coordinate back at const time; they are
indistinguishable through the contract and both are zero sized.

The word "degenerate" is what makes the option wrong rather than the reading. A degenerate
instance is one where a coordinate collapses. Nothing here collapses.

**Option 3, an orthogonal axis. Holds nowhere as an axis of the format.** The compilation
target is already an axis of the compilation, and every const parameter of every format is
resolved along it. Adding it to the parameterisation would put a coordinate into the format
that no value carries, that no operation reads, and that `contains` would have to quantify over
while being unable to vary it within a build. `258_probes/p02` shows the target reaching the
existing coordinate through the ordinary const generic position with nothing added.

It does hold somewhere else, and section 4.3 is that place: the target is an axis a **finding**
wants to be predicated over, and the canon has no such axis. That is a real gap and it is not
Q26's option 3, which is about the format's parameterisation rather than about a predicate's
region.

**Option 4, a different kind of thing the format concept need not account for. True as a
sentence about the canon, false as a reason.** The concept needs no amendment, and that is
already ratified: `proposal::the_concept_is_closed_and_the_inventory_is_open`, stamped by
`ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon`, says a new numeral joins by supplying the concept's
obligations rather than by amending the canon. So nothing has to be added for a platform-width
type. **But the reason is the opposite of the option's**: the concept needs no amendment because
the type is already inside it, not because it is outside and can be left alone. A design
deriving from option 4 as written would build a second mechanism beside the format concept, and
that is the expensive way to get the same answer.

### 1.3 The regions, written out

Predicates in the grammar `mock/registry/dimension.toml` declares, with one exception stated
rather than smuggled: **there is no declared axis for a compilation target**, so the regions
below that are indexed by one cannot be written as a registry predicate at all. Section 4.3 is
the finding; this is what it costs here.

| reading | where it holds |
|---|---|
| a target-indexed family of formats | the whole question, at any target the design can express |
| an instance of the shape family, not degenerate | within one compilation, at any single target |
| storage rather than format | nowhere |
| an orthogonal axis of the format | nowhere |
| a thing the concept need not account for | true of the concept, false as a ground |

For the two that hold, in the notation, over the axes the registry does declare:

```
target-indexed family:  integer_width any, fraction_width = 0, signedness any,
                        radix = 2, container any, operation any, arity any,
                        overflow_policy any, rounding any, chain_length any,
                        strategy any, threads any, target_features any,
                        toolchain = nightly-2026-05-28, build_profile any
```

`fraction_width = 0` is listed and not `any`, because a platform width is a count of
addressable units and nothing in the corpus or in either consumer asks for a fractional one.
`threads any` is written rather than omitted, and it is a positive universal: the claim is
structural, about what a type's coordinates are fixed by, and holds regardless of thread count.
`radix = 2` because every platform width in question is a bit count.

```
instance of the shape family:  as above, and additionally the target fixed
```

That second predicate cannot be written. It needs an axis that does not exist, and writing it
in prose beside the table rather than inventing a spelling is the honest form. Section 4.3.

## 2. What the canon says, opened rather than remembered

### 2.1 The clause Q26 option 1 is built on, and its exact standing

`ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon` is `rung = "ratified"`, `ratified_by = "both"`, and it
stamps four proposals together. One of them is
`proposal::a_format_is_identified_by_its_ambient_domain_and_its_representable_set`, whose
`says` reads, verbatim:

> A format is identified by its ambient domain and its representable set. The representable set
> is a constant of the type: a value set that depends on other data is not a format but storage.

So the sentence Q26's first option quotes is not a passing remark. It governs.

**Q26's note discounts it and the discount is now misleading.** The note reads: "Weight it
accordingly: 'storage' is named once, in passing, in a file about a different topic." Two things
about that. The file is `63_spj_consolidation_the_format_concept.md`, at `:146` and `:627`,
which is this topic's own consolidation rather than a file about a different one. And the
clause has since been ratified, which is the thing the note cannot have known: the note comes
from `OPTIONS.md`, and the ratification is at `213`.

What survives of the note, and it is worth keeping, is the narrow true thing: the **word**
"storage" names no developed tier. That is section 1.2's second reason to refuse option 1, and
it is a stronger objection than the one the note makes.

This is the shape `a-governing-claim-is-applied-where-it-hurts.md` names. A ratified claim
arrived and the question row's own weighting was not revisited, because revisiting a discount
reads as damage while closing a gap reads as progress.

### 2.2 What "other data" means, from the file that introduced it

The clause absorbs, in `63`'s own words at `:146`, "`08`'s locus clause as a condition on Q
rather than an external rider (`55b:62-68`)". So the establishing source is `08`, and it is
where the class is named.

`08_knuth_what_the_one_format_concept_covers.md:278` heads its section "The locus", and its
definition at `:280` is "whether the value set is a property of the datum alone". The excluded
class, at `:282`: block floating point, the shared-scale microscaling formats,
frame-of-reference and delta column encodings, dictionary encodings and run-length
encodings. The reason, at `:287`:

> **no per-datum type can express a constraint that holds between data.** The per-element value
> set of a block floating point element is the union over shared exponents, which is a float and
> is expressible; what is not expressible is that the block shares one exponent.

And at `:573`: "**'Fixed by the type alone' is the locus clause.** It excludes block floating
point, microscaling, delta and frame-of-reference encodings, which are the storage layer."

**Every member of that class shares one property: the value set is fixed by a sibling datum, at
runtime, per datum.** A block's exponent, a dictionary's code, a delta's base. Not one of them
is a compilation parameter, and the clause's reason ("a constraint that holds between data")
does not reach one.

`55b_smith_reply.md:62-68` is the intermediate step and says the same: the exclusion is by the
model once Q is a function of the type alone, and what it excludes is block floating point.

**So the clause's referent is a runtime sibling datum.** Reading it as excluding anything
outside the type's own parameters is a wider reading than the establishing source supports, and
it is the reading Q26's first option rests on.

### 2.3 The binding-time cut is ratified and it is the same cut

`ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path`, I15, ratified: branching is const-time,
erased by monomorphisation and const solving, invalids caught at compile time, and "never any
runtime checks, ever". The canon's fundamental division of when things happen is
compile-time against runtime.

A target's pointer width is on the compile-time side of that cut in the strongest available
sense: it is a `cfg`, resolved before any code exists. A block's shared exponent is on the
runtime side. **The locus clause and I15 cut in the same place**, which is what makes the
resolution-time reading a reading of the canon rather than a repair to it.

### 2.4 What the container premise settled, and what it leaves

`ruling::behaviour_is_stated_per_declared_signature_and_the_premise_dissolves`, ratified,
stamps that every operation the design declares is a function of the declared width and never of
the machine carrier. That row is why a platform-width type is not a container question: its
declared width is what everything is stated over, and the fact that the machine's own width
happens to equal it changes nothing about which of the two the statements range over.

It is worth saying because the confusion is available and cheap. A platform-width type looks
like the container premise wearing a different hat, and it is not: the container premise is
about a declared width being realised onto a carrier, and this is about where the declared
width's own value comes from.

### 2.5 The door, and what a coordinate is

`ruling::the_numeric_door_carries_the_coordinate_set_and_the_two_type_bound_is_not_canon`,
ratified on two blind instances, says what `arvo-format`'s introduction exemption carries out:
"the coordinate set of the ratified parameterisation, spelled in types the stack owns", with the
count explicitly left open.

That row is the reason section 4.2 can say `Cap` is in the wrong question. A capacity is a
coordinate, not a numeral, and the door is where coordinates come from.

## 3. The measurements

Four instruments. Each refusal is paired with a control identical to it except in the one thing
the pair is about, and a control that also refused would void its pair and say so in the script's
own output.

### 3.1 The shipped design cannot express a 64-bit platform width, three ways

`258_probes/p01_the_platform_width_has_no_format/`, six rustc invocations,
`build_the_refusal.sh`, output in `run.out`, every stderr committed.

**Pair A, the shipped family.** `UFixed<64, 0>` and `Integer<64>` are not `Format`s.
`E0277: 'Unsigned<64>' is not an admitted slot range`, twice, because `admit_widths!` at
`arvo-format/src/slots.rs:297` stops at 62. The control is the identical file at 62 and builds
with an empty stderr.

The crate's own diagnostic explains itself, and this sentence is what pair C is about:

> this crate implements the trait at widths 1 to 62. The count of slots is 2^width, and 2^63
> does not fit the signed 64-bit integer a slot index is carried in, so a wider declaration
> would invert its own range rather than describe one. **The trait is open, so another crate may
> implement it at a range of its own; what such an implementor owes is the `ADMITTED`
> obligation.**

**Pair B, an outside unsigned declarer.** Taking that escape for an unsigned 64-bit range fails
on the arithmetic: `Slot` is a newtype over `i64` (`slots.rs:41`), its only constructor takes an
`i64` (`:50`), and `2^64 - 1` is not one. Control at 62 builds.

**Pair C, an outside signed declarer, and this is the one worth reading.** A signed 64-bit range
runs `-2^63 ..= 2^63 - 1`, which is exactly the `i64` the coordinate carries, so every constant
in the declaration is writable and nothing overflows. **I predicted it would compile and that
the signed-against-unsigned asymmetry would be the finding. It does not compile.**
`arvo-format/src/slots.rs:219` caps `Slots::ADMITTED` at 62 for every implementor:

```
error[E0080]: evaluation panicked: declared width is wider than a slot index carries;
the count of slots is 2^width and 2^63 does not fit a signed 64-bit integer
```

The control is that declaration verbatim with the obligation not forced, and it builds. So the
refusal is the obligation and nothing else: not the orphan rules, not the arithmetic, not the
trait's shape.

**That is a defect, and it is small and specific.** The `E0277` tells an implementor the trait
is open and that what they owe is `ADMITTED`; `ADMITTED` then refuses them unconditionally. The
two sentences are in the same crate, about the same trait, and they contradict each other. The
honest form is either a diagnostic that does not offer an escape, or an obligation whose bound is
the coordinate's rather than the shipped list's. **I am not fixing it**: it is a `.rs` edit and
no round is open, so it stands as a finding.

**The test corrected me, which is the suite working.** My first draft asserted that unsigned 63
is unnameable. It is nameable: `2^63 - 1` is exactly `i64::MAX`. The `E0080` that caught it is
committed at `the_outside_signed_sixty_four_first_draft.stderr`. And the reason the crate stops
at 62 rather than 63 is not a width left on the table:
`arvo-format/src/tests/the_inventory.rs:304` derives the bound from the slot **count**, which is
`2^W` rather than `2^W - 1`, because `slot_count` is part of the surface and has to fit too.
Two criteria, two numbers, both right. The corrected arm runs both boundaries over their whole
range rather than sampling.

**Why this matters to Q26 rather than being a separate bug.** A platform-width type is the one
declared width a designer does not choose. Every other width is picked small enough to fit
whatever the coordinate is; this one is the machine's, and the machine's width is exactly the
width of the largest integer the coordinate can be carried in. **So a platform-width numeral
sits, by construction, at the boundary of the family's own self-description**, and any design
representing slot indices in a host integer will meet it there. That is a structural property
this width has and no other declared width does, and it is what a designer deriving from any
answer to Q26 hits first. Section 3.5 asks whether it is a wall or an artifact and finds an
artifact, which is the half of this that a designer needs.

### 3.2 The locus clause discriminates, and rustc says it in the same words

`258_probes/p02_the_locus_clause_discriminates/`, four rustc invocations plus a cargo build,
output in `run.out`.

Three declarations, all written as `Format` implementations.

**Arm one, an ordinary declared width.** `UFixed<32, 0>`. Builds.

**Arm two, a target-derived width.** `UFixed<{ PLATFORM_DERIVED_BITS }, 0>`, where the const is
selected by `cfg(target_pointer_width)`. Builds. Every coordinate reads back through the trait
at const time, `contains` answers over it, and it is zero sized, which is the control that says
no datum was smuggled in.

**Arm three, a block floating point element.** Its quantum's exponent is a field of the block it
sits in. Two spellings, because the first refusal is weak on its own:

- the exponent read off `self` in the impl draws `E0424`, which a reader may fairly answer is a
  fact about where `self` may be written;
- so a second arm removes `self` entirely and takes the block as an ordinary parameter, in hand,
  at the moment the format is named. That draws the diagnostic the clause is about:

```
error[E0435]: attempt to use a non-constant value in a constant
44 | pub fn the_quantum_of_this_blocks_elements(block: &Block) -> i32 {
   |                                            ----- this would need to be a `const`
45 |     <<BlockElement<{ block.shared_exponent }> as Format>::Quantum as Quantum>::BASE.power()
```

Each has a control identical but for the exponent coming from a const rather than a value,
including the borrowed block each is handed, and in the first pair the lifetime parameter as
well. Both controls build.

**So the ratified clause's criterion is constness at compilation, and rustc states it.** A value
set fixed by a sibling datum has no format because the coordinate cannot be written; the same set
fixed by a const has one. A `cfg`-selected width is a const. **Target-indexing does not count as
depending on other data**, which is the question `189` left open in those words, answered by
compilation rather than by reading.

The whole discrimination is mechanical in the shipped contract, and it is why: every coordinate
of `Format` is an associated type or an associated const (`arvo-format/src/format.rs:150`), never
a field. The crate says so itself at `format.rs:9`, that this is "the reason every coordinate
here is an associated item rather than a field". **"A constant of the type" is not a slogan in
this design, it is enforced by the trait's shape**, and that is what makes the clause testable at
all.

### 3.3 One name, two representable sets, four targets, one source

`258_probes/p03_one_name_two_formats/`, `measure.sh`, `measured.tsv`, sixteen stderrs.

The alias is declared once. The build runs for every installed target, twice per target: with an
arm asserting what is true of it at 64 bits, and with that arm cut out.

| target | pointer width | universal arm | family arm only |
|---|---|---|---|
| aarch64-apple-darwin | 64 | builds | builds |
| i686-unknown-linux-gnu | 32 | REFUSED | builds |
| thumbv6m-none-eabi | 32 | REFUSED | builds |
| x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu | 64 | builds | builds |

The refusal is `E0080` on the assertion, at both 32-bit targets, and the control column is green
on every row, so no row's verdict is a target that simply would not build. `arvo-format` and
`notko` are rebuilt per target rather than reused from the host, because reusing the host rlib
would hand every arm the host's width and the probe would report agreement it never saw.

**What it establishes.** By the ratified identity, a format is its ambient domain and its
representable set, so two declarations with different representable sets are two formats.
`USize` has different representable sets on these four targets. **So `USize` is not a format. It
is a function from a target to a format**, which is what "target-indexed family" means, stated
as a compile outcome rather than as a phrase.

**And that is the cost side of the answer, which the phrase alone hides.** A sentence about
`USize` reads exactly like a sentence about `UFixed<56, 0>` and is not one: it is true on some
targets and false on others, and nothing in how it is written says so. The two arms in the probe
are that difference made visible, one sentence each, one of them compiling everywhere and one
not.

### 3.4 There is no axis to predicate a target-indexed claim on

`258_probes/p04_no_axis_to_predicate_a_target_on/measure.sh`, output in `measure.out`.

`ruling::the_work_is_predicated_arms_composed` puts every finding under a predicate, and
`mock/registry/dimension.toml` is the roster of axes a predicate may be stated over.

- **25 axes declared.** None of them is a pointer width, a target width or a platform.
- **21 of the 25 are used** by predicates across the registry. Every key any predicate uses,
  other than provenance, is a declared axis, which is the control that says the tally is reading
  axes rather than matching noise.
- **`target_features` and `container` are the positive control** on the phrase search: 1
  declared axis each and 34 and 238 registry lines. Both nonzero, so the zeros above are zeros
  the pipeline could have avoided returning.
- The registry mentions `platform` on 22 lines and `pointer` on 13, so the subject is discussed
  and is never an axis.

**So no canon sentence today can say "this holds at 64-bit targets".** The vocabulary does not
exist, and under the notation an axis that is absent is not a hedge: it is the claim that the
finding holds in no situation involving that axis at all.

That is the sharpest practical consequence of answering Q26 the way section 1 answers it. **If a
platform-width numeral is a target-indexed family, the index is an axis, and it is not one.**

### 3.5 The wall in 3.1 is an artifact, and the answer is about something buildable

`258_probes/p05_is_the_wall_a_wall/`, one crate, one build, two negative controls committed.

Section 3.1 left a question the answer to Q26 turns on. If a platform-width numeral is a
target-indexed family of formats and arvo can never build one at the width every current machine
has, then section 1 answers a question about something that does not exist, and saying so would
be a better deliverable than the answer.

**The canon does not fix the shape that refuses it.**
`proposal::membership_of_the_representable_set_is_one_affine_predicate` says membership is "an
affine slot function, a quantum per magnitude and a phase". It says nothing about what host type
a slot index is carried in. The shipped `Slots` declares `MIN: Slot` and `MAX: Slot` where `Slot`
is a newtype over `i64`, and that is a design choice with a canon that is silent above it.

So the probe expresses the same predicate with the endpoints **derived on demand** rather than
**declared as constants**: a range is its width and its signedness, and `min_index`, `max_index`,
`index_count` and `contains_index` are functions over those, answered in whatever domain the
caller has. It builds, at 64, at 100, at 126, and at the target's own width.

**The agreement arm is the finding, not the 64-bit arm.** The derived endpoints equal the shipped
family's declared ones at every one of the 62 widths it admits, both signednesses, five
assertions each. The whole admitted set rather than three sampled widths, and a second arm
compares the covered count against `ADMITTED_WIDTHS.len()` so that a macro invocation one width
short fails rather than quietly covering less than it claims. Both were mutated and both fired:
`the_agreement_arm_can_fail.stderr` and `the_coverage_arm_can_fail.stderr`.

Without that arm this would be a different predicate that happens to reach further. With it,
**it is the same predicate and the bound was in the coordinate rather than in the concept.**

**What it does not establish, said plainly.** It is not a proposed design and I am not proposing
one. It hands back a bare `i128`, which is the wrong shape for a stack whose own rule is that no
public position carries a host primitive, and repairing that is the whole of the work this
sketch skips. There are at least three other shapes that would also lift the bound, none of them
tried here: widening `Slot`'s host type, stating the range as a minimum plus a count, or stating
the maximum as an exclusive bound. Each moves the ceiling somewhere else rather than removing it,
which is the property the derived form has and they do not, and that is an argument rather than a
measurement because I did not build them.

**What it does establish is the one thing section 1 needed.** The 64-bit refusal is a fact about
where the shipped design stores an endpoint, not about what the canon admits, so the answer to
Q26 is about a numeral arvo can build. It also gives whoever writes that design the property to
aim at, which is that the observer's domain rather than the declaration's is what an endpoint
costs: a caller comparing two indices of one range materialises no endpoint at all.

### 4.2 `Cap` is in the wrong question, and it is the load-bearing half of the row that gates this

`mock/PRINCIPLES.md.tmpl:219` publishes `usize` / `isize` as forbidden with
`USize` / `ISize` / `Cap` / a domain newtype as the replacement, third column "not built yet".
Q26's `keywords` carry `Cap` alongside `USize`.

**`Cap` is not a platform-width type and it is not a numeral.** `mock/PRINCIPLES.md.tmpl:170`,
live published prose in this repo, spells it `Seq<T, N: Cap>` and `Map<K, V, N: Cap>`: it is the
**type of a const generic parameter** naming a container's capacity. A capacity of eight is
eight on every target. Nothing about it is indexed by anything.

So that one table row conflates three different positions with three different answers:

- **a value at an API position** carrying a platform-sized number, which is
  `obligation::a_platform_sized_unsigned_integer_at_an_api_position` and hilavitkutin's
  `platform_code: USize`. This is the format question and it is what Q26 is about;
- **a const generic parameter** naming a capacity, which is the position op's own exception
  covers in his words in `obligation::a_primitive_for_every_position_a_bare_number_would_take`
  ("No bare usize other than in const generics"), and which
  `ruling::the_numeric_door_carries_the_coordinate_set_and_the_two_type_bound_is_not_canon`
  already answers: a capacity is a coordinate, and coordinates are what the door carries out;
- **a domain newtype**, which is the consumer's own and needs nothing from arvo.

**A designer deriving from Q26's answer and applying it to `Cap` builds the wrong thing.** The
answer in section 1 is about the first position only, and it should be read as bounded to it.

I am not proposing an edit to the PRINCIPLES table. It is a root document and ungated, but what
it needs is a decision about three positions rather than a rewording, and that decision is not
this dispatch's.

### 4.3 The axis, stated as what it would take rather than as a proposal

Section 3.4 measures that no axis exists. `mock/registry/dimension.toml`'s own header says the
set is incomplete by construction, append-only, that "declaring reveals a narrowness rather than
creating one", and that "the set still moves only on two independent readings, because which of
the corpus's keys are axes is a judgement and not a lookup".

So this is one reading and it says so. **A compilation target's pointer width passes the header's
own test for an axis**: it indexes a situation the world can be in rather than a run, a claim is
true or false at each value of it, and section 3.3 exhibits one sentence with two truth values
across it. It is also const-available, so it is in the gateable subset that arms get built over.

What it is not is mine to declare, and I am not declaring it. The second reading is owed, and it
should be on another persona.

**One thing to get right if it is declared.** It is not `target_features`. That axis is about
which instruction-set features the compiler was allowed to use, and its `moves` field says
"whether a lowering vectorises, and which instructions a backend reaches for". A pointer width
moves which format a name denotes, which is a different kind of thing entirely, and folding them
would make the wider axis mean two things.

## 6. The blocker I cleared, which was not arvo's and blocked everything

Reported and then attacked, per the rabbit-hole rule, because it stood between this dispatch and
committing anything, and evidence that is not committed does not exist.

Mid-dispatch, `cargo mock` stopped working. Every invocation reported six
`E0308: expected mockspace_lint_rules::LintPack, found LintPack`, refused to build the
custom-lint cdylib, and printed the BLOCKED banner, which says in as many words that nothing was
checked. **So the entire lint gate was off while the commands still exited as though they had
run.** The commit gate refused.

The cause is a URL spelling. The engine writes `[patch."<url>"]` into the generated lint pack's
manifest, keyed on the url the launcher hands it for `mockspace-lint-rules`, and the launcher
binary carries `https://github.com/hiisi-digital/mockspace.git` as its default. Every pack and
tool in the graph names the `ssh` spelling: the five tool crates under `mock/tools/`, and
`mockspace-extra-lints` at all eight revisions on this machine. Cargo keys a git source by its
url, so a patch collapses only what it is keyed on and those references were a second copy of the
crate.

**Measured all three ways before choosing a fix**, because two of them look equally reasonable
from the manifest: engine on `https` gives six errors; engine on `ssh` with the five tools moved
to `https` gives five, the same error having moved onto `mockspace-extra-lints`; engine and tools
both on `ssh` gives none and `all lints passed`. The fix is `mockspace_git` at the repo root,
which settles it for the repository rather than per crate and pins it so a launcher self-update
cannot flip it again. Committed at `83db4508`, with the tool lockfiles moved from `cf0fb06` to
`b57007c` since the engine has re-resolved `dev` past what `e98b7db3` pinned.

**A finding travels with it and I did not fix it.**
`mock/lints/the_tool_locks_pin_one_mockspace.rs` states the opposite of this in its module doc:

> **The generated lint crate is not where this bites, and the reason is worth knowing, because it
> is the obvious place to look.** [...] What keeps them from colliding there is the `[patch]`
> mockspace writes into the generated manifest, collapsing every reference to
> `mockspace-lint-rules`, the tools' included [...] So the story about two `LintPack` types
> meeting in one dependency graph describes something the patch already prevents.

That is where it bit, and it bit in exactly the shape the paragraph says the patch prevents. The
paragraph is a confident coverage claim above a guard, nearly true, false in the one case that
matters, and its practical effect was to tell the next reader not to look at the generated crate.
Correcting it is a source edit and no round is open, so it stands as a finding rather than a
change.

**The upstream fix is not arvo's.** `patch_section` in the engine
(`src/custom_lints.rs:192`) emits one patch keyed on one url; emitting one per known spelling
of the same repository, or normalising the url before keying, would make every consumer immune.
Its own comment anticipates the rev-spec mismatch and not the scheme mismatch, which is the hole.
