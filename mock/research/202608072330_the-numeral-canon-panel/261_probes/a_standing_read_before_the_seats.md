# A: what the `standing` declaration says, read before `247`, `248` and `249`

Committed on its own, before the three seats were opened, so the ordering `258` asked for is
checkable in the log rather than asserted in prose. The only inputs are the `proposal` namespace
header comment in `mockspace.toml` and the `standing` field's own `description` and `values`. No
panel file, no registry row, no other seat.

## The two texts

The namespace header:

> `standing` is how many independent instances reached it, and it is the field most likely to be
> inflated. Two derivations from one model family on one premise set are the weakest kind of two.
> Two instruments that both lack a dimension agree about that dimension perfectly, having varied
> nothing, and it reads exactly like a result. So the count is over the region the instances share,
> intersected over values rather than over dimension names, and where one instance spans an axis
> alone the honest row cites that instance for that axis rather than the convergence.

The field:

> How many independent instances reached it, over the region they share. `one_expert`;
> `two_experts`, each deriving before reading the other; `three_or_more`; `cross_topic` where
> separate topics arrived at it without citing each other, which is the strongest thing this panel
> produces; `contested` where it is stated because somebody stated it and somebody else disagrees.

## What I read out of them, in five statements

**1. The unit counted is an arrival, not an assent.** Both texts say "reached it" and the header
says "derivations" and "instruments". Neither says argument, agreement, endorsement or support. A
seat that reads a claim and finds it sound has not produced an instance of reaching it; it has
produced a reading of somebody else's instance.

**2. The blindness condition is written into the value, not left to convention.** `two_experts` is
glossed "each deriving before reading the other". That is a condition on the order of events, so it
is decidable after the fact from the record of who read what when, and it cannot be met
retroactively by a seat that has already read.

**3. The count is regional, not global.** "over the region they share, intersected over values
rather than over dimension names." Two instances whose regions overlap on a slice give the higher
standing on that slice only. Where one instance covers an axis alone, the row cites that instance
for that axis rather than the convergence. So a row's `standing` is not a single fact about the row
unless the instances' regions coincide.

**4. The failure the header names is false independence, and it names two shapes of it.** Shared
model family with a shared premise set; and two instruments that both omit a dimension, which
"agree about that dimension perfectly, having varied nothing". Both shapes produce a count of two
that carries the information of one. So independence is a property of the derivation path, not of
the identity of who ran it.

**5. `contested` is not a low count.** It is glossed by disagreement rather than by a number, so it
is orthogonal to the other four rather than beneath them.

## The gap I find in the declaration itself

**`three_or_more` carries no independence gloss and `cross_topic` does.** `two_experts` says "each
deriving before reading the other"; `cross_topic` says "without citing each other"; `three_or_more`
says nothing. A reader taking the values at face value can conclude that the blindness condition
attaches to `two_experts` specifically, and that a third and fourth instance may be arrived at
having read the first two. The header's warning about inflation applies to all of them, but the
header is a comment and the values are the schema, and the values are what a row-writer reads.

**That is the one place the declaration alone leaves room**, and it leaves it in the direction of
inflation rather than of strictness: the easiest way to a `three_or_more` is two blind instances
plus one reader.

## What would change my reading

A text with better provenance than the schema saying that `standing` counts present agreement rather
than arrival, or that reading before deriving is admissible. The schema is not ratified canon, it is
the configuration the canon is stored under, so such a text existing is possible and I have not
looked for one yet, on purpose.
