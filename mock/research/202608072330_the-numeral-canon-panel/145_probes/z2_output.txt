VERDICT
================================================================================================
  Under the conservative reading at 144's k = 0.5, 17.1% of families cannot
  carry the question at all, because at most one of their arms is established
  non-dominated. 144's zero is a statement about the 29 families that can,
  not about 35.

  And the gate's direction of conservatism is not stated anywhere and changes the
  answer. Under the symmetric reading the same corpus at the same k gives a different
  set of askable families, and the count moves non-monotonically in the gate strength,
  which is the shape of a gate whose two comparisons pull opposite ways.

  So the emptiness is partly reading (a) and partly reading (b), and the two have
  different consequences. Where the question is askable and the answer is no instance,
  that is evidence the linear selector suffices. Where the question is unaskable, nothing
  has been established in either direction and the family should not be counted as
  supporting the linear selector.

  What survives without an instance: the construction (144 F144-6), the rate in random
  tables (F144-5), and the cost where it bites (F144-9). What does not: any claim that
  the linear limit currently costs arvo anything, and any claim that the corpus has
  established it does not.
