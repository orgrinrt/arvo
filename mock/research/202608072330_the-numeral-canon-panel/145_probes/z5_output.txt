E1. The scalar placement, and why it needs no measurement
====================================================================================================
  cases: 5460, selections changed by the scalar normalisation: 0
  E1: CONFIRMED, and the reason is that dividing every
      arm's weighted sum by one positive constant preserves the argmin. Nothing could have
      come out otherwise, so 144's 0.0% is the theorem showing rather than evidence.

E2. Is the per-coordinate form exactly the substitution w -> w/b?
====================================================================================================
  (arm set, weight) pairs checked: 5460, disagreements: 0
  E2: CONFIRMED, they are the same operation
      So 'per-coordinate normalisation' and 'change the weighting to w/b' are one thing
      written two ways, and no measurement is needed to call it a reweighting.

E4. Do 144 section 6 and section 4.3 conflict on one fixed target?
====================================================================================================
  (arm set, weight) cases: 3640, per-coordinate normalisation picks a different arm than the declared weighting: 894 (24.6%)
  E4: CONFIRMED, the two obligations conflict
      Section 4.3 requires the per-coordinate form to make a weighting travel. Section 6
      forbids it because it changes which arm a fixed weighting picks. Both are correct
      about the same operation, and nothing in 144 says how a design does both.

====================================================================================================
E5/E6. The dissolution: read the division as the weighting's UNITS, not as a transform
====================================================================================================
  If a weighting is DECLARED in baseline-relative units, then the selection on a target is
  argmin over arms of sum_k w_k * c_ik / b_k by definition, and there is no other weighting
  it should have agreed with. Two things then have to hold, and they are opposite:

  E5: across a pure per-coordinate RESCALE of the target, the relative-units selection
      moves at 0 of 3640 cases (0.0%) -> CONFIRMED
  E6: across an ARBITRARY target change it moves at 2892 of 3640 cases (79.5%) -> CONFIRMED, so it is a change of units and not a claim that targets do not matter

====================================================================================================
VERDICT
====================================================================================================
  One named shared baseline names two operations and the word does not distinguish them.

    Applied once to the weighted scalar it is a REPORTING normalisation. It preserves the
    argmin as a theorem, so it changes no selection ever, and it is what makes two
    strategies' cost claims comparable. This is 144's repair and it is right.

    Applied per coordinate before the weighting it is a CHANGE OF UNITS, identical to the
    substitution w -> w/b. It is what makes a weighting travel across a per-coordinate
    rescale of the target, and it is a no-op for every weight exactly when the baseline is
    uniform across coordinates.

  The 24.6% is the disagreement between a weighting read in absolute units and the same
  numbers read in relative ones, which is two questions rather than one question answered
  wrongly. So the design owes a declaration of which units a weighting is expressed in, and
  once it declares that, both of 144's requirements hold at once and neither is violated.
