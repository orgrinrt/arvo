# 198. A compression quoted back as its source

Two more false claims in briefs of mine, caught by the two seats they were sent to, bringing this arc
to five. `190` records the first three and diagnosed them as figures lifted out of a subagent's report
and relayed without opening the artifact. **These two have a different mechanism and it is worse**,
because the artifact was opened, by me, and what got quoted was what I wrote down afterwards.

## The two

**To `197`.** My brief said `195` found "the algorithm surface is missing filing rather than
derivation". `195` says the opposite, in these words at its section 3.1:

> **So the answer for these five is: nobody did the work, and the work has never been the subject.**
> Not a filing problem.

**To `196`.** My brief said the corpus records eleven instrument-defect classes. `192` line 42 says
ten, and says it twice more elsewhere.

Both seats reported the error against the brief before doing the work, which is the first task
`panels-argue-the-intent-not-the-wording` assigns a member, and both then did the work anyway. `197`
also volunteered a correction against its own earlier file in the same breath, which is the half of
this worth copying.

## Where the sentence came from, and this is the finding

**Not from `195`.** From my own summary of `195`, written at a compaction boundary, which reads:

> Filing, not derivation, is missing on the algorithm surface: `35` (884 lines) and `43` (934 lines)
> derive it with committed instruments; 2 of `35`'s 14 figures and 0 of `43`'s 7 reached the registry.

Every number in that sentence is right. The clause in front of them is mine, and it is the opposite of
what the file it summarises concludes. **The numbers being correct is what made it survive**: a
sentence carrying four checkable figures reads as checked, and nobody checks the clause the figures
are attached to.

Then it was quoted back with `195`'s name on it, which converted my own compression into a finding by
somebody else. **That is the citation loop `open-the-evidence-before-relaying-it-upward` names, run
against myself rather than against a member**, and the existing rules do not quite catch it: they say
open the artifact before relaying a member's claim, and I had opened it. What I had not done was
re-open it before quoting my own note about it.

The defect-class count has the same shape at smaller scale. Ten is in the file; eleven is what I
remembered, and a count is the single easiest thing to check.

## What follows

**A brief quoting a panel file quotes the file, opened at the moment of writing.** Not the state file,
not a prior brief, not a summary, however recent and however carefully made. The rule already exists
for canon claims and for figures; what these two add is that **a summary of a panel file is a
secondary source about that file** and inherits none of its authority, including when the summary is
mine and was written an hour ago.

**And the cost is not zero even when the dispatch succeeds.** `197` did real work, filed nine rows and
reported honestly that coverage did not move, which is a true and useful answer to a question I should
not have asked in that form. The seat's own closing line puts it better than I can: the brief's premise
was the third in this arc that did not survive being opened.
