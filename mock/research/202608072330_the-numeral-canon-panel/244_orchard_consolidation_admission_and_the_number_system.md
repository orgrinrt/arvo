## 1. The located disagreement, which is the unit's centre

### 1.1 The object each reading is about

**`241`, section 2, in its own words:** "Given a candidate, it returns either a total
assignment of the ratified coordinate set, or the name of a coordinate the candidate failed to
fix." A sum with two arms. The predicate and the location of
`question::is_admission_a_predicate_or_a_location` are folds over it.

**`243`, section 5:** at the candidate tier, admission is a total map onto a quotient. Total,

not injective, and the fibres are what R1 ratifies out of identity. The obligations are a
separate relation over an already-total assignment. R2's second clause is a canon-revision
trigger, not a branch of a function.

### 1.2 The success arms are one object under two descriptions

This is my own reading and it is the maximally suspect tier, so it is marked and it needs two
independent agreements before anything may rest on it.

`241`'s success arm is "a total assignment of the ratified coordinate set". `243`'s map is

ratified through `ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon`, ratifies that adaptation choice and
encoding are realisation and not identity. **So the two descriptions denote one function and
they agree about its fibres.**

`243`'s section 3 argues non-injectivity as a refutation of `241`'s location. It is not one.
`241` never claimed injectivity; its location fold is "which coordinate", not "which
candidate". Nothing in `241`'s shape breaks when two candidates land on one point. **Section 3
refutes a bare location reading, which both seats reject**, and `243` says so itself in
section 5.
