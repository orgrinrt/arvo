# 213. Op: the experts ratify, the coordinator gates, and his stamp is a receipt

Op's own file, and the most consequential of the panel. His words verbatim, the
options each answer chose among, and the correction he made immediately after.
Required reading for every member after this.

**What changed is not a question in the canon. It is who may settle one.**

The round was put to him after a measurement answered his own question about how
far a finished canon was: **122 panel-derived proposals existed and not one had
ever been ratified.** Every ratified row in the namespace was him blessing his
own statements. Eighty-nine stood on one expert, thirty-three on two or more.

He had just written:

> How far are we from a real finished canon? I'd like for us to stop treading the
> water and going back and forth, just filling in the existing gaps, naming and
> formalising the solutions as appropriate for canon level, and get this over
> with, so we can start the design + impl phase

and, on the experts:

> And keep asking those question so we get all of them settled since the experts
> seem to be avoiding doing any work unless they have absolute answers to
> everything (which is, by the way, *their* job....)

---

## The format

**Options he was given**, all four selected: identity is the ambient domain plus
the representable set; membership is one affine predicate; arithmetic factors as
an exact operation composed with a named adaptation; the concept is closed while
the inventory stays open.

> I stamp them but all of these are subject to being changed if the experts
> themselves end up disagreeing or finding a better solution or wording or bound.
> These make sense to me, but I'm not a domain expert. So if two experts agree on
> this, it is equal to my blessing. With my blessing on top of it, it's basically
> not really worth much, because these get so theoretical I can't possibly
> understand the implications and all that plays into it. So let's change the
> wording so that if two separate experts agree on it heavily and can reason and
> spell it out, that is a ratification for canon, though distinct from something
> I've blessed. But ratification never the less and should be treated as such,
> and not reserved for me to stamp because this is not my speciality and I can't
> really add anything to it. The stamp is just a marker that I've read it, no
> matter if I understand it or not...

**The four are stamped. The instruction underneath them is the larger thing.**
Ratification is no longer reserved to him, his stamp is a receipt rather than the
higher authority, and a promotion is revisable if the experts later disagree or
find a better solution, wording or bound.

---

## The correction, which arrived before any of it was encoded

He sent it while the schema change was being written, and it is the half that
keeps this from becoming a rubber stamp:

> You will still act as the gatekeeper for the canon, so the experts only
> propose, still, you choose when it is justified and reasoned and demonstrated
> enough, that it deserves to get promoted to true canon

**Convergence is the bar, not the trigger.** Two experts agreeing does not
promote anything by arithmetic. The experts propose; the coordinator reads the
reasoning, the evidence and the stated region, and decides whether it is
demonstrated enough to be canon. So a promotion carries the judgement that
justified it, which can be argued with, rather than a count of agreeing experts,
which cannot.

He had already said as much in the same round, on how the remaining hundred and
twenty-one should be handled:

> As I said prior, if they have strong convergence and agreement between several
> experts, and you yourself think it's valid. Consider it ratified formally. But
> distinct from something I've stamped, so we have the extra heuristics for when
> we later solve things that got wrong. But in general, this is impossible to be
> held against me, I'm not a mathematician or a computer scientist.

**The distinction is kept for a stated reason**: when something later turns out
wrong, which route admitted it is the first useful thing to know.

---

## The organising shape

**Options he was given:** arms with const predicates over the regions where
something holds, composed, ratified as canon; or held back for more precise
wording first. **He took the first.**

> Option 1 is exactly the paradigm. We fill the full space, but instead of one or
> a couple of general statements that are uniform across the dimensions, we have
> small arms and spans that work where they are optimal, and nowhere else. It's
> the fundamental heart within arvo.

**"The fundamental heart within arvo" is the strongest framing he has given any
structural claim**, and it is the shape four topics reached independently, the
earliest of them before the rule requiring it existed.

---

## Parking questions

**Options he was given:** an expert may file only what is genuinely his; keep the
current latitude and let a comb catch the misfiled ones; or an expert never files
a question at all.

> It's fine, but they should first ask their peers in the panel and if they
> converge heavily, if they agree, then that is the answer, and shouldn't need me
> in the loop. They are the experts, the domain specialists. Not me. I can pretty
> much only answer things of intent, the fundamental design directions and broad
> strokes, but anything that concerns specifics, is out of my depth, and is the
> exact reason a panel or you managing them exist in the first place...

**A question is put to a peer before it is put to him**, and heavy convergence
among peers is the answer. This is why a panel has to be a small pool argued
across rounds rather than a relay of single opinions: asking a peer requires a
peer who holds the context, which a fresh dispatch per question does not give.

---

## What this changes, concretely

**The bottleneck was never his availability.** It was a rule reserving for him a
judgement he says he cannot make, and that rule is why a hundred and twenty-two
propositions had no exit.

- **The tier vocabulary now records the route.** A ratified row names whether it
  came by expert promotion, by his stamp, or by both.
- **A promotion carries its own justification**, so it is arguable rather than
  counted, and revisable on his stated condition.
- **He is reached for intent, design direction and broad strokes.** Specifics are
  out of his depth by his own words, and asking him anyway spends the one thing
  the batching exists to save.
