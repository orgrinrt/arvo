reading:
  a criterion with a congruence failure licenses substituting one
  primitive for another inside a composite and getting different
  behaviour out. nothing at the site of the substitution can detect
  it, because at that level the two really are interchangeable by
  the criterion that was applied.

  the weak criterion is what a reader reaches for when a primitive
  is thought of as a set of representable values. the medium one is
  what checking one operation buys, and it fails precisely where P1
  said it would: on a rounding mode that add cannot see and mul can.
  only the full criterion survives, and the encoding control shows
  it is not surviving by refusing to merge anything.
