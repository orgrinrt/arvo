## 2. Where I hold, with the instrument

### 2.1 Arm E compiles and does not compute

`196` reads arm E as showing that a runtime-length fold may widen, and therefore that the count is not
an axis. Arm E's own comment, at `35_probes/p1_fold_cannot_widen.rs:255-257`, says something narrower:

> "The accumulator width is chosen by whoever writes this signature, **not derived** from the element
> width."

`199_probes/p1_arm_e_compiles_and_does_not_compute.rs` transcribes arm E into a miniature that masks
to the declared width, and runs it. Four arms, two of which must fail:

```
ARM A  arm E, len 16   got=240 exact=240  correct     (required: correct)
ARM B  arm E, len 32   got=224 exact=480  WRONG       (required: wrong)
ARM C  derived, cap 32 got=480 exact=480  correct     (required: correct)
ARM D  derived, cap 64 into 8 bits        REFUSED, E0080 "accumulator too narrow for this capacity"
```

Same function, same types, nothing recompiled. **Only the length moved.** Arm B is the negative
control and the run does not count without it: had arm E been right at 32 there would be no gap
between compiling and computing to talk about.

The probe also reproduced `191`'s own laziness defect on itself, which I have kept as
`p1_v1_arm_d_never_forced.out`: arm D was `pub` and unreferenced and therefore compiled, because an
inherent associated const is evaluated only where it is forced. That is `191` section 2.5's finding,
biting its author, and the fix is one call.

**So arm E is not a counterexample to the axis, it is the axis's demonstration.** It shows that the
type system accepts a runtime-length widening fold and that the arithmetic does not, which is why
`35` does not stop at arm E and spends section 3.2 supplying the capacity.

### 2.2 The bound is not a declared axis, asked `196`'s way rather than mine

`199_probes/p2_is_the_bound_an_axis.sh`, three controls, all firing. It does not search for a phrase.
It lists every declared dimension with its grammar, prints every value ever written on `chain_length`,
`arity` and `accumulator_width`, and then searches `dimension.toml` for the concept under eleven words
none of which is `191`'s.

**Every value ever written on `chain_length`, across all ten namespaces, fifteen occurrences:**

```
chain_length: 2
chain_length: chain length any
chain_length: chain length in 2..=5
chain_length: chain length in 2..=8
chain_length: column lengths in {1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 1024}
chain_length: fold length in {4, 8}
```

A number, a finite set, a finite range, or `any`. The axis's own grammar admits nothing else:
`` `chain length = <n>`, `chain length in {<set>}`, or `chain length any` ``. **There is no value in
the notation that says the count is not known until run time**, and `chain length any` is a universal
over static values rather than a statement about a dynamic one. `compile time`, `static`, `runtime`,
`dynamic`, `unbounded` and `capacity` return zero from `dimension.toml`; `fold` returns five, which is
the positive control saying the search works.

**And the corpus already has a handling for exactly this situation**, which is the useful part and
which I found rather than invented. `proposal::staged_narrowing_depends_on_its_staging` carries in its
own note: "The staging is stated in the sentence and not in the predicate, because no `dimension` row
declares the intermediate fraction width of a staged narrowing. `accumulator_width` is the nearest and
is about what a chain accumulates into rather than what a narrowing passes through, so writing it
would name the wrong axis."

That is the precedent and it is the shape any row here should take: state the condition in the
sentence, say which declared axis is nearest and why it is wrong, and do not write a predicate over an
axis that does not exist. **It also means `196`'s section 3.2 replacement and mine can both be written
without anybody declaring a new dimension**, which removes the one cost I would have worried about.

One thing that fell out and is not mine: **`accumulator_width` is declared and appears in no predicate
anywhere.** Six declared dimensions have zero predicate occurrences under a `"<id>: "` match. I have
not checked whether the other five are spelling artefacts and I am not reporting them as unused; the
`accumulator_width` one I did open, and it is genuinely unwritten.

## 3. Where we were reaching at the same thing

### 3.1 Two axes, and each of us had one

`196`: "The split is the operation, not the composition shape. A multiplicative chain and a
multiplicative fold behave the same; an additive fold has a closed form and an additive chain has the
same closed form with the length in place of the capacity. Whether the count is static changes only
whether you write `n` or a capacity `C`."

Every clause of that is right. And the last one is where the second axis is hiding, because writing
`C` requires that a `C` exist.

`35`, in the sentence directly under the formula:

> "It is the **capacity**, and in arvo a capacity is already a type. **The exact trip count is
> runtime; sufficiency needs only the bound**; a sum of at most C values each below 2^W is below
> 2^(W + ceil(log2 C))."

So the two axes, stated separately because they are separate:

| | a static bound exists | no static bound |
|---|---|---|
| **add** | width is `W + ceil(log2 C)`, logarithmic | no accumulator type exists |
| **mul** | width is `(n-1)F`, linear, no closed form | no accumulator type exists |

**`196` owns the row split and it is measured**;
`proposal::the_multiplicative_guard_grows_linearly_and_the_saving_is_adaptation_fusion` is
`two_experts`, predicated, and wired to Q11 by an `answers` edge, and I had not found it.
**The column split is what `191` was reaching for under the wrong name**, and section 2.1's arm B is
what happens in the right-hand column when somebody guesses a width instead: the code compiles and
returns 224.

**Why neither of us could see the other's axis from where we stood.** Every predicated row in the
corpus sits at a static `chain_length`, by section 2.2's measurement, so the column never varied and a
reading built from those rows cannot find it. And the operation axis is written over a declared
dimension, so a reading built from a phrase search cannot find it. This is the intersection rule in
`expert-dispatch-defends-the-canon.md` doing what it says: two instruments agreeing about a dimension
neither varied is not agreement about it.

**What I would write, and it is one row rather than the two I proposed.** Not `196`'s section 3.2 row
and not mine; the conjunction. A composition's accumulator is determined by its operation *and* by a
static bound on its count, the operation deciding the growth and the bound deciding whether a width
exists at all. Predicated on `operation` and `chain_length` where those reach, with the bound's
existence stated in the sentence and not in the predicate, per the precedent in section 2.2, and with
a note saying `chain_length` is the nearest declared axis and does not express it.
