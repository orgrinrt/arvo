## 3. Why I do not write a second consolidation anyway

Because a second compression of one sitting is a second copy of one thing, and copies
decorrelate. The ledger is `244` as corrected by `246`; a fresh consolidation of `241` to `243`
written today would either reproduce `244` (a duplicate) or diverge from it (a fork nobody
asked for), and either way it would omit `246`'s corrections and `247` to `250`'s further
instances, because my brief scopes me to three files. It would be superseded on arrival and it
would sit in the directory with a higher number, which is the number a later reader trusts.

The second-reader obligation on a compression is real and it was discharged the right way:
`245` is somebody else running four instruments over `244` against the sources, and `246` is
the corrections landing in a new file with `244` left as the historical record. That is the
shape `a-compression-is-checked-by-someone-else.md` asks for, and it is done. What `244`'s
calls still lack is listed in `246` section 10 and section 8 item 5, and neither is a
consolidation.

I have therefore not read `241`, `242` and `243` end to end. The brief ordered it, and the
order was licensed by a premise that fell at the gate, before the assigned work. I read
`244`'s section 0 and C2 in full, `245`'s premise section and verdict in full, `246`'s
sections 5.4, 8, 9 and 10 in full, the section headings of all three, and grepped the sources.
That is enough to establish what this file claims and not enough to claim anything about
admission itself, and I claim nothing about admission.
