# 87. Op: the canon is written once at the end, and the next unit is chosen bottom-up

Op, in this panel, on 2026-08-13, answering four questions put to him while `86` was in flight. His words
are verbatim. What he was choosing among is recorded beside each, since the options lived in a tool call
nobody kept.

## 1. Nothing moves into the canon until the end, and the canon is written once

**The question.** Topic one and topic two have finished consolidations sitting as ordinary panel files and
topic three's is next. `where-the-canon-lives.md` reserves `mock/canon/` and records that no repo has
migrated. Nothing stated what turns a consolidation into canon rather than another agent-authored file.

**He was choosing among:** (1) op ratifies a consolidation and the blessing is what moves it into
`mock/canon/`; (2) the independent entailment check is enough and it moves without a further act; (3)
nothing moves until every topic is done, then the canon is written fresh from the consolidations as one
act and op ratifies that.

**He took the third.**

So **a consolidation is input, not canon in miniature.** It is the topic's best available compression and
it has no standing beyond that. `mock/canon/` stays empty until the whole set is in hand and the canon is
written from them in one act, which op then ratifies.

Two consequences worth stating because they change how the remaining work is done:

- **A consolidation is not the last chance to get a topic right.** It is a working artifact that the final
  writing reads alongside the members it compressed. A finding that a consolidation dropped is recoverable
  at the end from the file it came from, which is why the droplist and the compression checks matter and
  why a dropped item is a defect rather than a closed question.
- **The canon is written by someone reading every consolidation at once**, so cross-topic conflicts get
  found then rather than never. Three topics currently carry a placeholder pointing at the same unresolved
  thing, and under this shape that is a conflict the final writing has to resolve rather than inherit.

## 2. The branch waits for the canon

**The question.** `feat/arvo-shape-topic` is 599 commits ahead of `dev` and has never been PR'd. All
research artifacts and probes, no crate source. The workspace rule makes feature PRs into `dev` the
agent's to review and merge, and a 599-commit research branch is not what that rule was written for.

**He was choosing among:** (1) land what exists now as one PR and continue on a fresh branch; (2) wait
until the canon is written and ratified, then land the whole arc; (3) land now and keep working on the
same branch.

**He took the second.** No PR until the canon exists. The branch keeps growing and the arc lands as one
unit tied to a finished artifact.

This composes with section 1: the canon is one act at the end, and the branch is one PR at the end, and
they are the same end.

## 3. The next unit is chosen bottom-up, by how much it settles at once

**The question.** Topic four was named early as "the primitives as named compositions", the top-level topic
the other three sit under. Since then the unit has produced findings that could reorder what comes next.
The options offered were topic four as planned, the strategy set first, or a unit spent pricing what is
currently unpriced.

**He declined to pick and gave the criterion instead:**

> Whatever makes sense next and is natural and gives most answers that settle downstream questions at the
> same time and enable/unblock further design and exploration work. We want to build from ground up, so
> once the ery fundamentals and the basic paradigms and conventions are in place, we move one tier up to
> actually use those to define the things arvo needs defined and then one tier up etc etc.

So the ordering rule is: **strictly bottom-up, and among what is available at the current tier, take what
settles the most downstream at once.** This is `prefer-hard-unblocking-work.md` stated for this panel.

**The coordinator's call under that criterion, marked as the coordinator's and open to attack: the
strategy axis is next, and topic four follows it.**

The reasoning, and it is a fact about the panel's own record rather than a preference. Three topics have
now independently reached the same placeholder and stopped at it. `79` section 5 states the predicate's
dimension list as operation, sign domain, overflow policy, fraction width, representable-set shape, "and
whatever named axis a strategy resolves to", and says plainly that under I1 the strategy axis is not even
a settled name for a dimension yet. `82` and `84` inherit the same gap. Meanwhile I1 is demoted to open,
op has stated the set is not closed at four, and **nothing in 86 files has proposed an alternative set or
tested whether four is right.**

That makes it the lower tier, not the higher one. A primitive is a named composition *of* a format, a
number system, a law set and a strategy, so topic four cannot compose while one of its four inputs is a
placeholder. Doing the strategy axis first is the ground-up order his criterion asks for; doing topic four
first would be building the tier above an unfinished one.

**Pricing is not a unit and is not deferred either.** This unit has run no bench at all, and two files
claim vectorisation wins measured only in instructions per element read off emitted assembly, which the
workspace rule calls an ad-hoc quick spike with no substance for a how-much question. That is a dispatch
inside a unit rather than a tier of its own, and under the workspace rules a bench fork is the
coordinator's call rather than op's.

## 4. Cold's intent survives the strategy set being reshaped

**The question.** `arvo-toolbox-not-policer.md` is written throughout in terms of four named markers and
says Cold storage "is the reason arvo exists" and that arguments about deprioritising it must not be
entertained. I1 is demoted to open and arvo's own agent instructions say the set is not closed at four. An
expert reading both gets contradictory instructions.

**He was choosing among:** (1) the intent governs and the rule gets rewritten in terms of what a strategy
licenses; (2) the rule governs and four is settled after all; (3) both stand, since they are about
different things, with a note added.

**He took the first, and bounded it:**

> The intent governs, BUT in intent alone, not in any arbitrary amount that made sense back when it was
> written. The intent is that cold isn't to be depriorised. Whether the strategies are four or seventeen
> or a billion is besides the point of the intent. Reword it if need be. But the intent survives and
> should be written down in the intents here too, if it's not obvious

The intent and its vehicle come apart and only the intent binds. **The storage-minimising, aggressively
bitpacked path is not deprioritised**, and the count and naming of strategies is beside the point of that.

Recorded as **I17** in `INTENTS.md`, which is where he said it should be, and distinct from I6: I6 says
what Cold is *for*, I17 says it does not get traded away. `arvo-toolbox-not-policer.md` is reworded on his
instruction, keeping its content and dropping its dependence on there being exactly four markers, with the
marker names now read as naming concerns rather than fixed members of a fixed set.
