## 3. The keying rule: adopted, closing two residuals and reducing the third

**A fact is keyed on the coarsest layer whose identity its truth depends on.** Adopted as spec text. Two
identity notions rather than three, because value-uniqueness makes the encoding the value's
representative. The load-bearing half is that keying too finely is not imprecision but a **false
statement**: two faces for one literal denote one value, and a refusal distinguishing them is wrong about
the numbers, delivered with a compiler's authority.

The enforcement already exists and nobody arranged it: the seal file 61 reported as a cost is the keying
rule's enforcement for free. That is the design's parts confirming each other.

The residuals:

- **`Adjustment` gets its own entry point over one shared generator.** Two doors, one reduction. The
  role-swap probe moves a value from 11 to 84.33 silently under one door and refuses under two, and file
  66's symmetry proof depends on the scale-against-offset distinction. Closed.
- **Cross-call-site face identity is per declaration site, deliberately, and closes without a
  mechanism.** Nothing affecting compilation is keyed on the face, per-site display is the better
  diagnostic, and unifying faces would build the exact false-refusal failure the rule forbids. One spec
  sentence tells consumers so.
- **The pricing hazard's control is derivable:** a declaration's price is a fact about the obligations it
  forces, and two arms are comparable only when they force the same bounds. Adopted as bench discipline.

The reclassification lands too: `TotalOrd`'s bit-order comparison, the spectral NaN partition, and the
face-keyed refusal are one defect at three layers, and the general fix (a value-keyed operation consumes
its operand through a canonicalising projection that is the only door) subsumes the `TotalOrd` split
