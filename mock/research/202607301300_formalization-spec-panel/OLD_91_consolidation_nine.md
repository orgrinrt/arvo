## The four design rules, and the two requirements that check the review's own models

Unchanged in count and content from the eighth consolidation; every finding this stretch produced

on how the review verifies claims, not design rules governing what the design contains**, and this
document distinguishes them from the four accordingly.

83 confirmed the container width was already counted in the firing list at `78:128` ("container
widths") without being named as a level, and file 89's own compiled refusal (a moved count cannot

counts is a type, not a twelfth firing.

**The carrier-at-birth rule.** Unchanged in content. One candidate new instance this stretch,

**The layer-keying rule.** Unchanged in statement. Applied, not extended, throughout this stretch:

Its own display clause gains no new gap this stretch; section 1.22's mutation finding is a gap in a
different rule, the only-door sentence, which the layer-keying rule's instances specialise.

**The pricing pillar.** Unchanged in statement, sharpened by one clause this stretch made load-
bearing rather than incidental: rustc guarantees const evaluation in a const position and nowhere
else. A `const fn` called from value position folds or does not fold at the optimiser's discretion;
on this target it left division residue in both a standalone probe and an in-loop decode
(`81:220-239`, `82:456-506`, compiled). The standing test this earns: is a quantity computed inside a
per-element or per-step loop a function of the type's parameters alone? If so it belongs on the type
as an associated const, not a `const fn` called from value position, and it names the width **level**
it is a function of, because two levels coinciding at the one preset everyone measures is exactly how
a compile-time fact computed from the wrong level survives review (`83:290-316`, the fields/stored/
container distinction section 1.22 states in full). Known sites: the bitpacked decode and encode
plans (on the stored width), the value mask (on the fields), the far point and the write granule
(unchecked, flagged), the digest's own field mask (a second consumer of an already-named site,
unchecked). A known non-site: `Encoding::Canonical`'s trailing-zero removal, genuinely data-
dependent.

**The definitional-completeness line, a requirement on ratification, not a design rule.** *When a
structure is ratified, every term in its definition is either defined or named open in the ratifying

**The separation requirement, a requirement on the review's own models, not a design rule.** *A claim

### 1.14 The grade: two counts at two layers, and the design has been committed to both at once

**This is the largest single finding of the stretch, ruled persona-decided at `90b`, and every
occurrence of "the grade" or "an event" in this document names its count from here forward.**

The design's grade was ratified as a free commutative monoid over refusal causes and quantisation
events (`37:507-511`, carried through files 49, 50, `58` section 1.14, `78`). What was never given
was a membership predicate for the generator "event": an operation may contain a **quantiser site**,
a place where an exact intermediate is rounded onto a target grid and classified against a range,
and whether a site's rounding **changes** the value is a fact about the data reaching it, not about
the operation's type. Two readings of "how many events did this term raise" both survived, in
ratified or standing artifacts, on both sides:

- **The site count.** The multiset of quantiser sites a term contains, charged whether or not a site
  moves a value. A function of the monomorphised type. This is file 37's ratified finest-view table
  under one reading, and it is what the published-grade contract, `Folded<N>` as a type parameter
  (`37:441-444`, `49:464-475`), can be inhabited by at all: a moved count is refused with `E0435`
  when asked to fill it, compiled (`89_probes/probe_3b`).
- **The moved count.** The multiset of sites at which the delivered value differs from the exact
  one. A function of the values. This is IEEE 754's own raising condition for `inexact`, `underflow`,
  and `overflow`, and it is what file 37's probe actually computes (`resolve`, the only site that
  sets `e: 1`, fires only when the exact partial sum leaves the range, verified at source,
  `37_probes/probe_1:169-210`).

**The design was committed to both at once, in different ratified organs, for forty-odd files, and
nobody noticed because the two readings agree everywhere the review had checked them.** Region 1
(the exact family: `mul_full`, division by a fixed constant) is vacuous under both. Region 2 (the
fold family's own laws) hides the difference: every grouping of an n-element fold has exactly n-1
sites, so under the site count the event component of every associativity law holds by construction,
for every input, and file 37's own view lattice measures nothing there. Region 3 (a conditional
resolution, a conditionally exact quotient) is where the two readings actually differ, and it is
exactly where file 37 measured and file 43 assumed, compiled side by side (`89_probes/probe_1`): on
signed `Hot` wrapping the finest view moves from `(Exact, Ignore)` under the moved count to `(Exact,
Exact)` under the site count, and the whole measured lattice collapses from a genuine incomparable
pair to a chain under the site count, which is the review's own strongest evidence for building a
finest-view lattice at all rather than three named relations. The separation requirement caught this
in its own first stretch of existence (`89` section 3, `90:326-342`).

**The ruling, adopted close to verbatim from file 89's own proposal, its layer assignment two-read
confirmed (89 formed independently, then 90 re-read 89's section 8 and agreed).** The two counts key
at two different layers by the layer-keying rule's own coarsest-layer test, and a design carrying
both keys each where it belongs rather than picking one and calling the other its approximation.
**The site count is an operation-layer fact**: a function of the monomorphised type, the published
grade's own parameter, costing one instruction outside any loop, a sound pessimistic upper bound on
the moved count in the same over-approximating direction the design takes everywhere on lattice
containment. It is what a caller's `Folded<N>` contract carries and what a compile-time worst-case
error bound reads. **The moved count is a value-layer fact**: IEEE's own raising condition, read by
law verdicts and conformance compositions, never published by default, because a numeral publishing
it needs bookkeeping the ratified `Warm` float row forbids by name (correctly-rounded intermediates
delivered free by the hardware, invisibly; adding a flag read or a recompute step is exactly the
bookkeeping that row rejects, `78:448-455`). A numeral wanting it takes `Door = Quantised`. Measured,
the price of publishing the moved count is one instruction outside the loop for the site count
against seven to nine times the instruction count for the moved count on the float side, either
route (an `mrs`/`msr` FPSR flag read that stops the loop unrolling entirely, or a five-operation
recompute), and on the fixed-point side both counting and presence at the moved-count level cost the
same 5.0 instructions per element, doubling the loop body, so the intuition that presence is the
cheap level is false on this shape.

**Multiplicities live in the underlying monoid, appear only inside a law's verdict, never in a
shipped carrier.** A shared operand contributes once under an idempotent join (`49:490-497`); this
is independent of the site-versus-moved question and resolves the dispatch's own second reading of
the counting question ("carries it through" under sharing) without needing a rule change. One
derived consequence worth carrying: the site count at the `Exact` view is not computable from what a
value carries, only from the term at compile time, because two values with identical grades and
identical operations must give the same answer under a value-carried grade, and the site count under
sharing does not; the moved count is computable from either. The two readings therefore live at
different places in the design, not two settings of one dial.

**The cost, stated rather than smoothed.** Every "the grade" in the corpus now says which count.
`Folded<N>`'s own `N` is unambiguously the site count, the moved count riding beside the value where
a conformance surface asks for it. This is the cost `89:697-700` names and `90b` accepts paying once,
here.

taxonomy, applied to a specific operation first.**

**A failure is a point at which an operation cannot produce a datum of its result numeral, and there
are exactly three kinds.** Kind 1, the result value exists and lies outside the result numeral's
value set: a range event, the `Resolution` axis's own domain, best read as a totalisation axis whose
four members are four ways of making an otherwise partial operation total, ordered by how much they
lie. Kind 2, the result value does not exist mathematically: division by zero, `Recip` at zero,
`Sqrt` of a negative in a real domain, no range involved, its grade generators `invalid` and
`divideByZero`, ratified since file 50 unchanged through four consolidations. Kind 3, the operand is
not a datum: closed by construction, statement 0 already covers it, not a runtime failure kind in
this design at all.

**`quantize`'s hard failure is kind 1, not a new kind.** The operation targets `At<N, Q>`, the
numeral sharing `N`'s radix, precision, and domain with its exponent fixed at the requested quantum
`Q`, which is not new vocabulary but the constant exponent function the design's own founding
identity (fixed point and float as one formalisation differing in the exponent form, unchanged since
file 40) already requires the sealed `Exponent` vocabulary to express (independently confirmed at
`85` section 2.2, and typechecked as an ordinary instance of the sealed grammar at a model tower,
`86_probes/probe_3`). The failure is `OverRange` on `At<N, Q>`, checked cell for cell against the
design's own extended-grid overflow boundary over 16,000,000 operand pairs at a real decimal model:
three independently-stated predicates ("needs more than p digits," "above the far point," "past the
extended-grid rounding boundary") agree on all 5,679,000 refusal cells, a theorem rather than a model
coincidence, because the quotient of the operand's value by the quantum is never strictly inside the
half-ulp overflow window. The soft event (a coarser quantum rounds and delivers) is not a failure at
all; it is already the design's own `inexact` quantisation generator, ratified since file 50 and
never previously connected to `quantize`'s own value-determinism carve-out.

**The typed form removes the standard's own value-determinism carve-out, and the two forms are
confirmed to be the same fact seen from two sides.** `quantize`'s status as IEEE 754's one exception
to value-determinism is entirely an artifact of taking the quantum from a second datum; with the
quantum as a type parameter, `quantise::<Q>`, the result value is a function of the operand's value
alone, the carve-out does not apply, and the operation is value-keyed and law-eligible. Confirmed
(`86` section 1.2): `roundToIntegralExact` is `quantise::<0>` plus exactly one resolution the pinned
form is denied (exponent escape, the standard's own preferred-exponent freedom), so file 80's
decomposition (a value-keyed law plus a datum-keyed exponent selection) and the typed quantum's
carve-out removal are one mechanism, not two findings. Both forms ship; the datum-taking form stays
pair-keyed and never a law, a diagnostic rather than a directive. Measured: the typed form vectorises
two-wide with no branch but the loop back-edge, against a dynamic loop that keeps a hardware division
and a divide-by-zero check inside the body, because nothing in the program states the quantum.
Conformance to the standard's own invalid-operation behaviour is a declaration-site bound, refused
with `E0277`, not a runtime check.

**Which row governs `quantize` at a mixed-preset target is derivable, not a table fork**, by file
70's own method applied fresh: the row is whatever the target's own lowering gives away for free, so
a fixed-exponent decimal target through a software quantiser goes to the far point and a fixed-
exponent binary target in a native integer container reduces modulo, and the two tables were always
one derivation applied twice.

**The three-of-four-presets-have-no-refusal fork this raised at file 82 dissolves rather than needs
an answer**, because the failure is an ordinary range event and the presets already have their
`OverRange` rows.

### 1.22 External images: the byte image's third level, the digest contract, and the mutation perimeter

**This is the largest content change this stretch besides the grade, and it folds three prior
chapters (`72`, `73`, section 1.22 of the eighth consolidation) plus five deliverables (`80`, `83`,
`86`, `87`, `88`) into one shape.**

**The design has three width levels and exactly one width axis, confirmed on two independent reads
(`83`, then `85`).** A level is a place in the map chain where bits physically exist; an axis is a
declaration a consumer makes. The **fields extent** (`W_F`) is what `Encoding::Fields` occupies,
derived from the `Numeral`'s parameters, keyed on `Encoding`, statement 0's own quantifier domain.
The **stored width** (`W_S`) is the carrier, the one declared level, the `Lowering` axis (section
1.21), statement P's own domain (`[W_F, W_S)`), whose vacuity at `minimum` is the true statement that
no declared padding exists there. The **container width** (`W_C`) is what the dispatch actually
allocates, never declared: a type-valued projection of the stored width through the dispatch menu
under `Layout::Dense`, and the group arithmetic under `Layout::Bitpacked` (below). Two candidate
levels dissolve rather than join the table: `LogicalWidth` survives only as the friendly name of the
fields' extent, no longer a primitive `Numeral` axis; the container is never an axis, since declaring
it independently of its own projection would let a declaration disagree with a derived fact, exactly
the layer-keying rule's own named failure.

**Three maps, not two, correcting the byte-image chapter's own prior framing.** `embed : D -> Carrier`
carries statement P and the padding law, forced by a purity argument (a one-argument pure function of
the datum cannot express "preserve whatever padding was already there," compiled both halves,
`73_probes/probe_2`). `place : Carrier -> Container`, the previously unnamed middle map, carries the
new **statement C**: *the container's bits outside the carrier are canonical, established once by the
projection's own pure constructor, and every value- or datum-keyed observation consumes the container
through the canonicalising projection as its only door.* Discharged by the tower itself, an
obligation only at the constructor that accepts foreign bytes, by the identical purity argument
`embed` already uses; confirmed on independent derivation from the keying side (`86` section 1.3),
statement P bites at the declarer because `Lowering` is the declared axis, and the container is a
projection's own output on which an obligation would be keyed finer than any declarer's identity.
`materialise : Container -> Bytes` has the container, not the carrier, as its domain, which makes its
"pure relabelling" claim unconditionally true at both layouts, rather than quietly assuming the
stored width is a multiple of eight.

**The level ordering is a declaration-site refusal, compiled**: `W_F <= W_S <= W_C` fails at
declaration with `E0080` in the `ByteCap`/`ShortCap` coverage shape (`83_probes/probe_3`).

**`Layout::Bitpacked` selects the granularity at which the container level exists, and zero inter-
value padding is a theorem, not an obligation anyone discharges.** Under `Dense` the container is per
value, the dispatched primitive. Under `Bitpacked` it is per group: `P = 8/gcd(W_S, 8)` elements in
`G = W_S * P / 8` whole bytes, and `G * 8 = W_S * P` follows from `G`'s own definition by algebra
alone, checked at every width 1 through 57 rather than sampled, and proved algebraically (`86`
section 1.5). A partial tail group's bits are container padding at column granularity,
canonicalised by the packer's own pure constructor, the identical argument at a coarser grain.

**File 80's nine-bit companion (item I, sentence swap one, stated at the top of this document): the
probe is right, its original characterisation is not.** It measured the ungoverned container level,
not statement P's content, because at every eight-bit model the fields' extent equals the container
width and every padding claim in the corpus was checked where it is vacuously true; the nine-bit
model's seven padding bits sit at `[W_S, W_C)`, where statement P is vacuous by definition, and what
the probe's 65,024-pair misordering matrix actually demonstrated is that **no statement had a name
for that region at all**, which section 1's new statement C now closes.

**The niche collision: statement 0 stands unamended; the resolution is a second sealed vocabulary,
not a widened quantifier.** File 84's biased-niche lowering (store a bounded numeral's datum shifted
by one in `core::num::NonZero`, spending the excluded pattern, halving a refusing column at nine
extra instructions across sixty-four elements with no branch) collided, compiled, with statement 0's
own hardening: the niche's excluded pattern is reachable through an ordinary, compiling, unsafe
transmute (a warn-level lint, not a refusal), and a niche-shaped domain of size `2^w - k` is never a
power of two, so no field shrink expresses it. File 86's proposed repair, widen the quantifier to
"every inhabitant of the carrier type," was attacked and killed: field-shrinking closes a decode with
a hard `E0004` under any code path, safe or unsafe, because the excluded state does not exist as a
value of the type at all; a validity range closes it only under a warn-level lint the optimiser is
licensed to assume and any unsafe code anywhere can violate silently, a difference in kind, not
degree, compiled both ways (`87_probes/probe_1`, `probe_1b`). **Ratified at `90b`: statement 0's
hardening stands exactly as file 80 wrote it. The "quantify over inhabitants" amendment is dead. The
resolution is `NicheCarrier` (section 1.12), a second, closed, sealable vocabulary discharging its
own trusted-base obligation once, per numeral choosing a niche carrier, rather than a peer of field-
shrinking inside statement 0's own scope.** Section 1.12 covers the seal; its remaining scope
condition (the carrier's top pattern must be spare, or the shift must not wrap, checked at every
design witness including E4M3) is this one entry's own proof obligation.

**The mutation gap: real, present, not speculative, adopted as a two-tier repair, second read
owed.** The only-door rule as written (`68:138-139`) covers observation, not mutation, because a
value-keyed or datum-keyed read must pass through the canonicalising projection but nothing says
what a write may leave behind for that projection to later mask. This is not hypothetical:
`repr(transparent)` on the carrier makes every bit writable through a transmute for the identical
reason it already makes every bit observable, and this design has already, independently, committed
to shipping raw `from_raw`/`to_raw`-shaped mutable doors (`arvo-always-optimal-internals.md:36`,
`arvo-toolbox-not-policer.md:82`). Compiled and executed: an ordinary safe `&mut` into a padding
region, needing no niche and no unsafe transmute at all, leaves every value-keyed read correct while
the raw byte image and any raw-byte-keyed digest decorrelate silently from a fresh construction of
the identical value (`87_probes/probe_2`). This is a gap in the only-door rule's own general
statement, inherited uniformly by all four of its known instances (cohorts, padding, the digest
projection, the container level), not a defect local to statement C. Op held the one-clause fix at
`86b` for its own derivation; **adopted as a working shape at `90b`, second read owed**: a two-tier
repair, in the same shape section 1.12's niche resolution used. The safe surface never exposes a raw
accessor below the fields' own width, which closes the gap unconditionally and structurally for the
entire safe surface at zero new mechanism, because every safe mutation re-embeds through the same
pure constructor every ordinary construction already uses. The unsafe escape hatch the design will
ship regardless carries the re-canonicalisation obligation as a documented, trusted-base postcondition
on the door itself, worded exactly parallel to `Crosses`'s own statement P, one entry, audited once,
unenforced, because Rust has no reference-expiry hook and pretending one exists is the exact shape of
trusted-but-unchecked claim this review has already spent a stretch correcting once (`78:723`, the
`UFixed<0, F>::ONE` defect). **Adopted for the design's own text at `90b`: the general clause behind
both repairs**, `repr(transparent)`, together with any hand-off of the underlying representation
(a niche's validity range or a raw mutable accessor), moves a claim from the provable tier to the
trusted-base tier regardless of which language mechanism makes the hand-off convenient, and the
design's obligation is to name the hand-off once, in trusted-base vocabulary, never in the provable
tier's words. Whether the workspace-wide perimeter rule gains this clause is op's own hand, with a
lean toward yes.

**The digest contract, adopted in full at `90b`, and it needs no new mechanism.** A digest is a
composition of the tower's own established canonicalising projections, chosen by which equality it
is paired with, at exactly two useful stopping points. A **datum-keyed digest** masks the container
straight to the fields' own width, undoing statement C and statement P in one operation because both
canonicalise to the identical fixed value; compiled at the ratified `Warm`/`Precise` shape (fields 13,
stored 26, container 32), a digest masking only to the stored width is not immune to statement-P
dirt, confirming the correction has real content once a genuine three-level shape exists to test it
(`88_probes/probe_1`). A **value-keyed digest** additionally applies `Encoding::Canonical`'s own
class collapse, which reads the datum's content rather than discarding a fixed bit range and is
therefore never a masking operation, at any construction discipline, compiled (`88_probes/probe_3`).
A third stopping point paired with raw carrier identity is not a digest in this chapter's own sense,
since carrier identity has no equality this design should ever key facts on (`78:142-144`); what
remains there is the byte image itself, already fully specified by statements 0, P, and C, with a
hash applied for a consumer's own diagnostic convenience outside the digest law's scope.

**The mutation gap reaches only the free shortcut, and the gap is smaller under `Bitpacked` than
under `Dense`.** A datum-keyed column digest of honestly-constructed data is a theorem, not an
obligation, for both layouts: hash the raw contiguous byte buffer directly, at zero per-element cost,
because every padding bit at every level is canonical by construction along any safe path (compiled,
`88_probes/probe_2`, `probe_5`). Under `Bitpacked` the dirt surface this theorem protects is strictly
smaller (one tail-group region for the whole column, not one region per element), because interior
groups carry no padding at all under the ratified single meaning. This is exactly where the mutation
gap has real teeth, and only there: a column never exposed through a raw accessor below the fields'
own width gets the shortcut as a structural theorem; one that has been, or might have been, so
exposed inherits the raw door's own trusted-base postcondition, unenforced, the identical accounting
as any other hand-laid trusted fact. A **value-keyed** column digest never gets this shortcut, at any
construction discipline, because `Encoding::Canonical` reads content rather than discarding a mask,
and pays a genuine per-element canonicalisation pass; under `Bitpacked` that pass costs the same
order as the ratified decode multiple (1.29x to 1.50x, section 1.22's own bitpacking figures below).
The choice between the two digest kinds is a real cost fork, not a style preference, and the design
should expose it as a named choice per `arvo-toolbox-not-policer.md`, never pick one silently.
Freshness of a memoized digest across an ordinary safe mutation is explicitly out of scope: arvo
ships the pure digest function, not a cache, and invalidation is a consumer policy question with no
more design content than memoizing any other derived fact.

**Bitpacking's cost against `Layout::Dense`: 1.50x on a sum, 1.29x under per-element work, not
4.6x.** The multiple ratified at the eighth consolidation (4.6x to 5.5x sequential) was a property of
the decoder computing at runtime what the type already knew, not of the layout: the period, group
stride, window offsets, per-lane shifts, mask, load width, and read headroom are all functions of
the stored width alone and belong as associated consts, per the pricing pillar's own clause above.
Corrected and re-measured on a separately written harness, cross-checked against three prior errors
(a cache figure wrong by a factor of four, an instruction-count table describing a standalone probe
rather than the benched program and wrong by three to five times in the same direction so the wrong
ratio matched the true one, and a decoder silently wrong above 25 bits, now pinned by an assertion):
with the decode plan on the type, a plain column sum costs **1.50x** dense and a sum of a per-element
function costs **1.29x** dense with a decode that gathers into natural-width lanes (`81`, corrected
figures ratified at `82b`). Which decode is optimal is a joint property of the layout and the
consumer's own operation, so the design ships both and picks on the operation's own lane width. The
same period is the column's write granule: adjacent values share bytes, so no element is
independently writable, and a consumer partitioning the column for parallel writes must place every
boundary on a multiple of `P`. The measured multiple prices decode in a compute-bound regime where a
smaller footprint buys nothing; `Cold`'s own footprint intent is realised only under bandwidth
contention, which no artifact in this review measures and which the bench harness's own input path
(materialising a flat input on the stack, capped below the host's own L2) cannot currently build at
all.

**Arvo's own byte-image guarantee is a same-process, same-build-target fact, not a wire format**,
unchanged from the eighth consolidation. Cross-target portability needs the format's identity to
travel with the bytes or agree out of band; every one of its constituents is already a closed,
const-derivable bundle. No mechanism is proposed.

*Grounded on: ratified (`77b` via `78:552-556`, `78:142-144`, `78:643-648`, `68:138-139`, `90b`),

### 1.26 Capacity, resolved: a parameter, not an event, one seal, one derived operation

**Capacity is a type-level parameter that establishes an index domain, of the same kind as
`Precision`, `Exponent`, and `StoredWidth`, and not a value that flows through an operation and can
land outside its own domain.** Op's own reframing at `77b` reads two literalisms into one sentence:
capacity as a supremum of a numeral's own value set (which the far-point rule already governs), or
capacity as the supremum of a collection's valid index set, `{0, ..., N-1}`, whose lastmost member is
`N-1`, one predecessor step off the count itself. Two independent reads settle it (`79`, confirmed
independently by `80` and `82`): the far-point rule's own subject is what an *operation* resolves to
when its true result falls outside a value set a *parameter* already established; capacity is the
parameter, not the operation's own subject, and the far-point rule fires one construction downstream,
at index arithmetic.

**Capacity's value is a direct instance of the tower's `Nat`, not a second encoding and not an
alias.** `Capacity: Nat`, one seal, one ordering, one arithmetic, inherited wholesale, with `SIZE`
reading straight through the supertrait; compiled, zero feature gates, and exercised at all four uses
the far-point analogy names (array length, index-bound membership, an iteration terminator, an
arity), each consuming the same `Nat`-typed count through the same projection. This closes the two-
encodings finding from the seventh consolidation at the value layer completely.

**The array grammar is a paired, non-derived fact, forced by the language, not chosen.** No
expression of `[T; K]` computed from a type-level `Nat` exists under the permitted feature set (the
naive form and its rustc-suggested successor both refuse, citing `generic_const_exprs` and, past
that, an inductive step `min_generic_const_args` cannot express either). `Slot<N, const K: usize>`
pairs the `Nat` (direct) and a bare literal `K` (the language-forced position), checked to agree in
an inline const block at the one construction door. This is the crossing contract's own value-versus-
carrier split, restated at a different boundary, not invented for this one.

**The far-point rule's shape recurs one layer down, at index arithmetic, as `arvo-container`'s out-
of-bounds resolution, and this is genuinely new construction.** The last valid index below a
capacity is its predecessor, total over nonzero capacities by the identical supremum-over-the-
ordered-set logic, and undefined (refused at the type level, not clamped to a sentinel) over an
empty capacity, matching the far-point rule's own NaN-exclusion shape exactly rather than merely
resembling it: `last_index` is generic over `C: Pos + Dec`, and `Z` implements neither, so
`last_index::<Z>()` fails at the trait bound before monomorphisation, with rustc naming the missing
