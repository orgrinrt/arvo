# Op's twenty-first checkpoint: the separation requirement, and two things held

**Date:** 2026-08-05
**Position:** after `86_giesen_the_levels_assembled.md`. **Required reading** with the consolidation.

## The separation requirement is adopted, as a requirement on models

**Adopted.** A claim about a distinction is checked where that distinction is **nonvacuous**, and every
model states what it separates.

Not a fifth design rule. The four already assign homes; this checks that a home was tested rather than
assumed, and a model that cannot separate the thing being claimed is not evidence for the claim. Saying
what a model separates costs one sentence.

The evidence is seven instances across this stretch where a wrong subject survived review because it
coincided with the right one at exactly the widths and shapes everyone happened to build. Two of them
produced numbers whose *ratio* matched the truth while both absolutes were wrong by three to five times
in the same direction, which is the shape that makes the defect invisible. The instances span design,
measurement and the review's own bookkeeping, which is why the requirement is stated about models rather
than about any one domain.

The assembly's own framing is worth keeping with it, because it renames what this stretch was doing: the
common move is **wrong subject, not wrong binding time**. A quantity was being predicated of one thing
when it belongs to another.

## Held: the collision resolution goes back for a second read

Two proposals from different files, days apart, collide and the collision is compiled. The
biased-storage lowering makes an optional or fallible value the same width as the value, halving a
refusing column. The hardened crossing precondition says partiality is expressed by shrinking the fields
rather than by a side-condition on the domain, and the trick's domain (65,535 members) is not expressible
as any field width, asserted at every width.

The offered resolution quantifies over the carrier type's **inhabitants** rather than its patterns,
admitting compiler-enforced validity ranges as a second and stronger partiality mechanism. It is itself
one-pass, on a collision between two one-pass proposals, and it touches a ratified precondition.

**Op's call: second read before anything moves.**

## Held: the mutation perimeter gap wants its derivation, not a clause

The design's only-door rule covers observation and not mutation, so a mutable door onto a container
silently decorrelates a value's byte image from its digest while every observation of the value stays
correct. One clause was offered.

**Op's call: hold, and see the derivation first.** A perimeter statement does not change on a clause
proposed in passing, and this review has had three successive passes each believe a perimeter was closed
when it was not.
