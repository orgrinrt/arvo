## What this crate is

The derivation from a declared signature to a placement. A placement says where the bits sit. It says
nothing about what any operation computes, and no operation in the design reads it.

The crate exists because the floor made those two statements true at once, and they had been argued
about as though only one could be:

> every operation the design declares is a function of the declared width and never of the machine
> carrier [...] The footprint is nonetheless observable, through exactly one observation the design
> does not own, the host language's layout observation on a sized type

So the carrier is nameable, because otherwise the footprint is not observable and there is a ratified
intent about footprint that would denote nothing. And no operation reads it, because otherwise
behaviour is stated over the container, which is refuted: a container-stated numeral returns 8192
where MATLAB `fi` at thirteen bits under wrap documents 0.

## The derivation is a placement rather than a semantics

This is the sentence to keep. The derivation chooses where the value goes; it never chooses what the
value means. Two placements of one declared signature compute the same function, and they are
distinguishable only through an observation this design does not own.

## Its output count moves with occupancy, and that is two arms

`dimension::occupancy` is the axis: `sole` where the value is the sole logical occupant of its carrier
allocation, `shared` where it is not.

**At sole occupancy the derivation has one output: the carrier.** The host language supplies a
standalone sized type, the layout observation exists, and the footprint is observable through it and
through nothing else.

**At shared occupancy it has three: the carrier, the access width and the stride.** A per-element
footprint does not exist to observe at all, because the element is reached through its carrier and no
size-bounded contract ranges over it. What is observable is the allocation's stride, which is a
property of the column and not of the element.

The three are not three coordinates of one answer. The carrier answers where the bits live, the access
width answers how wide a read reaches, and the stride answers how far to the next element. The access
width is not a function of the carrier and neither is the converse, measured under both packing rules
with controls firing.

**These are two arms over a declared region, not one number with a special case.** That is the shape
the whole design is made of, and this is the crate's own instance of it: `ruling::arms_over_regions_are_the_fundamental_heart`
states the approach directly, and `derive_sole` / `derive_shared` are what it names when it says the full
space is filled by arms holding where they are optimal and nowhere else, composed, rather than by one
statement uniform across the dimensions.

## The footprint is a ratified objective, so the derivation is not free

`ruling::cold_is_for_cold_paths_and_cold_storage` is ratified and its subject is the footprint: the
storage-minimising intent aggressively minimises and bitpacks, and stays small for memory or disk.
That intent has a subject only because the layout observation exists, which is why the observability
half of the floor is load-bearing rather than a curiosity.

`ruling::cold_is_not_obliged_to_be_slow` bounds it from the other side and is stated as a test rather
than a preference: the storage-minimising path may use the same paths the speed-first one uses, not
because its intent demands them but because nothing in its intent fights them, and where a path fights
the intent it is not for that strategy. So the leeway to be inefficient goes unused whenever a fast
path costs nothing in residency.

Neither of those names a strategy this crate depends on. The objective is what the ladder is keyed on
and the strategy is a binding onto it, which is `arvo-strategy`'s subject.

## Two placements of one signature are two types, and that is correct

A consequence worth stating because it looks like a defect and is the design.

At sole occupancy, two placements of one declared signature are distinct in layout and identical in
denotation. They are therefore two types to the host language, and a homogeneous container built from
both is refused. That refusal is right: a column is keyed on the placement, and at shared occupancy
there is no per-element footprint to key on anything else.

The two are the same primitive under denotational sameness, which is the relation that licenses
substitution inside a composite, and different under representational sameness, which is the one that
licenses memory reinterpretation. Only the denotational relation is a congruence, so only it licenses
substitution, and the crate keeps them separate rather than collapsing them into one word.

## What this crate does not decide

The access ladder is known to run out of native containers before the carrier ladder does, measured at

**And what the format contract asks of a member of its inventory.** This crate declares one, below,
and what a format owes and what refuses it are stated where that contract is. Nothing about the

disagree and only one of them would be the rule.

## The laws this crate owes tests for

**No declared operation reads a placement.** This is the crate's whole reason to exist and it is the
one law here that is structural rather than computational: the type of an operation does not mention
the placement, so a violation does not compile. The test is a contract test, and if it cannot be
written the separation is not real.

**Two placements of one declared signature agree on every declared operation**, over the whole matrix
of signatures rather than a sample.

**They disagree on the layout observation at sole occupancy**, which is the other half and is what
makes the footprint objective denote something.

**At shared occupancy no per-element size observation exists.** Asserted by construction: there is no
expression that reaches one. A packed column with elements straddling carrier boundaries roundtrips
exactly, and the only reachable size observation is the allocation's.

**The output count is one at sole and three at shared**, and the three are independent: the access
width is not a function of the carrier, nor the converse.

**The derivation reads a format this crate declares itself.** The subject is a function from a declared
signature to a placement and a signature is a format paired with an adaptation, so a format written
here rather than borrowed from the four shipped points is what says the derivation is over the contract
rather than over the instances that happen to sit beside it. It supplies all of them, an ambient
domain, a quantum law and a slot range that nothing ships plus the phase the format carries, because
reusing the shipped parts exercises the one coordinate `Format` itself demands and nothing under it.

**Where that declaration lives is the point rather than an incidental.** `arvo-format` introduces the
numeric category, so the bare-primitive lints skip it whole and a format declared there is written
under a rule with nothing to refuse. This crate is read by those lints like every other consumer, so a
coordinate written as a host integer here stops the gate and the declaration standing is the
assertion. `proposal::the_concept_is_closed_and_the_inventory_is_open`, ratified through
`ruling::the_format_spine_is_canon`, is the clause it is about: admission is a check rather than a
negotiation, and a check that runs only where nothing can fail it is a negotiation with whoever reads
the lint config.

## The surface this design names

`Occupancy` is the axis, with `Sole` and `Shared` as its two values, and it is the
declared dimension the two arms gate on.

`Placement` is what a derivation returns: a `carrier`, an `access` and a `stride`,
each an `arvo_format::Width`, together with the `occupancy` that says which arm
produced it. `output_count` reports one at sole and three at shared, and
`footprint_is_observable` reports whether a per-element footprint exists at all.

`Carrier` is the contract a machine container satisfies, carrying its `BITS`.
`Carrier8`, `Carrier16`, `Carrier32` and `Carrier64` are the members the host
offers, and `LADDER` is them
in order, narrowest first. The ladder is what `narrowest_carrier` walks, and it
returns `Width::NONE` where it runs out rather than widening past what it has.

`Objective` is what the derivation is keyed on, with `Footprint` and `Access` as
its values. It is deliberately not a strategy: a strategy binds a name onto one of
these, and that binding is `arvo-strategy`'s.

`declared_width` reads the declared signature's slot range and nothing else, which
is the mechanical form of the rule that the derivation is a function of the
declaration. `derive_sole` and `derive_shared` are the two arms.

## What the objective selects, which is nothing at sole occupancy and the packing at shared

The derivation ladder is keyed on the objective, and this states what the two objectives actually
differ in, because a key that selects one answer everywhere is not a key.

**At sole occupancy they coincide, and that is a result rather than a collapse.** The value is the
sole logical occupant of its allocation, so there is no other element to trade against: the narrowest
carrier that holds the declared width is both the smallest footprint and the widest native access. One
answer for both objectives, asserted as an equality rather than left to look like an oversight.

**At shared occupancy they differ, and the difference is the packing.**

| Objective | Stride | Access | What it is doing |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Footprint` | the declared width | wide enough to cover an element that straddles a carrier boundary | packing, so elements share carriers and no bits are spent on alignment |
| `Access` | the carrier | the carrier | one element per carrier, padding rather than straddling, so every read is a single native access |

The footprint arm is derived from `ruling::cold_is_for_cold_paths_and_cold_storage`, which is ratified
and says the storage-minimising objective aggressively minimises and bitpacks. The access arm is what
not bitpacking means: an element that never crosses a boundary needs no wider read and no shift pair
to assemble.

**The three outputs at shared occupancy are what make the difference expressible.** With one output
there would be nothing for the objectives to disagree about, which is why this is stated here and not
in the sole-occupancy arm.

## The objective is a type, and the associated const on it is what the arms read

The objective reaches the derivation as a **type-level marker** bound by `ObjectiveKind`, not as a
const generic and not as an argument.

```rust
pub trait ObjectiveKind {
    const OBJECTIVE: Objective;
}

pub const fn derive_shared<S: DeclaredSignature, O: ObjectiveKind>() -> Placement
```

**Two properties have to hold at once and only this shape holds both.**

**The branch erases.** The arms match on `O::OBJECTIVE`, an associated const of a generic parameter,
which is a constant once the call is monomorphised, so the unselected arm is gone before the backend
sees it. That is what `ruling::never_a_runtime_check_and_one_lowered_path` asks for.

**A generic consumer composes.** A consumer generic over the strategy writes
`derive_sole::<F, S::Objective>()`, an ordinary generic call with an associated type in a type
position. That is `arvo-strategy`'s `Strategy::Objective` reaching this crate's arms: the strategy
crate supplies the const value the match here is gated on, and it is the pairing of the two crates,
not either alone, that makes `O::OBJECTIVE` a predicate rather than a runtime read.

### Why a const generic cannot carry it, recorded so it is not tried again

A const generic argument may not depend on a generic type parameter. `derive_sole::<F, { S::OBJECTIVE }>()`
is refused with `generic parameters may not be used in const operations`, and the feature rustc
suggests for it is on the forbidden list.

So a const generic buys the erasure and **severs composition**: only a call site naming a concrete
preset can derive anything, and a consumer generic over the strategy cannot reach the ladder at all.
The marker buys the erasure without that cost, and needs no unstable feature where the const generic
needed one.

**A design that stated the erasure and not the composition is what let this happen once already**, so
both are stated here.
